## Instalment 1 — UI and POP Definitions and Epidemiology, Care-Seeking, ICS Terminology, DIAPPERS, Frail-Elderly Algorithm (pp. 2166–2168)

Covers: the unheaded chapter-opening epidemiology, Definition and Impact of UI and POP, the bridging prose on knowledge and care-seeking, Table 105.1, Diagnostic Evaluation — General Considerations, Table 105.2 and Fig. 105.1.

### The numbers to lock in
- **About one woman in four** has POP, UI or FI: UI 17%, FI 9.4%, symptomatic POP 2.9%.
- **UI prevalence** is about 37% in women over 60 worldwide (meta-analysis). NHANES figures rose from 38% (1999–2000) to 50%, then 53%, then 61.8% (2015–2018). The range is 9.7% (Mexico) to 80% (frail elderly).
- **Commonest subtype:** isolated SUI (45.9%) > UUI (31.1%) > MUI (18.1%). UUI is rising in women aged 60 and over.
- **UI in pregnancy:** 9% in the first trimester, rising to 34% in the third.
- **Cumulative surgery risk by age 80:** SUI 13.6%, POP 12.6%.
- **About 50% of parous women** have anatomical POP. The diagnosis needs symptoms.
- **Commonest POP site:** anterior > posterior > apex.
- **Vaginal bulge** is the most POP-specific symptom and the one most likely to resolve after repair.
- **Only 18–50%** of women with PFDs seek care. Delay is 1 to ≥5 years.
- **Recurrence risk:** younger age and POP-Q stage 3–4. **Protective against primary POP:** caesarean delivery and smoking.
- **Frail-elderly algorithm (6th ICI 2021):** drain by catheter if PVR is 200–500 mL, then reassess. Do not treat asymptomatic bacteriuria.
- **Eponyms:** DIAPPERS (Resnick 1984) — transient causes of UI.
- **Traps:** anatomical POP is not the same as disease. Smoking protects against primary POP. Nocturnal enuresis is voiding, not leakage. Insensible UI is not the same as disability-associated UI.

## Instalment 2 — History, HPI, Occult and De Novo SUI, Compassionate Care, PMH, Medications, Genetics, SDOH (pp. 2168–2171)

Covers: History, History of Present Illness, Compassionate Care, Past Medical and Surgical History, Medications (Table 105.3), Other Contributing Factors, Social Determinants of Health, and Key Points boxes 1 and 2.

### The numbers to lock in
- **A positive SUI history** predicts urodynamic SUI in 73–74% (systematic reviews). With a negative history the chance is 16–34%.
- **POP becomes symptomatic at or beyond the hymen:** stage 2 is −1 to +1 cm and stage 3 is >+1 cm.
- **SUI coexists** in about 40% of women with POP. **Occult SUI** is found in 33.5% of vaginal POP repairs. **De novo SUI** occurs in 4.4–50% and has no ICS definition.
- **A strong doctor–patient relationship** raises medication adherence (bottom line; the study figures are dropped). Compassion lasting under 1 minute is still effective.
- **Local oestrogen** may improve UI. **Systemic oestrogen, including the oral contraceptive pill,** may worsen it.
- **Drug traps:** α-blockers worsen SUI. ACE inhibitors cause cough, which worsens SUI. Cholinesterase inhibitors cause UUI. SGLT2 inhibitors cause polyuria and UTI. Lithium causes diabetes insipidus. Glitazones cause oedema, then nocturnal polyuria.
- **Traps:** POP repair does not reliably cure OAB, so OAB treatment may need to be staged. Urinary symptoms may come before a neurological diagnosis.

## Instalment 3 — General and Frailty Examination, SUI Examination, POP Examination and POP-Q, Mesh Complications, Neurological Examination, Trauma-Informed Care, Chaperones (pp. 2170–2175)

Covers: Physical Examination (general, frailty, Table 105.4), Physical Examination for SUI, Physical Examination for POP (Figs. 105.2–105.4, Table 105.5), mesh (Table 105.6), neurological examination, Trauma-Informed Care, Chaperones, and Key Points box 3.

### The numbers to lock in
- **Frailty assessment** before surgery at age ≥65. The Fried index has 5 domains. The Clinical Frailty Scale has 9 points.
- **AUA/SUFU 2023:** demonstrate SUI objectively with a comfortably full bladder. Stand the patient if the supine test is negative.
- **CST at half functional capacity:** specificity 90%, sensitivity 83%. At a comfortably full bladder: specificity 58%, sensitivity 77%. These are study data and do not print; the bottom line is that half capacity is more specific.
- **Urethral hypermobility** is a Q-tip angle >30°. VUME is the routine test. The Q-tip test is for trials and teaching only.
- **POP-Q:** 9 points, with the hymen at 0. Aa and Ap range from −3 to +3. Ba and Bp range from −3 to +tvl. Stages: 0; I < −1; II −1 to +1; III > +1 to < +(tvl − 2); IV ≥ +(tvl − 2).
- **Fig. 105.4:** Bp +5 and tvl 8 give stage III. C at −6 means the cuff has descended 2 cm. The stage is derived here; the legend does not state it.
- **Posterior blade expelled** means loss of level I support (DeLancey).
- **BCR:** S2–S4. Present in 70% of women and 100% of men.
- **Child sexual abuse:** 1 in 4 women and 1 in 13 men. Genital examination triggers flashbacks in about 50% of trauma survivors.
- **Eponyms:** Baden-Walker (grades 1–4, ordinal). Bump/POP-Q (1996, quantitative). DeLancey levels I–III. Fried Frailty Index.
- **Traps:** unreduced POP masks SUI. An absent BCR in a woman is not by itself pathological. Prominence (no breach) differs from separation (breach). Extrusion differs from perforation. A sinus tract has no visible mesh.

## Instalment 4 — Guidelines for Supplemental Tests, Table 105.7, Bladder Diary, Questionnaires and ICIQ, Nomograms, Pad Tests, Dye Tests, Urinalysis, PVR (pp. 2174–2178)

Covers: Supplemental Evaluation, Table 105.7, Symptom Quantification Instruments (Bladder Diary, Questionnaires with Table 105.8, Nomograms, Pad Tests), Dye Testing, Urinalysis, Postvoid Residual, and Key Points box 4.

### The numbers to lock in
- **UA and PVR in most patients with UI.** AUA OAB guideline: UA is required and PVR is optional. AUA/SUFU SUI guideline: UA and PVR are both required.
- **Table 105.7 trigger:** POP-Q stage ≥3 without SUI on reduction.
- **ICIQ** is available in more than 30 languages. QUID has 6 items. OAB-V8 has 8 items. OAB-SS has 7 items. The ICIQ bladder diary runs for 3 days.
- **1-hour pad test:** 500 mL within 15 minutes, then a 30-minute walk. Mild 1–10 g, moderate 11–50 g, severe >50 g. Gain under 1.4 g may be non-urinary.
- **24-hour pad test:** mild 4–20 g, moderate 21–74 g, severe ≥75 g. Gain under 4.4 g may be non-urinary. It is more reproducible but does not predict urodynamic SUI.
- **Phenazopyridine** 100–200 mg three times daily. Double-dye test: orange means ureterovaginal, blue means vesicovaginal.
- **PVR cut-offs** range from 30 to 200 mL. In asymptomatic women the median is 19 mL, 95% are under 100 mL, and only 15% are over 50 mL.
- **Traps:** pad counts do not reflect pad weight. A single PVR proves nothing. PFDI measures symptoms; PFIQ measures impact.

## Instalment 5 — Cystoscopy, UDS Pointer, VCUG, Renal/Bladder/Translabial Ultrasound, MRI and HMO, Urethral Diverticulum, Management Overview, Conclusions (pp. 2178–2179)

Covers: Cystoscopy, Urodynamic Studies (pointer only), Radiographic Imaging (VCUG, Ultrasonography, MRI, Fig. 105.5), Management — UI Treatment Overview, and Conclusions.

### The numbers to lock in
- **Cystoscopy** is not routine in index SUI. In low-risk preoperative women the yield is about 2% (and 7% in an older unselected series), so it is not required.
- **Translabial US** is the only reliable way to image mesh. CT and MRI are poor for mesh.
- **MRI** is the standard of care for urethral diverticulum. The 3 Ds are dysuria, postvoid dribbling and dyspareunia.
- **HMO:** the H-line measures hiatus width, the M-line measures levator descent from the PCL, and POP means an organ below the H-line.
- **Traps:** translabial US is not the same as pelvic US. Imaging is not needed in uncomplicated UI.
