Chapter 105 — Evaluation and Management of Women With Urinary Incontinence and Pelvic Organ Prolapse

If you read nothing else

  • POP (pelvic organ prolapse) diagnosis needs symptoms — ~50% parous women have anatomical POP.
  • Vaginal bulge = most POP-specific symptom; most likely to resolve after repair.
  • POP symptomatic at/beyond hymen (stage ≥2).
  • SUI (stress urinary incontinence) index patient pre-surgery: UA (urinalysis) + PVR (postvoid residual) + objective SUI demonstration (AUA/SUFU [American Urological Association/Society of Urodynamics, Female Pelvic Medicine and Urogenital Reconstruction] 2023).
  • Cystoscopy, UDS (urodynamics), imaging not routine in index SUI.
  • POP-Q (POP Quantification) stage II = leading edge −1 to +1 cm; III >+1 to <+(tvl [total vaginal length] −2); IV ≥+(tvl−2).
  • Occult SUI ≈ one-third of vaginal POP repairs; SUI coexists in ~40% POP.
  • Urethral hypermobility = >30° from horizontal.
  • Translabial US (ultrasound) = only reliable mesh imaging; MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) = standard for urethral diverticulum.
  • Single PVR unreliable — repeat; no agreed cut-off (30–200 mL).

Definition and Impact

  • PFDs (pelvic floor disorders) = UI (urinary incontinence), FI (faecal incontinence), POP, LUT (lower urinary tract) sensory/emptying, defecatory dysfunction.
  • POP, UI or FI in ~1 in 4 women; rising (age, obesity).
  • UI (ICS, International Continence Society): complaint of involuntary loss of urine.
  • Commonest subtype: isolated SUI > UUI (urgency UI) > MUI (mixed UI); UUI rising ≥60 years.
  • UI all types ↑: age, obesity, smoking, comorbidity, postmenopausal HRT (hormone replacement therapy).
  • Vaginal delivery → SUI; caesarean/activity → less UUI.
  • Pregnancy UI: ~10% first → ~33% third trimester.
  • Black women: more UUI/DO (detrusor overactivity); White/Mexican-American: more SUI.
  • Lifetime surgery risk by 80: SUI 13.6%, POP 12.6%.
  • Commonest POP site: anterior > posterior > apex.
  • POP recurrence risk: younger age, POP-Q stage 3–4.
  • POP protective: caesarean delivery, smoking.
  • Care-seeking: only 18–50%; delay 1 to ≥5 years.
  • Occult SUI (ICS) = SUI only on POP reduction.
  • Insensible UI: aware of leak, not how/when.
  • DIAPPERS (Resnick): transient/reversible UI causes.

Diagnostic Evaluation

  • SUI = urethral; UUI = bladder; MUI both.
  • MUI: treat most bothersome component first.
  • Positive SUI history → urodynamic SUI in ~3/4.
  • Splinting → high-grade POP; high-grade anterior POP → obstructed voiding.
  • POP repair may improve OAB (overactive bladder) — not always; stage OAB treatment.
  • De novo SUI: no ICS definition; after POP surgery, no preop occult SUI.
  • Urinary symptoms may precede neurological diagnosis.
  • Local oestrogen may improve UI; systemic (incl. OCP, oral contraceptive pill) may worsen.
  • Frail elderly (6th ICI, International Consultation on Incontinence): catheter drainage if PVR 200–500 mL, then reassess.
  • Frail elderly red flags → refer: pain, haematuria, recurrent UTI (urinary tract infection), pelvic mass, irradiation, prolapse beyond introitus, fistula.
  • Do not treat asymptomatic bacteriuria.
  • α-blockers → ↓ outlet → worse SUI.
  • Cholinesterase inhibitors → ↑ contractility → UUI.
  • ACE (angiotensin-converting enzyme) inhibitors → cough → SUI.
  • SGLT2 (sodium–glucose cotransporter 2) inhibitors → polyuria, ↑ UTI; lithium → diabetes insipidus.
  • Glitazones → oedema → nocturnal polyuria.

Physical Examination

  • Frailty assessment: preoperative, age ≥65.
  • Fried Frailty Index: 5 domains incl. grip, walking speed.
  • Clinical Frailty Scale: 9-point pictorial, rapid.
  • Atrophic: pale, flat, dry, no rugae; oestrogenised: pink, rugated.
  • SUI demonstration: comfortably full bladder; stand if supine negative.
  • CST (cough stress test) at half functional capacity (largest diary void) > full bladder for specificity.
  • POP present → reduce anterior wall before SUI testing.
  • Q-tip: hypermobility >30°; now trials/teaching only.
  • VUME (visual urethral mobility examination) = routine; vaginal swab ≡ Q-tip.
  • POP exam: lithotomy + standing (foot on step).
  • Posterior blade expelled on strain → level I (cardinal–uterosacral) loss → vault prolapse.
  • Blade stays → isolated rectocele/enterocele.
  • DeLancey: levels I–III of vaginal support.
  • Baden-Walker: grades 1–4 relative to hymen; ordinal.
  • POP-Q (1996): 9 points, cm, relative to hymen, on Valsalva.
  • Aa/Ap: 3 cm inside hymen, range −3 to +3.
  • Ba/Bp: mobile leading points, −3 to +tvl.
  • D = posterior fornix; absent after hysterectomy.
  • tvl measured with prolapse reduced.
  • Stage 0: Aa, Ap, Ba, Bp −3; C/D ≤ −(tvl−2).
  • Stage I: leading edge < −1 cm.
  • BCR (bulbocavernosus reflex): S2–S4; present 70% women vs 100% men.
  • Anal tone = S2–S4.
  • IUGA (International Urogynecological Association)/ICS 2011: prominence = no epithelial breach; separation = breach.
  • Extrusion = out through tissue; perforation = into viscus.
  • Sinus tract = fistulous track, no visible mesh.
  • Mesh tenderness documented even without exposure.
  • Genital exam triggers flashbacks in ~50% of trauma survivors.
  • Child sexual abuse: 1 in 4 women, 1 in 13 men.
  • Chaperone: always honour request; use even with companion present.

Supplemental Evaluation

  • UA + PVR: most UI patients.
  • AUA OAB: UA required; PVR, culture, diary optional.
  • AUA OAB: no UDS, cystoscopy or US in uncomplicated patient.
  • Extra SUI tests if: diagnostic doubt, SUI not shown, neurogenic, haematuria/pyuria.
  • Extra SUI tests if: urgency-predominant MUI, high PVR, voiding dysfunction.
  • Extra SUI tests if: POP-Q ≥3 without SUI on reduction.
  • ICIQ-UI SF (ICI Questionnaire, UI short form) correlates with 1-h and 24-h pad tests.
  • QUID (Questionnaire for UI Diagnosis): 6 items, SUI vs UUI. OAB-V8 (OAB awareness tool): primary-care screen.
  • PFDI (Pelvic Floor Distress Inventory) = POP symptoms; PFIQ (Pelvic Floor Impact Questionnaire) = POP QoL (quality of life) impact.
  • ICIQ bladder diary: 3 days.
  • 1-h pad test: 500 mL in <15 min; walk 30 min; restart if voids.
  • 1-h severity: 1–10 g mild; 11–50 moderate; >50 severe.
  • 24-h severity: 4–20 g mild; 21–74 moderate; ≥75 severe.
  • Non-urine gain: <1.4 g (1-h); <4.4 g (24-h).
  • 24-h test more reproducible; does not predict urodynamic SUI.
  • Pad counts don't correlate with pad weight.
  • Oral phenazopyridine 100–200 mg tds → urine orange.
  • Double-dye: orange tampon = ureterovaginal; blue = vesicovaginal.
  • PVR: US preferred over catheter.
  • Asymptomatic women: almost all PVR <100 mL.
  • Cystoscopy: haematuria, urgency/irritative LUTS (lower urinary tract symptoms), prior mesh, radiation.
  • Low-risk pre-op cystoscopy not required.
  • Trabeculation → outlet obstruction and/or DO.

Urodynamic Studies — Imaging

  • No imaging in uncomplicated UI.
  • VCUG (voiding cystourethrogram): VUR (vesicoureteric reflux), urethral diverticulum, subtle SUI.
  • Upper-tract US if: retention, severe POP, fistula, neurogenic detrusor.
  • CT (computed tomography)/MRI poor for mesh unless infected.
  • Urethral diverticulum — 3 Ds: dysuria, dribbling, dyspareunia (often absent).
  • HMO (H-line, M-line, organ prolapse; MRI): H = hiatus width; M = levator descent from PCL (pubococcygeal line).
  • MRI POP = organ beyond H-line.
  • Dynamic MRI: symptoms ≫ findings, prior repair, complex.

Classic traps

  • Trap: anatomical POP = disease — Actually: symptoms required.
  • Trap: POP repair cures UI — Actually: distinct diagnoses; SUI may be unmasked.
  • Trap: older age → POP recurrence — Actually: younger age.
  • Trap: smoking promotes POP — Actually: protective for primary POP.
  • Trap: SUI test with POP unreduced — Actually: prolapse kinks urethra, masks SUI.
  • Trap: absent BCR in woman = neuropathy — Actually: absent in 30% normal women.
  • Trap: pad count = severity — Actually: poor correlation with pad weight.
  • Trap: one low PVR excludes retention — Actually: repeat; single value unreliable.
  • Trap: CT/MRI to find mesh — Actually: translabial US.
  • Trap: α-blockers harmless in women — Actually: worsen SUI.
