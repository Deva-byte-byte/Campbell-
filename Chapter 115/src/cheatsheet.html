Chapter 115 — Electrical Stimulation and Neuromodulation in Storage and Emptying Failure — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

IF YOU READ NOTHING ELSE

  • Sacral nerve stimulation (SNS) = third line for idiopathic overactive bladder (OAB): American (AUA) and European (EAU) guidelines agree.
  • SNS test positive: ≥50% improvement in ≥1 bladder-diary parameter.
  • Retention test positive: catheterisations ↓ ≥50%; post-void residual (PVR) = only objective marker.
  • S3 target: anal bellows + great-toe flexion; S2 calf/foot rotation; S4 no toe.
  • FDA: urge urinary incontinence (UUI) 1997; urgency–frequency + non-obstructive retention (NOUR) 1999.
  • Not FDA approved: bladder pain, pelvic pain, neurogenic bladder, chronic pudendal stimulation.
  • SUmiT: percutaneous tibial nerve stimulation (PTNS) 55% vs sham 21%.
  • PTNS: 34 G needle, 3–4 cm above medial malleolus; 12 × 30 min.
  • ROSETTA: onabotulinumtoxinA (BTX) 200 U ≈ SNS at 2 years; more urinary tract infection (UTI), clean intermittent catheterisation (CIC).
  • Detrusor overactivity (DO) does not predict SNS success.

HISTORY OF ELECTRICAL STIMULATION

  • CE 46 Scribonius Largus: torpedo fish for gout, headache.
  • Direct organ stimulation failed: painful, only local → afferent (indirect) route.
  • Brindley sacral anterior root stimulator (SARS): anterior roots → voiding, defecation, erection; + dorsal rhizotomy for DO.
  • ≈1985 sacral neuromodulation: Tanagho and Schmidt; decreases bladder activity.
  • McGuire 1983: first tibial nerve stimulation for bladder.

PATHOPHYSIOLOGY OF STORAGE AND VOIDING DISORDERS

  • Exclude anatomy first: stones, cancer, fistula, stricture, prolapse, enlarged prostate.
  • Pontine storage centre = L-region, lateral; pontine micturition centre (PMC) = M-region, medial.
  • Voiding: external sphincter relaxes → 1–2 s → detrusor contracts.
  • Sphincter relaxation: PMC → γ-aminobutyric acid (GABA)/glycine interneurons.
  • Sympathetic fires only from ≈60% capacity; not main continence mechanism.
  • Forebrain lesion: timing lost, reflex intact.
  • Filling afferents Aδ; OAB reflex via C fibres (naloxone-sensitive).

WORKING MECHANISM OF ELECTRICAL STIMULATION OF THE LOWER URINARY TRACT

  • Current view: afferents → forebrain awareness/alertness → ↓ PMC firing.
  • SNS recruits ≥1000× more axons, continuously.
  • Underactive bladder: SNS works mainly via sphincter relaxation.

INTRAVAGINAL ELECTRIC STIMULATION

  • For stress incontinence; safe; evidence low quality.

TRANSCUTANEOUS SACRAL DERMATOME STIMULATION

  • Paediatric S3 dermatome: 10 Hz, 20 min daily → less incontinence, UTI, PVR.
  • S3 dermatome: ↑ capacity; cannot acutely suppress DO (conditional fails).

PUDENDAL NERVE STIMULATION

  • S2–S4, Alcock's canal; off-label tined lead, posterior approach (perineal → infection).
  • Salvage after failed SNS; not FDA approved.
  • Indications: idiopathic DO, neurogenic DO, chronic pelvic pain.

DORSAL GENITAL NERVE STIMULATION

  • Experimental; purely sensory; confirm by genitoanal reflex.

TRANSCUTANEOUS TIBIAL NERVE STIMULATION

  • PTNS FDA 2000, National Institute for Health and Clinical Excellence (NICE) 2006.
  • Transcutaneous (TTNS) < percutaneous: skin capacitor. TTNS ≈ PTNS ≈ pelvic floor training; < antimuscarinics.

PERCUTANEOUS TIBIAL NERVE STIMULATION

  • Tibial fibres S2–S3; lower urinary tract innervation L2–S4.
  • SP-6: 6–8 cm above medial malleolus; electroacupuncture 2–15 Hz, 10–20 mA.
  • Settings: 9 V, 0–10 mA, 200 μs, 20 Hz; needle 60–90°.
  • Motor: toe flexion/fanning; sensory: sole tickling.
  • Objective success: urgency/UUI ↓ ≥50% + frequency ↓ ≥25%.
  • Outcomes: subjective ≈60%; diary 47–56%; placebo ≈21%.
  • SUmiT: n = 220, first validated sham, 12 weeks.
  • SUmiT extension: 14-week taper → 77% relapse-free at 3 years.
  • Durability: 53–77% at 2–3 years only with maintenance.
  • vs tolterodine: equal efficacy, fewer side effects.
  • Cochrane: antimuscarinics first; stimulation for antimuscarinic-refractory.
  • Children: PTNS after bladder training, pelvic floor therapy, antimuscarinics fail.
  • Regimen: 12 weeks best supported; 1–3 sessions/week.
  • Prognosis: better without DO; low Short Form-36 (SF-36) mental score → failure.
  • Implant trap: PTNS response ≠ implant response.
  • Implants: Urgent-SQ first (2006), ≈5 cm above malleolus; eCoin primary battery; Protect-PNS wireless.

SACRAL NERVE STIMULATION

  • InterStim Conformité Européenne (CE) mark 1994; rechargeables FDA 2019.
  • OAB definitions: normal ≤7 voids/24 h (1.5–2 L); OAB-dry ≥8 + urgency.
  • Frequency–volume diary first — excludes polydipsia.
  • InSite: SNS 61% vs medical 42% at 6 months.
  • ROSETTA 6 months: UUI −3.9 (BTX) vs −3.3 (SNS).
  • ROSETTA 2 years: UTI 24% vs 10%; CIC 6%; SNS revision 3%, removal 9%.
  • Registered BTX dose idiopathic OAB = 100 U (> placebo at 4 months).
  • NOUR: PVR often ≥100 mL; capacity >500 mL.
  • Bladder pain: offer SNS only with coexisting OAB (objective diary).
  • Neurogenic: success comparable, evidence low, unapproved.
  • NOUR odds ↑: women + psychiatric history; men + prior transurethral prostate resection/bladder neck incision; age ↓.
  • Test for all: direct implant theoretically ≥90% success, but test in practice.
  • Diaries: 3–5-day baseline vs test-period diary.
  • Responses: sensory before motor.
  • Percutaneous nerve evaluation (PNE): 5–7 days; positive ≈60% OAB; not for NOUR.
  • S3 landmarks: 9 cm from coccyx tip, 11 cm from anal verge, 1–2 cm lateral.
  • Staged: percutaneous since 2003; test ≤4 weeks; stage 2 at 2–4 weeks.
  • Staged > PNE conversion to implant; PNE lead dislocates.
  • International Continence Society (ICS) consensus: S3 preferred; responses on all 4 contacts, low amplitude.
  • Prophylaxis: consider meticillin-resistant S. aureus (MRSA) cover; cefazolin + metronidazole.
  • Programming: 210 μs, 10–16 Hz, sensory threshold.
  • Reprogramming: ≈3 in year 1, then ≈0.8/year.
  • Battery: non-rechargeable ≥10 years; rechargeable ≥15 years.
  • Cycling (16 s on/8 s off; 8 h on/16 h off) = continuous.
  • Bilateral not superior to unilateral → not standard.
  • Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI): whole-body 1.5 and 3 T conditional; remove all old components at replacement.

CLASSIC TRAPS

  • Trap: SNS works by pelvic floor contraction — Actually: afferent forebrain modulation.
  • Trap: urodynamic DO needed for SNS — Actually: DO does not predict success.
  • Trap: ROSETTA shows BTX beats SNS — Actually: used 200 U, twice registered dose; equal at 2 years.
  • Trap: SNS approved for bladder pain or neurogenic bladder — Actually: OAB and NOUR only.
  • Trap: PNE for retention — Actually: staged implant; PNE fails more.
  • Trap: PTNS effect is permanent — Actually: relapse likely without maintenance.
  • Trap: TTNS equals PTNS physiologically — Actually: skin impedance limits amplitude.
  • Trap: sympathetic tone maintains continence — Actually: fires only from ≈60% capacity.
  • Trap: pelvic floor physiotherapy for retention — Actually: usually ineffective; tone returns to baseline.
  • Trap: bilateral SNS after unilateral failure — Actually: not superior; not standard.
