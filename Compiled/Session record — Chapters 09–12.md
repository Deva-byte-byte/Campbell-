## CHAPTER 9 — OCCUPATIONAL HEALTH IN UROLOGY (pp. 137–144)

**Instalment 1 — Chapter Opening, Musculoskeletal Strain, Table 9.1, Figs 9.1–9.4, Presbyopia, Workstation Ergonomics** (pp. 137–140)
**Instalment 2 — Infectious, Smoke, Chemical and Radiation Exposures, Table 9.2, Distractions and Noise, Physical Health, Physiological Response, Sleep, Fitness, Burnout, Second Victim, Stress Continuum (Fig 9.5), CBT/DBT, Table 9.3, Conclusion, Key Points** (pp. 141–144)

### The numbers to lock in — Instalment 1
- **Key figures:** MSD prevalence 46–90%. Elbow 90–120° in every modality. Torso–arm angle <30°. Open surgery: neck flexion <25°, working area 2–4 in below the elbow. Robotic console: back flexion <15°, knee 90° at rest and 120° at most. Loupes: neck angle <20°. Gaze down about 20°. Screen 20–40 in away. Knees and elbows 90° at the desk.
- **Height reference by modality:** open = elbow; laparoscopic = surgeon's pubic symphysis; endourology = between symphysis and umbilicus.
- **Commonest:** open surgery → neck, shoulder and low back; robotic/endoscopic → eyes, neck and hand/wrist.
- **Traps:** a one-piece apron is acceptable if belted (the problem is load on the shoulders); microbreaks do not lengthen cases; with multifocal lenses, lower the monitor.

### The numbers to lock in — Instalment 2
- **Key figures:** burnout in urology about 50%, all providers 40–60%, general workforce 28%. ≥20% of time on meaningful work protects against burnout. Sleep 7–9 h (age 25–64) and 7–8 h (≥65). Sleep loss degrades performance by ≥12%. Exercise 150 min moderate or 75 min vigorous, plus strength work on 2 days. Cortisol is back to baseline about 30 min after surgery. Smoke evacuator tip within 2 in.
- **Gold standards:** ALARA. Pulsed fluoroscopy at the lowest rate (1 pulse/s). Fetal dosimeter at the waist, under the apron. N95 plus smoke evacuation for CO₂ laser treatment of condylomata. Two pairs of chemotherapy gloves for intravesical chemotherapy.
- **Eponyms:** Freudenberger (1980), who coined burnout. Leriche, the surgeon's "small cemetery". Stress Continuum Model (Nash, US military): Ready, Reacting, Injured, Ill.
- **Traps:** burnout is higher in mid-career than early career. Burnout sits in the Injured zone, not Ill. Magnification increases dose. The image intensifier goes close to the patient and the source far away.

## CHAPTER 10 — ETHICS AND INFORMED CONSENT (pp. 145–149)

**Instalment 1 — Chapter Opening, History of Medical Ethics, Box 10.1 (Hippocratic Oath), Four Principles, Four-Box Method (Table 10.1), History of Consent, Elements, Fig 10.1 Decision Models, Exceptions, Quality of Consent, Shared Decision Making, End-of-Life Decisions, Medical Research, Key Points** (pp. 145–149)

### The numbers to lock in
- **Key figures:** proposed material-risk cut-off of 1%. 16% of Medicare spend falls in the last 2 months of life. About 40 SDM models. Advance preferences become DNR orders in fewer than half of cases. Consent forms are often above 12th-grade reading level. Minors are under 18.
- **Dates and legislation:** Mohr v Williams 1905 (consulting is not consenting). Luka v Lowrie 1912 (implied consent in emergencies). Schloendorff 1914 (consent to EUA is not consent to surgery). Barnett v Bachrach 1943 (waiver for the unconscious emergency patient). Salgo 1957 (the term "informed consent"). Kansas 1960 (consent moves into negligence). Canterbury v Spence 1972 (reasonable-person standard). Potter 1970 ("bioethics"). Beauchamp and Childress 1979 (four principles). Jonsen 1998 (Four-Box). Rogers v Sells 1936 and In re Quackenbush 1978. PSDA (enacted 1990, in force 1991). Uniform Health-Care Decisions Act 2006. 45 CFR 46, Subparts B–D.
- **Gold standard:** the reasonable-patient disclosure standard. The most effective comprehension tool is repeat-back.
- **Commonest missed element:** assessment of understanding.
- **Traps:** Salgo, not Canterbury, coined the term. Autonomy is not supreme. Justice is not the same as equality. A conscious patient may refuse life-saving care. Reading the consent form does not improve understanding. Disclosing uncertainty increases trust.

## CHAPTER 11 — PERIOPERATIVE CARE IN UROLOGIC SURGERY (pp. 150–166)

**Split note:** this chapter carries 7 numbered tables and 5 boxes across 17 pages. Several six-page stretches exceed four numbered tables, so the chapter was worked as five instalments rather than three.

**Instalment 1 — Chapter Opening, Preoperative Evaluation, Presurgical Testing, ASA Classification (Box 11.1), Goldman (Table 11.1), Modified Cardiac Risk Index (Table 11.2), Cardiovascular Evaluation, Clinical Markers, Functional Capacity, DASI (Table 11.3), Surgery-Specific Risk, Pulmonary and Hepatobiliary Evaluation** (pp. 150–152)
**Instalment 2 — Elderly, Morbid Obesity, Pregnancy, Nutrition, Optimisation (Cardiac, Pulmonary, Diabetes, Thyroid, Steroids), Antithrombotic Therapy, Table 11.4** (pp. 152–155)
**Instalment 3 — Bowel Preparation and Ileus, Patient Environment (Hypothermia), Patient Safety, Positioning (Box 11.2), Antibiotic Prophylaxis (Boxes 11.3 and 11.4), VTE Text** (pp. 155–159)
**Instalment 4 — AUA Antimicrobial Table 11.5, VTE Tables 11.6 and 11.7, Box 11.5, Anaesthetic Considerations: MAC, Regional, Inhalational and IV Agents, Neuromuscular Blockade** (pp. 160–164)
**Instalment 5 — Skin Preparation, Transfusion, Pain Management, Key Points** (pp. 164–166)

### The numbers to lock in — Instalment 1
- **Cut-offs:** ECG if age >40 or cardiac history. Pregnancy test for every woman of childbearing age. ≥4 METs (two flights of stairs) → no further testing. Wait 4–6 weeks after MI; the 3–6-month dictum is abandoned. Major-predictor MI windows: <7 days, and 7 days to 1 month. Creatinine >2 mg/dL. Goldman: 6–12 → 7%, 13–25 → 14%, ≥26 → 78%. FEV₁ <0.8 L or <30% predicted. Smokers carry 4× morbidity and up to 10× mortality. Jaundice appears at bilirubin >3. Child mortality: A 10%, B 30%, C 76–82%. DASI: VO₂ = 0.43 × DASI + 9.6, METs = VO₂ × 0.286.
- **Eponyms:** Goldman (weighted; S3/JVD 11 points, MI 10). Child (includes clinical ascites and encephalopathy). MELD (objective: bilirubin, creatinine and INR). Duke (DASI).
- **Traps:** ASA grades physical status, not surgical risk. The Modified Cardiac Risk Index is unweighted. Restrictive disease fares better than obstructive.

### The numbers to lock in — Instalment 2
- **Cut-offs:** BMI ≥45 → 8–13 years of life lost. >3 CHD risk factors → non-invasive testing. Fetal radiation risk is greatest up to about 15 weeks; malformation threshold 10 cGy; CT about 1 cGy, IVP about 0.3 cGy. Weight loss >20 lb in 3 months = severe malnutrition. TPN for 7–10 days preoperatively only in severe malnutrition. Enteral feeding for 5–20 days cuts complications by 10–15%. Glucose 80–250 mg/dL. Steroids: ≤5 mg/day → no suppression; >20 mg/day for >3 weeks → suppressed; a suppressive dose within 1 year → supplement; hydrocortisone 50–100 mg at induction, then 25–50 mg 8-hourly for 24–48 h. Warfarin half-life 36–42 h → stop 5 days before, INR <1.5. Antiplatelets: stop 7–10 days before. DAPT: BMS 6 weeks, DES 12 months. Delay surgery ≥30 days after BMS and >1 year after DES. Premature interruption → 25–50% MI risk. Stop smoking ≥8 weeks before; >6 months = non-smoker risk. AF in about 20% of hyperthyroid patients.
- **Landmark trial:** Mangano (NEJM 1996) — atenolol improves outcome after non-cardiac surgery.
- **High thrombotic risk:** any mitral prosthesis; caged-ball or tilting-disc valve; CHADS₂ 5–6; stroke or TIA <3 months (<6 months with a valve); VTE <3 months; severe thrombophilia; rheumatic valve disease.
- **Traps:** stopping smoking less than 8 weeks before surgery may raise risk. NSAIDs are contraindicated in pregnancy. The second trimester is the safest. TPN harms the well-nourished.

### The numbers to lock in — Instalment 3
- **Cut-offs:** ileus >72–96 h → look for obstruction. Normothermia 36–38°C; SSI risk rises at 34–36°C. Arm abduction ≤90°. Lithotomy: hips 80–100° flexion, 30–45° abduction. SSIs are about 40% of nosocomial infections and add 7–10 days. Antibiotic within 60 min (SCIP) or 2 h (Classen); ≤30 min is better than 31–60; at or after incision gives about 2× SSI. Duration ≤24 h. Redose if the case exceeds 4 h. Compliance <60%. Colon flora 10¹⁰–10¹² organisms/g.
- **Landmark trials:** Nichols–Condon 1977 (neomycin + erythromycin; septic complications 43% → 9%). Kurz 1996 (hypothermia → 3× wound infection, +2.6 days). Harvard Medical Practice Study 1991 (48% of adverse events were surgical). Classen 1992 (timing).
- **Traps:** femoral neuropathy is caused by retractor pressure on psoas, not hip position. The axillary roll goes caudal to the axilla. MBP gives no benefit and raises SSI. Hair: clippers, not razors.

### The numbers to lock in — Instalment 4
- **Cut-offs:** VTE 10–40% without prophylaxis historically, 1–5% now. Enoxaparin 40 mg, or 30 mg if CrCl <30. UFH 5000 U 12-hourly (moderate) or 8-hourly (high/highest). Spinal hypotension 10–40%. PDPH <2% with a 29 G needle. Serious neurological deficit 0.05%. Halothane hepatitis 1 in 35,000. TOF 0.9 (formerly 0.7). Succinylcholine lasts about 5 min. Desflurane is advantageous for cases >3 h.
- **Gold standards:** single-dose prophylaxis; ≤24 h only for PCNL and prosthetics. Aztreonam replaces aminoglycoside in renal impairment. Highest-risk VTE → drug + IPC. Sevoflurane for inhalational induction. Ketamine for induction in hypovolaemic or asthmatic patients.
- **Contraindications:** N₂O — bowel obstruction, pneumothorax. Succinylcholine — risk of malignant hyperthermia or hyperkalaemia.
- **Traps:** simple cystoscopy with sterile urine needs no prophylaxis. Routine vancomycin is not recommended. MAC means minimum alveolar concentration here, but monitored anaesthesia care in Chapter 12.

### The numbers to lock in — Instalment 5
- **Cut-offs:** Hct >30% rarely transfuse, <21% often. PRBC Hct about 70% vs 40% for whole blood. Platelets <50,000 with surgery; 50–100,000 with a high-risk procedure or dysfunction. FFP if INR >1.5 with bleeding, or after 1 blood volume. HIV/HCV about 1 in 2 million; HBV 1 in 200,000; platelet bacterial contamination 1 in 5000. Massive transfusion protocol at >10 units/24 h. TRALI onset 1–2 h; TRALI caused 55% of transfusion deaths (2005–07). New persistent opioid use about 6%.
- **Gold standard:** 0.5% chlorhexidine in spirit (some evidence). Epidural analgesia beats parenteral opioids.
- **Traps:** Jehovah's Witnesses refuse blood, not care. NSAIDs should be avoided in renal insufficiency.

## CHAPTER 12 — FUNDAMENTALS OF INTRAOPERATIVE PATIENT SAFETY (pp. 167–174)

**Instalment 1 — Chapter Opening, Background, Time-Outs and Checklists, WHO Checklist (Fig 12.1), Wrong-Site Surgery Prevention, Preprocedure Verification, Site Marking, Time-Out, Systems Failures (Fig 12.2), Retained Surgical Items, Risk Factors, Prevention, Technology, Sharps Safety (Double Gloving, Blunt Needles, Sharps Zone)** (pp. 167–170)
**Instalment 2 — Fire Safety (Fig 12.3), Silverstein Assessment, Fire Management/RACE, Environmental Distractions, Communication Basics (Fig 12.4), Communication Errors, Non-Technical Skills, TeamSTEPPS (Fig 12.5), Simulation, Key Points** (pp. 170–174)

### The numbers to lock in — Instalment 1
- **Key figures:** IOM 1999 — 44,000–98,000 deaths. 2016 estimate — third leading cause of death. About 50% of adverse events are surgical, and about 50% of those are preventable. About 75% of malpractice errors are intraoperative. Wrong-site surgery about 1 in 112,000. ≤30% of wrong-site root causes arise before the day of surgery. RSIs 1 in 10,000 to 1 in 2800. Sponges are about ⅔ of RSIs. A count discrepancy gives about 100× the odds. Blood contact in up to half of operations. Surgeons and first assistants suffer about 60% of injuries. About ⅓ of injuring sharps recontact the patient. A single glove is punctured in up to 61% of cases, on average ~40 min in. Double gloving cuts blood on the needle by 95%.
- **Landmark trials and dates:** WHO checklist 2008 (19 items; deaths down 47%, complications down 36%). Pronovost 2006 (108 ICUs; bloodstream infection down 66%). Universal Protocol 2004. Joint Commission founded 1951.
- **Traps:** a correct count does not exclude an RSI. An item found after skin closure counts as an RSI. Mark the correct site only. Never use family members as interpreters. Trainee presence lowers RSI risk.

### The numbers to lock in — Instalment 2
- **Key figures:** about 600 surgical fires a year. 85% are in the head, neck or upper chest; 81% occur under monitored anaesthesia care. Oxygen is involved in 95%, and the Bovie is the ignition source in about 90%. ChloraPrep is about 70% isopropyl alcohol and needs ≥3 min to dry. For airway surgery, FiO₂ ≤30% and 3–5 min warning. Silverstein score: 1 = low, 2 = intermediate, 3 = high. Up to 70% of OR adverse events are communication failures. About 30% of exchanges fail.
- **Eponyms and acronyms:** Silverstein (3-point fire score). RACE. Sterile cockpit. SBAR. STEP. I'M SAFE. CUS. Two-challenge rule. TeamSTEPPS (AHRQ + Department of Defense, 2006).
- **Traps:** N₂O is as strong an oxidiser as O₂. A 3-minute drying time does not abolish the fire risk. Didactic TeamSTEPPS alone is no better than none. Training technical and non-technical skills together dilutes both.
