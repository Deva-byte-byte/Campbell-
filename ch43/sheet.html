Chapter 43 — Exstrophy-Epispadias Complex — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else

  • Template ≥3 × 3 cm → newborn closure; <3 cm/polypoid/fibrotic → delay
  • Osteotomy if closure >72 h, diastasis >4 cm or stiff pelvis
  • Gold-standard immobilisation: external fixator + modified Buck traction
  • Staged repair: closure birth → epispadias 6–12 mo → bladder neck 4–7 y
  • Bladder neck reconstruction only at capacity ≥100 mL + motivated child
  • Best continence predictors: template size + successful primary closure
  • Commonest closure error: undivided urogenital diaphragm fibres
  • VUR (vesicoureteric reflux) 100% after closure → continuous prophylaxis
  • Cloacal exstrophy: osteotomy always; staged closure; spinal defect 85–100%
  • Not dry 3 h by 2 y after bladder neck repair = failure

Incidence and inheritance

  • Classic bladder exstrophy (CBE): 2.15/100,000 (USA); ≈1 in 46,000
  • Male:female 2.3:1 (up to 5–6:1 in some series)
  • Family recurrence ≈1 in 100; affected parent → offspring 1 in 70 (≈500×)
  • In vitro fertilisation (IVF) ≈7.5×; early high-dose progesterone ≈10× risk
  • Smoking → cloacal; preconception folate → mildest (epispadias)
  • Commonest genetic variant: 22q11.2 duplication
  • ISL1 (5q11.1): confirmed susceptibility gene
  • First cloacal exstrophy: Schenck 1595; first epispadias: Heraclius

Embryology

  • Cause: cloacal membrane not reinforced by mesoderm
  • Classic exstrophy >50% of the complex
  • Marshall–Muecke: overdeveloped membrane, week 4; rupture timing sets variant

Classic bladder exstrophy

  • Posterior pelvis externally rotated 12°; anterior 18°, shortened 30%
  • Posterior segment length normal; bone histology normal
  • Anterior corpora ≈50% short; posterior corpora normal length
  • Urethra anterior to prostate; prostate never circumferential
  • PSA (prostate-specific antigen) below age norms → still screen (digital rectal examination + PSA)
  • Vagina ≤6 cm, normal calibre; cervix in anterior wall
  • Levator hiatus ≈2× wide; puborectalis angle <65° in dry adults
  • Prolapse predictor: adult diastasis width (osteotomy not protective)
  • Colorectal anomaly ≈2% (72×); commonest imperforate anus
  • Prolapse pre-closure → close; post-closure → outlet obstruction → cystoscopy
  • Inguinal hernia: common, bilateral → repair + explore contralateral at closure
  • Commonest renal anomaly: duplex system
  • Commonest variant: skin-covered (split symphysis)
  • Superior vesical fissure: intact sphincter → continent, no bladder neck surgery
  • All variants except superior vesical fissure → formal closure at birth
  • Cystitis glandularis in polyps → adenocarcinoma → lifelong cytology + cystoscopy

Prenatal diagnosis

  • Only ≈25% of BEEC (bladder exstrophy–epispadias complex) diagnosed prenatally
  • Five ultrasound signs: no bladder filling, low umbilicus, wide rami, small genitalia, growing lower mass
  • Pubic diastasis >10 mm; cord-to-tubercle distance <5th centile

Surgical reconstruction of bladder exstrophy

  • Newborn care: cord tied 2-0 silk; plastic wrap; saline irrigation
  • Precludes closure: penoscrotal duplication, hypoplastic bladder, bilateral hydronephrosis
  • Ectopic bowel in plate = relative contraindication
  • Delayed closure timing: ASAP, or 6–9 months
  • No growth by 6–12 mo → excise; colon conduit or ureterosigmoidostomy
  • Mean diastasis 4.8 cm; >6 cm → staged pelvic closure
  • <72 h + malleable → no osteotomy; doubt → osteotomise
  • Commonest osteotomy: combined anterior innominate + vertical iliac (supine)
  • Pubic stitch: No. 2 nylon horizontal mattress (strongest)
  • Fixator 4–6 wk; Buck traction ≈4 wk; Bryant only without osteotomy
  • Spica failure ≈47% vs fixator + traction ≈7.5%
  • Failure factors: immobilisation <4 wk; non-paediatric orthopaedic surgeon
  • Osteotomy complication: transient left femoral nerve palsy
  • Epidural: T10–T12; 0.25% bupivacaine 1 mL/kg + 1:200,000 adrenaline
  • Closure: 2-cm plate strip; taper over 12 Fr; meatus 14 Fr
  • Malecot suprapubic 4 wk; stents 10–14 d; urethra unstented
  • Combined closure + epispadias: boys >6 months only
  • Capacity <30 mL at 1–2 y → warn re continence surgery
  • Residual <50 mL + prophylaxis protects kidneys
  • Recurrent UTI (urinary tract infection) + full bladder → cystoscopy for pubic stitch erosion
  • CPRE (complete primary repair of exstrophy): ≈50% reimplant in year 1; most hypospadiac
  • Erlangen: total repair at 8 wk, no osteotomy
  • Mainz: ureterosigmoidostomy at 2 y; sigma pouch
  • Kelly: radical soft-tissue mobilisation; avoids osteotomy
  • Testosterone enanthate 2 mg/kg intramuscularly, 5 + 2 wk pre-urethroplasty
  • Epispadias repair <12 mo → better capacity
  • Modified Cantwell-Ransley: 18-mm strip; distal 1 cm left on glans; stent 8 Fr
  • Female closure: osteotomy if >4 cm; diastasis narrower than males
  • YDL (Young-Dees-Leadbetter): mucosal strip 15 × 30 mm; suspend to rectus
  • Voiding trial: clamp ≤1 h; fail → 8-Fr Foley 5 d
  • Growth after newborn closure: ≈54 mL in 24 months

Outcomes and results

  • Continence after staged repair: ≈70% males, ≈74% females; YDL series 60–82%
  • Capacity >100 mL → higher continence, dry in half the time
  • Dry by day ≈14 mo, night ≈23 mo
  • Delayed closure without osteotomy → ≈10% continent
  • Closure <1 y → far less augmentation
  • High-volume centre: >5 closures/year
  • Fistula site after Cantwell-Ransley: penile base, not corona
  • Ischaemic penile loss → stop, immediate osteotomy

Failures and complications

  • Reclosure wait ≥4 months (4–6)
  • Voided continence after reclosure ≈1 in 6
  • Stricture: within 60 d; watch for unexplained rectal prolapse
  • Diversion >6 mo → augmentation; <6 mo → usually bowel-free
  • Vesicocutaneous fistula = failed closure until proved otherwise
  • Redo YDL only if patulous neck, >100 mL, stable urodynamics
  • Bulking helps only if already dry 1–3 h
  • Transection + augmentation: bladder stones ≈30%
  • Ureterosigmoidostomy: needs normal anal continence; yearly colonoscopy
  • Too small to close → non-refluxing colon conduit

Adolescent and adult concerns

  • Augmentation cancer surveillance from 5 years; smoking adds risk
  • Male paternity ≈24%, half via ART (assisted reproductive technology)
  • Infertility cause: verumontanum injury, retrograde ejaculation
  • Uterine prolapse: mean age 16 y → sacral suspension, both sides
  • Delivery: planned caesarean, urologist present
  • Continence after age 5 → more acting-out behaviour

Epispadias

  • Male 1 in 117,000; female 1 in 484,000
  • ≈70% male complete (penopubic)
  • Incontinence: penopubic ≈all, penile most, glanular none
  • VUR male 30–40%; female 30–75%
  • Treat as exstrophy if diastasis >3 cm → osteotomy
  • Bladder neck at ≈100 mL, age 4–5 (YDL + Marshall-Marchetti-Krantz + reimplant)
  • Continence after bladder neck reconstruction: males 47–82%; females 70–87%
  • Davis (female): vesicular → subsymphyseal → retrosymphyseal
  • First cure of male incontinence: Young 1922

Cloacal exstrophy

  • Incidence 1 in 200,000–400,000; sex ≈1:1
  • OEIS: omphalocele, exstrophy, imperforate anus, spinal defects
  • Spinal defect 85–100%; lumbar ≈80%; lipomeningocele commonest
  • Spinal ultrasound ≈ MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) in infants
  • Omphalocele 88–100% → neonatal closure
  • Short gut ≈25% even with normal length → keep hindgut
  • Renal anomaly 41–60%; solitary kidney commonest (30× CBE)
  • Commonest müllerian anomaly: uterine duplication
  • Prenatal diagnosis ≈15%; Austin major: absent bladder commonest
  • Elephant-trunk sign: prolapsed terminal ileum
  • Manzoni grid: coded anatomy classification
  • Gender: assign congruent with karyotype
  • Osteotomy success: combined 90% > posterior 76% > anterior 61%
  • Staged closure ≈4× odds success; single-stage fails ≈48% vs 15%
  • Primary failure 31–43%; spica failure ≈72%
  • Diastasis >10 cm → osteotomy + gradual fixator closure
  • Buck traction + fixator 6 wk = gold standard
  • Median 2 continence procedures; continent ≈11 y
  • Disability determinant: neurological level; tethering in ≤33%
  • Ricketts score: 6-point bowel + bladder continence

Classic traps

  • Trap: penis short only from diastasis — Actually: anterior corpora 50% deficient
  • Trap: lengthening adds corporal tissue — Actually: apparent length only
  • Trap: all variants do better — Actually: only superior vesical fissure
  • Trap: osteotomy prevents uterine prolapse — Actually: diastasis width predicts
  • Trap: CPRE avoids bladder neck surgery — Actually: most need outlet procedure
  • Trap: osteotomy alone secures closure — Actually: spica still fails ≈30%
  • Trap: bladder neck repair early — Actually: wait for 100 mL and readiness
  • Trap: epispadias continence from pubertal prostate — Actually: pelvic floor maturation
  • Trap: exstrophy bladder neuropathic — Actually: immature, normal potential
  • Trap: osteotomy improves cloacal continence — Actually: closure only, not continence
