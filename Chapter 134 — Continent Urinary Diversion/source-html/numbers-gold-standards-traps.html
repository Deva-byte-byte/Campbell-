Chapter 134 — Continent Urinary Diversion — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else

  • Commonest diversion worldwide → ileal conduit (Bricker, 1950).
  • Neobladder candidates: 80–90% men, ~75% women.
  • Renal cut-off: estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) >35–40 mL/min or creatinine <1.7–2.2 mg/dL.
  • Urethral frozen section = definitive test, both sexes (American Urological Association, AUA, requires negative margin).
  • Male urethral recurrence risk → prostatic stromal invasion.
  • Female urethral risk → bladder-neck / anterior vaginal tumour; no skip lesions.
  • Mature pouch ≥500 mL; 44 cm ileum <200 mL → 500–600 mL by 1 year.
  • Antireflux unnecessary: Studer vs T pouch randomised controlled trial (RCT) — T pouch more reoperations.
  • Night incontinence 20–50%; day continence 80–90%.
  • Retention: men 4–10%, women 20–30%; treat with self-catheterisation.

History

  • First bowel diversion: Simon 1852, ureterosigmoidostomy, exstrophy.
  • Ureterosigmoidostomy late risks: hyperchloraemic acidosis, pyelonephritis, cancer at implant site.
  • Ileal conduit: Zaayer 1911 → Bricker 1950.
  • Gilchrist 1950: ileocaecal valve continence, first human continent cutaneous.
  • Kock 1982: intussuscepted nipple; established detubularisation + double folding.
  • Tizzoni–Poggi 1888: first orthotopic (dog). Lemoine 1913: first human.
  • Camey–Le Duc 1979: tubular ileum → high pressure, poor continence.
  • Women: continence via urethra alone, mid-1990s — bladder neck not needed.
  • RAZOR (extracorporeal): 21% continent. iROC (intracorporeal): 11%.
  • Guideline (AUA/Society of Urologic Oncology, European Association of Urology): counsel on all three diversion options.

Basic Principles

  • Three principles: reliable outlet; low pressure (detubularise, sphere); ≥500 mL.
  • Sphere → maximum volume per surface area.
  • Ileum stretches more than colon.

Patient Selection

  • Absolute contraindication (CI), orthotopic: invasive cancer at urethral margin; significant urethral stricture.
  • Absolute CI, all continent: severe renal/hepatic dysfunction; cannot self-catheterise.
  • Unplanned diversion change: up to ~10% → always mark conduit site.
  • Stoma: through middle of rectus → avoids parastomal hernia.
  • Algorithm (Fig 134.2): eGFR >40 → sphincter → bowel → not frail → tumour check.
  • Male urethral recurrence: ~7% 5 yr, ~9% 10 yr; median 2 yr.
  • Risk order: no prostate < carcinoma in situ (CIS)/ductal < stromal invasion.
  • Bladder CIS: not a predictor (either sex).
  • Neobladder protective vs urethral recurrence: hazard ratio (HR) 0.44 (meta-analysis).
  • Transurethral resection (TUR) prostatic biopsy: 5 and 7 o'clock beside verumontanum; not mandatory.
  • Stromal invasion → neoadjuvant chemotherapy (usually node-positive).
  • Persistent prostatic urethral disease → cutaneous diversion + urethrectomy.
  • Women: negative bladder neck → negative urethra (no skip lesions).
  • Female assessment: bimanual examination under anaesthesia; palpable neck/vaginal tumour → urethrectomy + cutaneous.
  • Local pelvic recurrence <10% (~7%); rarely affects neobladder.
  • Do not operate orthotopic if: gross positive margin at pubis, rectum or side wall.
  • pT3/N+ at surgery → not a contraindication.
  • Age, obesity → not contraindications.
  • Commonest technical conversion cause: short/fatty mesentery.
  • Self-catheterisation: 10–20% men, ~30% women, 100% cutaneous.
  • Poor candidates: multiple sclerosis, quadriplegia, frail, cognitively impaired → conduit.
  • Weak male sphincter → artificial urinary sphincter; female stress incontinence → Burch/sling (then intermittent self-catheterisation, ISC).
  • Post-radiation neobladder if no incontinence, stricture, fistula or severe bowel damage.
  • Worst sphincter scarring: prostate brachytherapy/high-dose radiotherapy, cervical vaginal implant.
  • Post-prostatectomy: continent before → continent after.
  • Prior bowel resection: reuse old anastomosis; extra 45–60 cm → short bowel risk.

Continence Preservation

  • Rhabdosphincter: omega-shaped, anterolateral; pudendal nerve.
  • Female continence zone: mid-urethra.
  • Male: urethral sutures before division (urethra retracts); absorbable dorsal venous complex (DVC) suture.
  • Female: urethra above endopelvic fascia, no retraction → suture after removal.
  • Apical tumour → urethrectomy + cutaneous/conduit; focal CIS → more distal margin.
  • Female: omental flap between vagina and neobladder; sacrocolpopexy.
  • Vaginal dissection: not dorsal to 2 and 10 o'clock.

Techniques for Continent Bladder Substitution

  • Gold standard segment: ileum (best compliance, mobile mesentery, mucosal atrophy).
  • Worst compliance: stomach and sigmoid.
  • Stomach: secretes acid → option in renal impairment; dysuria, haematuria.
  • Distal ileum loss → vitamin B12 deficiency (Indiana).
  • Le Duc stricture 7–29% vs direct anastomosis <3%.
  • Ureteroileal stricture lowest: Leadbetter/Wallace, interrupted absorbable, 3–6%.
  • Hautmann reflux switch (1997): stenosis 9.5% → 1%.
  • Studer reflux visible at ~300 mL; kidneys preserved ~95%.
  • Kock nipple late failure: stenosis, extussusception, staple stones, >10 yr, silent.
  • Suture: absorbable only; metal staples → stones.
  • Ileal reservoirs: 60–75 cm terminal ileum.
  • Studer: 40–44 cm reservoir + afferent limb; isolate 15–20 cm from ileocaecal valve.
  • Hautmann: ~70 cm W; larger capacity → earlier continence, more retention.
  • Serous-lined tunnel: 40 cm W; needs long, non-dilated ureters.
  • T pouch: 15-cm afferent limb; accepts dilated or short ureters.
  • Colon neobladder: diseased/resected ileum; needs larger initial volume.
  • Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS): no bowel prep (except colon), no nasogastric tube, alvimopan.
  • Commonest gastrointestinal complication: ileus; alvimopan reduces it.
  • Malnourished/frail: up to 70% of cystectomy patients.
  • Catheter out at 3 weeks; no routine pouchogram.
  • Umbilical stoma: least stenosis, easiest for paraplegics; medical alert bracelet.
  • Appendix channel: ≥5–6 cm, ≥12 Fr; takes 14–16 Fr only.
  • Mitrofanoff 1980: tunnelled appendix; Mitchell: caecal lengthening.
  • Yang–Monti: 2–3 cm ileum retubularised transversely.
  • Indiana: double Lembert imbrication of valve, 3–4 cm, over 16 Fr.
  • Right colon pouch: ~30 cm colon; ureteroileal; ileocaecal valve = antireflux.
  • Test channel: fill, compress, recatheterise before closure.

Results and Complications

  • Early/late cut-off: 90 days.
  • Early morbidity: neobladder = conduit; cutaneous → more infection, dehiscence.
  • Undrained leak → percutaneous drain / nephrostomies, not early open repair.
  • Efferent necrosis below fascia → explore.
  • Stomal stenosis up to 20%; late needs open revision.
  • Treat bacteriuria only if symptomatic or Proteus/Pseudomonas.
  • Late febrile urinary infection → exclude obstruction, stones, poor emptying.
  • Pouch perforation: cystogram; stable → catheter drainage.
  • Endoscopic stricture treatment: retreatment >50%; open repair better.
  • Pouch–vaginal fistula: 5–10%; lateral cystogram before catheter out.
  • Daytime incontinence risks: age >65, colon, no nerve sparing, diabetes.
  • Continence plateau: 12–18 months before intervening.
  • USC prospective: day 92% (12–18 mo), night 51% (18–36 mo).
  • Female questionnaire: ~60% self-catheterise daily.
  • Retention risk (men): excess bowel length, prostate sparing.
  • Male retention-free: 93% 3 yr, 88% 5 yr, 79% 10 yr.
  • Female retention: mechanical (posterior angulation), not neurogenic.
  • Sling/injection: never blind needles; bone anchors or prepubic route.

Follow-Up

  • Phases: <4 mo strictures; 4 mo–3 yr cancer; >3 yr stones, obstruction, urethral cancer.
  • Visits: 4-monthly yr 1 → 6-monthly to 3 yr → annual.
  • Annual: B12; urethral wash if CIS; prostate-specific antigen if prostate cancer.
  • After 5 yr: annual renal ultrasound, indefinitely.
  • Women: bimanual + voided cytology (urethral recurrence invisible on CT).
  • Health-related quality of life: no diversion proven superior; neobladder → better body image.

Classic traps

  • Trap: old age excludes neobladder — Actually: age alone never a contraindication.
  • Trap: bladder CIS bars orthotopic — Actually: negative urethral frozen section suffices.
  • Trap: preoperative prostatic TUR biopsy mandatory — Actually: frozen section decides.
  • Trap: women need bladder neck for continence — Actually: rhabdosphincter, mid-urethra.
  • Trap: antireflux protects kidneys — Actually: obstruction risk; refluxing Studer equal.
  • Trap: obesity forbids neobladder — Actually: may suit better than a stoma.
  • Trap: treat all neobladder bacteriuria — Actually: only symptomatic or Proteus/Pseudomonas.
  • Trap: early open repair of urine leak — Actually: percutaneous drainage first.
  • Trap: women mainly leak — Actually: women mainly retain; men leak.
  • Trap: "IC" in Tables 134.1/134.2 = ileal conduit — Actually: intermittent catheterisation.
