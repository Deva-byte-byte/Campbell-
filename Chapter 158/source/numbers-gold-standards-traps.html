Chapter 158 — Active Management Strategies for Localised Prostate Cancer — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else
  • Very low risk → active surveillance (AS) best option; low risk → AS for most.
  • Favourable intermediate = 1 intermediate factor + grade group (GG) 1–2 + <50% cores.
  • Staging imaging from unfavourable intermediate up: bone scan + CT/MRI.
  • proPSMA: PSMA PET sensitivity 85% vs 38%; accuracy 92% vs 65%.
  • ProtecT 15 yr: cancer death ~2–3% all arms; more metastasis on AS.
  • SPCG-4: RP vs watchful waiting RR 0.56, NNT 8; benefit <65, intermediate.
  • PIVOT: no benefit low risk; 19.5 yr benefit intermediate risk only.
  • Epstein: PSAD <0.15, Gleason ≤6, <3 cores, ≤50% any core.
  • RP → incontinence + ED; EBRT → bowel; brachy → worst acute LUTS.
  • Intermediate/high risk → discourage AS unless limited longevity.

Host Evaluation

  • Longevity tools (NCCN): Social Security tables or WHO Life Tables.
  • Charlson Comorbidity Index (CCI): built for 1-yr inpatient mortality.
  • PCCI (prostate-specific CCI): predicts 10-yr mortality.
  • CAN score beats CCI and PCCI for 5-yr overall survival.
  • PCOS: 10-yr cancer death 3–18% vs ≥33% other-cause with any comorbidity.
  • Operate if: high risk of metastasis/death within 10 yr, despite competing risks.
  • Comorbidity (cardiac, pulmonary, haematological) → may contraindicate surgery, not treatment.

Baseline Function

  • Instruments: IPSS (International Prostate Symptom Score); UCLA Prostate Cancer Index.
  • Obstructive LUTS / large gland → surgery; brachytherapy → retention.
  • Brachytherapy difficult >60 cm³ (pubic arch interference).
  • Neoadjuvant ADT shrinks gland 25–40%; may be needed if >80 g before RT.
  • TURP before, not after, EBRT; post-brachy TUR → incontinence.
  • Delay RT 6–8 wk after TURP → ↓ incontinence, bladder neck contracture.
  • Small gland <30 cm³ + large TURP defect → avoid brachy/cryo.
  • Limited TUR (median lobe/bladder neck) safe before brachytherapy.
  • Retention + radiation → switch catheter to intermittent catheterisation.
  • Severe compliance abnormality → surgery (RT fibroses bladder).
  • Absolute contraindication to RT: previous radical pelvic irradiation.
  • Relative (RT): IBD, bowel in field/hernia, bowel-trapping surgery, poor healing (diabetes).
  • Relative (surgery): chronic steroids.
  • Urologist's recommendation most influences choice.

Risk Assessment

  • Pillars: clinical stage, biopsy Gleason, pretreatment PSA.
  • AUA/ASTRO/SUO: 4 groups; NCCN splits intermediate (fav/unfav) and high (high/very high).
  • Very low: T1c, GG1, PSA <10, <3 cores, ≤50%/core, PSAD <0.15.
  • Low: T1–T2a, GG1, PSA <10.
  • Intermediate factors: T2b–T2c, GG2–3, PSA 10–20.
  • Unfavourable intermediate: 2–3 factors, or GG3, or ≥50% cores.
  • High: T3a, or GG4–5, or PSA >20.
  • Very high: T3b–T4, primary pattern 5, or >4 cores GG4–5.
  • Gleason 1966; ISUP 2005 → patterns 1–2 dropped, all cribriform = pattern 4.
  • Grade groups 2013 (ISUP 2014): 3+3=1, 3+4=2, 4+3=3, 4+4=4, 9–10=5.
  • Epstein insignificant: organ-confined, <0.5 mL; accuracy 79%.
  • Epstein caveat: validated on systematic, not MRI-targeted, biopsy.
  • Grade migration → historical vs contemporary Gleason 6 not comparable.

Staging

  • Gold standard for bone: technetium-99m MDP bone scan; low specificity.
  • Equivocal bone scan → PET/CT or MRI.
  • CT misses metastasis in nodes <10 mm; still endorsed for nodal staging.
  • MRI only test accurate for EPE/SVI; high PPV, poor NPV for EPE.
  • SVI on MRI: T2-hypointense mass contiguous with base tumour.
  • Choosing Wisely: ASCO 2012, AUA 2013 — no imaging in low risk.
  • proPSMA: 302 high-risk; specificity 98% vs 91%; management change 27%.
  • 18F-DCFPyL: specificity 97.9%, sensitivity 40.3%; PPV 86.7 vs 28.3%.
  • AUA 2022: PSMA after negative conventional; NCCN 2023: instead of it.

Nomograms, MRI, Genomics

  • Partin tables (1993): PSA+Gleason+stage → pathological stage; 72.4% accurate.
  • Kattan: AUC 0.79 for relapse after RP.
  • UCSF-CAPRA: adds age, % positive cores; 5-yr RFS 85% → 8%.
  • PI-RADS v2; ADC predicts progression and recurrence.
  • Tissue assays gold standard over liquid biomarkers.
  • NCCN genomics: AS candidates, low/fav intermediate, life expectancy >10 yr.
  • AUA genomics: not routine; not in small-volume low risk.

Comparative Studies

  • Best end points: cancer-specific and overall mortality, not biochemical.
  • SPCG-4: pre-PSA era, ~90% palpable, 23.2 yr follow-up.
  • SPCG-4: absolute cancer-death reduction 11.0 points.
  • PIVOT: 731 men; bone metastases 4.7% vs 10.6%.
  • PIVOT 10 yr: all-cause benefit only PSA >10.
  • PIVOT 19.5 yr: intermediate risk ARR 14.5%.
  • PIVOT 19.5 yr overall: all-cause ARR 5.5%, cancer 4.0% — both NS.
  • PIVOT flaws: underpowered; ~20% non-adherent; ~40% dead by 10 yr.
  • PCOS: RP overall HR 0.60, disease-specific 0.35 — selection bias.
  • ProtecT: 1643 randomised; RT arm with 3–6 mo ADT.
  • ProtecT 10 yr: cancer mortality ~1% vs 15% predicted → underpowered.
  • ProtecT cohort: 77% low risk; only 6% GG ≥3.
  • ProtecT 15 yr: 2.7 / 2.2 / 2.9%; AS crossover 60%.
  • ProtecT <65: surgery beats RT for cancer mortality.
  • Sooriakumaran: RT vs RP sHR 1.76 non-metastatic.
  • CEASAR: RP sexual deficit only in top baseline quartile.
  • CEASAR low risk: EBRT sexual score 12 points better at 3 yr.
  • Chen (JAMA 2017): RP incontinence meaningful to 24 mo; EBRT bowel at 3 mo.
  • PCOS function: RP vs RT differences vanish by 15 yr.
  • Open vs robotic: equal function at 12 wk (one RCT).

Stepwise Counselling

  • Order: treat? → partial vs whole → surgery vs RT vs ablation → delivery.
  • AS = active testing, deferral; watchful waiting = symptom-driven, ADT at progression.
  • Occult high grade in low-grade on systematic biopsy: 25–45%.
  • Reclassification predictors: PSAD >0.15, core density; not race or age.
  • Any Gleason pattern 4 → ↑ eventual metastasis; low-volume GG2 AS debated.
  • NICE: MRI mandatory for all AS; MRI at 12–18 mo.
  • Confirmatory biopsy within 6–12 mo (NCCN, ASCO).
  • ASCO: biopsy every 3–5 yr; NCCN: biopsy ≤ annually, PSA ≥6-monthly intervals.
  • Toronto (inclusive) 15-yr cancer death 5.7% vs Hopkins (Epstein) 0.6%.
  • Toronto treatment: 25 / 36 / 45% at 5 / 10 / 15 yr.
  • Hopkins treatment: 50 / 57% at 10 / 15 yr.
  • Toronto: non-prostate death 10× prostate cancer death.
  • PRIAS: PSAD ≤0.2; PSADT triggers biopsy.
  • Canary only programme admitting Gleason ≤7.
  • AS deaths: mostly cardiovascular.
  • Focal therapy: not for high risk; best for limited-focality intermediate risk; trials.
  • Cryo → ED almost always; HIFU retreatment 29–38%.
  • RT late risk: rectal/bladder cancer at 15–20 yr.
  • RP counselling: early incontinence and impotence are the norm.
  • RT + ADT (intermediate/high risk): ADT drives early toxicity.
Classic traps
  • Trap: 10-yr life expectancy decides treatment — Actually: weigh cancer aggressiveness too.
  • Trap: GG3 is favourable intermediate — Actually: GG3 → unfavourable.
  • Trap: Gleason 8 = GG5 — Actually: 8 = GG4; 9–10 = GG5.
  • Trap: CT excludes nodal disease — Actually: blind to normal-sized nodes.
  • Trap: PSMA PET highly sensitive for nodes — Actually: DCFPyL nodal sensitivity ~40%.
  • Trap: ProtecT shows AS equal on everything — Actually: more metastasis on AS.
  • Trap: EBRT worst for acute LUTS — Actually: brachytherapy worst.
  • Trap: TURP after radiotherapy is safe — Actually: TURP before; after → incontinence.
  • Trap: observational RP advantage is causal — Actually: selection bias (PCOS 3-yr divergence).
  • Trap: AS = watchful waiting — Actually: AS curative intent; watchful waiting palliative.
