Chapter 94 — Upper Urinary Tract Trauma — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else
  1. Contrast CT (computed tomography) with 10-min delays = gold standard, kidney and ureter
  2. Image blunt: gross haematuria, micro + SBP (systolic BP) <90, or deceleration
  3. Image penetrating: any haematuria
  4. Micro, no shock, no deceleration → observe, no imaging
  5. Operate: unstable despite resuscitation; expanding/pulsatile haematoma
  6. Stable grade I–IV (± select V) → nonoperative, any mechanism
  7. Urinary extravasation → observe; 76–90% heal
  8. Revascularise only solitary kidney or bilateral artery injury
  9. Proximal to iliacs → ureteroureterostomy over stent; distal → reimplant ± hitch
  10. No bowel interposition/autotransplant acutely

Renal injuries

Epidemiology:

  • Kidney = commonest injured urological organ; up to 5% of trauma
  • Blunt 80–90%; commonest blunt cause motor vehicle collision
  • Penetrating: firearms ~⅔; kidney in ~10–20% penetrating abdominal wounds
  • Most injuries AAST (American Association for the Surgery of Trauma) I–III
  • Commonest associated organs: liver, spleen
  • Ribs 9–12 fracture → strongest rib association

Mechanism:

  • Deceleration → hilum + UPJ (ureteropelvic junction): thrombosis, avulsion
  • High-velocity bullet >2000 ft/s (610 m/s) → cavitation, delayed necrosis
  • Anterior axillary stab → hilum; posterior axillary → parenchyma

Haematuria:

  • Microhaematuria = >5 RBC/hpf; hypotension = SBP <90 any time
  • Degree ≠ severity; ~50% UPJ injuries no haematuria
  • Use first urine aliquot (later = diluted)
  • Dipstick sens/spec >97%; microscopy = gold standard
  • False +ve dipstick: myoglobin, dehydration, exercise, menses, povidone-iodine

Grading (AAST 2018):

  • II vs III: laceration ≤1 cm vs >1 cm
  • III: contained vascular injury within Gerota’s
  • IV: collecting system, pelvis/UPJ, segmental vessel, bleeding beyond Gerota’s
  • V: main vessel, hilar avulsion, shattered, devascularised + bleeding
  • Advance one grade for multiple injuries, up to III

Children:

  • Image shock + micro (>5 RBC/hpf); liberal imaging
  • BP maintained until ~50% blood volume lost

CT:

  • Arterial ~80 s → bleeding; delayed → urine leak; optimal delay 9 min
  • Red flags: medial haematoma (pedicle), medial extravasation (UPJ), no enhancement/cortical rim (artery)
  • Grade IV high risk: ≥2 of rim >4 cm, medial laceration, vascular extravasation
  • MiGUTS (Multi-Institutional Genito-Urinary Trauma Study) nomogram: rim distance biggest; vascular extravasation 29 pts
  • CT misses renal vein injury
  • FAST (focused sonography): poor renal specificity; can’t tell blood from urine
  • Urinoma 0–20 HU; haematoma >30 HU; abscess >20 HU + rim

One-shot IVP (IV pyelogram):

  • 2 mL/kg IV push, single film at 10 min
  • Purpose: functioning contralateral kidney
  • Alternative: clamp ipsilateral ureter + IV methylene blue → blue urine

Nonoperative:

  • Serial Hb every 4–6 h; early mobilisation safe
  • Blunt conservative success >90%
  • Fails if: instability, 2–3 units transfused, new guarding
  • Rarely fails <24 h; most failures → stent, nephrostomy or AE
  • Reimage (AUA) ~48 h grade IV–V deep laceration or symptoms; not grade I–III
  • Extravasation reimage 48–72 h
  • Early discharge <48 h safe: select blunt grade I–III
  • Stent impossible (urethral distraction, transection) → PCN (percutaneous nephrostomy) ± antegrade
  • Delayed bleed: usually within 2 weeks (up to 21 days) → AE

Operate if (absolute):

  • Unstable, no/transient response
  • Expanding/pulsatile haematoma
  • Pedicle avulsion; UPJ avulsion

Do not operate:

  • Stable well-staged I–III at laparotomy → leave Gerota’s closed
  • Stable zone II haematoma → observe
  • Extravasation alone grade IV → 90% resolve; >25% devitalised → lower threshold

Angioembolisation (AE):

  • Superselective; repeat AE highly successful
  • AE fails: instability, grade V, extravasation, gross haematuria
  • Pseudoaneurysm/AVF (arteriovenous fistula) → AE

Exploration:

  • At laparotomy, Gerota’s opened → >½ nephrectomy
  • Transabdominal; vessel control before opening Gerota’s
  • Incision over aorta above IMA (inferior mesenteric artery) → Treitz → left renal vein
  • Haematoma → incise medial to IMV
  • Zones: I central, II lateral (kidney), III pelvis
  • Damage control: pack, return 24–48 h
  • Poor survival: GCS <8, base excess <−13.9

Vascular:

  • Warm ischaemia <120 min
  • Main artery repair salvages ~⅓
  • Bilateral: ~½ avoid dialysis
  • Hypertension after repair ≥43% → BP check 1 year
  • Left renal vein ligatable (collaterals); right not

Complications:

  • Goldblatt = arterial stenosis/occlusion
  • Page kidney = compression → ACEi/ARB; refractory → decortication
  • Hypertension ↑ after artery occlusion (32–50%)
  • Mortality high-grade ~5–8%; nephrectomy–mortality link conflicting
  • USANZ (Urological Society of Australia and New Zealand): US 4–6 wk, microhaematuria check 6 mo
  • Return to sport 2 wk–3 mo; rebleed risk 2–6 wk

Ureteral injuries

Epidemiology:

  • 1–2.5% genitourinary trauma; iatrogenic commonest overall
  • External: gunshot commonest; >90% associated injuries
  • External → proximal; iatrogenic → distal
  • 25–45% no haematuria
  • Mortality 6–9%

AAST ureter:

  • II <50%; III ≥50%; IV transection <2 cm devasc; V avulsion >2 cm

Iatrogenic:

  • Hysterectomy = leading cause; prolapse surgery highest gynae range
  • Lap > open gynae; lap = open colorectal
  • URS (ureteroscopy) avulsion <1%; stricture 0.3–3%
  • Risk order: EHL > Nd:YAG > Ho:YAG
  • Unstented ureter → sheath ≤14 Fr
  • Perforation → stop, stent
  • Endometriosis ↑ risk; ~10% women distal supply via uterine artery
  • Devascularisation presents 1 wk–2 mo: stenosis, urinoma, fistula
  • Hysterectomy injury: mean diagnosis day 10
  • Vascular: 85% not recognised; stenosis 1–2%
  • Ureteroarterial fistula → haematuria; chronic stent; covered endovascular stent

Dyes:

  • Methylene blue: MAOI → avoid SSRI/SNRI/MAOI, pregnancy, G6PD; false low SpO₂
  • Indigo carmine: t½ 4–5 min, blue in 5–10 min; hypotension
  • Phenazopyridine most cost-effective
  • ICG (indocyanine green) 5 mL of 2.5 mg/mL per ureter
  • Bilateral stents → anuria 1–5%

Imaging:

  • CT urogram, 10-min delay = best (stable)
  • Retrograde = most sensitive; test for missed injury
  • One-shot IVP non-diagnostic 33–100%
  • Missed injury: fever, leucocytosis, peritonism; ~11% missed at laparotomy

Repair principles:

  • Spatulated, tension-free, stented, watertight, drained
  • Non-tunnelled reimplant in trauma
  • Unstable → ligate (long silk) + delayed PCN; no open nephrostomy
  • Damage control: nothing/re-look 24 h, exteriorised stent, cutaneous ureterostomy, ligate + PCN
  • Missed contusion → stent 6 weeks (~85%)
  • Endoscopic injury → stent and/or PCN
  • Ureterovaginal fistula → stent first

By level:

  • Upper ⅔ → UU (ureteroureterostomy), up to 90% success; leak 10–24%
  • Nephropexy → left gains more
  • Psoas hitch 95–100%; spare genitofemoral/femoral nerves
  • Boari: width ≥4 cm, ≤3:1, gaps to 12 cm
  • TUU (transureteroureterostomy) 90–97% (children 70%); ureterotomy ≥2 cm
  • TUU avoid: urothelial cancer, stones, TB, radiation, RPF (retroperitoneal fibrosis), chronic pyelonephritis
  • Partial transection → Heineke–Mikulicz
  • Robotic reimplant ~95%; buccal ~87%
  • Ileal ureter 81–100%; acidosis ~20%
  • Yang–Monti = less bowel → less metabolic
  • Autotransplant = last resort; 5–10% lost
  • Follow-up: renogram/US 4–12 wk + 1 yr
Classic traps
  • Trap: No haematuria excludes injury — Actually: UPJ and pedicle injuries often have none
  • Trap: Hypotension reliable in children — Actually: late, after ~50% volume loss
  • Trap: Explore every renal injury at laparotomy — Actually: only expanding/pulsatile haematoma
  • Trap: Main vessel contained bleed = grade IV — Actually: grade V in 2018
  • Trap: Extravasation needs a stent — Actually: observe unless UPJ, obstruction, symptoms
  • Trap: Revascularise unilateral thrombosis — Actually: observe if contralateral normal
  • Trap: Right renal vein ligatable like left — Actually: right has no collaterals
  • Trap: Tunnelled reimplant in trauma — Actually: non-tunnelled, refluxing
  • Trap: Bowel or autotransplant acutely — Actually: delayed only
  • Trap: CT without delays excludes leak — Actually: delayed phase mandatory
