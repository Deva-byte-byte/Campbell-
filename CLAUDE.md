# Campbell-Walsh-Wein Chapter Notes — Standing Instructions

These are the user's standing instructions for every chapter-notes session in this repository. Follow them in full.

## Working method
- The user pastes a chapter in instalments of about six pages each. Summarise each one as it arrives.
- Number the instalments sequentially within the chapter, for working purposes only. The numbering NEVER appears in the delivered documents.
- Give each instalment a subheading that lists the topics it actually covers, e.g. "Instalment 4 — Obesity Thresholds, Frailty, Infection Screening, Algorithm".
- If a paste contains more than about six Level B sections or more than four numbered tables, split it into two or more consecutive instalments within one reply. Each gets its own numbers block, and the reply says the split was made.

## Coverage
- Work through the paste in source order and cover everything in it:
  - every Level A, B and C heading;
  - every numbered table, box and figure, including truncated ones;
  - all unheaded opening and bridging prose.
- Anything not covered is named in the closing appendix with the reason, and carried forward as pending.
- Silent omission is the worst failure mode.

## Summary style
- Bullet points throughout: concise, but carrying the concepts.
- No study data (sample sizes, percentages, raw figures), except for landmark trials, which keep their name and key numbers.
- No author–year citations for studies that are not landmark. Give the finding and, where it matters, the study type. Landmark trials keep their names (e.g. MMAS, PCPT, REINVENT). The source attribution printed with a table or figure is retained.
- Expand every abbreviation on first use. Keep a formal medical register, with no slang.
- Bold the operative phrase in each bullet. Use British spelling.
- Give a technique as a sequence (step → step → step).
- State an examinable contrast as an adjacent pair.
- Preserve figure and table legends as their own subsection, naming the source study and year.
- The chapter's opening prose is content: epidemiology, incidence, mortality, subtype proportions, sex ratio and framing. Strip only roadmap sentences ("this chapter will cover…").
- Close every major section with a conceptual takeaway.

## Tables
- Every numbered table and box is rebuilt or bulleted. None is skipped, folded silently into prose, or deferred. A table referred to but not yet pasted is flagged as pending.
- Never dump raw rows. Either:
  - bullet and summarise it; or
  - rebuild it as a clean table, grouped by the categories that matter.
- Tables of drugs, agents or devices are always rebuilt as tables. Mechanism, target, line of therapy, risk group, comparator, endpoint and route are columns.
- Follow each table with what it is for, the pattern across its rows, and the trap it sets.
- Drop study-by-study rows unless the study is landmark. Keep criteria, thresholds, cut-offs and ranges.
- Keep the table number, the full caption, and the source study and year.
- Where the body text and a table disagree, tabulate both values and name the reason for the difference.

## Mandatory comparisons (build unasked)
- Two or more models, classifications, scores or criteria → one table with shared rows, differences in bold.
- Three or more drugs of one class → one table of targets and one of signature toxicities.
- Two or more guideline bodies covering the same decision → one table.
- A series of trials answering the same question → one table: trial, comparator, population, endpoints, verdict.
- A governing contrast or asymmetry → a two-column pair, side by side.
- Prose is for reasoning and mechanism. Anything enumerable and parallel goes in a table.

## Density discipline
- Depth must not decay across an instalment. Before closing, reread the final third against the first third.
- Never compress by dropping facts; tighten the phrasing instead. This applies to the session and to Document 2.
- Reading-time budget for Document 2: about 360 words per source page, i.e. about 60 minutes for 20 pages at 120 words per minute.
  - Meet it by tightening phrasing and removing duplication between tables and prose, never by dropping facts or reasoning.
  - If the cap cannot be met without losing a fact or its reasoning, exceed it and report the overrun.

## Order of output
- The summary comes first; the source flags come last, in a closing appendix.
- A correction appears in corrected form inside the relevant bullet. The appendix records only that it was made.

## Handling the source text (live session only; none of this reaches the PDFs)
Flag the following in the appendix:
- truncations, naming the exact words where the text breaks (close the loop when the rest arrives);
- duplicates, and where to resume;
- internal duplicates within one paste;
- out-of-order pastes, once the order has been restored;
- source errors, inconsistencies and ambiguous expansions, with the correct version. This includes corrupted symbols (a comma for "<", a full stop for ">", "P 5" for "P ="), split numerals and dropped letters. Where one substitution runs through the whole paste, give its key once;
- unrelated material: answer it, keep it out of the notes, and ask where to file it;
- anything not covered, and why.

## Conceptual takeaways
- Close each Level A section, or each Level B section substantial enough to stand alone, with a takeaway:
  - 1–3 sentences, or a single bullet;
  - the organising idea, not a recap;
  - naming the governing asymmetry, contrast or causal chain where there is one;
  - with its operative phrase in bold.
- Purely enumerative sections get no takeaway.

## End of every instalment
Close with a block titled "The numbers to lock in", covering:
- key facts, figures, thresholds and cut-offs;
- gold standards, commonest findings and first-line options;
- eponyms, each with its distinguishing feature;
- landmark trials, with their key numbers;
- dates and legislation;
- absolute and relative contraindications, as two separate lists;
- when to operate, and when not to;
- classic traps.

These blocks are written in full, then the source-flag appendix follows.

## Compilation ("compile")
- Produce two PDFs per chapter, for black-and-white A4 printing. No Word file.
- Restructure the notes to the chapter's own heading hierarchy:
  - Level A: caps;
  - Level B;
  - Level C;
  - Level D: italic run-in.
- Strip:
  - instalment numbering and cross-references;
  - paste-order, resequencing, duplication and truncation notes;
  - source-error flags;
  - roadmap sentences;
  - legends that only duplicate the body text;
  - non-landmark author–year citations.
- Carry corrected values silently.
- Add nothing new at compile time. Takeaways and comparison tables are carried through from the session, not composed at compile.
- Before rendering:
  - run a coverage check against Document 2 and report anything that never reached it;
  - check Document 2 against the reading-time budget.
- Rasterise a page of each PDF in greyscale and confirm that boxes, tables and takeaways stay distinguishable from body text.

### Document 1 — "Chapter NN — Title — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps.pdf"
- Length: 10–15 minutes of reading. Target two A4 sides, ceiling three. About 1,200–1,600 words; report the word count.
- An item is included only if it is one of:
  - an examinable number or cut-off;
  - a gold standard, first-line option or "commonest";
  - an operative trigger (operate / do not operate);
  - an absolute contraindication;
  - a landmark trial with its key result;
  - an eponym with its distinguishing feature;
  - a classic trap.
- Excluded:
  - mechanism, rationale and history;
  - background epidemiology;
  - relative contraindications, unless classically examined;
  - takeaways;
  - anything obvious to a trainee.
- If over budget, cut in this order: historical eponyms → dates → secondary thresholds → relative contraindications. Never cut traps, gold standards or operative triggers.
- Wording:
  - telegraphic, one fact per line, 12 words or fewer;
  - arrows and symbols;
  - related cut-offs merged, duplicates collapsed;
  - abbreviations expanded on first use;
  - operative phrase in bold;
  - no gridded tables.
- Structure:
  - "IF YOU READ NOTHING ELSE" box (10 lines, spanning both columns);
  - then the Level A sections in chapter order, with bold run-in labels ("Cut-offs:", "Gold standard:", "Operate if:");
  - then a CLASSIC TRAPS box of 10 or fewer lines, each "Trap: X — Actually: Y."
- Layout:
  - Page: A4 portrait, two columns, 7 mm gutter. Margins 12 mm top and bottom, 13 mm left and right.
  - Type: Carlito, falling back to DejaVu Sans. Body 9.4 pt, line height 1.25, #000. 1 pt between bullets.
  - Title 14 pt bold with a 1 pt rule, spanning both columns. Level A heads 10.4 pt bold caps with a 0.5 pt rule.
  - "IF YOU READ NOTHING ELSE" box: 1 pt black border, no fill.
  - Traps box: #ececec fill, 5 pt double black left rule.
  - Ragged right, no hyphenation. Boxes may break across columns and pages.
  - Headings break-after: avoid. Orphans and widows 2.
  - Page numbers 8 pt, bottom centre.

### Document 2 — "Chapter NN — Title — Chapter Notes.pdf"
- Page: A4 portrait, single column. Margins 18 mm top and bottom, 20 mm left, 18 mm right.
- Type: Carlito, falling back to DejaVu Sans. Body 11 pt, line height 1.4, #000. 2.5 pt between bullets.
- Heading space: at least 8 pt before Level B and at least 12 pt before Level A.
- Headings:
  - title 20 pt bold over a 1.5 pt rule;
  - Level A 14 pt bold caps over a 1 pt rule;
  - Level B 12.5 pt bold;
  - Level C 11.5 pt bold;
  - Level D 11 pt italic run-in.
- KEY POINTS boxes: 0.75 pt border, no fill, 6 pt padding, bold caps title.
- Tables:
  - no fill and no vertical rules;
  - 1 pt rules above and below, 0.5 pt rule under the header row;
  - optional #f2f2f2 banding;
  - bold caps title with number and full caption;
  - body 10 pt or more, full width, header row repeated across pages;
  - the rebuilt table, followed by its summary bullets.
- Comparison tables take the same treatment, titled for what they compare.
- Takeaway: italic, body size, opening with bold "Concept —". No box. break-before: avoid.
- Running header 8.5 pt: chapter title on the left, current Level A section on the right, 0.3 pt rule beneath.
- Ragged right, no hyphenation. Headings break-after: avoid. Orphans and widows 2.
- Page numbers 9 pt, bottom centre, "page X of Y".

## Rendering
- Use WeasyPrint only, never wkhtmltopdf.
- Carlito throughout. Do not propose a serif face.
- All text pure black.
- Legibility floors:
  - Document 2: body 10.5 pt or more, line height 1.35 or more;
  - Document 1: body 9.4 pt or more, line height 1.25 or more.
- If Document 2 needs fewer pages, tighten the bullet gaps and heading spacing first.
- If Document 1 is over budget, cut by the selection order, never by shrinking the type.
- Report:
  - the page counts of both documents;
  - the word count of Document 1;
  - for Document 2: the source page count, the notes word count, the reading time at 120 words per minute, and any cap overrun.
