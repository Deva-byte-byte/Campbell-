Chapter 146 — Pathophysiology, Evaluation and Medical Management of Adrenal Disorders — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else
  1. Test every mass >1 cm: cortisol + metanephrines; aldosterone if hypertensive/hypokalaemic.
  2. Unenhanced computed tomography (CT) <10 Hounsfield units (HU) → lipid-rich adenoma.
  3. 15-min washout: absolute >60% or relative >40% → adenoma.
  4. Resect if >4 cm (not myelolipoma), suspicious imaging, or growth >1 cm.
  5. Exclude phaeochromocytoma before biopsy, sampling or surgery — surge can kill.
  6. Primary aldosteronism (PA): aldosterone-to-renin ratio (ARR) → confirm → adrenal vein sampling (AVS).
  7. 1-mg dexamethasone test: cortisol ≤1.8 µg/dL excludes; >5.0 = autonomous.
  8. Phaeochromocytoma prep: α-blockade 7–14 days; β only after α.
  9. Adrenocortical carcinoma (ACC): open if >6 cm/invasive; no preoperative biopsy.
  10. Prior contralateral nephrectomy → check remaining adrenal; crisis risk.

Embryology and Anatomy

  • Veins: right short → inferior vena cava; left long → left renal (+ inferior phrenic).
  • Anomalous venous drainage: up to 9% — matters for AVS.
  • Trap: testicular adrenal rests in congenital adrenal hyperplasia mimic testicular tumour.

Adrenal Physiology

  • Zones (out → in): glomerulosa aldosterone; fasciculata cortisol; reticularis androgens.
  • Aldosterone driven by angiotensin II + K+ → glomerulosa survives pituitary failure.
  • Medulla: epinephrine 80%, norepinephrine 19%, dopamine 1%.
  • Phenylethanolamine-N-methyltransferase (PNMT) (norepinephrine → epinephrine) essentially adrenal-only; glucocorticoid-potentiated.
  • >90% of circulating metanephrine is adrenal → basis of diagnosis.

Evaluation of Adrenal Lesions

  • Gold standard first test: unenhanced CT; ≤0 HU 100% specific.
  • Gold standard characterisation: CT washout (unenhanced 5 mm → 60 s → 15 min).
  • Chemical-shift magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) drop-out ≈ unenhanced CT; gadolinium washout not used.
  • Pitfalls: up to 7% of metastases <10 HU; renal cell (RCC) and hepatocellular (HCC) carcinoma metastases wash out.
  • Malignancy by size: <4 cm 0.1%; 4–6 cm 2.4%; >6 cm ≈20%.
  • <10 HU: no malignancy regardless of size (largest series).
  • Macroscopic fat (<−30 HU) = myelolipoma; >50% fat typical.
  • Follow-up (European Society of Endocrinology–European Network for the Study of Adrenal Tumors, ESE–ENSAT): <4 cm, benign, silent → none; others 6–12 months.
  • Growth concern: >20% diameter and ≥5 mm (classic: 1 cm).
  • Phaeochromocytoma staging: fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography (FDG PET) gold standard; multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2 (MEN-2) → metaiodobenzylguanidine (MIBG).
  • MIBG cold in von Hippel–Lindau (VHL) and succinate dehydrogenase B (SDHB); FDG PET sensitive there.
  • Incidentaloma function: ≈11% active; ≈18% potentially surgical.
  • Screen for PA if: ≥3 drugs incl. diuretic; ≥4 drugs; ≥150/100 ×3.
  • Hypertension + hypokalaemia, incidentaloma + hypertension, onset <20, stroke <50.
  • ARR: 8–10 am seated; renin floor 0.2 ng/mL/h.
  • Cut-offs: ARR >20–30; plasma aldosterone >15–20 ng/dL.
  • Stop: spironolactone/amiloride 4–6 weeks; angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors/angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs) may continue.
  • β-blocker → false-positive ARR; ACE inhibitor/ARB → false negative.
  • Undetectable renin on ACE inhibitor/ARB → confirm despite normal ARR.
  • Skip confirmation: spontaneous hypokalaemia + undetectable renin + aldosterone >20.
  • IV saline 2 L/4 h: aldosterone >5 = PA; >10 suggests adenoma.
  • Oral salt: urine Na >200 mmol; aldosterone >12 µg/day = PA.
  • AVS: selectivity 1.1–5:1; lateralisation 2–4:1 (higher with cosyntropin).
  • Skip AVS: age <40 + unilateral adenoma >1 cm + normal contralateral.
  • Cortisol first line: 1 mg dexamethasone 11 pm → cortisol 8–9 am.
  • Pregnancy → urinary free cortisol; epilepsy → salivary; renal failure → dexamethasone test.
  • Oral contraceptive → up to 50% false-positive dexamethasone test.
  • Late-night salivary cortisol: ≤145 ng/dL (mass spectrometry).
  • Commonest cause of hypercortisolism: exogenous steroid.
  • Gold standard pituitary vs ectopic: inferior petrosal sinus sampling + corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH).
  • First line phaeochromocytoma: plasma free (supine 20 min) or urinary fractionated metanephrines.
  • Plasma upper limits: normetanephrine 0.61, metanephrine 0.31 nmol/L.
  • Diagnostic: normetanephrine >2.2 or metanephrine >1.2 nmol/L.
  • Stop: paracetamol ≥5 days; tricyclics; phenoxybenzamine.
  • Plasma free metanephrines: sensitivity 97–99% (Lenders multicentre study).
  • Biopsy only if: known cancer + result changes management.
  • Never biopsy: suspected ACC (seeding); unexcluded phaeochromocytoma.

Adrenal Neoplasia

  • Incidentaloma: >1 cm; ≈5% of scans; up to 7% at ≥70.
  • Commonest: adenoma (>85%); ≈93% of adenomas inactive.
  • PA commonest secondary hypertension: 3–13% primary care; 30% tertiary.
  • PA subtypes: bilateral hyperplasia 60%; adenoma 35%.
  • PA vs essential hypertension: atrial fibrillation ×12, myocardial infarction ×6.5, stroke ×4.
  • Familial hyperaldosteronism (FH) I: CYP11B1/CYP11B2 chimera → glucocorticoid-remediable; FH-III: KCNJ5.
  • After adrenalectomy for PA: biochemical cure ≈94%; drug-free 37–52%.
  • Medical PA: spironolactone 25–400 mg; eplerenone 25–100 mg (selective).
  • Post-op insufficiency: mild autonomous cortisol ≈65%; overt Cushing ≈99.7%.
  • Cover: hydrocortisone 100 mg IV day of surgery, then taper.
  • Adrenal recovery: 6.5 months (mild) vs 11 months (Cushing).
  • Phaeochromocytoma: ≈5% of incidentalomas; ≈0.5% of hypertension.
  • Hereditary ≈⅓; 24% of “sporadic” carry mutation → offer testing to all.
  • Highest malignancy: SDHB (30–50%); MEN-2 almost always adrenal.
  • VHL: normetanephrine only (metanephrine normal).
  • Malignant = metastases only; extra-adrenal >⅓ malignant.
  • Triad: headache, sweating, tachycardia; paroxysmal hypertension only 30–50%.
  • CT: >10 HU (mean ≈35), no washout; iodinated contrast safe.
  • Phenoxybenzamine: 10 mg twice daily, 7–14 days, ≈1 mg/kg.
  • Target: 120–130/80 mmHg seated; mild postural drop acceptable.
  • Metyrosine blocks tyrosine hydroxylase; 1.5–2 g/day.
  • Post-op: hypotension; rebound hypoglycaemia.
  • Follow-up: metanephrines at 6 months, then annually lifelong; recurrence up to 16%.
  • Recurrence risk: extra-adrenal hazard ratio (HR) 11.2, hereditary 3.4, right-sided 3.1.
  • MEN-2/VHL → cortical-sparing adrenalectomy.
  • Metastatic: ¹³¹I-MIBG (US Food and Drug Administration approval 2018); cyclophosphamide–vincristine–dacarbazine (CVD); 5-year survival ≈50%.

Nonfunctional Benign Lesions

  • Myelolipoma: operate only if large/symptomatic; proposed 6–10 cm cut-offs.
  • Smallest bleeding myelolipoma 6.3 cm; bleeders average 10–14 cm.
  • Cyst: pseudocyst carries malignancy (≈7% of cysts).
  • Excise cyst if: >5 cm, thick wall, heterogeneous, symptomatic.
  • Aspirate cyst only after excluding phaeochromocytoma.
  • Ganglioneuroma: young; encases vessels without narrowing; ≤ muscle density.
  • Adrenal oncocytoma: >30% malignant — unlike kidney.

Malignant Adrenal Neoplasms

  • ACC: 0.7–2 per million; bimodal (first decade, 40s–50s).
  • >90% exceed 5 cm; mean 10–12 cm; unenhanced ≈39 HU.
  • Functional: 50–79% adults, >90% children; cortisol commonest.
  • Feminisation in a man → carcinoma.
  • Weiss: ≥3 of 9 criteria = malignant; >5 mitoses/50 high-power fields.
  • American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC): I ≤5 cm, II >5 cm confined; ENSAT IV = distant only.
  • Stage IV ≈40–50% at diagnosis; 5-year survival <5%.
  • Recurrence after resection: 60–80%.
  • Cytoreduce if: >90% of disease removable; else systemic therapy first.
  • FIRM-ACT: mitotane + etoposide–doxorubicin–cisplatin (M-EDP) first line — better response/progression-free survival, not overall survival.
  • ADJUVO: no benefit of adjuvant mitotane (R0 stage I–III).
  • Adjuvant radiotherapy: ↓ local recurrence, no survival gain.
  • Surveillance: CT 3-monthly 2–3 years, then 6-monthly to 5.
  • Paediatric ACC: virilisation; Li–Fraumeni (TP53); stage split 200 g.
  • Known cancer: >50% of new adrenal lesions are metastases.
  • Adrenalectomy at nephrectomy only if: abnormal adrenal or thrombus to adrenal vein.

Non-neoplastic Adrenal Disorders

  • Cushing causes: pituitary ≈70%; ectopic 10–15%; adrenal ≈10%.
  • Trans-sphenoidal cure: 60–80%; macroadenoma <15%.
  • Bilateral adrenalectomy: after failed pituitary treatment or life-threatening disease.
  • Nelson syndrome: 8–29% after bilateral adrenalectomy — pituitary growth.
  • Ectopic adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) commonest: bronchial carcinoid; phaeochromocytoma can cause.
  • ACTH-independent macronodular hyperplasia: huge nodular glands (McCune–Albright).
  • Primary pigmented nodular disease: small pigmented glands (Carney complex).
  • Cushing urology: urolithiasis up to 50%; hypogonadotropic hypogonadism.
  • Addison commonest: autoimmune (West); tuberculosis (developing).
  • Secondary insufficiency: aldosterone preserved → no fludrocortisone.
  • Primary only: hyperpigmentation, hyperkalaemia, salt craving.
  • Stress dose: minor 25; moderate 50–75; major 50 mg 8-hourly 48–72 h.
  • Hydrocortisone 20 ≈ prednisone 5 ≈ dexamethasone 0.5 mg.
  • Congenital adrenal hyperplasia: 21-hydroxylase >95%; don’t screen incidentalomas.
Classic traps
  • Trap: PA needs hypokalaemia — Actually: most are normokalaemic.
  • Trap: calcification means benign — Actually: up to 30% of ACCs calcify.
  • Trap: β-blocker first for phaeochromocytoma tachycardia — Actually: never before α-blockade.
  • Trap: IV contrast triggers crisis — Actually: no evidence; safe.
  • Trap: CT nodule decides PA side — Actually: AVS; ≈20% of adenomas <1 cm.
  • Trap: histology proves malignant phaeochromocytoma — Actually: only metastases.
  • Trap: MIBG best for all — Actually: FDG PET, except MEN-2.
  • Trap: stop ACE inhibitors before ARR — Actually: stop spironolactone/amiloride instead.
  • Trap: adrenal metastases cause Addison — Actually: need bilateral, bulky >4 cm.
  • Trap: <10 HU always benign in known cancer — Actually: up to 7% of metastases are <10 HU.
