Chapter 31 — Tuberculosis and Parasitic Infections of the Genitourinary Tract — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else
  • Urogenital tuberculosis (UG TB) gold standard: urine acid-fast bacilli (AFB) culture, first-void, 3–5 consecutive days.
  • Hallmark: sterile pyuria ± haematuria.
  • Kidney = 80% of UG TB; epididymis second haematogenous site.
  • Standard regimen: 2 months isoniazid (INH) + rifampicin + pyrazinamide (+ ethambutol) → 4 months INH + rifampicin.
  • Surgery timing: 4–6 weeks after starting anti-TB drugs.
  • UG TB relapse 6.3–22% → follow up to 10 years.
  • Schistosomiasis gold standard: eggs in urine (collect 09:00–15:00, peak noon).
  • Praziquantel (PZQ) 40 mg/kg (2 × 20 mg/kg, 6–8 h apart) = only WHO drug.
  • Schistosomal bladder cancer: squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) 60–90%, age 40–50.
  • Lymphatic filariasis (LF): diethylcarbamazine (DEC) = drug of choice; never DEC in onchocerciasis.

Urogenital Tuberculosis

  • Commonest: M. tuberculosis then M. bovis; M. bovis innately pyrazinamide-resistant.
  • UG TB: 15–20% of pulmonary TB (developing) vs 2–10% (developed).
  • Extrapulmonary rank: 2nd after nodes (developing); 3rd after pleural, lymphatic (US).
  • Male : female about 2 : 1; adults.
  • Latency: 90–95% controlled; <5% primary progression; reactivation 5–15% lifetime.
  • Latency to UG disease up to 46 years.
  • Route: haematogenous (principal) → kidney, epididymis.
  • Intravesical bacille Calmette-Guérin (BCG) → UG TB in about 0.9%.
  • UG TB not infectious unless aerosol-generating procedure or skin inoculation.
  • Constitutional symptoms <20%; renal colic <10%.
  • Autonephrectomy up to 33%; end-stage renal disease about 7%.
  • Ureteric stricture commonest at vesicoureteric junction; pan-ureteric → beaded corkscrew.
  • Bladder: dome first, trigone spared; symptoms at <100 mL; thimble <20 mL.
  • Epididymis 10–55%, bilateral one-third; starts in globus minor; scrotal sinus up to 50%.
  • Prostate 22–49%; urethra 1.9–4.5%; hydrocele about 5%.
  • Coexisting pulmonary TB up to 20% → chest radiograph for all.

Diagnosis

  • Culture: Löwenstein-Jensen 4–6 weeks; Mycobacteria Growth Indicator Tube (MGIT) ≥10 days; always + solid medium.
  • Ziehl-Neelsen urine sensitivity <50%.
  • GeneXpert: TB + rifampicin resistance in 1–2 days; rifampicin resistance = multidrug-resistant (MDR) surrogate.
  • Nucleic acid amplification test (NAAT): adjunct only; never excludes TB; useless for monitoring.
  • Lateral-flow urine lipoarabinomannan (LF-LAM): HIV + symptoms, advanced HIV, or CD4 <200.
  • Tuberculin skin test (TST) cut-offs: ≥5 mm immunosuppressed or contact; ≥10 mm at-risk; ≥15 mm no risk.
  • Interferon-γ release assay (IGRA) preferred if ≥5 years, born in endemic country.
  • T-SPOT.TB most sensitive IGRA; IGRA unaffected by BCG.
  • Bladder biopsy 19–52% sensitive if culture-negative.

Imaging eponyms and signs

  • Kerr’s kink: hiked-up pelvis, sharp pelviureteric junction angulation.
  • Lobar calcification (“putty”/“cement” kidney) = pathognomonic end-stage TB.
  • Phantom calyx: stenosed calyx fails to opacify.
  • Moth-eaten calyx = early intravenous urography (IVU) sign.
  • Pipestem or beaded-corkscrew ureter → TB.
  • Golf-hole ureteric orifice → image upper tract.
  • Computed tomography (CT) urography = commonest modality (developed world).
  • Ultrasound → monitor hydronephrosis on treatment.

Treatment

  • Toxicities: INH → neuropathy (give pyridoxine); ethambutol → red-green vision; pyrazinamide → urate, arthralgia.
  • All first-line except ethambutol hepatotoxic.
  • Stop ethambutol once fully susceptible.
  • Rifabutin → uveitis; rifamycins → antiretroviral and immunosuppressant interactions.
  • Contraindicated: rifapentine in HIV, cavitary or extrapulmonary TB.
  • No twice-weekly continuation; ≥3×/week only.
  • Complicated UG TB: 12–24 months, or ≥6 months after surgery.
  • Steroids: proven only in TB meningitis; UG use anecdotal.
  • Surgery needed in about 55%; reconstruction >half of operations.
  • Obstruction: retrograde double-J first (41% success) → antegrade stent → nephrostomy (PCN).
  • Nephrectomy if nonfunctioning kidney + recalcitrant TB or resistant hypertension (improves 65%).
  • Open surgery if no improvement after 6 weeks of drugs.
  • Gaps: bladder mobilisation 2–3 cm; psoas hitch ≤5 cm; Boari 10–15 cm.
  • Augmentation <100 mL; substitution for thimble <20 mL.
  • Emergency drainage if uraemia or sepsis; avoid high contrast pressure.
  • Epididymectomy if testis spared; vas ligation at external ring.
  • Follow-up: pulmonary 2 years vs UG up to 10 years (mean relapse 5.3 years).

Special situations

  • MDR: INH + rifampicin; cure 50–60% vs 94–97%.
  • Pre-XDR (pre-extensively drug-resistant) = MDR + fluoroquinolone OR injectable.
  • XDR: MDR + fluoroquinolone + injectable, or bedaquiline/linezolid; cure 30–50%.
  • BPaL (bedaquiline, pretomanid, linezolid) 6–9 months; not approved extrapulmonary.
  • Pregnancy: INH + rifampicin + ethambutol + pyridoxine × 9 months; breastfeeding allowed.
  • HIV: 15–20× risk; 6 months daily; 9 months if not on antiretrovirals.
  • Transplant: within 6 months; fever; rifamycin-free ≥18 months; mortality up to 36%.

Parasitic Infections of the Urogenital Tract

Schistosomiasis

  • S. haematobium: vesical plexus, eggs in urine, Bulinus snail.
  • Egg: about 140 µm, terminal spine.
  • Bilharz, Cairo 1852: worms linked to eggs.
  • Adults live 3–5 years on average; cercaria → adult 80–110 days.
  • No cercarial shedding <15 °C or >35 °C.
  • Peak burden age 5–15; dams increase transmission (Aswan).
  • Obstruction commonest at juxtavesical ureter.
  • Hydroureter precedes hydronephrosis; tubular function lost before glomerular.
  • Tonic hydroureter → reversible hydronephrosis; segmental rarely significant; atonic aperistaltic.
  • International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) Group 1 carcinogen.
  • Mass drug administration (MDA), Egypt: bladder cancer 28% → 12%, shift SCC → urothelial.
  • Katayama fever: 2–8 weeks; mainly S. japonicum; steroids + PZQ.
  • Oviposition from 8–12 weeks; serology positive 4–6 weeks.
  • Contracted bladder ≤50 mL.
  • End stage: 40–60% of obstructive uropathy presents here.
  • Female genital schistosomiasis in 33–75% of infected women.
  • Male genital schistosomiasis: diagnose by eggs in ejaculate.
  • Vesicoureteric reflux in 25% of infected ureters.
  • PZQ efficacy 60–90%; obstruction resolves in 1–2 months; polyps in children 2–6 weeks.
  • Rectal snip before bladder biopsy; squash preparation shows viability.
  • Circulating antigens (CAA/CCA) = active infection only; serology stays positive after cure.
  • Artemisinins = prophylaxis (schistosomulae); not in malaria-coendemic MDA.
  • Deep chronic ulcer → partial cystectomy (not fulguration).
  • Mortality: obstructive uropathy in 20s; cancer and pyelonephritis >40.
  • Egg input must fall about 90% to cut transmission.
  • WHA65.21 (2012): shift from morbidity control to elimination.

Filariasis

  • W. bancrofti >90% of LF; only species with male genital disease.
  • Microfilaraemia peaks at midnight; Giemsa smear.
  • Adults 5–7 years; mature over 6–9 months.
  • Bancroftian lymphoedema whole limb vs Brugia below knee.
  • Acute adenolymphangitis (ADL) retrograde vs dermatolymphangioadenitis (DLA) proximal and bacterial.
  • Filarial dance sign on ultrasound: ≥80% sensitive.
  • Antigen test for W. bancrofti only; none for Brugia.
  • DEC 2 mg/kg three times daily; 1 day = 12 days.
  • Doxycycline 200 mg daily for 4–8 weeks → sterilises adults (Wolbachia).
  • Filarial hydrocele pointers: cord/epididymal nodules, thick tunica with cholesterol or calcium.
  • Hydrocelectomy: excise intact sac; ligate leaking lymphatics.
  • Tropical pulmonary eosinophilia: nocturnal cough, high IgE; microfilaraemia absent; DEC 2–3 weeks.
  • Contraindicated: doxycycline in pregnancy and young children.
  • MDA: albendazole + ivermectin (Africa), albendazole + DEC (elsewhere).
  • Onchocerciasis: Simulium fly; hanging groin; ivermectin then doxycycline.
  • Loa loa: Chrysops fly; no Wolbachia; microfilariae >2500–8000/mL → encephalopathy risk.

Other parasites

  • Hydatid kidney about 2–3%; PAIR (puncture, aspiration, injection, reaspiration) not for renal cysts; albendazole 1–6 months.
  • Hydatid cyst rupture → anaphylaxis.
  • Pinworm: single albendazole or mebendazole; treat household; repeat at 2 weeks.
  • Amoebiasis: tinidazole or metronidazole → then luminal agent (paromomycin or iodoquinol).
Classic traps
  • Trap: sterile pyuria = partially treated urinary tract infection — Actually: think UG TB.
  • Trap: negative TST/IGRA excludes active TB — Actually: neither rules TB in or out.
  • Trap: negative NAAT excludes UG TB — Actually: adjunct only; culture remains standard.
  • Trap: treatment always improves obstruction — Actually: healing fibrosis can worsen it; keep imaging.
  • Trap: 4-month rifapentine–moxifloxacin regimen for UG TB — Actually: not for extrapulmonary TB.
  • Trap: ureteric calcification same in TB and bilharzia — Actually: TB intraluminal, nondilated; bilharzia intramural, dilated.
  • Trap: PZQ aborts early infection — Actually: poor against schistosomulae; repeat after weeks.
  • Trap: bladder biopsy first for schistosomiasis — Actually: rectal snip first; squash preparation.
  • Trap: treat Salmonella bacteriuria with antibiotics alone — Actually: add PZQ; bacteria shelter in tegument.
  • Trap: DEC for all filariae — Actually: never in onchocerciasis; caution with Loa loa.
