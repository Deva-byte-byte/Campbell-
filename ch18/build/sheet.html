Chapter 18 — Fundamentals of Laparoscopic and Robotic Urologic Surgery — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else

  • Working pressure 10–12 mm Hg; never >20 mm Hg for long.
  • Never insufflate until aspiration, saline, drop and advancement tests all pass.
  • Open (Hasson) access when adhesions expected; Palmer point for closed entry.
  • Blunt trocars: about 5× less epigastric injury; hernia 1.8% → 0.19%.
  • Gas embolism: end-tidal CO₂ ↑ then ↓, saturation ↓ → desufflate, left lateral, head down, 100% O₂.
  • Thermal bowel injury may present up to 18 days later; CT with oral contrast.
  • Monopolar bowel burn → resect with 6-cm margins; bipolar → excise visible area.
  • Exit: inspect every port at 5 mm Hg; remove under vision; scope last.
  • Never metal trocar + plastic retainer (capacitive coupling).
  • No shoulder braces in Trendelenburg (brachial plexus).

Preoperative preparation

Contraindications:

  • Uncorrectable coagulopathy · untreated obstruction · abdominal wall infection · massive haemoperitoneum · generalised peritonitis · malignant ascites.

Special cases:

  • COPD (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease) → arterial gases + lung function tests pre-op.
  • Obesity: renal or adrenal laparoscopy still beats open; prostatectomy and cystectomy riskier (BMI >30).
  • Pregnancy: 8–12 mm Hg; second trimester; Palmer point; difficult after 20 weeks.
  • Hip prosthesis cement → pelvic fibrosis.
  • Aneurysm → left upper quadrant Veress or Hasson.
  • Robot >20 cases → better outcomes.

Preparation:

  • No mechanical bowel prep (even cystectomy with conduit).
  • ERAS (enhanced recovery after surgery, 2001): solids 6 h, carbohydrate drink 2 h; stay −30%, complications −50%.
  • Group and screen: nephrectomy (transfusion 3–12%), prostatectomy (2.5%).
  • Crossmatch: partial nephrectomy, cystectomy, caval thrombus (early experience).

Operating room and team

  • Lateral: lower leg 45°, upper straight; axillary roll 3–4 fingers below axilla.
  • Rhabdomyolysis risk: male, BMI ≥25, kidney rest, full flexion; rest <20–30 min.
  • Plain mattress beats gel or egg-crate for flank pressure.
  • Heparin 5,000 U SC optional; compression stockings for all.
  • Xi: 8-mm camera any port, boom, laser targeting; Si: 12-mm camera.
  • SP (single port): single arm, articulating camera, 360° access.
  • Retroperitoneal: true 90° lateral; team faces back; robot over head.
  • Pelvis: 30° Trendelenburg; Si robot between legs; Xi either side, no stirrups.

Performing the procedure

Veress:

  • 14 G, 2 mm OD (outer diameter); flow check 2 L/min → ≤2 mm Hg.
  • Two pops: fascia, peritoneum.
  • Umbilicus: non-obese angle to pelvis; obese perpendicular.
  • Lateral: 2 fingers medial + 2 superior to ASIS (anterior superior iliac spine).
  • Advancement test: 1 cm free.
  • Insufflate 9 L/min to 15 mm Hg; initial pressure <8–10 = free.

Open access and retroperitoneum:

  • Hasson: 2-cm infra-umbilical incision; two 0 silk stays.
  • Hand port 6.5–7.5 cm; incision (cm) = glove size.
  • Retroperitoneal: 2–2.5 cm below 12th rib tip, midaxillary line; psoas behind, kidney in front.
  • Balloon max 800 mL (40 pumps); oblong retroperitoneal, round pelvic.
  • Gaur dilator (1992): glove on 8-Fr catheter, 110 mm Hg.
  • Extraperitoneal: 1.5–2 cm infra-umbilical; finger to pubis.
  • Balloon port → less emphysema and hypercarbia retroperitoneally.

Trocars:

  • Primary trocar: perpendicular (upper abdomen) vs 70° caudad (pelvis).
  • Blunt vs bladed: bleeding 3% vs 9%; complications 3% vs 10%.
  • AirSeal: stable pneumoperitoneum at 8–12 mm Hg; smoke evacuation.
  • Suturing angles: <55° to horizontal; 25–45° between instruments.
  • Robot ports 8–10 cm apart; assistant port caudal; black line below wall.

Instruments:

  • Thermal spread: monopolar 5–7 mm; bipolar 1–6; LigaSure 1–3; ultrasonic ≈1.
  • LigaSure seals ≤7 mm; one application only.
  • Ultrasonic 25–55 kHz; pacemakers OK; blade >200 °C, cools up to 45 s.
  • Staples: 2.0 grey / 2.5 white = vascular; 3.8 blue / 4.8 green = thick.
  • Endo-TA on donor renal vein → longer vein.
  • Never staple across clips; no diathermy near clips.
  • Lapra-Ty: 3.5 mm, polydioxanone, 14 days; avoid with 4-0.
  • Hem-o-lok donor artery: ≥2 clips, 2-mm cuff; donor use contraindicated by maker.
  • LapSac: most tear-resistant, 2 kg, morcellation; ring forceps.
  • EndoWrist: 7 degrees of freedom, 90° articulation, 10-use limit.
  • 5-mm robotic scope = 2D.
  • Robot failure <1%; malfunction 0.4%.

Exit:

  • Bladed: close all 10–12-mm sites (children ≥5 mm).
  • Blunt: close midline >10 mm only.
  • Hand port removed and closed first.
  • Carter-Thomason for BMI >30.

Physiology

  • CO₂ standard; helium for COPD or hypercarbia (embolism risk; start with CO₂).
  • N₂O supports combustion → no diathermy; air and O₂ abandoned.
  • 12 mm Hg = no stroke-volume change; 10 mm Hg → less oliguria.
  • 40 mm Hg → venous return, output and heart rate ↓.
  • Oliguria = renal vein and parenchymal compression; mannitol 12.5–25 g, dopamine 2 µg/kg/min; target UO (urine output) >100 mL/h.
  • CVP (central venous pressure) unreliable under pneumoperitoneum.
  • COPD: arterial gases if insufflation >1 h; continue in recovery.
  • CO₂ absorption plateaus after 30–60 min.
  • Head-down: CO ↑ (cardiac output), MAP ↑ (mean arterial pressure), HR ↓ (heart rate); head-up: CO ↓.
  • Hypercarbia peaks after extubation.

Complications

  • Contemporary complications 13–22%; mortality 0.03–0.08% historically.
  • Commonest: vascular, then adjacent organ.
  • Training: course + preceptorship (≥5 cases) + mentor.
  • Preperitoneal: pressure spike at about 500 mL; camera sees fat.
  • Bowel insufflation: asymmetry, flatus, high pressure <2 L.
  • Barotrauma >15 mm Hg → restart at 10.
  • Pneumothorax 1.6–4%: 16 G, 2nd–3rd intercostal space, MCL (midclavicular line) → drain.
  • Pneumopericardium about 0.8%; trocars below 12th rib.
  • Vascular injury 0.11–2%: aorta and common iliac artery commonest; retroperitoneal > pelvic.
  • Trocar in vessel → leave in, laparotomy along trocar.
  • Venous bleed → pressure 25 mm Hg, tamponade; pinch IVC (inferior vena cava) walls.
  • Port-site bleed: cantilever to find; Keith needle over bolster.
  • Bladder injury: indigo carmine; always repair.
  • Free air persists up to 9 days; left shift > white count.
  • Nerve injury about 2.8%; brachial plexus commonest.
  • Hand-port hernia 4–7% → interrupted non-absorbable closure.
  • Transverse incision: hernia 5.4% → 0.4% after robotic prostatectomy.
  • DVT prophylaxis (deep vein thrombosis): compression 48–72 h; high risk + heparin 5,000 U q12h.
  • Hand-port wound infection about 9%.
  • Rhabdomyolysis: CK (creatine kinase) >5,000; kidney rest <1 h; cases <5 h.
  • Bowel entrapment: day 2–3, ileus + port tenderness.

Training

  • E-BLUS (EAU Basics in Laparoscopy, 2013): peg transfer, circle cutting, needle guidance, suturing.
  • FRS (Fundamentals of Robotic Surgery): RCT → better speed and accuracy.
  • VR (virtual reality) beats box or none for novices; swine commonest animal.
  • Practice suture: 3-0 on SH (small half-circle) needle.
  • Mini-residency (5 days): about 90% doing robotic prostatectomy at 3 years.

Classic traps

  • Trap: lift fascia during Veress — Actually: widens preperitoneal gap only.
  • Trap: Veress angled to pelvis in obese — Actually: perpendicular (umbilicus lower).
  • Trap: shoulder braces in steep Trendelenburg — Actually: brachial plexus injury; use straps or beanbag.
  • Trap: trust CVP intraoperatively — Actually: falsely raised.
  • Trap: fluid boluses for oliguria — Actually: lower pressure; mannitol or furosemide or dopamine.
  • Trap: ultrasonic = safe near bowel — Actually: blade >200 °C after firing.
  • Trap: repeat LigaSure for security — Actually: weakens the seal.
  • Trap: catheter drainage alone for trocar bladder injury — Actually: always repair.
  • Trap: remove trocar from injured vessel — Actually: leave it in, open along it.
  • Trap: normal day-1 course excludes bowel injury — Actually: thermal necrosis up to 18 days.
