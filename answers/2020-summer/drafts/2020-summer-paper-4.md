## Q1. Describe immunobiology of renal transplant and newer immunosuppressant drugs.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

### Introduction
- The recipient's immune system recognises donor antigens as foreign. Immunosuppression has cut acute rejection from at least 60% (pre-cyclosporine era) to **below 10%**. Slow, subclinical **antibody-mediated injury** is now the main cause of late graft loss.

### Transplant antigens
| Antigen | Features |
|---|---|
| **HLA (human leucocyte antigen) = MHC (major histocompatibility complex)** | Class I (A, B, C) on all nucleated cells; class II (DR, DQ, DP) on antigen-presenting and endothelial cells. Class II mismatch is most immunogenic |
| **ABO** blood group | On endothelium; incompatibility causes hyperacute rejection unless desensitised |
| Minor and non-HLA antigens | Rejection despite HLA identity |

- **Sensitisation**: transfusion, pregnancy or previous transplant create preformed anti-HLA antibodies.
- **Testing**: CDC (complement-dependent cytotoxicity) and flow crossmatch (donor lymphocytes with recipient serum); **Luminex single-antigen beads** define each recipient's antibodies and allow a **virtual crossmatch**; cPRA (calculated panel-reactive antibody) expresses sensitisation.

### Innate immunity: the first trigger
- **Ischaemia–reperfusion injury** releases danger signals (DAMPs, damage-associated molecular patterns). These activate **Toll-like receptors**, complement and macrophages, and up-regulate MHC on the graft. Hence DGF (delayed graft function) raises rejection risk.
- **Dendritic cells** bridge innate and adaptive immunity: activated, they up-regulate MHC and costimulatory B7 and migrate to lymph nodes.

### Allorecognition
| Pathway | Mechanism | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct** | Recipient T cells recognise **intact donor MHC** on donor "passenger" dendritic cells | Very strong (high T-cell frequency); early acute T-cell rejection |
| **Indirect** | Recipient APCs (antigen-presenting cells) present **processed donor peptides on self-MHC** | Lifelong; helps B cells make DSA (donor-specific antibody); chronic rejection |
| **Semi-direct** | Recipient APCs acquire intact donor MHC by membrane transfer | Links both pathways |

### Three-signal model of T-cell activation
1. **Signal 1**: TCR (T-cell receptor) binds MHC–peptide → **calcineurin** dephosphorylates NFAT (nuclear factor of activated T cells) → IL-2 (interleukin-2) transcription.
2. **Signal 2 (costimulation)**: **CD28 binds CD80/86 (B7)**. Signal 1 without signal 2 causes **anergy**. CTLA-4 (cytotoxic T-lymphocyte antigen-4) is the natural brake.
3. **Signal 3**: IL-2 binds **CD25** (IL-2 receptor) → **mTOR (mammalian target of rapamycin)** → cell cycle G1 to S → clonal proliferation, which needs de novo purine synthesis.

### Effector mechanisms
- **Cellular**: CD4 Th1 cells (IL-2, IFN-γ (interferon-γ)) recruit macrophages; CD8 cytotoxic T cells kill by perforin and granzyme → **tubulitis, endarteritis**.
- **Humoral**: B cells with T-cell help become plasma cells → **DSA** binds endothelium → complement (leaves **C4d** in peritubular capillaries) → **microvascular inflammation**. NK (natural killer) cells cause complement-independent injury.
- **Regulation**: **Tregs** (regulatory T cells, FoxP3+) suppress responses; the basis of tolerance research.

### Rejection (brief; detail in 2020-Summer Paper 1, Q3d)
- **Hyperacute** (preformed antibody; prevented by crossmatch); **acute TCMR** (T-cell-mediated rejection); **acute AMR** (antibody-mediated rejection); **subclinical**; **chronic active AMR** (transplant glomerulopathy). **Banff** classification grades biopsy findings.
- **Monitoring**: creatinine, **de novo DSA**, biopsy, **dd-cfDNA (donor-derived cell-free DNA)** (negative predictive value >90%), gene-expression panels.
- **Treatment**: TCMR: IV (intravenous) steroids, ATG (antithymocyte globulin) for Banff II–III. AMR: steroids, IVIG (intravenous immunoglobulin), plasmapheresis, rituximab.

### Standard immunosuppression
- **Induction** (>90%) plus **tacrolimus + mycophenolate** ± steroids.

### Newer immunosuppressant drugs
| Drug | Mechanism (site) | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| **Belatacept** | **CTLA4-Ig**: binds CD80/86, blocks **signal 2** | IV monthly; CNI (calcineurin inhibitor)-free. **BENEFIT**: better GFR (glomerular filtration rate), more early acute rejection; 7-year better patient and graft survival. **Contraindicated if EBV (Epstein–Barr virus)-seronegative** (PTLD (post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder)) |
| **Extended-release tacrolimus** | Binds FKBP12 → inhibits calcineurin (**signal 1**) | Once daily, better adherence, steadier levels |
| **mTOR inhibitors** (sirolimus, everolimus) | Bind FKBP12 → block mTOR (**signal 3**) | Not nephrotoxic; fewer CMV (cytomegalovirus) infections; antitumour effect. **TRANSFORM (2018)**: everolimus + reduced CNI non-inferior. Poor wound healing, **lymphocele**, proteinuria, dyslipidaemia |
| **Basiliximab** | Anti-CD25, non-depleting (**signal 3**) | Low-risk induction, days 0 and 4 |
| **rATG** (rabbit ATG); **alemtuzumab** | T-cell depletion; anti-CD52 | High-risk induction; infection, PTLD |
| **Rituximab; bortezomib** | Anti-CD20 (B cells); proteasome inhibitor (plasma cells) | AMR, desensitisation |
| **Eculizumab** | Anti-C5 (complement) | Selected AMR; aHUS (atypical haemolytic uraemic syndrome) |
| **Imlifidase** | Enzyme that **cleaves IgG** | EMA (European Medicines Agency) 2020: highly sensitised, crossmatch-positive |
| **Anti-IL-6** (tocilizumab, clazakizumab) | Blocks IL-6 pathway | Chronic active AMR |

- **Also know:** mycophenolate inhibits IMPDH (inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase), blocking lymphocyte purine synthesis; anti-CD40 costimulation blockade under trial.

### Recent advances
- **Tolerance induction** by mixed chimerism (combined kidney and donor stem-cell transplant) and Treg therapy; **pig-kidney xenotransplant** into a living human (2024).

**Diagram to draw:** APC–T-cell synapse showing signals 1–3 with drug sites (CNI at 1, belatacept at 2, basiliximab and mTOR inhibitors at 3, mycophenolate at DNA synthesis).

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Direct pathway drives early TCMR; indirect pathway drives DSA and chronic rejection.
> - Signal 1 calcineurin, signal 2 CD28–B7, signal 3 IL-2R–mTOR.
> - Belatacept blocks signal 2; better GFR, avoid if EBV-negative.
> - Imlifidase (IgG cleavage) for highly sensitised; dd-cfDNA NPV >90%.

## Q2. Describe etiopathogenesis of stone formation and medical management of urolithiasis.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

### Introduction
- Urolithiasis is a **chronic metabolic disease** punctuated by stone episodes; recurrence is up to 50% within 5 years. Removal is surgical; recurrence is metabolic.
- **Composition**: calcium oxalate about 60%, mixed with apatite about 20%, uric acid and struvite about 7% each, cystine 1–3%.

### Aetiology: risk factors
- **Epidemiological**: male sex (gap narrowing), hot climate and occupation (low urine volume), diet (animal protein, salt), family history.
- **Systemic**: **metabolic syndrome**, obesity, diabetes (insulin resistance lowers urine pH), gout, primary hyperparathyroidism, RTA (renal tubular acidosis), bowel disease or bypass, sarcoidosis.
- **Anatomical**: PUJ (pelviureteric junction) obstruction, horseshoe kidney, calyceal diverticulum, medullary sponge kidney (stasis plus metabolic defects).
- **Drugs**: direct (indinavir, triamterene); indirect (acetazolamide, topiramate, vitamin C and D excess).

### Physicochemistry: state of saturation
| State | Concentration product (CP) | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Undersaturated | < Ksp (solubility product) | No crystals; stones may dissolve |
| **Metastable** | Between Ksp and Kf (formation product) | No spontaneous nucleation; growth on existing crystals; inhibitors work. **Zone of therapy** |
| Unstable | > Kf | Spontaneous nucleation, growth, aggregation |

- Crystals form in metastable urine by local high CP, stasis, or **heterogeneous nucleation** on cells, debris or other crystals (needs less energy).

### Steps of stone formation
1. **Supersaturation** (low volume, high calcium, oxalate, uric acid; low citrate).
2. **Nucleation**, then **growth** and **aggregation** (aggregation can occlude a tubule within the 5–7 minute nephron transit).
3. **Retention**: fixed-particle theory. **Randall plaque** (calcium apatite) starts in the basement membrane of the thin loop of Henle, spreads through the interstitium, erodes the papillary urothelium and anchors calcium oxalate growth (idiopathic formers). **Ductal plugs** (crystals in collecting ducts) suggest a secondary cause (enteric hyperoxaluria, distal RTA, hyperparathyroidism, cystinuria).

### Inhibitors, promoters, matrix
- **Inhibitors**: **citrate** (complexes calcium), **magnesium** (complexes oxalate), pyrophosphate, glycosaminoglycans, nephrocalcin, **Tamm-Horsfall protein**, osteopontin, bikunin. There is **no inhibitor of uric acid**.
- **Matrix** (about 2.5% of stone weight; matrix substance A): role uncertain.

### Pathogenesis by abnormality
| Abnormality | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Hypercalciuria** (commonest) | Absorptive (gut), renal leak (high PTH (parathyroid hormone)), resorptive (primary hyperparathyroidism) |
| Hyperoxaluria | Primary (hepatic enzyme defect, PH1 AGXT), **enteric** (fat binds calcium, freeing oxalate), dietary |
| Hyperuricosuria | Urate promotes calcium oxalate nucleation |
| **Hypocitraturia** | Distal RTA, diarrhoea, hypokalaemia, animal protein (intracellular acidosis) |
| **Uric acid** | **Low urine pH** (<5.5; pKa 5.5) more than excess uric acid |
| **Cystine** | Autosomal recessive defect in renal cystine reabsorption |
| **Struvite** | **Urease** (Proteus) splits urea → ammonia, pH >7.2 → magnesium ammonium phosphate + carbonate apatite; biofilm |

### Medical management
**1. Acute colic and MET (medical expulsive therapy)**
- **NSAIDs (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs)** first line; opioids only if refractory.
- **α-blocker** (tamsulosin; silodosin faster) for **distal ureteric stones 5–10 mm**: relaxes ureteric muscle. **SUSPEND (2015)** was negative because it included all sizes and sites. Trial of passage 4–6 weeks.

**2. Dissolution**: uric acid stones (radiolucent) dissolve with oral potassium citrate (EAU (European Association of Urology): pH 7.0–7.2 for dissolution).

**3. Prevention: general (every former)**
- **Fluid** for urine **≥2.5 L/day** (Borghi 1996: 12.1% vs 27% recurrence at 5 years).
- **Normal calcium**, low sodium, less animal protein, more fruit and vegetables (Borghi 2002: halved recurrence versus low-calcium diet).

**4. Metabolic evaluation** (high-risk and recurrent formers): stone analysis, serum chemistry, one or two **24-h urines**; repeat within 6 months of therapy, then yearly (see 2015-Summer Paper 2, Q2).

**5. Selective drug therapy**
| Abnormality | Drug and dose | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Hypercalciuria | **Thiazide**: hydrochlorothiazide 25 mg twice daily, chlorthalidone 25–50 mg, indapamide 2.5 mg | Distal calcium reabsorption; add potassium citrate (hypokalaemia → hypocitraturia) |
| Hypocitraturia, distal RTA | **Potassium citrate 30–60 mEq/day** | Alkali load reduces tubular citrate reabsorption |
| Hyperuricosuria | Allopurinol 300 mg/day | Watch hypersensitivity |
| Uric acid stones | Potassium citrate to **pH 6.0–6.5** | pH disease |
| Enteric hyperoxaluria | **Calcium with meals**, low oxalate and fat, magnesium | Binds gut oxalate |
| Primary hyperoxaluria type 1 | Pyridoxine; **lumasiran** (RNA (ribonucleic acid) interference) | ILLUMINATE-A |
| Cystinuria | Urine ≥3 L, pH **7.0–7.5**; **tiopronin** | Cystine <250 mg/L; mixed disulfide |
| Struvite | Complete clearance; AHA (acetohydroxamic acid) 250 mg three times daily | Urease inhibitor; thromboembolism |

- **Also know:** recurrent calcium stones with normal work-up: thiazide or citrate (AUA (American Urological Association)); NOSTONE (2023): hydrochlorothiazide did not reduce recurrence; citrus juices are adjuncts.

**Diagram to draw:** saturation zones (Ksp, Kf) and Randall plaque on a papilla.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Metastable zone between Ksp and Kf is where inhibitors and therapy act.
> - Randall plaque = interstitial apatite anchor; ductal plugs suggest secondary cause.
> - Urine ≥2.5 L; MET for distal 5–10 mm stones.
> - Uric acid pH 6.0–6.5; cystine pH 7.0–7.5, <250 mg/L.

## Q3a. Neobladder.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition
- An **orthotopic** reservoir of detubularised bowel (usually ileum) joined to the native urethra after radical cystectomy, allowing **volitional voiding** without a stoma. 80–90% of men and about 75% of women are medically eligible.

### Principles
1. **Reliable outlet**: intact rhabdosphincter, healthy urethra.
2. **Low pressure**: complete **detubularisation** abolishes coordinated contractions; folding into a **sphere** gives most volume for surface area (Laplace).
3. **Adequate volume**: <200 mL initially, **500–600 mL** by 1 year.

### Selection
- **Absolute contraindications**: tumour at the **urethral margin** (frozen section), urethral stricture, severe renal (eGFR (estimated glomerular filtration rate) <40 mL/min) or hepatic failure, inability to self-catheterise.
- **Relative**: prostatic stromal invasion (men), bladder-neck or anterior vaginal tumour (women), poor sphincter, irradiated pelvis, frailty. Age alone is not a bar.

### Types
| Design | Segment | Ureters |
|---|---|---|
| **Studer** | 40–44 cm ileum + afferent limb | Refluxing, isoperistaltic limb |
| **Hautmann W** | About 70 cm ileum | Refluxing; largest capacity |
| T pouch | 44 cm + T limb | Antireflux flap valve |
| Camey II; Padua | Ileum | Antireflux (Le Duc) |
| Mainz III; sigmoid | Ileocaecal; colon | Tunnelled |

### Studer technique (brief)
- Isolate ileum 15–25 cm from the ileocaecal valve; open the distal part on the antimesenteric border, sew into a U, fold into a sphere; end-to-side ureteric anastomoses to the afferent limb; anastomose the dependent point to the urethra.

### Outcomes
- **Day continence 80–90%** (3–6 months); **night incontinence 20–50%** (no reflex, mucosal water secretion).
- **Retention**: men 4–10%, **women 20–30%**: men leak, women retain.

### Complications
- **Early**: urine leak, ileus, infection, mucus retention.
- **Late**: **hyperchloraemic metabolic acidosis**, **vitamin B12** deficiency, ureteroileal stricture (<3%), stones, pouch rupture, hernia, urethral recurrence.

### Care and follow-up
- Mucus irrigation, bicarbonate; voiding training (sit, relax pelvic floor, strain), timed voiding, night alarm.
- Annual metabolic panel, B12, upper-tract imaging; urethral wash cytology if CIS (carcinoma in situ).

**Diagram to draw:** Studer U-pouch with afferent limb and urethral anastomosis.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Detubularise and make a sphere; 500–600 mL by 1 year.
> - Absolute bar: positive urethral margin, stricture, eGFR <40.
> - Day continence 80–90%, night 20–50%; women retain (20–30%).

## Q3b. Vascular access for dialysis.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Introduction
- Haemodialysis needs blood flow of **250–350 mL/min**; access failure is a major cause of morbidity in ESKD (end-stage kidney disease).

### Types
| Access | Construction | Ready | Comment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Radiocephalic AVF** (arteriovenous fistula; Brescia–Cimino, 1966) | Radial artery to cephalic vein at wrist (vein end to artery side) | 6–8 weeks | **First choice**: best patency, fewest infections; most fail to mature |
| Brachiocephalic AVF | Elbow | 6–8 weeks | Higher flow, more steal |
| **AVG** (arteriovenous graft) | PTFE (polytetrafluoroethylene) loop or straight | 2–3 weeks | More stenosis, thrombosis, infection |
| **Tunnelled cuffed CVC** (central venous catheter) | Right internal jugular | At once | Bridge or last resort; infection, central stenosis |

- Non-tunnelled 10–12 Fr jugular or femoral catheters: acute dialysis only.

### Planning
- **Hierarchy**: AVF → AVG → CVC; **distal before proximal; non-dominant arm first**. KDOQI (Kidney Disease Outcomes Quality Initiative) 2019: individual "ESKD Life-Plan".
- **Timing**: refer at eGFR (estimated glomerular filtration rate) <30; create AVF about **6 months** before dialysis.
- **Vein preservation**: no venepuncture, cannula or PICC (peripherally inserted central catheter) in that arm; avoid subclavian lines.
- **Assessment**: pulses, **Allen test** (ulnar collateral flow), **Doppler vein mapping** (vessels about ≥2 mm).
- **Maturity "rule of 6s"**: flow >600 mL/min, diameter >6 mm, depth <6 mm, at 6 weeks.

### Complications
| Complication | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Failure to mature (juxta-anastomotic stenosis) | Angioplasty, ligate side branches |
| Stenosis (neointimal hyperplasia) | PTA (percutaneous transluminal angioplasty), stent graft |
| **Thrombosis** (usually on a stenosis) | Thrombectomy, then fix stenosis |
| **Steal syndrome** (hand ischaemia) | Banding, DRIL (distal revascularisation–interval ligation) |
| Aneurysm; infection (CVC > AVG > AVF) | Repair; antibiotics, excise graft |

- **Also know:** brachiobasilic transposition AVF; venous hypertension; high-output cardiac failure.

### Monitoring
- Thrill and bruit, arm-elevation test, prolonged bleeding after needling, rising venous pressure; duplex when abnormal.

### Recent advances
- **Endovascular AVF** (WavelinQ, Ellipsys); drug-coated balloons; bioengineered vessels.

**Diagram to draw:** radiocephalic end-to-side fistula at the wrist.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - AVF first, distal, non-dominant, 6 months ahead.
> - Rule of 6s: >600 mL/min, >6 mm, <6 mm deep, 6 weeks.
> - Thrombosis sits on stenosis; steal → banding or DRIL.

## Q3c. STING technique for VUR.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition and principle
- **STING (subureteric Teflon injection)**: endoscopic injection of a **bulking agent** beneath the ureteric orifice in VUR (vesicoureteral reflux). The mound supports the intramural ureter, restoring the **flap-valve**.
- Matouschek, 1981; popularised by **O'Donnell and Puri, 1986**.

### Bulking agents
| Agent | Particle | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| Teflon (PTFE (polytetrafluoroethylene) paste) | 90% <40 µm, permanent | **Migration**; never FDA (Food and Drug Administration) approved |
| **Dx/HA (dextranomer/hyaluronic acid, Deflux)** | **80–250 µm**; gel resorbed | Volume loss about 23%; FDA approved 2001; standard agent |
| Polyacrylate (Vantris) | 300 µm, permanent | Late ureteric obstruction |

### Technique
1. Antibiotic at induction; offset-lens cystoscope; rigid 3.7–5 Fr needle; bladder partly filled.
2. **STING**: needle enters **2–3 mm below the orifice at 6 o'clock**, advanced **4–5 mm** submucosally.
3. Inject slowly; a mound should appear within **0.1–0.2 mL**, else reposition.
4. Aim for a **"volcano" mound** with a slit-like orifice. Do not cauterise bleeding. Day case.

| Technique | Needle entry | Result |
|---|---|---|
| STING | Below orifice | Baseline |
| **HIT** (hydrodistension implantation technique) | Inside hydrodistended orifice | Better than STING, all grades |
| **Double HIT** | Two intraureteric sites | About 90% resolution |

### Indications
- Persistent primary VUR (grades II–IV) with breakthrough febrile UTI (urinary tract infection), new scars, poor adherence or parental preference; residual VUR after reimplantation.
- **Relative contraindications**: grade V, **UVJ (ureterovesical junction) obstruction**, untreated BBD (bladder and bowel dysfunction).

### Results
- Low-grade VUR about 90% after one injection. **Elder 2006 meta-analysis**: grade I–II about 79%, III 72%, IV 63%, V 51%.
- **Swedish Reflux Trial**: 20% recurrence at 2 years.

### Complications
- Failure or recurrence (repeat 1–2 injections, then reimplant); new contralateral VUR; **early or late obstruction**; calcified mound mimics a stone. **Also know:** Macroplastique.

### Follow-up
- Antibiotics 3 months; ultrasound at 2–6 weeks; ultrasound ± VCUG (voiding cystourethrogram) at 3 months.

**Diagram to draw:** needle sites: STING below orifice, HIT and double HIT inside the tunnel, with volcano mound.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - O'Donnell and Puri 1986; needle 2–3 mm below orifice at 6 o'clock.
> - Dx/HA 80–250 µm; HIT better than STING; double HIT about 90%.
> - Watch late obstruction; calcified mound mimics stone.

## Q3d. HOLEP.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

*Read as: holmium laser enucleation of the prostate for BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia).*

### Principle
- **HoLEP (holmium laser enucleation of the prostate)** removes the whole adenoma along the **surgical capsule**: the endoscopic equivalent of open simple prostatectomy.
- **Ho:YAG (holmium:yttrium–aluminium–garnet), 2120 nm**: absorbed by water; pulsed; **0.4 mm penetration**; heat over 2–3 mm coagulates vessels. Normal saline irrigation (no TUR (transurethral resection) syndrome).

### Indications
- **Size-independent** (<30, 30–80, >80 mL), with or without a median lobe; **anticoagulated or high bleeding risk** (AUA (American Urological Association) recommendation); alternative to open prostatectomy for large glands.

### Steps
- 60–150 W laser, 550 µm end-firing fibre, 24–28 Fr resectoscope; 2.0 J × 40–50 Hz to enucleate.
1. Bladder-neck incisions at 5 and 7 o'clock to the capsule (median lobe) or one at 6 o'clock.
2. Perimontanal incision; **early apical release** to protect the sphincter.
3. Develop the capsular plane to 12 o'clock; enucleate lobes into the bladder (**en bloc, 2-lobe or 3-lobe**).
4. Haemostasis with a defocused beam.
5. **Morcellation** with a full bladder; 22 Fr three-way catheter.

### Outcomes
- Versus **TURP (transurethral resection of the prostate)**: **better IPSS (International Prostate Symptom Score) and Qmax (maximum flow rate)**, durable at 3–8 years; less bleeding, shorter catheter time and stay; longer operation. The **only endoscopic procedure superior to TURP**.
- **Retreatment rare**: whole transition zone removed.

### Complications
- **Retrograde ejaculation about 75%**; transient storage symptoms and early SUI (stress urinary incontinence).
- BNC (bladder neck contracture) 0–3.2%; urethral stricture; capsular perforation; ureteric orifice injury; **morcellator bladder injury**; retained adenoma.
- Erectile function unchanged.

### Limitation and comparison
- **Steep learning curve** (efficiency plateaus after about 50 cases). Versus open prostatectomy: equal function, less morbidity.

### Recent advances
- **Moses pulse modulation**; thulium enucleation (ThuLEP); median-lobe-only HoLEP (antegrade ejaculation about 90%); same-day discharge.

**Diagram to draw:** sagittal prostate showing enucleation along the capsule.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Ho:YAG 2120 nm, water chromophore, 0.4 mm.
> - Size-independent; best for anticoagulated men.
> - Only endoscopic method superior to TURP; retreatment rare.
> - Retrograde ejaculation about 75%; learning curve about 50 cases.

## Q3e. Nitrofurantoin.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Introduction
- A synthetic **nitrofuran**; **first-line for acute uncomplicated cystitis** (EAU (European Association of Urology)) because it concentrates in urine and spares bowel and vaginal flora.

### Mechanism
- Reduced by bacterial flavoproteins (nitroreductases) to **reactive intermediates** that damage ribosomal proteins, DNA and several enzyme systems.
- **Bacteriostatic**, bactericidal at high urinary concentration.
- Multiple targets keep **acquired resistance low**.

### Pharmacokinetics
- Well absorbed (better with food); **rapidly excreted in urine**. Serum and tissue levels are sub-therapeutic.
- Hence **useless for pyelonephritis, prostatitis, febrile or bloodstream infection**.
- Macrocrystals: slower absorption, less nausea.

### Spectrum
| Susceptible | Resistant |
|---|---|
| ***E. coli*** (most), ***Enterococcus faecalis***, *Staphylococcus saprophyticus*, *S. aureus* (not MRSA (methicillin-resistant *S. aureus*)), many *Klebsiella*, *Citrobacter* | ***Proteus*** (intrinsic), *Pseudomonas*, *Serratia*, *Providencia*, *Morganella*, *Acinetobacter* |

- Active against many ESBL (extended-spectrum β-lactamase)-producing *E. coli*.

### Uses and doses
| Indication | Regimen |
|---|---|
| Uncomplicated cystitis (women) | **100 mg twice daily × 5 days** (modified release) |
| Recurrent UTI (urinary tract infection) prophylaxis, adults | 50–100 mg at night, or post-coital |
| Children: treatment / prophylaxis | 3–5 mg/kg/day in 2 doses / 1–2 mg/kg at night (VUR (vesicoureteral reflux), after 3 months of age) |
| Pregnancy | Second and third trimesters; first trimester only if no alternative; **avoid near term** |

### Adverse effects
- **GI (gastrointestinal) upset** (commonest).
- **Pulmonary**: acute hypersensitivity (fever, cough, dyspnoea); **chronic interstitial fibrosis** with long-term use.
- **Peripheral neuropathy** (worse with renal impairment, diabetes, anaemia, B-vitamin deficiency).
- **Haemolysis in G6PD (glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase) deficiency**, including the neonate (hence avoid at term and <3 months of age).
- Hepatotoxicity (cholestatic or chronic hepatitis), rare.

### Precautions
- **Renal impairment**: inadequate urine levels and more toxicity. Historically avoided if CrCl (creatinine clearance) <60; Beers Criteria (2015) allow short courses down to **CrCl 30 mL/min**; avoid long-term use in the elderly.
- Interactions: probenecid (blocks excretion), magnesium antacids, quinolones (antagonism).

**Diagram to draw:** spectrum table (susceptible versus resistant).

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Urine-only drug: cystitis yes, pyelonephritis or prostatitis no.
> - 100 mg twice daily × 5 days; prophylaxis 50–100 mg at night (children 1–2 mg/kg).
> - Proteus intrinsically resistant; resistance in E. coli low.
> - Toxicity: pulmonary fibrosis, neuropathy, G6PD haemolysis; CrCl ≥30 (Beers 2015).

## Q3f. Describe evidence based medicine.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition
- **EBM (evidence-based medicine)**: "the conscientious, explicit and judicious use of current best evidence in making decisions about the care of individual patients" (**Sackett, 1996**).
- It integrates best research evidence, **clinical expertise** and **patient values**.

### Steps ("5 As")
1. **Ask** a focused question in **PICO** form (Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome).
2. **Acquire**: PubMed, Cochrane Library, guidelines.
3. **Appraise**: validity (randomisation, blinding), effect size (ARR (absolute risk reduction), **NNT (number needed to treat) = 1/ARR**), precision (95% CI (confidence interval)), applicability.
4. **Apply** with expertise and patient preference.
5. **Assess** (audit) the outcome.

### Hierarchy: levels of evidence (Oxford CEBM (Centre for Evidence-Based Medicine), as adapted by EAU (European Association of Urology))
| Level | Type of evidence |
|---|---|
| **1a** | Systematic review/meta-analysis of RCTs (randomised controlled trials) |
| **1b** | At least one RCT |
| 2a | Well-designed controlled study without randomisation |
| 2b | Quasi-experimental (cohort) study |
| 3 | Non-experimental: case-control, correlation, case reports |
| 4 | Expert committee reports or opinion |

- **Grades**: A (consistent level 1), B (level 2–3), C (level 4 or consensus); D (some systems) = no recommendation possible.
- **GRADE** (Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation): certainty high to very low; RCTs start high, observational studies low; **downgrade** for bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, publication bias; **upgrade** for large effect or dose–response. Recommendations **strong or weak** (now used by EAU). AUA (American Urological Association): evidence strength A/B/C; strong, moderate or conditional recommendations.

### Pitfalls in appraisal
- **Bias**: selection, confounding (comorbidity, frailty: needs risk adjustment), **lead-time**, **length-time**, stage migration (Will Rogers), attribution.
- **Surrogate end points** must meet the **Prentice criteria** (prostate cancer: only metastasis-free survival does).
- **Also know:** Clavien–Dindo; validated PROs (patient-reported outcomes); CONSORT, PRISMA, STROBE checklists; Donabedian model.

### Limitations
- Few surgical RCTs; trial patients differ from real ones; publication bias; "cookbook medicine" if patient values are ignored.

**Diagram to draw:** evidence pyramid and the three overlapping circles of EBM.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Sackett 1996: best evidence + clinical expertise + patient values.
> - 5 As: Ask (PICO), Acquire, Appraise, Apply, Assess.
> - Level 1a = systematic review of RCTs; GRADE: strong or weak.
> - NNT = 1/ARR; beware lead-time, length-time and confounding bias.
