## Q1. Mention in brief, urological complications in OBS-GYN. Describe in detail treatment of ureterovaginal fistula.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)
(OBGY complications also answered in Practice Set 3 Paper 3, Q1; ureterovaginal fistula in 2015-Summer Paper 3, Q3e and 2017-Summer Paper 3, Q3b)

*Assumed meaning: OBS-GYN = obstetric and gynaecological practice and surgery.*

### Part A: Urological complications in OBS-GYN
- The bladder, distal ureter and urethra lie against the cervix, uterine vessels and anterior vaginal wall. Risk rises with distorted anatomy: fibroids, endometriosis, cancer, previous caesarean, radiation, heavy bleeding.

| Setting | Complications |
|---|---|
| **Obstetric** | **Obstructed labour** (fetal head impacted for days): pressure necrosis, so large VVF (vesicovaginal fistula), urethral loss, ureteric involvement; over 90% of VVF in the developing world. **Caesarean**: bladder injury (about 0.3%), ureteric injury, **vesicouterine fistula** (Youssef syndrome: cyclical haematuria, amenorrhoea, continence preserved). Post-partum or post-epidural **retention** |
| **Gynaecological surgery** | **Bladder injury** (commonest organ; hysterectomy about 0.3–0.75%). **Ureteric injury** (hysterectomy 0.2–2.4%; over 90% distal third; ligation commonest). **Fistulae**: VVF (over 75% follow hysterectomy in the developed world), UVF (ureterovaginal fistula), urethrovaginal fistula |
| **Radical hysterectomy** | Pelvic plexus injury: retention, poor compliance; manage with CIC (clean intermittent catheterisation) |
| **Prolapse and continence surgery** | Ureteric kinking at apical suspension; mesh or sling exposure, perforation, outlet obstruction, pain |
| **Radiotherapy** | Radiation cystitis, VVF, ureteric stricture |

- **Prevention:** know the four ureteric danger sites; cystoscopy after hysterectomy where indicated; prophylactic stents only selectively (they aid recognition but do not prevent injury).

### Part B: Ureterovaginal fistula (UVF)
**Aetiology and presentation (brief)**
- About **three-quarters follow an uneventful abdominal hysterectomy**: an unrecognised distal ureteric injury (ligation, crush, thermal, devascularisation) necroses and drains into the vaginal cuff. Diagnosis is delayed in 18–88%; **delayed detection raises fistula risk about fourfold**.
- **Continuous leakage with normal voiding** (the other kidney fills the bladder), usually **within 4 weeks**, often with fever or flank pain.

**Confirming the diagnosis before treatment**
| Test | Method and reading |
|---|---|
| **Double dye test** | Oral phenazopyridine 100–200 mg; after 1 hour, blue dye into the bladder; tampon; walk. **Orange = UVF**; blue = VVF; both = both |
| **CTU (computed tomography urography), delayed phase** | Hydronephrosis, extravasation; MR (magnetic resonance) urography if contrast is contraindicated |
| **Cystoscopy + RGP (retrograde pyelogram)** | Abrupt cut-off or contrast leak; stent at the same sitting |
- **Exclude a coexisting VVF** (up to 25% of VVF have a UVF) and bilateral injury.

### Treatment
**Goals:** dryness and preservation of renal function. Management has moved from nephrectomy, through reimplantation, to endourology.

**1. Endourological (first line)**
- **Retrograde double-J stent as soon as possible.** Stenting **within 2 weeks** of detection succeeds far more often than at 2–6 weeks, because the ureter heals over the stent before fibrosis.
- Wire will not pass: **PCN (percutaneous nephrostomy)** with antegrade stent, or rendezvous.
- Stent for **4–6 weeks**, then remove and re-image. Most stentable fistulae close; a stricture may follow.

**2. Surgical repair (stent fails or impossible)**
- **Timing:** no consensus. Early repair took less time and blood loss with equal success; a systematic review found equal success before and after 6 weeks. Delay only if tissue is still inflamed.
- **Ureteroneocystostomy** (distal third, so usually feasible):
  1. Transperitoneal or extraperitoneal approach; identify ureter above the fistula.
  2. Divide above the diseased segment; dissect as distally as possible for length; **preserve periureteric adventitia**, especially laterally where vessels enter.
  3. Mobilise the bladder; spatulate; tension-free mucosa-to-mucosa anastomosis over a double-J stent; refluxing or tunnelled.
  4. Drain; catheter 7–14 days.
- **Adding length:**

| Technique | Extra reach |
|---|---|
| **Psoas hitch** (sutures parallel to psoas fibres, spare genitofemoral nerve) | Up to about 5 cm |
| **Boari flap** (base ≥4 cm, length:base ≤3:1) | 10–15 cm |
| Downward nephropexy; ileal ureter | Long defects |
- Open, **laparoscopic and robotic repair give equal results**; vaginal reimplantation reported. **Success ≥90%.**
- The vaginal opening is left to heal; no separate vaginal repair is needed.

```
Suspected UVF -> exclude VVF (dye test, cystoscopy) -> CTU / RGP
  -> stent passes? Yes -> double-J 4-6 wk -> re-image -> healed / persistent
                   No  -> PCN + antegrade stent -> fails
  -> ureteroneocystostomy + psoas hitch / Boari flap
```

### Complications and follow-up
- Hydronephrosis, infection, ischaemic stricture.
- Ultrasound 1–2 weeks after stent removal; some add a renal scan at 6 weeks. Stricture usually appears within a year but **up to 30 months**, so follow beyond this.

**Diagram to draw:** ureter passing under the uterine artery; ureteroneocystostomy with psoas hitch.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Bladder is the commonest organ injured; obstructed labour causes most fistulae worldwide.
> - UVF: leak with normal voiding; orange tampon on double dye test.
> - Stent within 2 weeks; remove at 4–6 weeks.
> - Reimplant with psoas hitch or Boari flap; success ≥90%; follow 30 months.

## Q2. Discuss the developmental basis of extrophy epispadias complex. Briefly outline management
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)
(theories also answered in Practice Set 6 Paper 1, Q2; single-stage repair in Practice Set 3 Paper 3, Q3e)

### Introduction
- **EEC (exstrophy–epispadias complex)** is one spectrum: glanular epispadias → **CBE (classic bladder exstrophy)**, over 50% → cloacal exstrophy.
- CBE about 1 in 46,000; male:female 2.3:1. Offspring of an affected parent: 1 in 70.

### Developmental basis
**1. Normal development**
- The **cloacal membrane** (bilaminar ectoderm–endoderm) occupies the infraumbilical wall. **Mesenchyme grows in** between its layers and forms the lower abdominal muscles and pelvic bones.
- The **urorectal septum** divides the cloaca into bladder (anterior) and rectum (posterior), meets the membrane, which perforates into urogenital and anal openings. Paired genital tubercles fuse **cephalad** to the membrane.

**2. Core defect**
- The cloacal membrane is **not reinforced by mesodermal ingrowth**, so it ruptures. **Timing and extent of rupture decide the variant:** before septum descent gives cloacal exstrophy; after gives CBE; limited rupture gives epispadias.

**3. Theories**
| Theory | Proposed defect | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Cloacal membrane overdevelopment (Marshall–Muecke)** | Abnormally large membrane in week 4 blocks medial mesenchymal migration | Timing of rupture gives the variant |
| Lateral body-wall fold failure | Folds fail to meet in midline | Abdomen and pelvis → cloacal; pelvis alone → classic |
| Caudal genital hillocks | Tubercles fuse **below** the membrane | Dorsal urethral defect (epispadias) |
| Abnormal body-stalk insertion | Caudal insertion stops mesenchyme interposing | Unstable membrane disintegrates |
| Bony pelvis first | Pelvic ring primordium fails to rotate | Bladder herniates |
- All converge on **absent infraumbilical midline mesoderm**.

**4. Aetiology**
- Multifactorial (inconsistent twin concordance). **22q11.2 duplication** commonest variant; **ISL1** susceptibility gene; p63 under-expressed in exstrophic urothelium.
- **IVF (in vitro fertilisation)** about 7.5-fold and early high-dose progesterone about tenfold raise risk; maternal smoking linked to cloacal exstrophy; folate to the mildest form.

**5. Resulting anatomy (why each defect occurs)**
- **Pelvis:** externally rotated (posterior 12°, anterior 18° each side), **anterior pubic segment 30% short**, **diastasis mean 4.8 cm**.
- **Male genitalia:** anterior corpora about 50% shorter; short, upturned penis with dorsal chordee.
- **Female:** bifid clitoris, short vagina, anterior stenotic introitus; later prolapse.
- **Bladder:** immature, not neuropathic (raised type III collagen falls after closure). Ureters enter with no obliquity, so **VUR (vesicoureteric reflux) in 100%** after closure.
- Anterior anus, wide levator hiatus, inguinal hernias, low umbilicus.

### Outline of management
**At birth**
- Tie cord with silk; cover the plate with plastic wrap; saline irrigation. Counselling; deliver in an exstrophy centre. Prenatal diagnosis only about 25%: absent bladder filling, low umbilicus, wide pubic rami, small genitalia, enlarging lower abdominal mass.
- **Assess:** template size (**≥3 × 3 cm** suits newborn closure), polyps, diastasis, pelvic malleability.
- Small template, penoscrotal duplication, hypoplastic bladder: **delayed closure at 6–9 months**.

**MSRE (modern staged repair of exstrophy)**
- Aim of closure: convert exstrophy into incontinent epispadias, with enough outlet resistance for bladder growth but not enough to harm the kidneys.
| Stage | Age | Procedure |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Newborn | Bladder, posterior urethra and abdominal wall closure; ± osteotomy |
| 2 | 6–12 months | Epispadias repair (**modified Cantwell-Ransley**) |
| 3 | 5–7 years (capacity ≥100 mL) | Bladder neck reconstruction (**modified Young-Dees-Leadbetter**) + Cohen reimplantation |

- **Osteotomy** (combined anterior innominate + vertical iliac, external fixator, Buck traction 4–6 weeks): if closure after 72 hours, diastasis >4 cm, poorly malleable pelvis, failed closure, or cloacal exstrophy. Under 72 hours with malleable pubes: modified Bryant traction for 4 weeks.
- Key to closure: **radical division of the urogenital diaphragm fibres** so the bladder sits deep in the pelvis.
- **Alternatives:** **CPRE (complete primary repair, Mitchell)** with penile disassembly; Kelly radical soft-tissue mobilisation; Erlangen total repair; Mainz ureterosigmoidostomy.

**Outcomes**
- Daytime dryness after MSRE about **70–74%**; CPRE + YDL about 56%. **Bladder capacity at neck repair is the main predictor**; each failed closure lowers it.
- Failures: augmentation, bladder neck closure with continent catheterisable stoma.

**Cloacal exstrophy (OEIS: omphalocele, exstrophy, imperforate anus, spinal defects)**
- Stabilise; neurosurgery (spinal defects 85–100%); separate bowel from hemibladders; colostomy; osteotomy always; gender congruent with karyotype; continence mostly by augmentation with CIC (clean intermittent catheterisation).

**Also know:** variants (superior vesical fissure, duplicate, pseudoexstrophy); adenocarcinoma risk (cytology, cystoscopy); fertility and prolapse in adults; female epispadias.

**Diagram to draw:** cloacal membrane with urorectal septum and failed mesodermal ingrowth.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Failed mesodermal reinforcement of the cloacal membrane; rupture timing decides variant.
> - Diastasis 4.8 cm; anterior pubis 30% short; VUR in 100%.
> - MSRE: closure → epispadias repair 6–12 months → YDL at 5–7 years.
> - Osteotomy if >72 h or >4 cm; capacity ≥100 mL predicts continence.

## Q3a. Energy sources in lap uro.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in 2015-Summer Paper 2, Q3d)

### Principles
- All devices achieve haemostasis by **denaturing protein**, by heat (current, light) or mechanical vibration.
- **Electrosurgery** uses high-frequency alternating current (about 500 kHz) that does not stimulate nerve or muscle. **Current density** decides heating: the small tip burns, the large pad does not.

| Mode | Waveform | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Cut** (50–80 W) | Continuous, low voltage | Cell water flash-boils; clean cut |
| **Coagulation** (30–50 W) | Interrupted (about 6% duty cycle), high voltage | Desiccation, deeper spread |
| Fulguration (spray) | Higher voltage, sparks | Surface charring of oozing beds |
| Blend | Cut with interruptions | Cut plus coagulation |

### Devices
| Device | Principle | Lateral spread | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Monopolar** (hook, scissors) | Tip → patient → return pad | **5–7 mm** | Versatile; stray-current risk |
| **Bipolar** | Current only between jaws | 1–6 mm | No pad; no capacitive coupling |
| **Vessel sealer** (LigaSure) | Bipolar + compression + impedance feedback; built-in blade | 1–3 mm | Seals vessels **≤7 mm**; one application |
| **Ultrasonic** (Harmonic) | Blade vibrating at **25–55 kHz** | **≈1 mm** | No current: safe with pacemakers; jaws **>200 °C**, slow to cool, so do not touch bowel |
| **Argon beam** | Current through argon, non-contact | Superficial | Liver, spleen, raw surfaces; gas embolism |

- **Also know:** lasers (KTP [potassium titanyl phosphate], holmium) rarely used; laparoscopic RFA (radiofrequency ablation, 60 °C) and cryoablation (below −40 °C, two freeze–thaw cycles); robotic HotShears, bipolar Maryland, Vessel Sealer.

### Electrosurgical injuries
| Mechanism | Prevention |
|---|---|
| Direct activation out of view | Activate only with tip in view |
| **Direct coupling** to another metal instrument | Whole metal tip visible |
| **Capacitive coupling** (metal cannula in plastic retainer) | Never mix metal and plastic ports |
| **Insulation failure** | Inspect; **AEM (active electrode monitoring)** |
| Pad burns; surgical fire | Pad on muscle, away from implants; REM (return electrode monitoring) |
- Bowel injuries present late (days) as peritonitis.

**Diagram to draw:** monopolar vs bipolar circuits.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Spread: monopolar 5–7 > bipolar 1–6 > sealer 1–3 > ultrasonic ≈1 mm.
> - LigaSure seals ≤7 mm; ultrasonic blade hottest after use.
> - Capacitive coupling: avoid hybrid trocars; AEM detects it.

## Q3b. Describe in details anatomy of prostate with special reference to newer concept in surgical management of localized CA prostate
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in Practice Set 2 Paper 4, Q1)

### Anatomy
- About 20 g; base at bladder neck, apex at urogenital diaphragm. **No true capsule**.

| Zone (McNeal) | Glands | Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Peripheral | 70% | **70% of cancers**; posterolateral extension along nerves |
| Central | 25% | Around ejaculatory ducts |
| Transition | 5% | BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia); anterior cancers |
| AFMS (anterior fibromuscular stroma) | None | Anterior margin |

- **Fascial planes:** intrafascial (most nerves, more margins), **interfascial (standard)**, extrafascial (widest margin). Denonvilliers fascia excised if posterior extension suspected.
- **NVB (neurovascular bundle):** cavernous nerves posterolateral, **5 and 7 o'clock** at the apex; diffuse "hammock" distribution. Injured by heat and traction.
- **Accessory pudendal arteries:** preserve. **DVC (dorsal venous complex)** tamponaded by pneumoperitoneum.
- **Continence:** striated sphincter at apex; anterior support (puboprostatic ligaments, detrusor apron).
- **Lymphatics:** obturator, external and internal iliac, presacral nodes.

### Newer surgical concepts
1. **RARP (robot-assisted radical prostatectomy):** Yaxley 2016 RCT (randomised controlled trial): function equal to open, less blood loss.
2. **Risk-graded nerve sparing** using mpMRI (multiparametric MRI) and biopsy, side by side.
3. **NeuroSAFE:** frozen section of posterolateral margin guides nerve sparing.
4. **Retzius-sparing** (Bocciardi 2010), through the pouch of Douglas: earlier continence, more margins anteriorly; **Hood technique** preserves anterior support.
5. **Apical dissection:** maximal urethral length, cold division (commonest margin site); posterior (Rocco) reconstruction.
6. **Extended PLND (pelvic lymph node dissection)** when nodal risk >5% (EAU [European Association of Urology]): captures about 63% of drainage; staging, not therapeutic.
7. **Single-port robot**, extraperitoneal; **focal therapy** of the index lesion (HIFU [high-intensity focused ultrasound], cryotherapy).

**Diagram to draw:** axial prostate with three fascial planes and NVB at 5 and 7 o'clock.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Peripheral zone: 70% of cancers; no true capsule.
> - Interfascial plane standard; apex commonest margin site.
> - Retzius-sparing: early continence, more margins.

## Q3c. APOGEE
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

*Assumed meaning: the Apogee transvaginal mesh kit (American Medical Systems) for posterior vaginal wall and vault prolapse. It is not described in the chapter notes; facts below are general.*

### Definition
- A **prepackaged polypropylene mesh kit** placed transvaginally to support the **posterior compartment and vaginal apex** (DeLancey levels I and II). Anterior companion: **Perigee** (transobturator).
- Mesh: **type I macroporous monofilament polypropylene** (Amid classification), the only type that integrates into tissue.

### Indications (historical)
- Symptomatic posterior and vault prolapse, mostly **recurrent** or with poor native tissue, avoiding abdominal surgery.

### Technique (principle)
1. Midline posterior vaginal incision; dissect the rectovaginal space to the ischial spines.
2. Two small perianal incisions lateral and posterior to the anus.
3. Curved trocars pass through the **ischiorectal fossa**, piercing the iliococcygeus near the ischial spine, and deliver the mesh arms.
4. Mesh body fixed to the apex and laid over the rectovaginal septum **tension-free**; epithelium closed without trimming.

### Complications
- **Mesh exposure** (IUGA/ICS [International Urogynecological Association/International Continence Society] term; "erosion" deprecated), extrusion, **perforation** into rectum or bladder.
- **Dyspareunia**, buttock and pelvic pain, mesh contraction, defecatory dysfunction, ischiorectal haematoma or abscess, rectal injury.
- **Management:** small exposure → vaginal oestrogen, then partial excision; pain or perforation → excision of mesh and arms.

### Regulatory status
- **FDA (US Food and Drug Administration) 2011:** serious complications of transvaginal POP (pelvic organ prolapse) mesh "not rare", with no clear benefit over native repair. Reclassified high-risk in 2016.
- **2019:** FDA ordered remaining manufacturers to **stop selling transvaginal POP mesh**; Apogee-type kits are off the market. Synthetic mesh improves anatomy but complicates more (Cochrane 2016).
- This does **not** apply to mid-urethral slings or **sacrocolpopexy mesh**.

### Current alternatives
- Native posterior colporrhaphy or site-specific repair; uterosacral suspension or **sacrospinous fixation**; **sacrocolpopexy** (abdominal, laparoscopic, robotic) for apical prolapse; colpocleisis in the frail.

**Diagram to draw:** posterior mesh with two arms through the ischiorectal fossae.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Apogee: transvaginal posterior/apical polypropylene mesh kit.
> - Exposure, dyspareunia and pain drove withdrawal.
> - FDA 2019 stopped transvaginal POP mesh sales.

## Q3d. Newer techniques of stone analysis
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in 2024-Winter Paper 4, Q3a)

### Why analyse stones
- Analyse every first stone (AUA [American Urological Association], EAU [European Association of Urology]); repeat on recurrence despite treatment or early recurrence after clearance.
- Composition points to the cause: **uric acid** metabolic, **cystine** genetic, **struvite** infection.
- Composition can change over time (e.g. calcium oxalate converting to brushite), so re-analyse recurrent stones.

### Limits of wet chemical analysis
- Detects ions, not **crystal phases**: cannot separate COM (calcium oxalate monohydrate) from COD (dihydrate), or apatite from brushite. High error rates; misses rare stones; needs a large sample.

### Newer methods
| Method | Principle | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| **FTIR (Fourier-transform infrared) spectroscopy** | Each compound absorbs a characteristic infrared spectrum | Commonest reference method; small sample; detects drugs and proteins |
| **XRD (X-ray diffraction)** | Crystal lattice gives a unique diffraction pattern | Gold standard for crystals; misses amorphous material |
| Polarising microscopy | Birefringence | Cheap; observer-dependent |
| **Morpho-constitutional analysis (Daudon)** | Stereomicroscopy of surface and section + FTIR | Subtypes point to cause (e.g. Ic: primary hyperoxaluria) |
| SEM (scanning electron microscopy) ± EDX (energy-dispersive X-ray) | Surface structure, elements | Research |
| Micro-CT (micro-computed tomography) | 3-D, non-destructive | Research |
| Raman spectroscopy | Scattered light spectrum | Research |

### In vivo prediction
- **DECT (dual-energy CT):** two tube energies (e.g. 80 and 140 kVp) separate **uric acid from non-uric acid** stones.
- **Hounsfield units:** >1000 HU predicts SWL (shock wave lithotripsy) failure.
- **Endoscopic recognition** of morphology (lost when stones are dusted).

### Significance in management
- **Uric acid:** oral dissolution (potassium citrate).
- **Struvite:** complete clearance, culture-directed antibiotics.
- **Cystine:** hydration, alkalinisation, tiopronin.
- **COM, brushite, cystine:** SWL-resistant; prefer endoscopy.
- **Brushite:** avoid over-alkalinisation.
- **2,8-DHA (dihydroxyadenine):** APRT deficiency, allopurinol. **Drug stones** (indinavir, triamterene): stop the drug.

**Diagram to draw:** COM (dumb-bell) vs COD (envelope) crystals.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - FTIR and XRD are reference methods; wet chemistry obsolete.
> - DECT identifies uric acid stones in vivo.
> - Composition guides dissolution, SWL choice and prevention.

## Q3e. Describe principles of tissue engineering and what its status in managemet of urological diseases is.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in 2023-Winter Paper 1, Q1 and 2022-Summer Paper 4, Q2)

### Definition
- **Tissue engineering** applies engineering and life sciences to build biological substitutes that restore or improve tissue function (Langer and Vacanti, 1993).
- **Rationale:** bowel in the urinary tract causes mucus, stones, acidosis and cancer; grafts are scarce.

### Principles
```
Biopsy -> cells expanded -> seeded on scaffold (+ signals)
       -> bioreactor conditioning -> implant -> host vascularises
```
| Element | Details |
|---|---|
| **Cells** | Autologous urothelial and smooth muscle cells (no rejection); adult stem cells (bone marrow, adipose, urine-derived; mainly paracrine); embryonic and iPSCs (induced pluripotent stem cells): tumour risk |
| **Scaffolds** | Biocompatible, porous, degrade as tissue forms. Natural (collagen), **acellular matrices** (bladder matrix, SIS [small intestinal submucosa]), synthetic (PGA, PLGA [polyglycolic, poly(lactic-co-glycolic) acid]) |
| **Seeding** | Urothelium inside, muscle outside. **Unseeded** matrix works only for short onlay defects; tubes need seeded scaffolds or they contract |
| **Signals** | Growth factors (e.g. VEGF [vascular endothelial growth factor]), matrix and mechanical cues |
| **Bioreactor** | Perfusion and cyclic stretch to mature the construct |
| **Vascularity** | Oxygen diffuses only **100–200 µm**: main barrier; omental wrap, angiogenic factors |

### Status in urology
| Organ | Status |
|---|---|
| Bladder | **Atala 2006**: seeded constructs in 7 myelomeningocele patients improved compliance; phase II trial showed **no significant gain**. Experimental |
| Conduit | Neo-Urinary Conduit trial: **half developed stenosis** |
| Urethra | Acellular onlay for short strictures; **Raya-Rivera 2011**: seeded tubular urethras in **5 boys**, functional up to 6 years |
| Sphincter | Muscle-derived cell injection for SUI (stress urinary incontinence): investigational |
| Vagina | Engineered vaginas in MRKH (Mayer–Rokitansky–Küster–Hauser) syndrome |
| Penis, kidney, testis | Corporal grafts, kidney organoids, spermatogonial stem cell banking: experimental |

- **Recent advances:** 3D bioprinting, organoids, vascularised composite bladder allograft.
- **Challenges:** vascularity, innervation, fibrosis, cost, regulation.

**Diagram to draw:** biopsy → culture → scaffold → bioreactor → implant.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Cells, scaffold, signals, bioreactor, blood supply.
> - Diffusion limit 100–200 µm.
> - Still experimental except short urethral onlays.

## Q3f. HOLEP
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in 2020-Summer Paper 4, Q3d)

*Read as: holmium laser enucleation of the prostate for BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia).*

### Principle
- **HoLEP (holmium laser enucleation of the prostate)** removes the whole adenoma along the **surgical capsule**: the endoscopic equivalent of open simple prostatectomy.
- **Ho:YAG (holmium:yttrium–aluminium–garnet), 2120 nm**: water absorbs it; pulsed; **0.4 mm penetration**; coagulates vessels. Saline irrigation, so no TUR (transurethral resection) syndrome.

### Indications
- **Size-independent** (<30, 30–80, >80 mL), with or without median lobe.
- **Anticoagulated or high bleeding risk** (AUA [American Urological Association]); alternative to open prostatectomy for large glands.

### Steps
- 60–150 W laser, 550 µm end-firing fibre, 24–28 Fr scope; **2.0 J × 40–50 Hz** for enucleation.
1. Bladder-neck incisions at 5 and 7 o'clock (median lobe) or one at 6 o'clock.
2. Perimontanal incision; **early apical release** protects the sphincter.
3. Develop the capsular plane to 12 o'clock; push lobes into the bladder (**en bloc, two- or three-lobe**).
4. Haemostasis with a defocused beam.
5. **Morcellation** with a full bladder; three-way catheter.

### Outcomes
- Versus **TURP (transurethral resection of the prostate)**: **better IPSS (International Prostate Symptom Score) and Qmax (maximum flow rate)**, durable; less bleeding, shorter catheter time and stay; longer operating time.
- **Only endoscopic procedure superior to TURP**; retreatment rare (whole transition zone removed).

### Complications
- **Retrograde ejaculation about 75%**; transient storage symptoms and early SUI (stress urinary incontinence).
- BNC (bladder neck contracture) 0–3.2%; stricture; capsular perforation; ureteric orifice injury; **morcellator bladder injury**.
- Erectile function unchanged.

### Limitation
- **Steep learning curve** (about 50 cases).

### Recent advances
- **Moses pulse modulation**; ThuLEP (thulium enucleation); median-lobe-only HoLEP (antegrade ejaculation about 90%); same-day discharge.

**Diagram to draw:** sagittal prostate with enucleation plane along the capsule.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Ho:YAG 2120 nm, water chromophore, 0.4 mm.
> - Size-independent; suits anticoagulated men.
> - Only endoscopic method superior to TURP; retrograde ejaculation about 75%.
