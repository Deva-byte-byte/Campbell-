## Q1. Describe developmental embryology of testes and its physiological descent. Mention various abnormalities associated with testicular descent.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)
(descent also answered in 2024-Winter Paper 1, Q2; maldescent in Practice Set 5 Paper 3, Q3b)

### Development of the testis
- **Indifferent gonad:** the gonadal (germinal) ridge forms at **4 weeks** on the medial urogenital ridge. It has four components: coelomic epithelium, underlying mesenchyme, adjacent mesonephros and **PGCs (primordial germ cells)**.
- PGCs arise in the **yolk sac wall in week 3** and migrate along the dorsal mesentery of the hindgut to the ridge (about 6 weeks).
- **SRY (sex-determining region Y)** on the short arm of Y, partly activated by SF1 (steroidogenic factor 1), switches on **SOX9** in pre-Sertoli cells. Sertoli cells then appear (about 40 days). Without SRY no Sertoli cells form and the gonad becomes an ovary.
- Sertoli cells enclose germ cells as **testicular cords (about 6 weeks)**, which canalise into seminiferous tubules.
- **Rete testis** forms with the cords and joins **5–12 mesonephric tubules**, which become efferent ductules. Full continuity (tubules, rete, efferent ductules, epididymis) is established by the **third month**.
- The testis turns round and a thick **tunica albuginea** forms. Testes become spherical after 7–8 weeks; ovaries stay oblong.

| Hormone | Cell | Action | Male remnants |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AMH (anti-Müllerian hormone)** | Sertoli | Müllerian regression, weeks 8–10 | Appendix testis, prostatic utricle |
| **Testosterone** | Leydig (from 8 weeks; driven by placental hCG (human chorionic gonadotrophin), later fetal LH (luteinising hormone)) | Wolffian duct to epididymis, vas, seminal vesicle (weeks 8–12) | Appendix epididymis, paradidymis |
| **DHT (dihydrotestosterone)** | Formed locally by 5α-reductase type 2 | External genitalia, prostate, scrotum (fused by 10–12 weeks) | – |
| **INSL3 (insulin-like peptide 3)** | Leydig | Gubernacular swelling (via RXFP2 receptor) | – |

### Physiological descent
- At **7–8 weeks** the testis lies near the kidney, held by the **CSL (cranial suspensory ligament)** above and the **gubernaculum** below. The gubernaculum appears at 7 weeks and attaches to the epididymis.

| | Transabdominal phase | Inguinoscrotal phase |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | Weeks 10–15 | From the 7th month; passage rarely before 22 weeks; most testes scrotal after 27 weeks |
| Events | **CSL regresses**; **gubernaculum swells**; its cord shortens, holding the testis at the internal ring while the kidney moves cranially | Gubernaculum migrates **3–5 cm** to the scrotum; **processus vaginalis** forms within it; bulb then regresses and processus closes |
| Control | CSL regression: androgen. Swelling: INSL3 (occurs even in androgen resistance) | **Androgen**, mostly via the **GFN (genitofemoral nerve)** releasing **CGRP (calcitonin gene-related peptide)**; intra-abdominal pressure |
| Failure gives | Intra-abdominal testis | Canalicular or ectopic testis |

- **Modern view:** transabdominal "descent" is largely relative. Barteczko and Jacob describe **five phases (5–28 weeks)**. The gubernaculum **swells to make room** (outgrowth), then **shrinks to let the testis follow** (regression). Testosterone (peak 14–16 weeks) and INSL3 (15–17 weeks) act together on gubernacular and cremaster myogenesis.
- Spontaneous postnatal descent occurs mainly in the first 3–6 months.

### Abnormalities of descent

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Undescended (cryptorchid)** | Arrested on the normal path: abdominal, "peeping" at the ring, canalicular, prescrotal. 1–4% of term boys, up to 45% preterm; 75–80% palpable; 60–70% unilateral |
| **Ectopic** | Off the path: **superficial inguinal pouch** (commonest), perineal, femoral, prepubic, penile, contralateral scrotum |
| **Retractile** | Scrotal testis pulled up by the cremaster; stays down after sustained traction; some become acquired |
| **Acquired (ascending)** | Documented scrotal earlier; diagnosed at 8–11 years; fibrous processus remnant |
| Recurrent, secondary, iatrogenic | Re-ascent after spontaneous descent; after hernia repair or orchidopexy |
| **Vanishing** vs agenesis | Lost after formation (perinatal torsion; monorchia, anorchia) vs never formed |

| Failed step | Result |
|---|---|
| CSL retained | Intra-abdominal testis |
| Abnormal or ectopic gubernacular attachment | Canalicular or ectopic testis |
| Processus not closed | Hernia, hydrocele (patent in 87% of unilateral cases) |
| Processus not fully involuted | Acquired cryptorchidism |

### Associated anomalies and sequelae
- **Testicular:** **TTE (transverse testicular ectopia)**, polyorchidism, splenogonadal fusion; epididymal non-fusion.
- **PMDS (persistent Müllerian duct syndrome):** AMH or AMHR2 mutation; bilateral cryptorchidism or TTE.
- **Genital and DSD (differences of sex development):** hypospadias (12–24%); bilateral non-palpable testes need karyotype and 17-OHP (17-hydroxyprogesterone) to exclude **CAH (congenital adrenal hyperplasia)**.
- **Syndromes:** prune belly (all), Klinefelter, androgen insensitivity, hypogonadotrophic hypogonadism, cerebral palsy, gastroschisis, omphalocele, myelomeningocele, PUV (posterior urethral valves).
- **Sequelae:** subfertility (worse if bilateral), **TGCT (testicular germ cell tumour) 2–5-fold**, torsion, hernia.
- **Treatment in brief:** orchidopexy from 6 months, by 18 months; laparoscopy for non-palpable; no imaging, no hormones.

**Diagram to draw:** sagittal fetus showing CSL, gubernaculum, processus vaginalis and both phases.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - SRY → SOX9 → Sertoli cells (AMH); Leydig cells → testosterone and INSL3.
> - Transabdominal: CSL regression and INSL3 swelling; inguinoscrotal: androgen via GFN and CGRP.
> - Ectopic = off the path; commonest site superficial inguinal pouch.
> - Most testes scrotal after 27 weeks.

## Q2. Describe staging of CA prostate and management of nonorgan confined CA prostate
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)
(also answered in Practice Set 10 Paper 2, Q1)

### Staging: TNM (AJCC (American Joint Committee on Cancer) 8th edition, 2017)

| Stage | Definition |
|---|---|
| T1 | Not palpable: T1a incidental in ≤5% of resected tissue; T1b >5%; T1c found on needle biopsy |
| T2 | Palpable, confined: T2a ≤ half of one lobe; T2b > half of one lobe; T2c both lobes |
| **T3** | **T3a** extraprostatic extension (including microscopic bladder neck invasion); **T3b** seminal vesicle invasion |
| **T4** | Fixed or invading external sphincter, rectum, bladder, levators, pelvic wall |
| N | N0; **N1 regional (pelvic) nodes** |
| M | **M1a** non-regional nodes; **M1b** bone; **M1c** other sites |

- **Traps:** seminal vesicle invasion is T3b, not T4; non-regional nodes are M1a.
- **ISUP (International Society of Urological Pathology) grade groups:** GG1 ≤6; GG2 3+4; GG3 4+3; GG4 8; GG5 9–10.
- **Risk groups (EAU (European Association of Urology)):** high risk = PSA (prostate-specific antigen) >20 ng/mL, GG4–5 or cT2c; **locally advanced = cT3–4 or cN1**. AJCC prognostic groups combine T, N, M, PSA and grade.

### Staging work-up
- DRE (digital rectal examination) sets clinical T stage but under- and over-stages. PSA predicts extent: organ-confined about 80% if PSA <4, under 50% if >10; nodes in about 75% if >50 ng/mL.
- **mpMRI (multiparametric MRI):** extraprostatic extension and seminal vesicle invasion; best specificity in higher-risk men.
- **High risk:** bone scan plus CT (computed tomography) or MRI; **PSMA (prostate-specific membrane antigen) PET (positron emission tomography)**. **proPSMA:** accuracy 92% vs 65% for conventional imaging.
- Nomograms (Partin, Kattan, CAPRA (Cancer of the Prostate Risk Assessment)) and genomic classifiers (Decipher) refine risk.

### Management principle
- Non-organ-confined disease (T3–4 and/or N1, M0) has a high chance of micrometastases. **Single-modality treatment usually fails**, so treatment is **multimodal**: local therapy plus systemic ADT (androgen deprivation therapy).

### A. Locally advanced (cT3–4 N0 M0)
**1. EBRT (external beam radiotherapy) plus long-term ADT: standard**
- Dose-escalated IMRT (intensity-modulated RT) with image guidance, ≥74–78 Gy, with **ADT for 18–36 months** (NCCN; EAU 2–3 years). Neoadjuvant ADT shrinks the target and is synergistic with radiation.
- **EORTC 22863:** 3 years of ADT with RT raised 5-year overall survival (78% vs 62%). **RTOG 92-02:** 28 vs 4 months; survival gain in Gleason 8–10.
- **SPCG-7 and MRC PR07/NCIC PR.3:** adding RT to ADT reduced cancer death (RR 0.44; HR 0.46). ADT alone is inferior.
- **Brachytherapy boost** improves biochemical control (ASCENDE-RT).
- **Very high risk:** add **abiraterone for 2 years** (STAMPEDE M0: metastasis-free survival HR 0.53). Eligible: N1, or two of cT3–4, Gleason ≥8, PSA ≥40 ng/mL.

**2. Radical prostatectomy with extended PLND (pelvic lymph node dissection): selected men**
- Fit, life expectancy >10 years, resectable (mainly cT3a). Clinical overstaging occurs in 7–27%; 10-year disease-specific survival about 80–90%.
- Neoadjuvant ADT downstages clinically but gives **no survival gain**.
- **After surgery:** pT3 or positive margin: **observation with early salvage RT** (RADICALS-RT, RAVES, GETUG-AFU 17); adjuvant RT only for very high-risk pathology (GG4–5, pT3b, margins).

**3. Other options**
- **ADT alone:** only for men unfit or unwilling for local therapy; immediate if symptomatic, high PSA or rapid doubling.
- **Docetaxel with RT and ADT:** RTOG 0521 overall survival HR 0.69; not routine.
- Cryotherapy or HIFU (high-intensity focused ultrasound): investigational in high risk.

### B. Node-positive (N1 M0)
- **cN1:** RT to prostate and pelvic nodes with long-term ADT plus 2 years of abiraterone; prostatectomy with extended PLND in selected men.
- **pN1 after prostatectomy:** **ECOG 7887**: immediate ADT improved overall survival; ADT plus pelvic RT beats ADT alone. Observation is an option for ≤2 nodes with undetectable PSA.

### C. Metastatic (M1), in brief
- ADT plus an **ARPI (androgen receptor pathway inhibitor)** (abiraterone, enzalutamide, apalutamide) or a triplet with docetaxel; prostate RT for low-volume disease (see 2025-Winter Paper 3, Q3b).

```
Non-organ-confined, M0
 ├─ Fit, cT3a, resectable → RP + ePLND → early salvage RT ± ADT
 ├─ cT3–4 or cN1 (most) → EBRT + ADT 18–36 mo
 │       very high risk / N1 → + abiraterone 2 years
 └─ Unfit for local therapy → ADT (immediate if symptomatic)
```

### Local complications and follow-up
- Bladder outlet obstruction (channel TURP), ureteric obstruction (stent or nephrostomy), haematuria (palliative RT).
- PSA every 3–6 months; bone health, metabolic and cardiovascular monitoring on ADT.

**Diagram to draw:** sagittal pelvis showing T3a, T3b and T4 spread.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - T3b = seminal vesicle; M1a = non-regional nodes.
> - EBRT + 2–3 years ADT is standard; EORTC 22863 and SPCG-7.
> - STAMPEDE: abiraterone for 2 years in very high-risk or N1 disease.
> - After RP: early salvage RT beats routine adjuvant RT.

## Q3a. Testicular microlithiasis
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition
- **TM (testicular microlithiasis):** multiple small (<3 mm) bright echogenic foci **without acoustic shadowing** scattered in the testicular parenchyma on ultrasound.
- **Classic TM:** ≥5 microliths per testis (or per image); **limited TM:** <5, of no clinical significance.

### Pathology
- Laminated calcified bodies **inside seminiferous tubules**, formed on degenerated tubular cells that Sertoli cells fail to clear. Affected tubules are often obliterated.

### Prevalence

| Population | Prevalence |
|---|---|
| General population | 5–10% (population-based 4.2%, rising with age) |
| Multicentre review, >37,000 males | 2.9% |
| Boys with cryptorchidism | 3.5% |
| Previously cryptorchid testis vs contralateral | 14% vs 19% (no difference) |

### Associations
- Cryptorchidism, subfertility, **GCNIS (germ cell neoplasia in situ)** and TGCT (testicular germ cell tumour), Klinefelter syndrome, Down syndrome.

### Cancer significance
- Tumours were found in 4.64% with TM vs 0.33% without, but cryptorchidism was not accounted for.
- **TM is not clearly an independent risk factor** for TGCT. An EAU (European Association of Urology) paediatric panel review of several hundred children found only one cancer through puberty.
- **Exception:** contralateral TM in a man with previous GCT signals a higher GCNIS risk.

### Management
- **Isolated TM, no risk factors:** reassure; **no further evaluation, no routine ultrasound follow-up**.
- **TM with risk factors** (previous GCT, cryptorchidism, infertility, atrophic testis): teach **monthly self-examination from puberty**; ultrasound only for a palpable change.
- **Biopsy:** not for TM alone. Contralateral biopsy at orchidectomy is considered when GCNIS risk is high: atrophic testis (<12 mL), cryptorchidism, age <40.
- Counsel carefully: incidental TM causes anxiety and unnecessary tests.

### Differential diagnosis on ultrasound
- Coarse calcification, "burned-out" tumour scar, stellate scar (these need orchidectomy if intratesticular), scrotal pearls, rete testis ectasia.

**Also know:** routine screening ultrasound for infertility is discouraged because of incidental TM; follow-up schedules remain undefined.

**Diagram to draw:** ultrasound sketch of a testis with scattered non-shadowing echogenic dots.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Classic TM ≥5 microliths; limited <5.
> - Not an independent cancer risk factor in isolation.
> - Self-examination only with risk factors; no routine imaging.

## Q3b. Postoperative lymphatic leak
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

*Taken as lymphocele and chylous ascites after RPLND, nephrectomy, pelvic node dissection and transplantation.*

### Types and settings

| Type | Definition | Setting and frequency |
|---|---|---|
| **Lymphocele** | Lymph collection **without epithelial lining** | **PLND (pelvic lymph node dissection):** radiological up to 60%, significant 2–5%. **Transplant:** symptomatic 0.03–26%, peak 6 weeks. **RPLND (retroperitoneal lymph node dissection):** ≤1.7% |
| **Chylous ascites** | Lipid-rich lymph in the peritoneum from the cisterna chyli or major trunks | RPLND: 0.2–2.1% primary, **2–12% post-chemotherapy**; left nephrectomy with hilar dissection |

- **Risk factors:** suprahilar dissection near the cisterna chyli (L1–L2), extended templates, post-chemotherapy surgery; at transplant: mTOR (mammalian target of rapamycin) inhibitors, obesity, re-transplant.

### Clinical features
- **Lymphocele:** usually silent. Large ones compress the iliac vein (leg oedema, DVT (deep vein thrombosis)), bladder (frequency, pelvic pressure), ureter (hydronephrosis, graft dysfunction); may become infected.
- **Chylous ascites:** rising girth, early satiety, dyspnoea; milky drain fluid.

### Diagnosis
- **Ultrasound or CT (computed tomography):** thin-walled collection; air or rim enhancement means infection.
- **Aspirate:** lymph creatinine equals serum (urinoma: much higher). Chyle: **triglyceride 2–8 × serum**, protein >3 g/dL.

### Management
**Lymphocele** (treat by symptoms, not size alone)
```
Asymptomatic, <5 cm → observe
Symptomatic → percutaneous drain (± sclerotherapy)
Recurrent / thick-walled → laparoscopic peritoneal fenestration (best)
Infected → drainage + antibiotics; exclude urine leak
```
- Aspiration alone recurs; sclerosants (povidone-iodine, doxycycline) may hinder later fenestration.

**Chylous ascites** (stepwise)
1. Paracentesis; drain if it reaccumulates.
2. **Very low-fat, high-protein diet with MCT (medium-chain triglycerides)**, which are absorbed directly into portal blood, bypassing lacteals; plus **octreotide**.
3. Nil by mouth with **TPN (total parenteral nutrition)**.
4. Refractory: lymphangiography with embolisation, peritoneovenous shunt or surgical ligation.
- Continue a low-fat diet for **≥4 weeks** after resolution.

### Prevention
- **Clip or ligate lymphatics** (cephalad at the renal hilum in RPLND; perivascular at transplant); avoid unneeded suprahilar dissection; peritoneal flap after robotic PLND.

**Diagram to draw:** pelvic lymphocele compressing the iliac vein and bladder.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Lymphocele: commonest PLND complication; laparoscopic fenestration is best.
> - Chylous ascites: 2–12% after post-chemotherapy RPLND; triglyceride 2–8 × serum.
> - Ladder: diet with MCT and octreotide → TPN → embolisation or shunt.

## Q3c. Hyperfiltration injury in AKI and CKD
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition
- **Hyperfiltration:** a rise in **SNGFR (single-nephron glomerular filtration rate)** in surviving nephrons when nephron mass falls; whole-kidney GFR (glomerular filtration rate) may look normal.
- **Brenner hypothesis (1982):** the adaptation itself damages glomeruli.

### Physiology
- Net filtration pressure ≈ 10 mmHg (hydrostatic 60 − Bowman's 18 − oncotic 32).
- Afferent dilatation raises flow and filtration; **efferent constriction (angiotensin II) raises filtration pressure**.

### Mechanism
```
Nephron loss → afferent dilatation + efferent constriction
→ raised glomerular capillary pressure and flow (hyperfiltration)
→ glomerular hypertrophy; podocytes cannot divide → stretch, detachment
→ proteinuria → FSGS (focal segmental glomerulosclerosis)
→ tubulointerstitial inflammation, fibrosis → more nephron loss (vicious cycle)
```

### In AKI (acute kidney injury)
- Incomplete repair after AKI leaves fewer nephrons, which hyperfilter. Creatinine may return to baseline while **renal reserve is lost**.
- This drives the **AKI-to-CKD (chronic kidney disease) transition**; AKI persisting ≥90 days becomes CKD.
- Each perioperative AKI episode is a **modifiable risk factor** for CKD progression.

### In CKD
- Final common pathway of progression; marked by **albuminuria (KDIGO (Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes) A stages)**, hypertension, falling eGFR.

### Urological settings

| Setting | Point |
|---|---|
| Nephrectomy or donation | Compensatory hypertrophy 20–30%; donor function 60–80% of pre-donation |
| Partial nephrectomy, solitary kidney | >50% parenchymal loss → proteinuria, FSGS; ESRD (end-stage renal disease) risk if total nephron mass falls >80% |
| Congenital solitary kidney, MCDK (multicystic dysplastic kidney) | Monitor BP, proteinuria, yearly ultrasound |

- **Also know:** reflux nephropathy, infundibulopelvic stenosis, diabetes, obesity, pregnancy, low birth weight (low nephron endowment).

### Prevention and treatment
- Nephron-sparing surgery; avoid nephrotoxins and NSAIDs (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs).
- **BP (blood pressure) <130/80 mmHg**; **ACE (angiotensin-converting enzyme) inhibitor or ARB (angiotensin receptor blocker)** in proteinuric disease: efferent dilatation lowers glomerular pressure.
- **SGLT2 (sodium–glucose cotransporter-2) inhibitors:** restore tubuloglomerular feedback, causing afferent constriction. **DAPA-CKD** and **EMPA-KIDNEY** slowed progression with or without diabetes.
- Finerenone (type 2 diabetic CKD); dietary protein restriction.

**Diagram to draw:** glomerulus with dilated afferent and constricted efferent arterioles.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Hyperfiltration = raised SNGFR; Brenner 1982.
> - Leads to FSGS, proteinuria and the AKI-to-CKD transition.
> - ACE inhibitors and SGLT2 inhibitors lower glomerular pressure.

## Q3d. Pelvicalyceal anomalies other than PUJ obstruction
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Introduction
- Congenital pelvicalyceal variants occur in about 4% of people.
- **Key question:** is there obstruction? Dilatation without obstruction needs no treatment; stasis causes stones and infection.

### Anomalies

| Anomaly | Features | Management |
|---|---|---|
| **Calyceal diverticulum** | Transitional-lined intrarenal cavity joined to a calyx (Type I, commoner, small) or pelvis (Type II, larger, symptomatic) by a narrow neck; 4.5 per 1000; congenital (persistent 3rd–4th generation ureteric branches) or acquired. **Stones in almost 40%**; milk of calcium | Asymptomatic: observe. Pain, infection, stones: ureteroscopic neck incision, **percutaneous ablation with fulguration**, laparoscopic marsupialisation; SWL (shock wave lithotripsy) ineffective |
| **Hydrocalycosis** | Dilated major calyx connected to the pelvis; infundibular obstruction by vessel, stenosis, scarring or achalasia | Symptomatic only: infundibulotomy or dilatation |
| **Megacalycosis** | **Non-obstructive**; malformed papillae; **12–20 calyces**; pelvis normal; male 6:1; normal diuretic renogram | **None**; may coexist with segmental megaureter |
| **Infundibular / infundibulopelvic stenosis** | Narrow infundibula ± small pelvis; usually bilateral, with VUR (vesicoureteric reflux), dysplasia; over a third reach renal failure (hyperfiltration) | Monitor creatinine and eGFR; ACE (angiotensin-converting enzyme) inhibitors; surgery only if obstruction harms function: endoscopic, calicocalicostomy, ureterocalicostomy |
| **Extrarenal pelvis** | Large pelvis outside the sinus; mimics dilatation | None |
| **Bifid pelvis** | About 10%; normal variant | None |
| **Unipapillary kidney** | Single papilla and calyx; rare; usually hypoplastic kidney | None |

- **Also know:** acquired infundibular stenosis (tuberculosis, post-PCNL (percutaneous nephrolithotomy)); Fraley syndrome (crossing artery on upper infundibulum).

### Evaluation
- **Ultrasound:** cyst-like space; milk of calcium layers and shifts with position.
- **CT (computed tomography) urography or MRU (magnetic resonance urography):** **delayed contrast pooling** in a diverticulum.
- **Diuretic renogram (MAG3):** separates obstruction (hydrocalycosis) from non-obstruction (megacalycosis, extrarenal pelvis).
- **Retrograde pyelography:** maps the neck.

### Differential diagnosis
- Obstructive hydronephrosis, papillary necrosis, pyelonephritic clubbing, tuberculosis, renal cyst.
- **Stones:** retrograde first (find, dilate or laser-incise the ostium); percutaneous for lower-pole diverticula.

**Diagram to draw:** kidney showing diverticulum with narrow neck, hydrocalyx and megacalyces.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Diverticulum: Type I to calyx, Type II to pelvis; stones in 40%.
> - Megacalycosis: non-obstructive, 12–20 calyces, no treatment.
> - Infundibulopelvic stenosis: bilateral, renal failure risk.

## Q3e. TURP instruments
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(irrigation fluids also answered in 2017-Summer Paper 2, Q3a)

### Introduction
- **TURP (transurethral resection of the prostate):** an electrified loop removes transition-zone adenoma from bladder neck to verumontanum, down to the surgical capsule.

### Resectoscope

| Part | Description |
|---|---|
| **Outer sheath** | 24–28 Fr; **insulated beak** prevents arcing to the urethra |
| **Inner sheath** | Creates **continuous flow**: inflow through the inner, outflow between sheaths (passive or suction). Lower bladder pressure, clear vision, no repeated emptying |
| **Obturator** | Blunt or visual; atraumatic passage |
| **Working element** | Moves the loop. **Passive:** spring holds loop inside sheath; cutting stroke by spring recoil. **Active:** surgeon's thumb pulls the extended loop in |
| **Telescope** | **Hopkins rod-lens**, usually **30°** (12° alternative) |

### Electrodes
- **Cutting loop:** tungsten (monopolar); **platinum–iridium (bipolar plasma)**.
- Roller ball or barrel (coagulation), vaporisation button, **Collins knife** (TUIP (transurethral incision of the prostate)).

### Energy
- **Generator:** about 500 kHz, too fast to stimulate nerves.
- **Cut:** continuous waveform. **Coagulation:** interrupted, higher voltage. **Blend:** better haemostasis, cuts less well.

| Monopolar | Bipolar |
|---|---|
| Loop → patient → **dispersive pad** | Active and return poles on the loop |
| **Non-conducting, hypo-osmolar irrigant:** glycine 1.5%, sorbitol, water | **Normal saline** |
| TUR (transurethral resection) syndrome risk | Virtually none |

### Imaging and irrigation
- **Light source:** xenon or LED (light-emitting diode) with fibreoptic cable; camera and monitor.
- **Irrigant** at body temperature; bag about **60 cm** high (each extra 10 cm more than doubles absorption).

### Accessories
- **Ellik evacuator or Toomey syringe** for chips.
- Van Buren sounds or Otis urethrotome: dilate meatus 2 Fr above sheath size (prevents stricture).
- **Three-way Foley catheter** (22–24 Fr) with catheter guide; continuous irrigation set.

### Troubleshooting a loop that will not cut
```
Scope–generator connection → foot pedal →
irrigant matches generator (glycine for monopolar, saline for bipolar) →
patient grounding (pad contact)
```

### Recent advances
- Bipolar plasma resection; chip-on-tip digital optics; laser enucleation scopes; single-use resectoscopes.

**Diagram to draw:** assembled resectoscope labelling sheaths, working element, loop and flow channels.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Continuous-flow 24–28 Fr sheath, 30° telescope.
> - Tungsten loop (monopolar) vs platinum–iridium (bipolar).
> - Glycine 1.5% for monopolar, saline for bipolar; bag 60 cm.

## Q3f. Methods to estimate prostate size
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(TRUS also answered in 2017-Winter Paper 1, Q3e)

### Why measure
- Chooses treatment: 5ARI (5α-reductase inhibitor) if >30 mL (AUA (American Urological Association)) or >40 mL (EAU (European Association of Urology)); type of BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia) surgery.
- **PSAD (PSA density)** = PSA (prostate-specific antigen) ÷ volume; >0.15 suggests cancer. Also AUR (acute urinary retention) risk, brachytherapy planning.
- Normal adult gland 20–25 g; about 48 g at 60–70 years. **1 cm³ ≈ 1 g**.

### Methods

| Method | Technique | Accuracy and use |
|---|---|---|
| **DRE (digital rectal examination)** | Estimate size, consistency | **Underestimates** above 30 mL (lateral lobes impalpable); flat gland predicts <30 mL |
| **PSA as surrogate** | Age-specific cut-offs for **>40 mL:** >1.6 ng/mL (50s), >2.0 (60s), >2.3 (70s) | AUC (area under curve) 0.76–0.78; within ±20% of TRUS in most |
| **TAUS (transabdominal ultrasound)** | Full bladder; transverse width and height, sagittal length | Less precise than TRUS; also PVR (post-void residual) and **IPP (intravesical prostatic protrusion)** |
| **TRUS (transrectal ultrasound)** | Ellipsoid formula; planimetry | **Method of choice** |
| **MRI (magnetic resonance imaging)** | T2-weighted ellipsoid or segmentation (manual or automated) | Most accurate and reproducible |
| **CT (computed tomography)** | Axial images | Overestimates; poor capsule definition |
| **Cystoscopy** | Urethral length, lobes, median lobe | Rough; guides procedure choice |

### TRUS formulas

| Shape assumed | Formula |
|---|---|
| **Ellipsoid (standard)** | **π/6 (0.52) × transverse × AP (anteroposterior) × length** |
| Sphere | π/6 × transverse³ |
| Prolate spheroid | π/6 × transverse² × AP |
| **Planimetry** | Sum of serial transverse areas |

- Transverse and AP at the widest axial section; length sagittally. All formulas correlate >0.90 with specimen weight.
- **Planimetry:** probe on a stepper, images every 3–5 mm, areas summed; most precise; used for brachytherapy.

### IPP grading (suprapubic, sagittal, bladder 100–200 mL)
- Grade I <5 mm, II 5–10 mm, III >10 mm; ≥10 mm predicts BOO (bladder outlet obstruction) and failed trial without catheter.

**Diagram to draw:** TRUS transverse and sagittal views with three measured diameters.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Ellipsoid volume = 0.52 × T × AP × L; 1 mL ≈ 1 g.
> - DRE underestimates above 30 mL; PSA >1.6/2.0/2.3 by decade predicts >40 mL.
> - Planimetry most precise; MRI most reproducible.
