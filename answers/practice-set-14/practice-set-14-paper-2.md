## Q1. 48 yr male presented with mixed voiding symptoms with PSA 18. Discuss management option in brief.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)
(related: prognosis in 2019-Summer Paper 4, Q3e; staging of non-organ-confined disease in Practice Set 10 Paper 2, Q1)

*"Mixed voiding symptoms" is read as mixed storage and voiding LUTS (lower urinary tract symptoms).*

### Introduction
- In a 48-year-old, a PSA (prostate-specific antigen) of 18 ng/mL is far above what BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia) usually produces. **Prostate cancer must be excluded first**; the LUTS are then treated on their own merits.
- PSA is organ-specific, not cancer-specific: BPH, prostatitis, UTI (urinary tract infection), retention and instrumentation also raise it. At PSA >10 ng/mL, biopsy yield is 43–65% with a normal DRE (digital rectal examination) and 70–90% with a suspicious one.

### History
- **LUTS:** IPSS (International Prostate Symptom Score) with bother score; storage versus voiding; retention, haematuria, recurrent UTI.
- **Red flags:** bone pain, weight loss, leg oedema, cord compression.
- Family history (prostate cancer <65 years; BRCA (breast cancer gene)-related cancers), recent catheter, fever or perineal pain (prostatitis), 5ARI (5α-reductase inhibitor) use, neurological disease, erectile function.

### Examination
- **DRE:** size, nodule, induration, extension, fixity (clinical T stage). Palpable bladder, neurological and spinal examination.

### Investigations
| Test | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Urinalysis, culture | Exclude UTI or prostatitis; treat and repeat PSA later (no empirical antibiotics without symptoms) |
| Repeat PSA, free PSA | Confirm the rise; low free PSA favours cancer |
| Creatinine, ultrasound KUB (kidney, ureter, bladder) | Upper tracts, prostate volume (PSA density) |
| Uroflowmetry, PVR (post-void residual) | Baseline LUTS assessment |
| **mpMRI (multiparametric MRI) before biopsy** | PI-RADS (Prostate Imaging Reporting and Data System) score; guides targeted cores; local staging |
| **Biopsy** | Targeted (PI-RADS ≥3) **plus systematic** 12 cores; **transperineal** preferred (lower sepsis) |

- At PSA 18, biopsy is needed **even if MRI is negative**. Blood or urine biomarkers (PHI (Prostate Health Index), 4Kscore, PCA3) matter mainly in the 2–10 ng/mL grey zone.

### Staging if cancer is found
- Grade group, cores involved, PSA, T stage → risk group. PSA 10–20 makes him at least **intermediate risk** (D'Amico, NCCN (National Comprehensive Cancer Network)).
- **High risk:** bone scan with CT (computed tomography), or **PSMA PET (prostate-specific membrane antigen positron emission tomography)**. Organ-confined disease is <50% at PSA >10; nodes about 20% at PSA >20.
- **Germline testing** (BRCA2, mismatch repair genes) for high-risk or metastatic disease or strong family history.

### Management of the cancer (life expectancy >25 years)
| Situation | Management |
|---|---|
| Low or favourable intermediate risk | AS (active surveillance) is possible, but uncommon at PSA 18 |
| Localised intermediate or high risk | **RP (radical prostatectomy)**, usually robotic, with extended PLND (pelvic lymph node dissection) when node risk exceeds the nomogram threshold; or **EBRT (external beam radiotherapy) + ADT (androgen deprivation therapy)**: 4–6 months intermediate, 18–36 months high risk |
| Locally advanced (T3–T4, N1) | Multimodal: RP ± adjuvant or early salvage RT, or EBRT + long-term ADT; abiraterone added for high-risk node-positive or high-risk N0 disease (STAMPEDE) |
| Metastatic | ADT + ARPI (androgen receptor pathway inhibitor) ± docetaxel; RT to the prostate for low volume |

- **Why surgery suits him:** young, gives pathological staging, and **removes the obstructing gland**, curing the LUTS. SPCG-4: surgical benefit is concentrated in men <65.
- Brachytherapy is relatively contraindicated with severe LUTS, a large gland or prior TURP (transurethral resection of the prostate) (retention, incontinence).

### If biopsy is negative
- PSA 18 still needs explaining (prostatitis, large gland, missed anterior tumour): repeat MRI-targeted transperineal biopsy; %free PSA, PCA3, ConfirmMDx; close PSA follow-up.

### Management of LUTS
- **Behavioural measures** (fluid timing, less caffeine) and an **α-blocker** (e.g. tamsulosin) at once; it does not alter PSA.
- **Avoid starting a 5ARI until cancer is excluded:** it halves PSA and masks cancer; if used later, record a baseline PSA and double readings after 12 months.
- Persistent storage symptoms with low PVR: add an antimuscarinic or β3-agonist (mirabegron).
- **Surgery** (TURP, HoLEP (holmium laser enucleation of the prostate)) for retention, refractory symptoms or complications, only once cancer is excluded. In locally advanced cancer with retention, a channel TURP relieves obstruction.
- **Also know:** pressure-flow study before surgery at age <50 with mainly voiding LUTS (EAU (European Association of Urology)).

```
PSA 18 + LUTS → history, DRE, urine culture → repeat PSA
      → mpMRI → targeted + systematic biopsy (transperineal)
   Cancer → risk group + staging → RP / RT + ADT / systemic
   Benign → explain PSA, re-biopsy if needed → BPH pathway
LUTS throughout: α-blocker; no 5ARI until cancer excluded
```

**Diagram to draw:** the flowchart above.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - PSA 18 at 48: exclude cancer first; MRI before biopsy, transperineal targeted + systematic cores.
> - PSA >10: organ-confined <50%; PSA >20: nodes about 20%.
> - Young fit man: RP treats both cancer and obstruction.
> - α-blocker for LUTS; no 5ARI until cancer excluded (halves PSA).

## Q2. Principles of uses of bowel segments in urology. Describe and compare techniques of orthotopic neobladder.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)
(related: intestinal segments in 2023-Winter Paper 2, Q2; continent diversion and neobladder in Practice Set 3 Paper 4, Q2)

### Uses of bowel
- Conduit (ileal, colon), continent cutaneous pouch (Indiana, Mainz), **orthotopic neobladder**, augmentation cystoplasty, ileal ureter, catheterisable channels (Mitrofanoff, Yang–Monti).

### Principles of using bowel
**1. Choice of segment**
| Segment | Metabolic effect | Use and drawbacks |
|---|---|---|
| Stomach | Hypochloraemic hypokalaemic alkalosis | Renal failure, short gut; haematuria–dysuria |
| Jejunum | Hyponatraemic, hyperkalaemic acidosis | Avoid (salt loss) |
| **Ileum** | **Hyperchloraemic metabolic acidosis** | Default: mobile, compliant; B12 and bile-salt loss |
| Colon | Hyperchloraemic acidosis, hypokalaemia | Easy tunnels; transverse colon outside radiation field |

**2. Surgical principles**
- ERAS (enhanced recovery after surgery); routine mechanical bowel preparation not needed. Mark the stoma site before surgery when a stoma is possible.
- Preserve the mesenteric arcade; tension-free, well-vascularised bowel anastomosis; close the mesenteric window.
- **Detubularisation:** opening bowel on its antimesenteric border and refolding it **abolishes coordinated peristaltic contractions**. A **sphere** gives the largest volume for its surface area; by Laplace's law (pressure ∝ wall tension/radius) a larger radius means lower pressure.
- **Ureterointestinal anastomosis:** spatulated, tension-free, mucosa to mucosa, stented. Refluxing (Bricker, Wallace; stricture about 3–6%) versus antireflux tunnels (more strictures). Obstruction, not low-pressure reflux, damages kidneys.

**3. Patient selection for continent diversion**
- Creatinine <2 mg/dL or GFR (glomerular filtration rate) >35 mL/min; urine pH ≤5.8 after ammonium chloride load; urine osmolality ≥600 mOsm/kg after water deprivation; normal liver function; ability to self-catheterise.

**4. Complications to plan for**
- **Metabolic:** ammonium chloride absorption → hyperchloraemic acidosis (treat with bicarbonate or citrate); hypokalaemia; B12 deficiency (after 5–7 years); osteomalacia; drug reabsorption.
- Mucus, stones (mucus, staples), bacteriuria, ureterointestinal stricture, pouch rupture, bowel obstruction, secondary malignancy (ureterosigmoidostomy, augments).

### Orthotopic neobladder
**Indication:** after radical cystectomy, when the urethra is tumour-free, to allow voiding per urethra without a stoma.

**Principles:** (1) reliable outlet: intact rhabdosphincter and tumour-free urethra; (2) low pressure; (3) capacity ≥500 mL when mature (44 cm double-folded ileum: <200 mL early, 500–600 mL by 1 year); (4) unobstructed upper tracts.

**Contraindications:** positive urethral margin (frozen section), urethral stricture, poor renal or liver function, inability to catheterise. Relative: prostatic stromal invasion, bladder-neck or anterior vaginal tumour, prior radiation, frailty. Age and obesity alone are not bars.

### Techniques compared
| Design | Segment and shape | Ureters | Advantage | Drawback |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Studer** | 40–44 cm ileum, double-folded U + afferent limb | Refluxing, isoperistaltic limb | Simple, no staples, short ureters; stricture <3% | Reflux on cystogram (benign) |
| **Hautmann** | About 70 cm ileum, W | Refluxing (since 1997) | Largest early capacity, early continence | More late retention, more absorption |
| T pouch | 44 cm + 15 cm T limb | Flap-valve antireflux | Accepts dilated or short ureters | More reoperations than Studer |
| Abol-Enein | 40 cm ileum, W | Serous-lined tunnel | Durable antireflux | Needs long, non-dilated ureters |
| Camey II | 65 cm ileum, U | Le Duc tunnel | Early detubularised design | Le Duc strictures 7–29% |
| Hemi-Kock | 61 cm ileum | Intussuscepted nipple | Good early results | Nipple stenosis, staple stones |
| Padua (VIP) | About 42 cm ileum, funnel | Le Duc | Anatomical funnel; robotic | — |
| Mainz III | 10–15 cm caecum + 20–30 cm ileum | Submucosal tunnel | Good compliance | Loses ileocaecal valve |
| Sigmoid (Reddy) | 35 cm sigmoid, U | Tunnelled | Spares ileum (short bowel) | Poor compliance, urgency |

- **Hautmann vs Studer:** larger initial capacity gives earlier continence but more late retention and absorption.
- **Studer steps:** isolate ileum 15–20 cm proximal to the ileocaecal valve; open distal 40–44 cm antimesenterically; sew into a U and fold into a sphere; ureters end-to-side to the afferent limb; dependent point to urethra.
- **USC (University of Southern California) RCT (randomised controlled trial):** Studer vs T pouch, no difference in renal function at 3 years; T pouch needed more secondary procedures.

### Outcomes
| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Day continence | 80–90% |
| Night incontinence | 20–50% (no sphincter reflex, mucosal water secretion) |
| Retention needing CIC (clean intermittent catheterisation) | Men 4–10%; women 20–30% |
| Urethral recurrence (men) | About 7% at 5 years |

- **Also know:** robotic intracorporeal neobladder comparable to open; uptake is low despite 80–90% of men being eligible; lifelong follow-up of electrolytes, B12, upper tracts.

**Diagram to draw:** Studer pouch with afferent limb, ureters and urethral anastomosis.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Ileum/colon: hyperchloraemic acidosis; jejunum: hyperkalaemia; stomach: alkalosis.
> - Detubularise and make a sphere; ≥500 mL; GFR >35 for continent diversion.
> - Studer and Hautmann (refluxing) are commonest; USC RCT: Studer = T pouch.
> - Day continence 80–90%, night 20–50%; women retain (20–30%).

## Q3a. Role of UDS before prostate surgery
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Why consider UDS (urodynamic study)
- Symptoms, flow rate, PVR (post-void residual), prostate volume and cystoscopy **cannot separate BOO (bladder outlet obstruction) from DUA (detrusor underactivity)**: low Qmax (maximum flow rate) occurs in both. DUA is found in 9–48% of men with LUTS (lower urinary tract symptoms) studied.
- Surgery helps obstruction, not a weak bladder. **PFS (pressure–flow study) is the gold standard** for BOO.

### Components
- **Filling cystometry:** sensation, compliance, **DO (detrusor overactivity)**.
- **PFS:** detrusor pressure at Qmax plotted against flow (ICS (International Continence Society) nomogram).

| Index | Formula | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| BOOI (bladder outlet obstruction index) | PdetQmax − 2Qmax | >40 obstructed; 20–40 equivocal; <20 unobstructed |
| BCI (bladder contractility index) | PdetQmax + 5Qmax | >150 strong; 100–150 normal; <100 weak |

- High pressure, low flow = BOO; low pressure, low flow = DUA.
- **Videourodynamics** adds anatomy (diverticula, reflux).

### Indications before BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia) surgery (EAU (European Association of Urology))
- Previous failed invasive treatment.
- Cannot void >150 mL (unreliable flow).
- PVR >300 mL.
- Age >80 years with mainly voiding LUTS.
- Age <50 years with mainly voiding LUTS.
- **Also:** suspected neurogenic bladder, prior pelvic surgery or radiation, Qmax >10 mL/s (AUA (American Urological Association): diagnostic uncertainty).

### How results change management
- **Unobstructed or DUA:** poorer surgical results; counsel, consider CIC (clean intermittent catheterisation).
- **DO with BOO:** storage symptoms persist in about one-third after TURP (transurethral resection of the prostate); warn the patient.
- **Low compliance:** the only "absolute" urodynamic indication to relieve BOO (protects upper tracts).

### Evidence and limitations
- Not routine. **UPSTREAM RCT (randomised controlled trial):** routine UDS did not change IPSS (International Prostate Symptom Score) or surgery rates.
- Invasive: UTI (urinary tract infection), discomfort, cost. Before **radical prostatectomy**, not routine.

**Diagram to draw:** ICS nomogram with obstructed, equivocal and unobstructed zones.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - PFS is the only test that separates BOO from DUA.
> - BOOI >40 obstructed; BCI <100 weak.
> - EAU indications: failed surgery, <150 mL voided, PVR >300 mL, age <50 or >80.
> - UPSTREAM: routine UDS did not change outcomes.

## Q3b. Zolendronic acid
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(related: bone metastases in Practice Set 3 Paper 2, Q2)

*Read as zoledronic acid.*

### Introduction
- A potent IV (intravenous) **nitrogen-containing bisphosphonate**.

### Mechanism
- Binds hydroxyapatite at sites of active bone turnover and is taken up by **osteoclasts**.
- Inhibits **farnesyl pyrophosphate synthase** (mevalonate pathway), causing osteoclast dysfunction and apoptosis, so resorption falls.
- Breaks the "vicious cycle": tumour cytokines activate osteoclasts, and resorbed bone releases growth factors that feed the tumour.
- Excreted unchanged by the kidneys.

### Indications and doses
| Indication | Dose |
|---|---|
| **SRE (skeletal-related event) prevention, mCRPC (metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer)** and other bone metastases (RCC (renal cell carcinoma), bladder) | **4 mg IV over ≥15 min every 4 weeks**, adjusted for creatinine clearance |
| ADT (androgen deprivation therapy) bone loss (by DEXA (dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry) and FRAX) | 4–5 mg IV yearly |
| Hypercalcaemia of malignancy | 4 mg IV with saline hydration |

- 12-weekly dosing after the first doses is non-inferior to 4-weekly (CALGB 70604).
- **Not indicated at SRE doses in mHSPC (metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer):** no benefit (CALGB 90202, STAMPEDE).

### Evidence
- **Zometa 039 trial (mCRPC):** fewer SREs than placebo (about 33% vs 44%).
- **Denosumab vs zoledronate (1904 men, mCRPC):** time to first SRE 20.7 vs 17.1 months. **Neither improves survival.**

### Precautions and monitoring
- Creatinine before every dose; avoid if creatinine clearance <30 mL/min.
- **Dental review before starting**; avoid invasive dental work during therapy.
- Calcium and vitamin D supplements.

### Adverse effects
- **Acute-phase reaction** (fever, myalgia) after the first infusion.
- Nephrotoxicity, hypocalcaemia, hypophosphataemia.
- **ONJ (osteonecrosis of the jaw)**, atypical femoral fracture; ocular inflammation (rare).

### Zoledronate vs denosumab
| | Zoledronate | Denosumab |
|---|---|---|
| Target | Osteoclast enzyme | RANKL (receptor activator of nuclear factor-κB ligand) |
| Route | IV 4-weekly | 120 mg SC (subcutaneous) 4-weekly |
| Renal adjustment | Yes | No |

**Diagram to draw:** tumour–osteoclast vicious cycle with zoledronate's site of action.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - 4 mg IV 4-weekly for SREs in mCRPC; adjust for creatinine clearance.
> - No survival benefit; not for SREs in mHSPC.
> - Dental check first (ONJ); calcium and vitamin D.
> - Denosumab delays SREs longer (20.7 vs 17.1 months).

## Q3c. LHRH analogue
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in 2019-Summer Paper 4, Q3b and 2017-Winter Paper 4, Q3c)

### Physiology
- **LHRH (luteinising hormone-releasing hormone), also GnRH (gonadotrophin-releasing hormone)**, is a hypothalamic decapeptide released in **pulses**. It drives pituitary LH (luteinising hormone) and FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone); LH stimulates Leydig cells (about 95% of testosterone).
- **Continuous** exposure desensitises and down-regulates the pituitary receptor: the basis of agonist therapy.

### Agonists
| Drug | Formulation |
|---|---|
| Leuprolide | Depot 1, 3, 6 monthly |
| Goserelin | SC (subcutaneous) implant 1 or 3 monthly |
| Triptorelin | IM (intramuscular) depot |
| Histrelin | Implant, 1 year |

- Substitution at **position 6** raises potency and half-life.

```
Agonist → LH surge (flare) → receptor down-regulation
        → LH falls → castrate testosterone in 2–4 weeks
```
- Survival **equals orchidectomy**. Castrate level **<50 ng/dL**; aim **<20 ng/dL** (orchidectomy median about 15).

### Flare phenomenon
- LH rises up to **10-fold for 10–20 days**: bone pain, **spinal cord compression**, retention.
- **Prevent:** antiandrogen (bicalutamide) started with or before the agonist, for **21–28 days**.
- Impending cord compression → antagonist or orchidectomy.

### Antagonists
- Immediate receptor block, **no flare**, FSH falls.
- **Degarelix** (SC monthly): castrate by day 3 in >95%.
- **Relugolix** (oral). **HERO** vs leuprolide: sustained castration 97% vs 89%, faster recovery, fewer MACE (major adverse cardiovascular events).

### Indications
- Metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (with ARPI (androgen receptor pathway inhibitor) or docetaxel).
- With radiotherapy for intermediate/high-risk and locally advanced disease.
- BCR (biochemical recurrence) with short doubling time; continued in CRPC (castration-resistant prostate cancer).
- **Also know:** intermittent ADT (androgen deprivation therapy) acceptable; precocious puberty, endometriosis.

### Side effects
- Hot flushes, loss of libido, ED (erectile dysfunction).
- **Osteoporosis:** DEXA (dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry), calcium, vitamin D, denosumab or zoledronic acid.
- Diabetes, cardiovascular events, sarcopenia, anaemia, gynaecomastia (≤5%); slow testosterone recovery.

**Diagram to draw:** hypothalamic–pituitary–testis axis showing agonist and antagonist sites.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Agonist: surge, then down-regulation; castrate in 2–4 weeks.
> - Flare: LH 10-fold for 10–20 days; antiandrogen 21–28 days.
> - Castrate <50 ng/dL; aim <20 ng/dL.
> - Antagonist: no flare; preferred in cord compression.

## Q3d. Expectant treatment of CA prostate
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Rationale
- Overdiagnosis: autopsy prevalence of microfocal cancer roughly equals age as a percentage; lifetime risk of death is about 3%.
- **Grade group 1 (Gleason 6) has virtually no metastatic potential.** Deaths on observation come from higher grade missed at biopsy (about 30% upgraded at prostatectomy).

### Two forms of expectant treatment
| | **AS (active surveillance)** | **WW (watchful waiting)** |
|---|---|---|
| Patient | Fit for radical therapy, life expectancy ≥10 years | Limited life expectancy (age, comorbidity) |
| Intent | **Curative, deferred** | **Palliative** |
| Monitoring | PSA (prostate-specific antigen), DRE (digital rectal examination), MRI, biopsy | Infrequent PSA and review; no biopsy |
| Action | Radical treatment on reclassification | ADT (androgen deprivation therapy) or palliation for symptoms |

### Evidence
| Trial | Result |
|---|---|
| **SPCG-4** (pre-PSA) | Prostatectomy cut cancer death (19.6% vs 31.3%), mainly <65 years and higher grade |
| **PIVOT** | No significant mortality benefit; low-risk cancer death 2.7% |
| **ProtecT** (15 years) | Cancer death <3% in all arms; metastasis doubled on active monitoring (9.4% vs about 5%) |

### Selection for AS
- **Very low risk (Epstein):** grade group 1, ≤2 positive cores, no core >50%, PSA density <0.15.
- **Low risk:** grade group 1, PSA <10 ng/mL; all guidelines prefer AS.
- **Favourable intermediate risk** (small-volume grade group 2): selected cases.
- Caution: **BRCA2** carriers, high-volume disease.

### AS protocol
- MRI and **confirmatory biopsy** within 1–3 years; PSA 6-monthly; DRE every 1–2 years; MRI every 18–24 months; biopsy every 3–5 years.
- **Triggers:** grade group ≥3 → treat; volume or PSA rise → MRI or biopsy first.

### Outcomes
- Cancer mortality **0.5–5% at 15 years** by entry criteria (Johns Hopkins vs Toronto); about **25% treated by 5 years**.

### Watchful waiting
- Switch AS to WW when life expectancy falls below about 5–10 years.
- **Also know:** genomic tests refine selection; 5ARI (5α-reductase inhibitor) has no proven role.

**Diagram to draw:** AS flowchart: diagnosis → MRI + confirmatory biopsy → surveillance → trigger → treatment.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - AS is deferred cure; WW is palliation.
> - Grade group 1 hardly ever metastasises; risk lies in missed higher grade.
> - ProtecT: no mortality difference at 15 years; more metastases on monitoring.
> - About 25% leave AS by 5 years.

## Q3e. Molecular staging of CA prostate
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(related: prognosis in 2019-Summer Paper 4, Q3e)

### Definition and rationale
- Clinical staging (DRE (digital rectal examination), PSA (prostate-specific antigen), grade, imaging) misses occult spread and cannot fully predict behaviour.
- **Molecular staging** uses DNA, RNA or protein markers in tissue, blood or nodes to find hidden disease and predict outcome.

### 1. Detection of occult spread
- **RT-PCR (reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction)** for PSA or PSMA (prostate-specific membrane antigen) mRNA in blood, marrow or "negative" nodes: detects micrometastases, but inconsistent, so not used clinically.
- **CTCs (circulating tumour cells):** counts are prognostic in metastatic disease; **AR-V7** (splice variant lacking the ligand-binding domain) predicts resistance to ARPIs (androgen receptor pathway inhibitors).
- **ctDNA (circulating tumour DNA)** liquid biopsy: DNA-repair mutations; clonal haematopoiesis gives false calls.

### 2. Tissue genomic classifiers
| Test | Basis | Predicts |
|---|---|---|
| **Decipher** | 22-RNA classifier | Metastasis, cancer death; post-prostatectomy radiotherapy decision |
| **Oncotype DX GPS (Genomic Prostate Score)** | 17 genes (12 cancer, 5 reference) by RT-PCR | Adverse pathology; AS (active surveillance) choice |
| **Prolaris** | 31 cell-cycle progression genes | Progression, cancer death |

- **Also know:** PORTOS (24-gene radiotherapy response score); ConfirmMDx (methylation, after negative biopsy).

### 3. Genetic markers
- **Germline:** BRCA2 (aggressive disease), BRCA1, ATM, CHEK2, Lynch genes, HOXB13 (risk, not aggressiveness); test in metastatic or high-risk disease.
- **Somatic:** PTEN loss, TMPRSS2:ERG fusion, homologous recombination repair defects (guide PARP (poly ADP-ribose polymerase) inhibitors).

### 4. Molecular imaging
- **PSMA PET (positron emission tomography):** more sensitive and specific than CT and bone scan for nodes and metastases (proPSMA), but cannot exclude micrometastases.

### Clinical use
- Refines **AS vs treatment** and post-operative radiotherapy decisions; guides targeted therapy.

### Limitations
- Mostly retrospective; **multifocality and heterogeneity** (the sampled focus may not be the worst); cost; added value still being tested (G-MAJOR trial).

**Diagram to draw:** clinical stage vs molecular stage (tissue, blood, nodes, imaging).

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Molecular staging finds occult disease and predicts behaviour beyond TNM.
> - Decipher 22 genes; Oncotype 17 genes; Prolaris cell-cycle genes.
> - AR-V7 on CTCs predicts ARPI resistance; BRCA2 means aggressive disease.
> - Limitation: heterogeneity and mostly retrospective data.

## Q3f. CRE & BEP
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

*"CRE" is read as carbapenem-resistant Enterobacterales, and BEP as bleomycin, etoposide and cisplatin chemotherapy for germ cell tumours; the two are answered separately.*

### Part A: CRE (carbapenem-resistant Enterobacterales)
- **Definition:** Enterobacterales (Klebsiella pneumoniae, Escherichia coli, Enterobacter) resistant to at least one carbapenem, or producing a carbapenemase.

| Mechanism | Example |
|---|---|
| Class A carbapenemase | KPC (Klebsiella pneumoniae carbapenemase) |
| Class B metallo-β-lactamase | **NDM (New Delhi metallo-β-lactamase)**: common in India |
| Class D | OXA-48 |
| Non-enzymatic | ESBL (extended-spectrum β-lactamase) or AmpC plus porin loss |

- **Urological risk:** catheters, stents, prolonged stay, prior carbapenem or fluoroquinolone use, transrectal biopsy, ICU (intensive care unit) care.
- **Treatment** (by culture and carbapenemase type; ID (infectious disease) input):
  - KPC, OXA-48: ceftazidime–avibactam; meropenem–vaborbactam or imipenem–relebactam for KPC.
  - **NDM:** ceftazidime–avibactam plus aztreonam, or cefiderocol.
  - Cystitis: single-dose aminoglycoside, fosfomycin; polymyxins as last resort. Avoid tigecycline for UTI (urinary tract infection) (low urine levels).
- **Prevention:** antibiotic stewardship, contact precautions, rectal screening of high-risk patients, early catheter removal, transperineal rather than transrectal biopsy.

### Part B: BEP (bleomycin, etoposide, cisplatin)
- Standard first-line chemotherapy for metastatic **GCT (germ cell tumour)**; replaced PVB (less neuromuscular toxicity).
- **Usual 21-day cycle:** cisplatin 20 mg/m² days 1–5, etoposide 100 mg/m² days 1–5, bleomycin 30 units days 1, 8, 15.

| IGCCCG (International Germ Cell Cancer Collaborative Group) risk | Regimen |
|---|---|
| Good | BEP ×3 or EP ×4 |
| Intermediate/poor | BEP ×4 (VIP ×4 if lungs poor) |
| Stage I NSGCT (non-seminomatous GCT), high risk | BEP ×1 adjuvant |

- **Toxicity:** bleomycin lung fibrosis (keep FiO₂ low at later RPLND (retroperitoneal lymph node dissection)) and Raynaud phenomenon; cisplatin nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity, neuropathy; myelosuppression; infertility (bank sperm); late cardiovascular disease and second cancers.

**Diagram to draw:** BEP 21-day cycle timeline.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - CRE: NDM common in India; ceftazidime–avibactam + aztreonam for metallo-β-lactamase.
> - BEP ×3 good risk, ×4 intermediate/poor; EP ×4 if bleomycin avoided.
> - Bleomycin lung: low FiO₂ and restricted fluids at surgery.

#### Q2. Principles of uses of bowel segments in urology. Describe and compare techniques of orthotopic neobladder. (LAQ)

- Ileum/colon: hyperchloraemic acidosis; jejunum: hyperkalaemia; stomach: alkalosis.
- Detubularise and make a sphere; ≥500 mL; GFR >35 for continent diversion.
- Studer and Hautmann (refluxing) are commonest; USC RCT: Studer = T pouch.
- Day continence 80–90%, night 20–50%; women retain (20–30%).

#### Q3a. Role of UDS before prostate surgery (SAQ)

- PFS is the only test that separates BOO from DUA.
- BOOI >40 obstructed; BCI <100 weak.
- EAU indications: failed surgery, <150 mL voided, PVR >300 mL, age <50 or >80.
- UPSTREAM: routine UDS did not change outcomes.

#### Q3b. Zolendronic acid (SAQ)

- 4 mg IV 4-weekly for SREs in mCRPC; adjust for creatinine clearance.
- No survival benefit; not for SREs in mHSPC.
- Dental check first (ONJ); calcium and vitamin D.
- Denosumab delays SREs longer (20.7 vs 17.1 months).

#### Q3c. LHRH analogue (SAQ)

- Agonist: surge, then down-regulation; castrate in 2–4 weeks.
- Flare: LH 10-fold for 10–20 days; antiandrogen 21–28 days.
- Castrate <50 ng/dL; aim <20 ng/dL.
- Antagonist: no flare; preferred in cord compression.

#### Q3d. Expectant treatment of CA prostate (SAQ)

- AS is deferred cure; WW is palliation.
- Grade group 1 hardly ever metastasises; risk lies in missed higher grade.
- ProtecT: no mortality difference at 15 years; more metastases on monitoring.
- About 25% leave AS by 5 years.

#### Q3e. Molecular staging of CA prostate (SAQ)

- Molecular staging finds occult disease and predicts behaviour beyond TNM.
- Decipher 22 genes; Oncotype 17 genes; Prolaris cell-cycle genes.
- AR-V7 on CTCs predicts ARPI resistance; BRCA2 means aggressive disease.
- Limitation: heterogeneity and mostly retrospective data.

#### Q3f. CRE & BEP (SAQ)

- CRE: NDM common in India; ceftazidime–avibactam + aztreonam for metallo-β-lactamase.
- BEP ×3 good risk, ×4 intermediate/poor; EP ×4 if bleomycin avoided.
- Bleomycin lung: low FiO₂ and restricted fluids at surgery.
