## Q1. Describe anatomy of arenal glands AND steps in production of adrenal hormones & perioperative management of a patient of pheochromocytoma
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)
(pre-operative management also answered in 2020-Summer Paper 2, Q3c)

*Assumed meaning: "arenal" = adrenal glands.*

### A. Anatomy
**Gross**
- Paired retroperitoneal glands on the upper renal poles at the level of the 11th–12th ribs, inside Gerota (perirenal) fascia, separated from the kidney by a thin connective tissue layer. Yellow-orange, 4–6 cm long, 2–3 cm wide, about 5 g each.

| Feature | Right | Left |
|---|---|---|
| Shape, level | Triangular; higher | Crescentic; lower (to L1) |
| Relations | Liver anteriorly, duodenum anteromedially, IVC (inferior vena cava) medially, diaphragmatic crus behind | Pancreas body and splenic vessels anteriorly, aorta medially, crus behind |
| Vein | **Short (<1 cm), into posterior IVC**: tears easily | **Longer, into left renal vein**, often joined by inferior phrenic vein |

**Blood supply**
- **"Many arteries in, one vein out."** Superior adrenal (from inferior phrenic), middle (aorta), inferior (renal artery) arteries break into 10–50 small branches.
- Cortical sinusoids drain into the medulla, so cortisol-rich blood reaches the chromaffin cells. **Cortisol induces PNMT (phenylethanolamine-N-methyltransferase)**, which is why the medulla makes adrenaline.

**Nerves:** preganglionic sympathetic fibres (T11–L2, via splanchnic nerves, no synapse in coeliac ganglion) end directly on chromaffin cells. The medulla is a modified sympathetic ganglion.

**Embryology:** cortex from coelomic mesoderm (adrenogonadal ridge, weeks 3–4); medulla from neural crest. Hence adrenals stay orthotopic in renal agenesis, and adrenal rests occur along the path of gonadal descent.

**Histology ("GFR, salt–sugar–sex")**
| Zone | Share | Product | Main control |
|---|---|---|---|
| Glomerulosa | ~15% of cortex | Aldosterone | Angiotensin II, potassium |
| Fasciculata | ~80% | Cortisol (up to 20 mg/day) | ACTH (adrenocorticotropic hormone) |
| Reticularis | 5–7% | DHEA (dehydroepiandrosterone), DHEA-S, androstenedione | ACTH |
| Medulla | ~10% of gland | Adrenaline 80%, noradrenaline 19%, dopamine 1% | Sympathetic nerves |

### B. Steps in hormone production
**1. Steroids (cortex)**
- Cholesterol (mainly from LDL [low-density lipoprotein]) is carried into mitochondria by **StAR (steroidogenic acute regulatory protein)**: the rate-limiting step, driven by ACTH.
- **Side-chain cleavage enzyme (CYP11A1)** converts it to **pregnenolone**. The enzymes present in each zone then decide the product:
```
Pregnenolone -3βHSD-> Progesterone -21OH-> 11-deoxycorticosterone -11βOH-> Corticosterone -aldosterone synthase (CYP11B2)-> ALDOSTERONE (glomerulosa only)
Pregnenolone -17αOH-> 17-OH pregnenolone/progesterone -21OH-> 11-deoxycortisol -11βOH-> CORTISOL (fasciculata)
17-OH pregnenolone -17,20-lyase-> DHEA -> androstenedione (reticularis)
```
- 3βHSD (3β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase). Steroids are not stored; they diffuse out and act on intracellular receptors.
- **Regulation:** CRH (corticotropin-releasing hormone) → ACTH → cortisol, with negative feedback and morning peak; RAAS (renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system) and potassium for aldosterone.
- **Clinical link:** 21-hydroxylase deficiency is the commonest cause of CAH (congenital adrenal hyperplasia).

**2. Catecholamines (medulla)**
```
Tyrosine -tyrosine hydroxylase (rate-limiting)-> L-DOPA -decarboxylase-> Dopamine
-dopamine β-hydroxylase-> Noradrenaline -PNMT (cortisol-induced)-> Adrenaline
```
- Stored in vesicles, released by exocytosis. **COMT (catechol-O-methyltransferase)** inside chromaffin cells forms **metanephrine and normetanephrine** continuously, which is why metanephrines are the best test. MAO (monoamine oxidase) and liver form VMA (vanillylmandelic acid).

### C. Perioperative management of phaeochromocytoma
**Why:** handling the tumour causes catecholamine surges (crisis, arrhythmia); vein ligation causes sudden withdrawal (hypotension, hypoglycaemia). Mortality fell from up to 50% to <3% with blockade.

**Pre-operative (7–14 days)**
- Confirm with plasma free or urinary fractionated metanephrines; CT (computed tomography) or MRI; functional imaging (⁶⁸Ga-DOTATATE PET [positron emission tomography], MIBG [metaiodobenzylguanidine]) if extra-adrenal or hereditary; **never biopsy**; genetic testing (about one-third hereditary).
- ECG (electrocardiogram) and echocardiography for catecholamine cardiomyopathy.

| Drug | Use |
|---|---|
| **Phenoxybenzamine** (irreversible non-selective α) | First line: 10 mg twice daily, increased every 2–3 days; about 1 mg/kg total |
| Doxazosin, prazosin (selective α1) | Fewer side effects |
| Calcium channel blockers | Alternative strategy or add-on |
| β1-blocker (atenolol, metoprolol) | **Only after α-blockade**, for tachycardia |
| Metyrosine | Blocks tyrosine hydroxylase; add-on; sedation, extrapyramidal effects |

- **Never β before α:** losing β2 vasodilatation leaves α-vasoconstriction unopposed: crisis.
- **Target:** seated BP 120–130/80 mmHg with mild postural drop.
- **Volume expansion:** high-salt diet and fluids; 1–2 L IV (intravenous) fluid the night before. Last phenoxybenzamine dose the night before surgery.

**Intra-operative**
- Arterial and central lines; laparoscopic adrenalectomy (open for large or invasive tumours); minimal handling; **early adrenal vein ligation** after warning the anaesthetist.
- Surges: nitroprusside, nicardipine, phentolamine. Tachyarrhythmia: esmolol, lidocaine. Post-ligation hypotension: fluids ± vasopressors.
- Cortical-sparing adrenalectomy in MEN2 (multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2) and VHL (von Hippel–Lindau).

**Post-operative**
- HDU (high dependency unit): hypotension, **rebound hypoglycaemia** (insulin no longer suppressed), steroid cover if bilateral.
- Metanephrines after surgery, then **yearly for life** (recurrence up to 16%).

**Diagram to draw:** posterior view of both adrenals with three arteries and the two veins.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Right vein short into IVC; left vein into left renal vein.
> - StAR is rate-limiting for steroids; tyrosine hydroxylase for catecholamines.
> - Cortisol induces PNMT; COMT forms metanephrines inside the tumour.
> - α-blockade 7–14 days, β only after α, volume expansion; watch glucose.

## Q2. Describe embryology of bladder. What are the proposed theories for extrophy epispadias complex? Describe skeletal abnormalities.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)
(bladder embryology also answered in 2021-Summer Paper 3, Q1)

### A. Embryology of the bladder
**1. Division of the cloaca (weeks 5–6)**
- The **urorectal septum** (Tourneux and Rathke folds) grows caudally and divides the endodermal cloaca into a dorsal **anorectal canal** and a ventral **UGS (urogenital sinus)**. It meets the cloacal membrane, which perforates to form the urogenital and anal openings.
- The **entry of the Wolffian (mesonephric) ducts** divides the UGS:
  - **Cranial vesicourethral canal** (continuous with the allantois) → bladder and pelvic urethra.
  - **Caudal (definitive) UGS** → phallic urethra in males; vestibule and distal vagina in females.

**2. Wall layers**
- Urothelium from UGS endoderm. Lamina propria, detrusor and adventitia from surrounding splanchnic mesenchyme, by epithelial–mesenchymal signalling.

**3. Trigone (weeks 5–6)**
- The common excretory ducts dilate and are absorbed into the UGS. **Apoptosis** separates the ureters from the Wolffian ducts.
- Ureteric orifices migrate **cranially and laterally**; Wolffian ducts end as ejaculatory ducts at the verumontanum.
- Classic view: trigone muscle is ureteric. Current view (mouse lineage tracing): **mostly bladder smooth muscle**.
- **Weigert–Meyer:** bud too low → lateral orifice → VUR (vesicoureteral reflux); too high → caudal, medial, ectopic orifice.

**4. Urachus:** the bladder descends into the pelvis (months 4–5); the allantois obliterates into the **urachus** (median umbilical ligament).

**5. Function:** urine from about week 10; after week 16 amniotic fluid is mainly fetal urine.

### B. Exstrophy–epispadias complex (EEC): theories
- **Spectrum:** glanular epispadias → classic bladder exstrophy (CBE, >50%; about 1 in 46,000; male:female 2.3:1) → cloacal exstrophy.
- **Core defect:** the **cloacal membrane is not reinforced by mesodermal ingrowth**. This mesoderm normally forms the lower abdominal muscles and pelvic bones, so the thin membrane ruptures. **Timing and extent of rupture** decide the variant.

| Theory | Proposed defect | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Cloacal membrane overdevelopment (Marshall–Muecke)** | Abnormally large membrane in week 4 blocks medial mesenchymal migration | Rupture before septum descent → cloacal exstrophy; after → bladder exstrophy; limited → epispadias |
| Lateral body-wall fold failure | Folds fail to meet in midline | Abdomen and pelvis → cloacal; pelvis alone → classic |
| Caudal genital hillocks | Genital tubercles fuse **below** rather than above the membrane | Dorsal urethral defect (epispadias) |
| Abnormal body-stalk insertion | Caudal insertion prevents mesenchyme interposition | Superficial unstable membrane disintegrates |
| **Bony pelvis first** | Pelvic ring primordium fails to rotate | Pelvic structures cannot meet → bladder herniates |

- All theories converge on **absent infraumbilical midline mesoderm**.
- **Also know (aetiology):** multifactorial; recurrence in offspring of an affected parent 1 in 70; 22q11.2 duplication (commonest variant), ISL1 susceptibility gene, p63 under-expression; IVF (in vitro fertilisation) and high-dose early progesterone raise risk; maternal smoking linked to cloacal exstrophy.

### C. Skeletal abnormalities
**1. Classic bladder exstrophy (3D CT studies)**
- **Rotational:** posterior pelvis externally rotated (mean 12° each side); anterior segment externally rotated (mean 18° each side); sacroiliac joint rotated; acetabular and femoral retroversion.
- **Dimensional:** **pubic diastasis** (mean 4.8 cm); **anterior pubic segment shortened by 30%**; intertriradiate distance increased by about 31%; posterior segment length normal; larger sacrum.
- Bone histology is normal, so growth potential is normal.
- **Clinical effects:** short pendular penis (anterior segment short and splayed); wide hips, waddling gait and out-toeing in early childhood. These cause little disability and improve with growth.
- **Pelvic floor:** levator ani displaced posteriorly, flattened; anterior anus; later pelvic organ prolapse.
- **Spine:** minor variants (spina bifida occulta, lumbarisation, sacralisation) fairly common; scoliosis and dysraphism uncommon.

**2. Cloacal exstrophy**
- Widest, **asymmetric** diastasis (about twice CBE), sacroiliac malformation, hip dislocation.
- **Spinal anomalies in 85–100%** (lumbar myelodysplasia commonest; lipomeningocele); MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) for all.
- Limb defects: **clubfoot** (commonest), limb shortening, tibial and fibular deformity.

**3. Isolated epispadias:** male symphysis widened (less than CBE); female symphysis usually closed or narrow.

**Surgical significance: pelvic osteotomy**
- Closing the pelvic ring places the bladder neck deep in the pelvis, reduces tension and helps continence.
- **Indications:** diastasis >4 cm or poorly malleable pelvis; closure after 72 hours; failed closure; all cloacal exstrophy. Under 72 hours with malleable pubes: closure without osteotomy and traction.
- **Combined bilateral anterior innominate + vertical iliac osteotomy** (most used) with external fixator.
- Epispadias repair and continence surgery: see Practice Set 6 Paper 3, Q2.

**Diagram to draw:** cloacal membrane with urorectal septum and failed mesodermal ingrowth; exstrophy pelvis with diastasis.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Vesicourethral canal → bladder; urachus from allantois.
> - Marshall–Muecke: overdeveloped cloacal membrane; rupture timing decides variant.
> - CBE: external rotation 12°/18°, anterior pubis 30% short, diastasis ~4.8 cm.
> - Cloacal exstrophy: spinal anomalies 85–100%; osteotomy always.

## Q3a. Premalignant lesion of penis
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(premalignant conditions also answered in 2021-Summer Paper 1, Q3a)

### Definition
- A lesion with raised risk of invasive SCC (squamous cell carcinoma). The key entity is **PeIN (penile intraepithelial neoplasia)**: dysplastic squamous epithelium with an **intact basement membrane**.

### Classification
**1. PeIN (HPV-associated or HPV-independent)**
- **HPV (human papillomavirus)-associated** (commonest; basaloid and warty): viral E6 inactivates p53, E7 inactivates Rb (retinoblastoma protein); **p16** is the marker.
- **HPV-independent (differentiated):** arises in lichen sclerosus.
- **WHO (World Health Organization) 2022:** all PeIN is high grade; grades I–III abandoned.

| Clinical entity | Site, appearance | Risk of invasion |
|---|---|---|
| **Erythroplasia of Queyrat** | Glans or prepuce; red velvety plaque | **10–33%** |
| **Bowen disease** | Shaft; scaly red plaque | About 5% |
| Bowenoid papulosis | Shaft papules, young men; HPV 16 | Does not progress; may regress |

**2. Other lesions**
- **Lichen sclerosus (BXO [balanitis xerotica obliterans]):** SCC in **2.3–9%** (HPV-independent type).
- **Buschke–Löwenstein tumour** (giant condyloma; verrucous carcinoma): HPV 6/11; locally destructive, rarely metastasises; may harbour SCC.
- **Also know:** PEKMB (pseudoepitheliomatous keratotic and micaceous balanitis, controversial); condyloma acuminatum; chronic phimosis; cutaneous horn; Kaposi sarcoma (HHV-8 [human herpesvirus 8], check HIV [human immunodeficiency virus]).

### Evaluation
- **Multiple biopsies of adequate depth** to exclude invasion; p16 staining. Examine inguinal nodes; partner screening for cervical neoplasia.

### Treatment (organ-preserving)
| Situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Prepuce | Circumcision or excision with clear margin |
| Glans | **Topical 5-FU (5-fluorouracil) or 5% imiquimod**; **laser** (Nd:YAG [neodymium:yttrium-aluminium-garnet] preferred; CO₂ too superficial); **glans resurfacing** with skin graft |
| Shaft | Excision; Mohs surgery |
| Lichen sclerosus | Clobetasol 0.05% for 3 months then maintenance; circumcision |
| Buschke–Löwenstein | Wide excision; avoid radiotherapy (anaplastic change) |

- Long-term follow-up (recurrence common after organ-preserving treatment). **Prevention:** neonatal circumcision, HPV vaccination, avoiding smoking.

**Diagram to draw:** epithelium with full-thickness atypia above an intact basement membrane.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - PeIN: intact basement membrane; WHO 2022 all high grade.
> - Queyrat 10–33%, Bowen about 5%, bowenoid papulosis does not progress.
> - Lichen sclerosus: SCC 2.3–9%.

## Q3b. EPN. Prognostic factors & conserv. mgt.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(prognostic indicators also answered in 2024-Winter Paper 3, Q3c)

*Assumed meaning: EPN = emphysematous pyelonephritis.*

### Definition
- Acute **necrotising** infection of renal parenchyma and perirenal tissue by **gas-forming organisms** (*E. coli*, *Klebsiella*, *Proteus*). Mostly **diabetics**; obstruction in about a quarter. Mortality 19–43%.
- **Mechanism:** high tissue glucose fermented to CO₂ in poorly perfused tissue.
- **CT (computed tomography)** is the imaging of choice.

### Prognostic factors
**1. Huang–Tseng CT classification (2000)**
| Class | Gas location |
|---|---|
| 1 | Collecting system only |
| 2 | Parenchyma only |
| 3A | Perinephric space |
| 3B | Pararenal space |
| 4 | Bilateral or solitary kidney |

**2. Wan CT pattern**
- **Type I:** streaky gas, **no fluid**: mortality 50–60%.
- **Type II:** fluid with bubbly or loculated gas: <20%. Fluid means a contained collection that can be drained.

**3. Clinical risk factors (Huang–Tseng):** **thrombocytopenia, AKI (acute kidney injury), altered sensorium, shock**.
- **Also adverse:** hypoalbuminaemia, bacteraemia, need for haemodialysis, polymicrobial infection.

### Conservative management (now standard)
**1. Resuscitation:** fluids, inotropes, ICU (intensive care unit) if shocked; **tight glucose control**; correct electrolytes.

**2. Antibiotics:** broad-spectrum IV (intravenous), adjusted to culture; usually several weeks in total.

**3. Drainage**
- **Relieve obstruction:** DJ (double-J) stent or PCN (percutaneous nephrostomy).
- **PCD (percutaneous catheter drainage)** of gas and fluid collections under CT or ultrasound; multiple drains if needed; repeat CT to check.

**Algorithm**
```
All: resuscitation + glucose control + IV antibiotics + relieve obstruction
Class 1–2                      -> antibiotics ± PCD
Class 3–4, <2 risk factors     -> antibiotics + PCD
Class 3–4, >=2 risk factors,
or PCD failure                 -> nephrectomy (emergency or delayed)
```
- Emergency nephrectomy carried 40–50% mortality, so drainage-first care replaced it. Delayed nephrectomy for a non-functioning kidney on renal scan.
- **Emphysematous pyelitis** (class 1) often responds to antibiotics alone.

**Diagram to draw:** CT sketches of Huang–Tseng classes.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Huang–Tseng: 1 pelvis, 2 parenchyma, 3A perinephric, 3B pararenal, 4 bilateral/solitary.
> - Risk factors: thrombocytopenia, AKI, altered sensorium, shock.
> - Wan type I (no fluid) 50–60% mortality; drainage first, nephrectomy for failure.

## Q3c. VCUG
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition
- **VCUG (voiding cystourethrogram; MCU, micturating cystourethrogram):** fluoroscopic study of bladder filling and voiding after catheter instillation of contrast. It shows the bladder, **posterior urethra** and UVJ (ureterovesical junction) competence.
- **Gold standard for VUR (vesicoureteral reflux)** and PUV (posterior urethral valves).

### Indications
- **Children:** febrile UTI (urinary tract infection) with abnormal ultrasound or recurrent infection (EAU [European Association of Urology]: also age <12 months); bilateral hydroureteronephrosis (PUV); dilated ureter; ureterocele; neurogenic bladder.
- **Adults:** stricture (with RGU [retrograde urethrogram]), diverticula, fistula, post-operative leak, voiding dysfunction.

### Technique
- **Sterile urine** first. After UTI, wait until urine is sterile and the child is improving (about a week); the old 4–6-week delay is unnecessary.
- Scout film → catheter: **5 Fr feeding tube (infants), 6–8 Fr (children), 14–16 Fr (adults)** → confirm tip position.
- **Gravity fill** (≤70 cm H₂O) to expected capacity: <2 years weight (kg) × 7; 2–14 years (age × 30) + 30 mL; >14 years 300–500 mL. (Koff formula: (age + 2) × 30 mL.)
- **Oblique views** (UVJ, prostatic urethra) → remove catheter → **void under fluoroscopy** → **post-void film**.
- **Cyclic VCUG** (2–3 fill–void cycles) detects 12–20% more VUR.

### Findings
- **VUR grade (International Reflux Study, 1981):**

| Grade | Appearance |
|---|---|
| I | Ureter only, not dilated |
| II | Pelvis and calyces, no dilatation |
| III | Mild–moderate dilatation, minimal blunting |
| IV | Moderate dilatation and tortuosity |
| V | Gross dilatation; papillary impressions lost |

- Filling-phase reflux has a poorer resolution prognosis than voiding-only reflux.
- **PUV:** dilated, elongated posterior urethra, prominent bladder neck, trabeculation.
- Ureterocele, diverticula, residual urine; post-void pelvic retention suggests PUJ (pelviureteric junction) obstruction.

### Limitations and complications
- Invasive, radiation, UTI, catheter trauma; snapshot (occult VUR); interobserver variability; autonomic dysreflexia (injury above T6).

### Alternatives
- **Direct radionuclide cystography:** more sensitive, lower dose, poor anatomy.
- **CeVUS (contrast-enhanced voiding urosonography):** no radiation, accuracy close to VCUG.
- **PIC (positional instillation of contrast) cystography:** occult VUR with negative VCUG.

**Diagram to draw:** VUR grades I–V.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Gold standard for VUR and PUV; sterile urine first.
> - Capacity (age × 30) + 30 mL (2–14 years); cyclic study detects 12–20% more.
> - Grades I–II non-dilating, III–V dilating.

## Q3d. Criteria for evaluation of quaLliy of medical research papers
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(evidence-based medicine also answered in 2020-Summer Paper 4, Q3f)

*Assumed meaning: criteria for evaluating the quality of medical research papers (critical appraisal).*

### Definition
- **Critical appraisal:** systematic assessment of a paper's **validity, results and applicability** before using it. It is the "Appraise" step of EBM (evidence-based medicine).

### Three core questions
**1. Is it valid? (internal validity)**
- **PICO question** (Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome); suitable design.
- **RCTs (randomised controlled trials):** true randomisation, **allocation concealment**, comparable groups at baseline, **blinding**, complete follow-up (>80%), **ITT (intention-to-treat) analysis**.
- **Bias** checked:

| Bias | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Selection | Groups differ at entry |
| Confounding | Third factor (comorbidity) linked to exposure and outcome |
| Performance, detection, attrition | Unequal care, outcome assessment or dropouts |
| Lead-time, length-time | Screening seems to prolong survival |
| Stage migration (Will Rogers) | Better tests up-stage patients |
| Publication | Negative studies unpublished |

**2. What are the results?**
- Effect: RR (relative risk), **ARR (absolute risk reduction), NNT (number needed to treat) = 1/ARR**, HR (hazard ratio).
- Precision: **95% CI (confidence interval)**; p value; **sample size and power** (usually 80%; α 0.05).
- Surrogate end points valid only if they meet the **Prentice criteria**.
- Validated outcome tools; complications graded (Clavien–Dindo).

**3. Does it apply? (external validity)**
- Similar patients and setting; benefits outweigh harms and cost.

### Levels of evidence (Oxford CEBM [Centre for Evidence-Based Medicine], as used by EAU [European Association of Urology])
| Level | Design |
|---|---|
| 1a / 1b | Systematic review of RCTs / single RCT |
| 2a / 2b | Controlled non-randomised / cohort study |
| 3 | Case-control, case series |
| 4 | Expert opinion |
- **GRADE** (Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development and Evaluation): certainty high to very low; strong or weak recommendations.

### Reporting checklists and appraisal tools
| Design | Checklist |
|---|---|
| RCT | **CONSORT** |
| Systematic review / meta-analysis | **PRISMA**; AMSTAR 2 for quality |
| Observational | **STROBE**; Newcastle–Ottawa scale |
| Diagnostic accuracy | **STARD**; QUADAS-2 |
| Surgical innovation | **IDEAL** framework |
- **Also know:** Cochrane RoB 2 (risk-of-bias) tool; CASP checklists; trial registration; conflicts of interest.

**Diagram to draw:** evidence pyramid.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Three questions: valid, results, applicable.
> - Randomisation, concealment, blinding, ITT, follow-up >80%.
> - CONSORT (RCT), PRISMA (reviews), STROBE (observational), STARD (diagnostic).

## Q3e. Mgt. of acute hyperkalemia.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in 2025-Winter Paper 1, Q3e)

### Definition and physiology
- Serum potassium **>5.5 mmol/L**; mild 5.5–5.9, moderate 6.0–6.4, **severe ≥6.5** (UK Kidney Association).
- 98% of potassium is intracellular; the ratio sets the resting membrane potential, so high levels cause **arrhythmia and cardiac arrest**.
- Defence: shift into cells (insulin, β₂-agonists via Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase), then renal excretion (distal sodium delivery, urine flow, aldosterone).

### Causes
| Mechanism | Examples |
|---|---|
| Pseudohyperkalaemia | Haemolysis, tourniquet, fist clenching, thrombocytosis |
| Reduced excretion | AKI (acute kidney injury), CKD (chronic kidney disease), **obstructive uropathy** (type 4 RTA [renal tubular acidosis]), adrenal insufficiency; drugs: ACE (angiotensin-converting enzyme) inhibitors, ARBs (angiotensin receptor blockers), spironolactone, NSAIDs (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs), tacrolimus |
| Shift out of cells | Acidosis, insulin deficiency, β-blockers |
| Cell release | **Rhabdomyolysis** (prolonged lithotomy or flank position), tumour lysis, crush |
| Urological | **Jejunal conduit** (reabsorbs urinary potassium) |

### Assessment
- Repeat the sample to **exclude pseudohyperkalaemia** if the ECG (electrocardiogram) is normal.
- **ECG:** peaked T (earliest) → prolonged PR, wide QRS → loss of P → sine wave → ventricular fibrillation. Ascending weakness.
- Creatinine, blood gas, CK (creatine kinase); stop potassium sources.

### Emergency treatment (in order)
| Step | Drug and dose | Onset and effect |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Protect the heart** | **Calcium gluconate 10%, 10 mL IV (intravenous)** over 5–10 min; repeat if ECG unchanged | 1–3 min; does not lower potassium |
| **2. Shift into cells** | **Insulin 10 units regular IV with 50 mL of 50% dextrose** | 15–30 min; lowers about 0.6–1 mmol/L; monitor glucose |
| | **Salbutamol 20 mg nebulised** | Additive to insulin |
| | Sodium bicarbonate | Only with metabolic acidosis |
| **3. Remove** | Loop diuretic if passing urine; **binders**: sodium zirconium cyclosilicate (about 1 h), patiromer (about 7 h), sodium polystyrene sulfonate (slow; colonic necrosis) | Hours |
| | **Haemodialysis** | Refractory, oliguric, ESKD (end-stage kidney disease) |

- **Relieve obstruction** (catheter, nephrostomy); watch for post-obstructive diuresis.
- Monitor ECG, potassium and glucose; treat the cause.

**Diagram to draw:** flowchart: ECG change or K⁺ ≥6.5 → calcium → shift → remove.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Calcium stabilises the membrane but does not lower potassium.
> - Insulin 10 units + 50 mL 50% dextrose; salbutamol 20 mg nebulised.
> - Binders, diuretics and dialysis remove potassium; relieve obstruction.

## Q3f. Oncogene
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in 2017-Winter Paper 1, Q3f)

### Definition
- **Proto-oncogene:** normal gene that promotes cell growth and division (an "accelerator").
- **Oncogene:** its mutated or overactive form, driving uncontrolled proliferation. The change is **gain of function** and **dominant**: one altered allele is enough.
- The first human oncogene, **HRAS**, was found in the **T24 bladder cancer** cell line.

### Mechanisms of activation
| Mechanism | Effect | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Activating point mutation** | Protein permanently "on" | RAS; FGFR3 (fibroblast growth factor receptor 3) in low-grade papillary bladder cancer; germline MET in hereditary papillary RCC (renal cell carcinoma) |
| **Gene amplification** | More copies → overexpression | MYC (prostate, bladder); AR (androgen receptor) in castration-resistant prostate cancer; HER2 (human epidermal growth factor receptor 2) |
| **Translocation / fusion** | Gene under a strong promoter, or fusion protein | **TMPRSS2–ERG** (about half of prostate cancers); TFE3 fusions (translocation RCC) |
- **Also know:** promoter hypomethylation can switch oncogenes on.

### Functional classes
| Class | Examples |
|---|---|
| Growth factors | PDGF (platelet-derived growth factor) |
| Receptor tyrosine kinases | EGFR (epidermal growth factor receptor), HER2, FGFR3, MET, KIT |
| Signal transducers | **RAS** (GTP [guanosine triphosphate]-binding; mutation locks it on), BRAF, PI3K |
| Transcription factors | **MYC**, ERG |
| Cell cycle / apoptosis | Cyclin D1, BCL-2 |

### Oncogene versus tumour suppressor gene
| | Oncogene | Tumour suppressor |
|---|---|---|
| Role | Accelerator | Brake, DNA repair |
| Change | Gain of function | Loss of function |
| Alleles | One | Usually both (**Knudson two-hit**) |
| GU (genitourinary) examples | MYC, MET, FGFR3, ERG | RB1, TP53, VHL, PTEN |

### Urological significance
- **Bladder:** FGFR3 and TERT promoter mutations in >60% of low-grade papillary tumours; HRAS mutually exclusive with FGFR3; MYC overexpressed in over half.
- **Kidney:** MET activation in papillary RCC.
- **Prostate:** TMPRSS2–ERG, MYC (8q gain), AR amplification under hormone therapy.

### Targeted therapy ("oncogene addiction")
- **Erdafitinib** (FGFR inhibitor) for FGFR3/2-altered metastatic urothelial cancer; MET-active TKIs (tyrosine kinase inhibitors) in papillary RCC; AR-targeted drugs in prostate cancer.

**Diagram to draw:** growth factor → receptor → RAS → RAF → MEK → ERK → MYC.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Gain of function, one allele; mutation, amplification, translocation.
> - HRAS first found in T24 bladder cancer.
> - TMPRSS2–ERG in about half of prostate cancers; MET in papillary RCC.
