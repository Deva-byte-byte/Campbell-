## Q3a. Fungal UTI
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Introduction and organisms
- Fungal UTI (urinary tract infection) is rising with antibiotics, catheters and immunosuppression. **Candida** is commonest: *C. albicans*, then *C. glabrata* (often fluconazole-resistant); *C. krusei* is intrinsically resistant.
- Others in AIDS (acquired immunodeficiency syndrome) or transplant patients: Aspergillus, Cryptococcus, Mucorales, Histoplasma.

### Risk factors and routes
- Antibiotics, indwelling catheters and stents, diabetes, steroids, neutropenia, prematurity, parenteral nutrition, obstruction.
- **Ascending** (catheter) causes most candiduria. **Haematogenous** spread from candidaemia: in disseminated candidiasis the kidney is the organ most often involved.

### Clinical spectrum
- Asymptomatic candiduria (most) → cystitis → pyelonephritis → **fungus ball (bezoar)** causing obstruction → renal abscess or candidaemia.

### Diagnosis
- Microscopy: budding yeasts and pseudohyphae (may mimic red cells). Repeat culture: contamination is common.
- Children: treat when repeated cultures exceed **10,000–15,000 CFU (colony-forming units)/mL**.
- Persistent funguria: ultrasound or CT (computed tomography) for bezoar; blood cultures if high risk.

### Management
| Situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Asymptomatic | Remove or change catheter, stop antibiotics; no antifungal |
| Treat asymptomatic only if | Neutropenia, very-low-birth-weight infant, before a urological procedure |
| Cystitis | Fluconazole 200 mg (3 mg/kg) daily for 2 weeks |
| Pyelonephritis | Fluconazole 200–400 mg (3–6 mg/kg) daily for 2 weeks |
| Fluconazole-resistant (*C. glabrata*, *C. krusei*) | Amphotericin B deoxycholate 0.3–0.6 mg/kg/day for 1–7 days ± flucytosine |
| Resistant cystitis | Amphotericin B bladder irrigation 50 mg/L |
| Fungus ball | PCN (percutaneous nephrostomy) drainage + systemic antifungal + local amphotericin irrigation; endoscopic or open removal if persistent |

- Fluconazole concentrates in urine; **echinocandins and lipid amphotericin do not.**
- **Before procedures:** IDSA (Infectious Diseases Society of America) 2016 treats all candiduria (fluconazole 400 mg daily for several days before and after). AUA (American Urological Association) Best Practice Statement 2019 gives single-dose antifungal prophylaxis only for urothelium-breaching surgery. The two disagree.
- **Also know:** alkalinisation for bezoar; fluconazole caution under 6 months.

**Diagram to draw:** budding yeast with pseudohyphae; fungus ball in renal pelvis with nephrostomy.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Candida commonest; *C. glabrata* often fluconazole-resistant.
> - Asymptomatic candiduria: remove catheter, no antifungal (except neutropenia, neonate, procedure).
> - Fluconazole 2 weeks; echinocandins fail in urine.
> - Fungus ball: drain, antifungal, remove.

## Q3b. Antimicrobial resistance
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in part in 2015-Summer Paper 1, Q3f)

### Definition
- AMR (antimicrobial resistance): a microbe grows despite drug levels achievable in the patient. Reported as S (sensitive), I (intermediate), R (resistant) with the MIC (minimum inhibitory concentration).
- One of the most serious global health threats.

### Types
| Type | Mechanism | Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Intrinsic | Species lacks the target | Predictable (enterococci and cephalosporins) |
| Acquired chromosomal | Mutants selected when urine level is close to MIC | Underdosing, poor compliance |
| Plasmid (extrachromosomal) | Horizontal transfer in bowel flora | **Commonest**; multidrug resistance after antibiotics |

### Mechanisms
- **Enzymes:** β-lactamases; aminoglycoside-modifying enzymes.
- **Altered target:** penicillin-binding protein; DNA gyrase mutation (quinolones).
- **Reduced entry** (porin loss) and **efflux pumps**.
- **Bypass:** enterococci scavenge folate (escape TMP-SMX (trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole)).

### Key resistant organisms
- **ESBL (extended-spectrum β-lactamase)** (plasmid, 1983): resists 3rd-generation cephalosporins; often co-resistant to fluoroquinolones and aminoglycosides. *E. coli* ST131 carries it.
- **AmpC; KPC (*Klebsiella pneumoniae* carbapenemase, 2001); NDM (New Delhi metallo-β-lactamase)**: carbapenem resistance.
- MRSA (methicillin-resistant *S. aureus*), VRE (vancomycin-resistant enterococci).

### Drivers in urology
- Overuse of fluoroquinolones and cephalosporins; treating ASB (asymptomatic bacteriuria); long prophylaxis; catheters, stents, stones (biofilm).
- Transrectal biopsy: fluoroquinolone-resistant rectal *E. coli* causes sepsis.

### Prevention: stewardship
1. Culture before treatment; follow the **local antibiogram**.
2. TMP-SMX only if local resistance ≤20%; oral fluoroquinolone for pyelonephritis only if ≤10%.
3. Prefer narrow, urine-confined drugs for cystitis (nitrofurantoin, fosfomycin, pivmecillinam: low resistance).
4. Shortest course; de-escalate to culture.
5. Do not treat ASB, except in pregnancy and before mucosa-breaching procedures.
6. Single-dose surgical prophylaxis; transperineal biopsy or rectal-swab-targeted prophylaxis.
7. Infection control and hand hygiene.
- **Also know:** FDA (Food and Drug Administration) 2016 fluoroquinolone warning; *C. difficile*; ESBL: carbapenem, or fosfomycin/nitrofurantoin for cystitis.

### Recent advances
- New β-lactam/β-lactamase inhibitor combinations (ceftazidime–avibactam, meropenem–vaborbactam); cefiderocol; gepotidacin.

**Diagram to draw:** Gram-negative cell showing β-lactamase, porin loss, efflux pump, altered target.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Plasmid-mediated resistance is commonest.
> - ESBL: plasmid, co-resistant; KPC/NDM: carbapenem resistance.
> - TMP-SMX if resistance ≤20%; fluoroquinolone for pyelonephritis if ≤10%.
> - Stewardship: culture, narrow, short, no ASB treatment.

## Q3c. Biofilm
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in part in 2015-Summer Paper 3, Q3d)

### Definition
- A **structured microbial community in a self-made polymeric matrix**, adherent to a living or inert surface. The matrix contains exopolysaccharide, extracellular DNA and proteins.

### Stages of formation
1. **Conditioning film:** host proteins coat the device within minutes.
2. **Attachment:** reversible, then irreversible (pili, adhesins, flagella).
3. **Matrix production and microcolonies.**
4. **Maturation:** 3-D (three-dimensional) structure with water channels; cell-to-cell signalling (quorum sensing).
5. **Dispersion:** free bacteria seed new infection.

### Why antibiotics and immunity fail
- Poor drug penetration through the matrix.
- Slow-growing, dormant cells resist drugs that need active growth.
- Altered genes, envelope and targets.
- Survive **1000–1500 times** the usual killing concentration. Neutrophils are excluded.

### Urological examples
| Site | Point |
|---|---|
| Urethral catheter | Forms within minutes; **the catheter, not the bladder, is infected**. Proteus urease makes crystalline biofilm → encrustation and blockage |
| Ureteric stent, nephrostomy | Stent colonised more often than urine shows; nephrostomy changed every 3 months |
| Penile prosthesis, AUS (artificial urinary sphincter) | Revision of an "uninfected" device: about 10% infection |
| Infection stones | Bacteria inside struvite; antibiotics cannot sterilise |
| Chronic bacterial prostatitis | Biofilm commoner in prostatitis *E. coli*; needs longer, penetrating therapy |
| Recurrent cystitis | **Intracellular bacterial communities**: biofilm-like pods inside umbrella cells |

- Common organisms: Pseudomonas, Proteus, *E. coli*, Enterococcus, coagulase-negative staphylococci, Candida.

### Prevention and management
- Avoid devices; shortest dwell; aseptic insertion; closed dependent drainage.
- **Change the catheter** when treating symptomatic CAUTI (catheter-associated UTI (urinary tract infection)); remove infected stent or implant.
- Silver-alloy catheters reduce bacteriuria, not clinical UTI.
- Implants: antibiotic-coated devices, no-touch technique, **antiseptic washout** at revision (infection about 10% → 3%).
- Stones: complete clearance. Chemodissolution for catheter encrustation. Macrolides penetrate biofilm (prostatitis).
- **Also know (research):** quorum-sensing inhibitors, bacteriophages, new coatings.

**Diagram to draw:** five-stage biofilm cycle on a catheter surface.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Biofilm: microbes in self-made matrix on a surface.
> - Survives 1000–1500 times normal antibiotic levels.
> - Treat CAUTI by changing the catheter.
> - Revision washout cuts prosthesis infection from about 10% to 3%.

## Q3d. Tumour markers in urology
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition and uses
- A substance made by a tumour, measured in blood, urine or tissue.
- Uses: screening, diagnosis, staging, prognosis, response, and detecting relapse. Ideal: specific, sensitive, proportional to bulk, short half-life.

### Prostate
- **PSA (prostate-specific antigen):** a kallikrein serine protease (hK3) from luminal cells; **organ-specific, not cancer-specific** (BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia), prostatitis, instrumentation raise it). Best marker for post-treatment follow-up.
- **Refinements:** density (PSA ÷ volume; cut-offs 0.08–0.15); %free PSA (lower in cancer; for PSA 4–10 ng/mL); velocity (little value); **PHI (Prostate Health Index) = (p2PSA ÷ free PSA) × √total PSA**; 4Kscore.
- **Urine:** PCA3 (prostate cancer antigen 3) after DRE (digital rectal examination); score = PCA3 mRNA ÷ KLK3 mRNA × 1000.
- **Also know:** tissue genomic classifiers.

### Testis (germ cell tumours)
| Marker | Half-life | Source | Never raised in |
|---|---|---|---|
| AFP (α-fetoprotein) | 5–7 days | Embryonal, yolk sac | Pure seminoma, choriocarcinoma |
| β-hCG (human chorionic gonadotrophin) | 24–36 hours | Choriocarcinoma, embryonal; seminoma in ~15% | Yolk sac tumour |
| LDH (lactate dehydrogenase) | 24 hours | Non-specific; reflects bulk | — |

- Measure before and after orchidectomy, on chemotherapy, in follow-up. Post-orchidectomy values give the **S stage** (S1: LDH <1.5 × normal, hCG <5000 mIU/mL, AFP <1000 ng/mL) and the **IGCCCG (International Germ Cell Cancer Collaborative Group)** risk group.
- Raised AFP with "pure seminoma" histology → treat as NSGCT (non-seminomatous germ cell tumour). False positives: liver disease, marijuana, LH (luteinising hormone) cross-reaction.
- **miR-371a-3p:** sensitive new marker; not raised by teratoma.

### Bladder and upper tract
- **Urine cytology:** sensitive and specific for high-grade cancer, poor for low grade; for surveillance, not screening.
- NMP22 (nuclear matrix protein 22), BTA (bladder tumour antigen), ImmunoCyt, UroVysion FISH (fluorescence in situ hybridisation): selected use only.

### Others
- **Kidney and penis:** no validated serum marker.
- **Adrenal:** metanephrines, cortisol, aldosterone–renin ratio. **Neuroblastoma:** urinary VMA (vanillylmandelic acid)/HVA (homovanillic acid) raised in 90–95%.

**Diagram to draw:** marker decline curve after orchidectomy using half-lives.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - PSA: organ-specific, not cancer-specific.
> - AFP never in pure seminoma; half-lives AFP 5–7 days, hCG 24–36 hours.
> - S stage uses post-orchidectomy values.
> - Cytology: good for high grade only.

## Q3e. Haemostatic agents in urology
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Introduction
- Topical agents are **adjuncts** to surgical haemostasis (suture, clip, energy), used for diffuse ooze. No randomised study shows clear benefit of any single agent in partial nephrectomy.

### Classification
| Class | Examples | Mechanism / note |
|---|---|---|
| **Passive (mechanical)** | Gelatin sponge (Gelfoam), **oxidised regenerated cellulose (Surgicel)**, microfibrillar collagen, polysaccharide spheres | Matrix for platelets; needs intact clotting. Cellulose is acidic (antibacterial) |
| **Active** | Topical thrombin | Converts the patient's fibrinogen to fibrin |
| **Flowable** | Gelatin matrix + thrombin (**Floseal**) | Fills irregular beds, swells |
| **Fibrin sealants** | Fibrinogen + thrombin (Tisseel, Evicel) | Final clotting step; works despite coagulopathy; also seals leaks |
| **Synthetic sealants and glues** | PEG (polyethylene glycol) hydrogel (Coseal), cyanoacrylate, albumin–glutaraldehyde (BioGlue) | Seal; adhesive |
| **Systemic** | Tranexamic acid, aminocaproic acid, desmopressin | Antifibrinolytic; desmopressin for uraemic platelets |

### Urological uses
- **Partial nephrectomy:** renorrhaphy ± sealant or Surgicel bolster; sutureless repair in selected cases.
- **Laparoscopic venous injury:** pressure → sealant → suture.
- PCNL (percutaneous nephrolithotomy) tract sealing in tubeless cases; adrenal and spleen/liver ooze; prostatectomy and lymphadenectomy beds.
- **Fibrin glue:** ureteroscopic fulguration for upper-tract bleeding; fistula plugs.
- **Tranexamic acid:** lowers transfusion at radical cystectomy.

### Intravesical agents (haemorrhagic cystitis)
| Agent | Note |
|---|---|
| Alum 1% (50 g in 5 L) | First line; avoid in renal failure (aluminium toxicity) |
| Silver nitrate 0.5–1% | Chemical coagulation |
| Aminocaproic acid 200 mg/L irrigation | Thromboembolism and clot retention |
| Formalin 1–4% | Most effective; under anaesthesia; **cystogram first** to exclude reflux |

### Complications
- Swelling compresses nerves, ureter or spinal cord (gelatin, cellulose): use the least amount.
- Mimics abscess on imaging; foreign-body reaction; infection.
- Flowables entering open vessels → embolism or thrombosis.
- Bovine thrombin → antibodies and coagulopathy; human products → theoretical viral risk.
- **Also know:** energy adjuncts (bipolar, argon beam, vessel sealers); hyperbaric oxygen in radiation cystitis.

**Diagram to draw:** clotting cascade showing where passive agents, thrombin and fibrin sealant act.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Agents are adjuncts, not substitutes for sutures.
> - Fibrin sealant works despite coagulopathy; passive agents need normal clotting.
> - Surgicel: acidic, may mimic abscess.
> - Alum first line; formalin last, after cystogram.

## Q3f. Urethral catheters, guidewires and DJ stents
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in part in 2016-Summer Paper 1, Q3e and 2015-Summer Paper 3, Q1)

### Urethral catheters
- **Size:** Fr (French) = 3 × diameter in mm. Adult 14–16 Fr; clot retention 20–24 Fr three-way. Male meatus is the narrowest point (24 Fr).
- **Lumens:** one (intermittent, specimen); two (drainage + balloon, routine); three (adds irrigation; stiff wall resists collapse). Each lumen shrinks drainage.
- **Tips:** straight; **coudé** (curved, for prostatic enlargement, tip up); **Councill** (open tip, over a wire).
- **Materials:** latex, silicone (better urethral tolerance), PVC (polyvinyl chloride); silver coatings reduce bacteriuria, not clinical infection.
- **Special:** Malecot (winged, no balloon); Lapides diagnostic (rings 1 cm apart); urodynamic.
- Inflate balloon with sterile water, only after urine flows at the hub. **Suspected urethral injury → RUG (retrograde urethrogram) first.**

### Guidewires
| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Uses | Access, safety wire, straightening ureter, guiding dilation and stents |
| Construction | Stainless-steel core with outer wrap; **nitinol** core is kink-resistant |
| Coating | PTFE (polytetrafluoroethylene): standard safety wire. **Hydrophilic** "glide": passes impacted stones, but too slippery to be a safety wire. Hybrid: hydrophilic tip, PTFE shaft |
| Size | 0.018–0.038 inch (commonly 0.035/0.038); 80–260 cm, 145 cm most useful; floppy tip 1–3 cm |
| Tip and stiffness | Straight, angled, J; standard to super-stiff (straightens tortuous ureter) |

### DJ (double-J) stents
- Self-retaining double-pigtail (Finney, 1978); adult usually 6 Fr, length by height.
- **Materials:** polyurethane, silicone (hydrocoated silicone less painful); **metallic (Resonance)** for extrinsic malignant compression.
- **Indications:** obstruction (infected obstructed kidney, malignancy, pregnancy), after URS (ureteroscopy) with injury, pyeloplasty, transplant, leak.
- **Placement:** retrograde pyelogram → wire → stent over wire under fluoroscopy or vision.
- **Problems:** stent symptoms (bladder irritation and reflux) eased by α-blockers and antimuscarinics; migration; encrustation (forgotten stent); failure with extrinsic compression.

**Diagram to draw:** three-way Foley cross-section; DJ stent in situ.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Fr = 3 × diameter (mm); coudé for prostatic enlargement, Councill over a wire.
> - Hydrophilic wire to pass, PTFE wire to stay (safety).
> - DJ symptoms: α-blockers and antimuscarinics help.
> - Long-dwelling stents: monitor for encrustation.
