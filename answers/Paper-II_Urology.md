## II-1. Describe medical management of BPH and role of various surgical modalities in its management.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

### Definition
- **BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia)** = histological; **BPO (benign prostatic obstruction)** → **LUTS (lower urinary tract symptoms)**: storage, voiding, post-micturition.
- Drug targets: static (bulk, DHT (dihydrotestosterone)) → 5-ARIs (5α-reductase inhibitors); dynamic (α1A tone) → α-blockers.

### Evaluation
- **IPSS (International Prostate Symptom Score)** 0–35: mild 0–7, moderate 8–19, severe 20–35. FVC (frequency-volume chart), 3 days.
- DRE (digital rectal examination), urinalysis, **PSA (prostate-specific antigen)** (if life expectancy >10 y); optional Qmax (maximum flow rate; <10 mL/s suggests obstruction), PVR (post-void residual).
- **Before surgery** (AUA (American Urological Association)): prostate size/shape; pressure-flow study if diagnosis uncertain.

### Medical management
**1. Watchful waiting** – mild/non-bothersome: fluid timing, ↓ caffeine/alcohol.

**2. α1-blockers** – first line, bothersome LUTS; act within days.

| Drug | Dose | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Tamsulosin | 0.4 mg OD (once daily) | α1A/α1D selective |
| Alfuzosin | 10 mg OD | Least ejaculatory dysfunction |
| Silodosin | 8 mg OD | Highest anejaculation |
| Doxazosin/terazosin | Titrate | Non-selective; orthostatic hypotension |

- IPSS ↓ ~30–40%; **no ↓ volume or AUR (acute urinary retention)/surgery risk**.
- Side effects: postural hypotension, dizziness, ejaculatory dysfunction.
- **IFIS (intraoperative floppy iris syndrome)** – strongest with tamsulosin; **defer starting α-blocker if cataract surgery planned**.

**3. 5-ARIs** – finasteride 5 mg (type 2); dutasteride 0.5 mg (types 1 + 2).
- For **enlarged prostate** (AUA: PSA >1.5 ng/mL or >30 mL; EAU (European Association of Urology): >40 mL).
- Volume ↓ **~20–25%** over 6–12 months; **PSA halved** (×2 after 6 months); AUR/surgery ↓ ~50%.
- Side effects: ↓ libido, ED (erectile dysfunction), gynaecomastia; ↑ high-grade cancer detection → FDA (Food and Drug Administration) warning (2011).

**4. Combination**
- **MTOPS (Medical Therapy of Prostatic Symptoms)**: doxazosin + finasteride ↓ progression **66%** vs placebo (doxazosin 39%, finasteride 34%).
- **CombAT (Combination of Avodart and Tamsulosin; 4-yr)**: dutasteride + tamsulosin, volume ≥30 mL; AUR/surgery ↓ **~66% vs tamsulosin**.
- Consider stopping α-blocker after 6–12 months.

**5. Storage** – antimuscarinics (caution **PVR >250–300 mL**, cognitive effects) or β3-agonists (mirabegron 25–50 mg).
**6. Tadalafil 5 mg OD** – LUTS ± ED; no nitrates.
**7. Nocturnal polyuria** – low-dose desmopressin; **serum sodium** at baseline, first week, periodically.
**8. Phytotherapy** – AUA: not recommended.

```
LUTS → IPSS, DRE, urinalysis, PSA, FVC
  Mild → watchful waiting
  Voiding → α-blocker (± 5-ARI if >30–40 mL)
  Storage persists → antimuscarinic/β3 (check PVR)
  Nocturnal polyuria → desmopressin
  Failure/complications → surgery (size-based)
```

### Surgical management
**Indications**: refractory retention, recurrent UTI (urinary tract infection), refractory haematuria, bladder stones, renal insufficiency from BPO; LUTS failing medical therapy.

| Modality | Size (AUA) | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TURP (transurethral resection of prostate)** | **30–80 mL** | Reference standard | Bleeding, retrograde ejaculation ~65–75%, TUR syndrome, stricture |
| **TUIP (transurethral incision of prostate)** | **≤30 mL** | Less retrograde ejaculation | Higher retreatment |
| **PVP (photoselective vaporization)** | 30–80 mL | Anticoagulated patients | No histology |
| **HoLEP (holmium laser enucleation)** | **Size-independent** | Lowest retreatment, least bleeding | Learning curve |
| **Simple prostatectomy** | **>80–100 mL** | Complete removal | Morbidity, transfusion |
| **Aquablation** | 30–80; consider **80–150 mL** | Ejaculation preserved | Bleeding |
| **Rezūm** (water vapour) | 30–80 mL | Day care, treats median lobe | Delayed relief |
| **PUL (prostatic urethral lift)** | 30–80 mL, **no median lobe** | Sexual function preserved | Higher retreatment |
| **PAE (prostatic artery embolisation)** | Larger glands | No general anaesthesia | Less effective than TURP |

**TUR syndrome** (monopolar, 1.5% glycine): dilutional hyponatraemia, fluid overload, glycine toxicity (confusion, bradycardia, visual disturbance); risk ↑ resection >60–90 min. Treat: stop surgery, diuretics, 3% saline if severe. **Bipolar TURP (saline)** virtually eliminates it.

**Landmark trials**
- **GOLIATH**: PVP **non-inferior** to TURP.
- **WATER (Waterjet Ablation Therapy for Endoscopic Resection of prostate tissue)**: Aquablation non-inferior to TURP, less anejaculation; **WATER II**: 80–150 mL.

### Complications
- Bleeding, TUR syndrome, sepsis; retrograde ejaculation, transient incontinence, stricture, bladder neck contracture.

### Follow-up
- IPSS at 4–12 weeks (α-blocker), 3–6 months (5-ARI), then yearly.

### Recent advances
- **Optilume BPH** (paclitaxel-coated balloon) FDA-approved 2023.
- Office MISTs (minimally invasive surgical therapies) preserve ejaculation.

**Diagram to draw:** Size-based algorithm (≤30 TUIP → 30–80 TURP/PVP → >80 enucleation/simple prostatectomy).

## II-2. Discuss metabolic evaluation in urinary calculus disease.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

### Introduction
- Recurrence ~50% at 5–10 years without prevention → find correctable risk factors.
- **Supersaturation** → nucleation → growth → aggregation. Promoters: Ca, oxalate, uric acid, low volume. Inhibitors: citrate, magnesium, pyrophosphate, Tamm-Horsfall protein.
- **RSS (relative supersaturation)** from 24-h urine guides therapy (target <1).

### Who needs it
- **Basic – every stone former**; **comprehensive (24-h urine)** – high-risk, recurrent, interested first-time formers.
- **High-risk (EAU (European Association of Urology))**: early onset/children, family history, recurrent, solitary kidney, bilateral/large burden; brushite, uric acid, infection, cystine stones; **primary hyperparathyroidism**, GI (gastrointestinal) disease/bariatric surgery; cystinuria, primary hyperoxaluria, **RTA (renal tubular acidosis) type 1**; drugs (topiramate, acetazolamide, indinavir); medullary sponge kidney, calyceal diverticulum, horseshoe kidney.

### Basic evaluation (all)
- **History**: age at onset, episodes, family history, diet (fluid, salt, animal protein, oxalate), drugs, bowel disease, gout.
- **Imaging**: NCCT (non-contrast computed tomography) – uric acid <500 HU (Hounsfield units).
- **Serum**: creatinine, **calcium**, **uric acid**, **bicarbonate** (low → RTA), phosphate; **PTH (parathyroid hormone)** if Ca high-normal/raised.
- **Urine**: **pH** (>6.5–7 → RTA/infection; <5.5 → uric acid), crystals (hexagonal = cystine; coffin-lid = struvite), culture (urease organisms).
- **Stone analysis** – first episode and recurrence on therapy: **infrared spectroscopy or X-ray diffraction**.
- **Cystine screen**: cyanide-nitroprusside test → confirm with 24-h cystine.

### Comprehensive – 24-h urine
- **Two collections** (AUA (American Urological Association) one or two; EAU two), usual diet, ≥2–4 weeks after stone event, stone- and infection-free.
- Adequacy: **urinary creatinine** (men ~20–25, women ~15–20 mg/kg/d).

| Parameter | Desirable | Abnormality |
|---|---|---|
| Volume | **>2.5 L** | <2 L – commonest |
| Calcium | <250 mg (F), <300 mg (M), or **<4 mg/kg** | Hypercalciuria |
| Oxalate | **<40–45 mg** | Hyperoxaluria |
| Citrate | **>320 mg** | Hypocitraturia |
| Uric acid | <750 mg (F), **<800 mg (M)** | Hyperuricosuria |
| Sodium | <100 mmol/d | ↑ Ca excretion |
| pH | 5.8–6.2 | <5.5 uric acid; >6.5–7 CaP (calcium phosphate)/RTA |
| Magnesium | >70 mg | Hypomagnesiuria (GI) |
| Cystine | Very low | Cystinuria (**>400 mg/d**) |

### Diagnostic categories
1. **Hypercalciuria** – absorptive, renal leak (↑ PTH, normal serum Ca), resorptive (primary hyperparathyroidism – ↑ Ca, ↑ PTH); thiazide works for all idiopathic.
2. **Hyperoxaluria** – dietary (45–60 mg/d); **enteric** (fat malabsorption: fatty acids bind Ca → free oxalate absorbed in colon; + hypocitraturia, low volume); **primary** (PH (primary hyperoxaluria); often >75–100 mg/d; genetic testing).
3. **Hypocitraturia** – idiopathic, **dRTA (distal RTA)**, chronic diarrhoea, hypokalaemia.
4. **Hyperuricosuria** – promotes **CaOx (calcium oxalate)** stones. **Uric acid stones**: **urine pH <5.5** (metabolic syndrome, diabetes).
5. **dRTA** – urine pH >5.5–6, hypocitraturia, CaP stones; incomplete form → **ammonium chloride test** (0.1 g/kg; pH fails to fall **<5.5**).
6. **Cystinuria** – autosomal recessive; COLA (cystine, ornithine, lysine, arginine); aim **<250 mg/L**.
7. **Infection stones** – urease organisms, pH >7, struvite.

```
Stone → basic evaluation
  Low risk → general advice (output >2.5 L, ↓ Na/protein, normal Ca)
  High risk/recurrent → two 24-h urines → specific abnormality
    → targeted therapy → repeat 24-h urine ~6 months → yearly
```

### Treatment by abnormality

| Abnormality | Diet | Drug |
|---|---|---|
| Hypercalciuria | Na <2.3 g/d, normal Ca, ↓ animal protein | **Thiazide** (HCTZ (hydrochlorothiazide) 25–50 mg, chlorthalidone 25 mg, indapamide 2.5 mg) + K citrate |
| Primary hyperparathyroidism | – | **Parathyroidectomy** |
| Hypocitraturia, dRTA | ↑ fruit/vegetables | **K citrate** 30–60 mEq/d |
| Enteric hyperoxaluria | Low fat/oxalate, Ca with meals | Ca citrate, cholestyramine |
| **PH1** | High fluid | **Pyridoxine**, **lumasiran**; liver ± kidney transplant |
| Hyperuricosuria | ↓ purine | **Allopurinol** 100–300 mg |
| Uric acid stones | – | **K citrate** to pH 6.0–6.5 |
| Cystinuria | Fluid >3–4 L | Alkalinise **pH 7.0–7.5**; **tiopronin** |
| Infection | – | Complete clearance, antibiotics |

### Special groups
- **Children**: **all need full evaluation**; spot urine ratios (to creatinine) if 24-h impractical; screen cystinuria, PH, RTA.
- **Pregnancy**: defer 24-h urine until **after delivery/lactation** (physiological hypercalciuria); avoid thiazides, allopurinol, tiopronin.

### Follow-up
- **Repeat 24-h urine within 6 months**, then annually; drug toxicity checks; periodic imaging.

### Recent advances
- **RNAi (ribonucleic acid interference) for PH1** (lumasiran, nedosiran).
- **NOSTONE (2023)**: hydrochlorothiazide did not ↓ recurrence vs placebo.

## II-3a. Management of priapism
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition
- Full or partial erection **>4 hours** beyond or unrelated to sexual stimulation (AUA/SMSNA (American Urological Association/Sexual Medicine Society of North America) 2021).

### Classification
| | **Ischaemic (low-flow)** | **Non-ischaemic (high-flow)** | **Stuttering** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | Veno-occlusive, compartment syndrome | Cavernous artery–sinusoidal fistula | Recurrent ischaemic |
| Cause | ICI (intracavernosal injection), SCD (sickle cell disease), antipsychotics | Perineal trauma | SCD |
| Penis | Rigid, painful | Partially rigid, painless | Painful |
| Emergency | **Yes** | No | Prevent progression |

### Diagnosis
- History (duration, drugs, trauma, SCD); glans soft in ischaemic.
- **Corporal blood gas** – ischaemic: **pO2 <30, pCO2 >60 mmHg, pH <7.25** (normal arterial: >90, <40, 7.40).
- **Colour Doppler**: absent flow (ischaemic); fistula (non-ischaemic).
- Blood count, haemoglobin electrophoresis, toxicology.

### Ischaemic priapism
```
Dorsal penile block → aspiration (16–18G) ± saline irrigation
→ Phenylephrine 100–500 µg/mL, 1 mL every 3–5 min, up to ~1 h (monitor blood pressure/pulse)
→ Failure → distal shunt → proximal shunt
→ >36 h / failed shunts → immediate penile prosthesis
```
- **Distal shunts**: Winter (Tru-cut needle), Ebbehoj, T-shunt, Al-Ghorab (open); corporal tunnelling.
- Oral therapy alone not recommended.
- **Proximal**: Quackels (cavernoso-spongiosal), Grayhack (cavernoso-saphenous).
- Ischaemia >24–48 h → fibrosis, ED (erectile dysfunction); **early prosthesis** easier and preserves length (counsel re infection).
- **SCD**: intracavernosal therapy first + concurrent haematology care; do not delay.

### Non-ischaemic
- **Observation** first (many resolve); persistent → **selective embolisation** (gelfoam/clot – lower ED); surgical ligation rarely.

### Stuttering
- Treat episodes as ischaemic; prevent with **daily PDE5i (phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitor)**, pseudoephedrine, self-injection phenylephrine, GnRH (gonadotropin-releasing hormone) agonists/antiandrogens (not if fertility desired); SCD – hydroxyurea.

**Diagram to draw:** Penile cross-section – distal vs proximal shunts.

## II-3b. Renal tubular acidosis
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition
- **RTA (renal tubular acidosis)** = **hyperchloraemic, normal AG (anion gap) metabolic acidosis** from impaired HCO3 reabsorption or H+ excretion, with preserved GFR (glomerular filtration rate).

### Types

| | **Type 1 (distal)** | **Type 2 (proximal)** | **Type 4 (hyperkalaemic)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Defect | ↓ H+ secretion, collecting duct | ↓ HCO3 reabsorption, proximal tubule | Aldosterone deficiency/resistance |
| Urine pH | **>5.5 always** | <5.5 once HCO3 below threshold | <5.5 |
| Serum K | Low | Low | **High** |
| Urine citrate | **Low** | Normal/high | Normal |
| Stones/nephrocalcinosis | **Common (calcium phosphate)** | Rare | Absent |
| Causes | Genetic, Sjogren's, amphotericin, MSK (medullary sponge kidney) | Fanconi, myeloma, acetazolamide | Diabetes, **obstructive uropathy**, spironolactone |
| Treatment | **K citrate 1–2 mEq/kg/d** | Alkali 10–15 mEq/kg/d + K | **Fludrocortisone**, low K diet |

- **Type 3**: rare, carbonic anhydrase II deficiency.
- **Incomplete dRTA (distal RTA)**: normal HCO3 but cannot acidify urine – common in recurrent calcium phosphate stone formers.

### Diagnosis
- **UAG (urine anion gap) = (Na + K) – Cl**: **negative** → GI (gastrointestinal) loss/proximal; **positive** → distal/type 4.
- **Ammonium chloride test** (0.1 g/kg): pH fails to fall **<5.5** = dRTA.
- **Furosemide–fludrocortisone test** (40 mg + 1 mg): better tolerated.
- FE (fractional excretion) HCO3 >15% on bicarbonate loading = proximal.

### Urological relevance
- dRTA → **recurrent calcium phosphate stones, nephrocalcinosis**, hypocitraturia.
- **MSK** associated with incomplete dRTA.
- **Obstructive uropathy** (posterior urethral valves, prostatic obstruction) → type 4; improves after relief.
- **Topiramate/acetazolamide** → stones.
- **Urinary diversion** → hyperchloraemic acidosis mimicking RTA.
- K citrate preferred over sodium bicarbonate (sodium ↑ Ca excretion).

**Diagram to draw:** Nephron – proximal tubule (type 2), α-intercalated cell (type 1), aldosterone site (type 4).

## II-3c. Millin's prostatectomy
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition
- **Retropubic extravesical simple prostatectomy** through **transverse anterior capsulotomy**, bladder not opened – **Millin, 1945**. Contrast **Freyer's transvesical**.

### Indications
- Gland **>80–100 mL** when HoLEP (holmium laser enucleation of prostate) unavailable; usual BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia) surgery indications.
- **Best for** large lateral lobes, small/no median lobe.
- **Relative contraindications**: large median lobe, diverticulum/large stones (transvesical preferred), small fibrous gland, prior pelvic surgery/radiation, prostate cancer.

### Steps
1. Pfannenstiel; extraperitoneal **space of Retzius**.
2. **Haemostatic sutures** across anterior capsule (Santorini plexus).
3. **Transverse capsulotomy** ~1–2 cm distal to bladder neck.
4. **Finger enucleation** in plane between adenoma and capsule.
5. **Divide urethra at apex under vision** (protects sphincter).
6. **Posterior bladder neck wedge excision** (prevents contracture).
7. Sutures at **5 and 7 o'clock**.
8. 20–22 Fr three-way catheter; **watertight capsule closure**; drain.

### Millin vs Freyer

| | **Millin** | **Freyer** |
|---|---|---|
| Bladder | Not opened | Opened |
| Apex | Under vision – better continence | Blind finger fracture |
| Haemostasis | Fossa visible | Less visible |
| Stones/diverticulum, median lobe | Difficult | Ideal |
| Catheter/stay | Shorter | Longer |
| Specific risk | Santorini bleeding, osteitis pubis | Bladder spasm, suprapubic fistula |

### Complications
- **Haemorrhage/transfusion**, clot retention, infection; **retrograde ejaculation** (most), bladder neck contracture, stricture, **osteitis pubis**.

### Modern alternatives
- **HoLEP** – size-independent, less bleeding; EAU (European Association of Urology) first choice >80 mL.
- **RASP (robotic-assisted simple prostatectomy)** – transvesical or Millin-type; less blood loss, shorter stay; allows diverticulectomy/stone removal.

## II-3d. Energy sources in laparoscopic urology
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Introduction
- Aims: cutting, coagulation, vessel sealing. ~60 °C – coagulation; ~100 °C – vaporization (cutting); >200 °C – carbonization.

### Types

| Device | Principle | Feature | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Monopolar** | RF (radiofrequency) current through patient to return pad; cut vs coag waveforms | Cheap, versatile | Greatest lateral spread; stray current |
| **Bipolar** | Current between jaws | Near nerves/bowel | Slow; no cutting |
| **Advanced bipolar** (LigaSure) | Pressure + impedance-controlled energy | Seals **up to 7 mm** | Cost; spread ~1–3 mm |
| **Ultrasonic** (Harmonic) | Blade ~55.5 kHz, <100 °C | Cut + seal **up to 5 mm**; no current through patient | Hot blade |
| **Hybrid** (Thunderbeat) | Ultrasonic + bipolar | Seals up to 7 mm | Cost |
| **Argon beam** | Monopolar via argon, non-contact | Partial nephrectomy bed | **Gas embolism** |
| **Lasers**, hydro-jet | Holmium, thulium; water jet | Endourology; partial nephrectomy | Smoke; not haemostatic |

### Mechanisms of electrosurgical injury
- Often unrecognised → delayed bowel perforation.
1. **Insulation failure** – current leaks.
2. **Direct coupling** – electrode touches another metal instrument.
3. **Capacitive coupling** – current induced through intact insulation; **avoid hybrid trocars**.
4. **Thermal spread** – ureter, nerves, bowel.
5. **Return electrode burns** – poor pad contact.

### Safety measures
- **AEM (active electrode monitoring)** – shuts generator on insulation failure/capacitive coupling.
- **REM (return electrode contact quality monitoring)**.
- Lowest power, brief activation, tip in view; bipolar near critical structures and with pacemakers.

### Recent advances
- Smart generators with real-time impedance feedback; robotic vessel sealers; ultrasonic sealing up to 7 mm.

**Diagram to draw:** Monopolar vs bipolar circuit; capacitive coupling across hybrid trocar.

## II-3e. Role of vascular grafts in urology
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Introduction
- Grafts replace, bypass or extend vessels when primary repair impossible – transplantation, extended oncological resections.

### Types

| Type | Examples | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Autologous vein** | Saphenous, gonadal | Low infection, small-vessel patency | Limited length |
| **Autologous artery** | Internal iliac, inferior epigastric, Carrel aortic patch | Renal artery reconstruction | Limited availability |
| **Synthetic** | **PTFE (polytetrafluoroethylene)**, **Dacron** | Off-the-shelf; IVC (inferior vena cava), aorta | Infection; not in contaminated field |
| **Biological** | Cadaveric vessels; bovine pericardium | Infected fields | Availability |

### Applications
1. **Renal transplantation** – multiple arteries: bench side-to-side conjoining or end-to-side implantation; short right vein → gonadal/saphenous/IVC extension; Carrel patch; synthetic aorto-iliac bypass for diseased recipient iliacs.
2. **Renovascular** – stenosis, **renal artery aneurysm**: aortorenal bypass (saphenous, hypogastric, PTFE); ex vivo bench repair.
3. **RCC (renal cell carcinoma) with IVC thrombus** – cavotomy + primary closure; **patch cavoplasty** if lumen narrowed >50%; **PTFE IVC replacement** if wall invaded and collaterals inadequate; infrarenal IVC often ligated if collaterals.
4. **Post-chemotherapy RPLND (retroperitoneal lymph node dissection)** – encased aorta → **Dacron/PTFE interposition graft**.
5. **Dialysis access** – PTFE AV (arteriovenous) graft.
6. **Vascular injury** – saphenous/PTFE interposition.

### Principles
- **Autologous** in contaminated/urine-exposed fields and small vessels; synthetic for large high-flow vessels.
- Tension-free, spatulated, intima-to-intima anastomosis; heparin during clamping; Doppler surveillance.
- Omental wrap isolates synthetic graft from urinary tract/bowel.

### Complications
- Thrombosis, stenosis, pseudoaneurysm, **graft infection** (often explantation), graft-enteric/urinary fistula.

### Recent advances
- Robotic IVC thrombectomy; human acellular vessel for dialysis access/trauma.

## II-3f. Transrectal ultrasonography
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Introduction
- Probe **6–10 MHz (commonly 7.5)**, **biplanar** (transverse + sagittal); ↑ frequency = ↑ resolution, ↓ penetration.
- Left lateral; DRE (digital rectal examination) first; transverse base→apex, then sagittal.

### Zonal anatomy (McNeal)
| Zone | Volume | Echo | Cancers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Peripheral | ~70% | Isoechoic | ~70% |
| Transition | ~5–10% | Hypoechoic | ~20–25% |
| Central | ~25% | Slightly hyperechoic | ~5–10% |

### Measurements
- **Volume = 0.52 × L × W × H** (length × width × height).
- **PSAD (PSA (prostate-specific antigen) density) >0.15** suspicious; used after MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) for PI-RADS (Prostate Imaging Reporting and Data System) 3.
- Cancer: **hypoechoic peripheral lesion** (low specificity).

### Indications
- **Biopsy guidance**; volume for BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia) surgery planning.
- **Ejaculatory duct obstruction**: seminal vesicles >1.5 cm AP (anteroposterior), midline cysts; vesiculography.
- Prostatic abscess drainage; **brachytherapy**, HIFU (high-intensity focused ultrasound), cryotherapy.

### TRUS (transrectal ultrasound) biopsy
- **12-core systematic** (base, mid, apex; medial + lateral) ± targeted; **periprostatic nerve block** (lidocaine at base–seminal vesicle junction).
- Complications: haematospermia (commonest), haematuria, rectal bleeding, **sepsis** (FQ (fluoroquinolone)-resistant E. coli).
- Prophylaxis: povidone-iodine rectal cleansing; FQ restricted.

### Current shift
- **EAU (European Association of Urology) 2024/25: transperineal biopsy recommended** – lower infection; prophylaxis may be omitted.
- **Pre-biopsy mpMRI (multiparametric MRI)** + targeted biopsy:
  - **PROMIS (2017)**: sensitivity for csPCa (clinically significant prostate cancer) 93% vs TRUS biopsy 48%.
  - **PRECISION (2018)**: MRI-targeted detected more csPCa (38% vs 26%), less insignificant cancer.

### Recent advances
- **Micro-ultrasound (29 MHz)** – OPTIMUM RCT (randomised controlled trial): non-inferior to MRI-targeted biopsy.
- Contrast-enhanced ultrasound, shear-wave elastography.

**Diagram to draw:** Transverse/sagittal zonal views; 12-core template.

- HoLEP and robotic simple prostatectomy are modern alternatives for >80–100 mL.

#### II-3d. Energy sources in laparoscopic urology (SAQ)

- LigaSure: vessels up to 7 mm; Harmonic: up to 5 mm at ~55.5 kHz.
- Stray current injury: insulation failure, direct coupling, capacitive coupling.
- Avoid hybrid trocars; use AEM and REM pads.
- Argon beam – risk of gas embolism.

#### II-3e. Role of vascular grafts in urology (SAQ)

- Autologous (saphenous, hypogastric, gonadal) for small vessels/contaminated fields; PTFE/Dacron for IVC/aorta.
- Transplant: Carrel patch, bench reconstruction, gonadal/IVC extension of short right vein.
- IVC: patch cavoplasty if narrowed; PTFE replacement if wall invaded.
- Post-chemo RPLND may need aortic graft replacement.

#### II-3f. Transrectal ultrasonography (SAQ)

- Biplanar probe 6–10 MHz; volume = 0.52 × L × W × H; PSAD >0.15 suspicious.
- 12-core systematic biopsy with periprostatic block.
- EAU: transperineal preferred (lower sepsis); FQ prophylaxis restricted.
- PROMIS and PRECISION established MRI-first pathway.
