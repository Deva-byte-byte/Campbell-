## Q1. Describe lymphatic system of urinary tract with reference to etiopathogenesis of chyluria.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)
(also answered in 2018-Summer Paper 1, Q1 and 2024-Winter Paper 3, Q2)

### General plan of the lymphatic system
- Lymph flows **cephalad and right to left** and returns to the left innominate (brachiocephalic) vein.
- Pelvic nodes (external iliac, internal iliac, obturator, presacral) drain to common iliac nodes, then to **lumbar nodes** and the lumbar trunks. Pelvic lymph crosses freely between the two sides.
- **Lumbar groups:** left lumbar (preaortic, left para-aortic, retroaortic), interaortocaval, and right lumbar (precaval, paracaval, retrocaval). They drain organs supplied by lateral aortic branches: kidneys, adrenals, ureters and gonads.
- Preaortic nodes (coeliac, superior and inferior mesenteric) form the **intestinal trunk**, which carries **chyle** from the intestinal lacteals.
- Lumbar and intestinal trunks join at the **cisterna chyli** (in front of L1–L2, behind the right side of the aorta). It continues as the **thoracic duct**, which rises behind the aorta to the left innominate vein.

### Lymphatic drainage of the urinary tract
| Organ | Intrinsic lymphatics | Regional nodes |
|---|---|---|
| **Kidney** | Superficial capsular and deep hilar networks along the interlobular, arcuate and interlobar arteries, to nodes at the renal vein | **Left:** left lateral para-aortic (sometimes retrocrural, or thoracic duct above the diaphragm). **Right:** interaortocaval and right paracaval (may cross to left para-aortic) |
| **Ureter** | Plexuses in the muscle and adventitia | Abdominal: left para-aortic (left); paracaval and interaortocaval (right). Middle: common iliac. Pelvic: common, external and internal iliac |
| **Bladder** | Begins in the lamina propria; a few perivesical nodes | Mainly **external iliac**; also internal iliac and obturator; cross-drainage to both sides |
| **Prostate** | Periprostatic plexus | Obturator and internal iliac; presacral; external iliac |
| **Urethra** | Submucosal plexus | Male prostatic and membranous: internal iliac, obturator; penile: inguinal, then external iliac. Female: proximal to pelvic, distal to inguinal nodes |

- **Applied:** upper tract cancers spread to lumbar nodes; bladder and prostate cancers to pelvic nodes. Renal drainage is asymmetric: right renal lymph can cross left; left stays left.
- Valves in the renal hilar lymphatics normally stop backflow from the cisterna chyli. **Failure of these valves is the basis of chyluria.**

### Chyluria: definition
- Passage of **chyle** (intestinal lymph rich in chylomicrons, triglyceride and protein) in urine, making it milky.
- It needs an abnormal **lymphourinary fistula**, usually at a renal calyceal fornix (**pyelolymphatic fistula**).

### Etiology
| Parasitic (commonest; India, China) | Non-parasitic |
|---|---|
| ***Wuchereria bancrofti*** (lymphatic filariasis; *Culex* mosquito) | Congenital lymphangiectasia, renal lymphangioma |
| *Brugia* (lymphatic filariasis that spares the genitals; rarely urological) | Trauma, surgery (partial nephrectomy, RPLND [retroperitoneal lymph node dissection]) |
|  | Retroperitoneal tumour or fibrosis, tuberculosis, pregnancy |

### Pathogenesis
- **Life cycle:** mosquito-borne larvae mature into adult worms that live for years in retroperitoneal and genital lymphatics. Females release microfilariae with **nocturnal periodicity**.
- **Obstructive (reflux) theory:**
```
Adult worms + host immune reaction -> lymphangitis and fibrosis
 -> obstruction between intestinal trunks/cisterna chyli and thoracic duct
 -> dilatation of retroperitoneal lymphatics, valve incompetence
 -> reflux of chyle into renal hilar and peripelvic lymphatics
 -> raised pressure ruptures thin-walled lymphatics into a calyceal fornix
 -> pyelolymphatic fistula -> chyluria
```
- The fistula is usually renal (hilar lymphatics lie against thin fornices); rarely ureteric or vesical.
- Chyluria appears **earlier than genital elephantiasis**, is usually **intermittent** and may remit spontaneously. It worsens after fatty meals, when lacteal flow is highest.
- **Non-parasitic causes** work the same way: lymphatic obstruction (tumour, fibrosis), direct disruption (surgery, trauma) or congenital lymphangiectasia.
- **Effects:** loss of fat, protein and lymphocytes causes weight loss, hypoproteinaemia and lowered immunity. Fibrin in chyle forms clots.

### Clinical features
- Milky urine after fatty meals; chylous clots (colic, retention); haematochyluria; weight loss, oedema. **Severity:** mild (intermittent), moderate (persistent, clots), severe (haematochyluria, hypoalbuminaemia).

### Evaluation
- **Ether test:** milky urine shaken with ether clears (fat dissolves); urine triglycerides positive. Exclude phosphaturia (clears with acetic acid).
- Night blood smear (often negative), filarial antigen test.
- **Cystoscopy after a fatty meal** lateralises the milky efflux; retrograde pyelography or **lymphoscintigraphy** shows the fistula.

### Management (outline)
```
Mild               -> low-fat, high-protein diet + MCT oil + DEC
Moderate/persistent -> endoscopic sclerotherapy (silver nitrate 0.5-1%, povidone-iodine)
Severe or failed   -> renal hilar lymphatic disconnection (open, laparoscopic, robotic)
```
- MCT (medium-chain triglyceride) is absorbed into portal blood, not lacteals, so less chyle forms. DEC (diethylcarbamazine) kills microfilariae.

**Diagram to draw:** retroperitoneal lymphatics with cisterna chyli, a block above it, and reflux to a pyelolymphatic fistula at a calyceal fornix.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Lymph flows right to left; cisterna chyli at L1–L2, behind the right side of the aorta.
> - Bladder drains mainly to external iliac nodes; left kidney to left para-aortic nodes.
> - Chyluria = pyelolymphatic fistula from lymphatic obstruction and valve failure, usually *W. bancrofti*.
> - Ether test clears milky urine; disconnection surgery for severe or refractory cases.

## Q2. Pathophysiology of Renovascular hypertension
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)
(also answered in 2022-Summer Paper 2, Q2, with treatment in detail)

### Definitions
- **RVH (renovascular hypertension):** hypertension caused by RAS (renal artery stenosis) and relieved by revascularisation or nephrectomy. RAS is common in older vascular patients; RVH is proved only by cure.
- **Ischaemic nephropathy:** loss of GFR (glomerular filtration rate) from RAS affecting the **whole renal mass** (bilateral RAS or stenosis to a solitary kidney).

### Causes
| | Atherosclerotic RAS | FMD (fibromuscular dysplasia) |
|---|---|---|
| Share | Most (about 90%) | Most of the rest |
| Patient | Older, smokers, diabetic, vascular disease | Young women |
| Site | **Ostium and proximal third** | **Mid and distal artery, branches**; "string of beads" (medial fibroplasia) |
| Course | Progresses to occlusion and renal loss | Rarely occludes |

**Also know:** Takayasu arteritis, neurofibromatosis, midaortic syndrome (children), aneurysm, dissection, embolism, transplant RAS, **Page kidney** (perinephric compression, e.g. subcapsular haematoma).

### The RAAS (renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system)
- **Renin** is released by juxtaglomerular cells of the afferent arteriole in response to:
  1. low afferent arteriolar pressure (intrarenal baroreceptor);
  2. low sodium chloride delivery to the **macula densa**;
  3. sympathetic **β1** stimulation.
- Renin cleaves hepatic **angiotensinogen** to angiotensin I. **ACE (angiotensin-converting enzyme)**, mainly in lung endothelium, converts it to **angiotensin II**.
- **Angiotensin II (AT1 receptor) actions:** systemic arteriolar vasoconstriction; **aldosterone** release (sodium retention, potassium loss); proximal tubular sodium reabsorption; thirst and ADH (antidiuretic hormone); sympathetic facilitation; **efferent arteriolar constriction**, which maintains GFR; cardiac and vascular hypertrophy and fibrosis.
- Normally, rising blood pressure (BP) and sodium suppress renin (negative feedback). In RVH the stenosed kidney "sees" low pressure, so this feedback fails.

### Goldblatt experiments
- **Goldblatt (1934)** clipped the canine renal artery and produced sustained hypertension, reversed by early clip removal.

| | **Two-kidney, one-clip (2K1C)** | **One-kidney, one-clip (1K1C)** |
|---|---|---|
| Human equivalent | Unilateral RAS, normal opposite kidney | Bilateral RAS or stenosed solitary kidney |
| Sodium | Normal kidney excretes extra sodium (**pressure natriuresis**) | No normal kidney: sodium and water retained |
| Renin | **High** from stenotic side; contralateral suppressed | Rises, then normalises as volume expands |
| Type | **Renin (angiotensin)-dependent** | **Volume-dependent** |
| ACE inhibitor effect | BP falls; captopril tests positive | Less BP effect; may cause **acute kidney injury** |

### Phases of 2K1C hypertension
1. **Acute phase:** renin-dependent; ACE inhibitor or clip removal reverses it.
2. **Transition:** sodium retention and secondary hyperaldosteronism; renin falls towards normal.
3. **Chronic phase:** hypertension damages the contralateral kidney (nephrosclerosis) and resistance vessels. BP no longer depends on the stenosis, so **revascularisation cures less**. This explains poorer results in long-standing hypertension.

### Other contributing mechanisms
- Renal afferent nerve signals raise **sympathetic activity**.
- Angiotensin II generates **oxidative stress** (reactive oxygen species), lowers **nitric oxide** and raises **endothelin**, worsening vasoconstriction.
- Inflammation and fibrosis; left ventricular hypertrophy and diastolic dysfunction.

### Pathophysiology of ischaemic nephropathy
- The kidney receives about **20–25% of cardiac output**, far more than its metabolic need. **Autoregulation** holds GFR until stenosis is severe (about 70% or more, or a significant translesional pressure gradient).
- Beyond this, GFR depends on angiotensin II-mediated efferent constriction. Blocking it (ACE inhibitor or ARB [angiotensin receptor blocker]) drops GFR: hence **creatinine rise >30%** after these drugs.
- Chronic hypoperfusion causes hypoxia, inflammation, microvascular rarefaction, **tubulointerstitial fibrosis** and atrophy. Atheroemboli and hypertensive injury add damage.
- Early disease is reversible ("hibernating" kidney); established fibrosis is not.
- **Flash pulmonary oedema:** bilateral RAS causes volume retention plus angiotensin II-driven diastolic dysfunction.

### Clinical clues
- Onset <30 years (FMD) or severe hypertension after 55; **resistant** (≥3 drugs) or accelerated hypertension; sudden loss of control.
- Flash pulmonary oedema; kidneys differing by >1.5 cm; abdominal bruit; **hypokalaemia** (secondary hyperaldosteronism).

### Investigations based on pathophysiology
- **Duplex Doppler:** high peak systolic velocity, renal/aortic ratio >3.5. **RI (resistive index) >0.8** means parenchymal disease and poor response.
- **CT (computed tomography) or MR (magnetic resonance) angiography** for anatomy; **catheter angiography** with translesional gradient is the gold standard.
- **Captopril renography:** removing angiotensin II drops GFR in the stenosed kidney (delayed uptake), proving renin-dependence.
- **Renal vein renin ratio >1.5** lateralises.

### Treatment implications (brief)
- **ACE inhibitor or ARB** treats renin-dependent RVH (monitor creatinine and potassium), plus statin, antiplatelet and smoking cessation.
- **ASTRAL (2009)** and **CORAL (2014):** stenting added no benefit over medical therapy in atherosclerotic RAS.
- **FMD:** balloon angioplasty without stent. Revascularise atherosclerotic RAS for flash pulmonary oedema, rapidly falling function or truly resistant hypertension.

**Diagram to draw:** RAAS cascade from renin release to angiotensin II actions, beside the 2K1C and 1K1C models.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Goldblatt 1934: 2K1C = renin-dependent; 1K1C = volume-dependent.
> - Chronic phase: contralateral nephrosclerosis makes RVH stenosis-independent.
> - Angiotensin II keeps GFR by efferent constriction; ACE inhibitor causes acute kidney injury in bilateral RAS.
> - ASTRAL and CORAL: no routine stenting; FMD = angioplasty alone.

## Q3a. Lymphocele
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition
- A collection of lymph **without an epithelial lining** (a pseudocyst with a fibrous wall), caused by divided lymphatics that keep leaking. It is the **commonest complication of PLND (pelvic lymph node dissection)**.

### Settings and incidence
- **PLND** (radical prostatectomy, cystectomy): radiological in up to 60%, clinically significant 2–5%. An extended template roughly doubles the rate.
- **Renal transplantation:** from lymphatics around the recipient iliac vessels or donor hilum. Radiological 0.6–33.9%; most appear within 6 months, peaking at 6 weeks.
- Also after RPLND (retroperitoneal lymph node dissection), inguinal dissection, nephrectomy.
- **Risk factors:** extensive dissection, obesity, heparin prophylaxis, mTOR (mammalian target of rapamycin) inhibitors, acute rejection, retransplantation.

### Clinical features
- Most are silent. Large ones compress:
  - iliac vein: leg oedema, **DVT (deep vein thrombosis)**;
  - bladder: frequency, urgency, pelvic pressure;
  - ureter or graft: hydronephrosis, rising creatinine.
- Infection: fever, rigors, sepsis.

### Diagnosis
- **Ultrasound:** anechoic, often septated, thin-walled collection. **CT (computed tomography)** maps it before intervention.
- **Aspirate:** lymph has creatinine equal to serum (urinoma: much higher), lymphocytes; culture excludes abscess.

### Management
| Situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Asymptomatic, <5 cm | Observe; most resolve |
| Symptomatic | **Percutaneous drain** ± sclerotherapy (povidone-iodine, doxycycline) |
| Recurrent or thick-walled | **Laparoscopic fenestration (marsupialisation)** into a peritoneal window |
| Infected | Antibiotics + percutaneous drainage; exclude urine leak |
| Laparoscopy unsafe | Open drainage |

- **Success:** laparoscopic fenestration best, open next, drain or sclerotherapy intermediate, **aspiration alone worst** (reaccumulates, risks infection).
- Fenestration lets the peritoneum absorb lymph (internal drainage): the definitive cure.
- **Lymphangiography with embolisation:** high success; long-term data awaited.

### Prevention
- Clip or ligate visible lymphatics (clips often preferred; no robust data). Spare lymphatics over the external iliac artery (leg lymph).
- **Peritoneal interposition flap** fixed to the bladder reduces clinically significant lymphocele. Transperitoneal access is **not** protective (fluid loculates within 48–72 h).
- Drain after transplantation and major nodal dissections.

**Diagram to draw:** pelvic lymphocele compressing the external iliac vein and bladder, with a laparoscopic peritoneal window.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Commonest PLND complication; radiological up to 60%, significant 2–5%.
> - Lymph creatinine = serum; urinoma creatinine higher.
> - Laparoscopic fenestration best; aspiration alone worst.

## Q3b. Docetaxel
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Introduction
- Semisynthetic **taxane** cytotoxic. Since **TAX 327 (2004)** it is the standard first-line chemotherapy for mCRPC (metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer).

### Mechanism of action
- Binds **β-tubulin** and **stabilises microtubules** (GTP [guanosine triphosphate]-independent polymerisation). Cells arrest in **G2/M**.
- Induces apoptosis independent of TP53, through **BCL2 phosphorylation** and p27 induction.
- Prostate-specific: blocks microtubule transport of ligand-bound **AR (androgen receptor)** into the nucleus.

### Dose and administration
- **75 mg/m² IV (intravenous) every 3 weeks with prednisone 5 mg twice daily**; 6 cycles in mHSPC (metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer). Steroid premedication limits fluid retention and hypersensitivity. Hepatic metabolism: caution in liver dysfunction.

### Uses in urology
| Setting | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **mCRPC** | **TAX 327:** overall survival (OS) 18.9 vs 16.4 months versus mitoxantrone (HR [hazard ratio] 0.76); better pain and PSA (prostate-specific antigen) response. SWOG 9916 (with estramustine) also positive |
| **mHSPC** | **CHAARTED:** OS 57.6 vs 44.0 months (HR 0.61), benefit in **high volume**. STAMPEDE: OS HR 0.78. GETUG-AFU 15 not significant |
| **Triplet** | ADT (androgen deprivation therapy) + docetaxel + abiraterone (**PEACE-1**) or darolutamide (**ARASENS**) improves OS |
| High-risk localised | RTOG 0521: added to RT (radiotherapy) + long-term ADT, OS HR 0.69 |
| Bladder | Second-line single agent (response about 13%); **intravesical gemcitabine–docetaxel** for BCG (bacillus Calmette–Guérin)-unresponsive or BCG-naive high-grade NMIBC (non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer) |
| Penis | **TPF** (docetaxel, cisplatin, 5-fluorouracil) |

**CHAARTED high volume:** ≥4 bone metastases with ≥1 outside the axial skeleton, or visceral metastasis.

### Toxicity
- **Neutropenia** and febrile neutropenia (dose-limiting), fatigue, **fluid retention** (peripheral oedema), **peripheral neuropathy**, hyperlacrimation, **nail dystrophy**, alopecia, liver dysfunction, hypersensitivity.
- 3-weekly: more neutropenia; weekly: more fatigue, diarrhoea, tearing.

### Resistance and sequencing
- P-glycoprotein efflux pump mediates resistance. **Cabazitaxel** has poor P-glycoprotein affinity, so it works after docetaxel (**TROPIC**; **CARD** beat a second ARPI [androgen receptor pathway inhibitor]).
- **Also know:** failed combinations (bevacizumab, atrasentan).

**Diagram to draw:** microtubule stabilised by docetaxel causing G2/M arrest and blocking AR entry into the nucleus.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - 75 mg/m² 3-weekly + prednisone; TAX 327 HR 0.76.
> - CHAARTED: benefit in high-volume mHSPC (HR 0.61).
> - Neutropenia, oedema, neuropathy, nail change; cabazitaxel after failure.

## Q3c. Cephalsporins
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

*Assumed meaning: cephalosporins (generations, spectrum, urological use).*

### Introduction and mechanism
- **β-lactam** antibiotics. They bind **PBPs (penicillin-binding proteins)** and block peptidoglycan cross-linking, so the cell wall fails: **bactericidal**, time-dependent killing.
- Mostly renal excretion (adjust dose in renal failure); **ceftriaxone** also has biliary excretion (no adjustment).
- Resistance: β-lactamases (**ESBL [extended-spectrum β-lactamase]**, AmpC), altered PBPs, reduced porin entry.

### Generations
| Generation | Examples | Spectrum | Urological use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **First** | Cefazolin (intravenous), cephalexin, cefadroxil (oral) | Gram-positive (not MRSA [methicillin-resistant *Staphylococcus aureus*]); *E. coli*, *Klebsiella*, *P. mirabilis* | **Surgical prophylaxis (cefazolin)**; cystitis in pregnancy; paediatric prophylaxis |
| **Second** | Cefuroxime, cefaclor; cefoxitin, cefotetan | Wider Gram-negative; cefoxitin and cefotetan add **anaerobes** | Prophylaxis for vaginal and bowel surgery; SWL (shock-wave lithotripsy) |
| **Third** | Ceftriaxone, cefotaxime, cefixime, cefpodoxime; **ceftazidime** | Most Enterobacterales; **only ceftazidime covers *Pseudomonas*** | Pyelonephritis, urosepsis; **gonorrhoea (ceftriaxone 500 mg IM [intramuscular])**; prostate biopsy prophylaxis |
| **Fourth** | Cefepime | Gram-negatives incl. *Pseudomonas*, stable to AmpC; Gram-positive | Complicated, nosocomial UTI (urinary tract infection) |
| **Fifth** | Ceftaroline | MRSA + Gram-negatives | Limited urological role |

- **Newer combinations** for complicated UTI with multidrug resistance: ceftazidime–avibactam, ceftolozane–tazobactam, cefiderocol.

### Gaps in spectrum
- **Enterococci** (intrinsically resistant; never treat with cephalosporins), *Listeria*, MRSA (except ceftaroline), atypical organisms.
- ESBL and AmpC producers hydrolyse third-generation agents.

### Adverse effects
- Hypersensitivity, less than penicillins. Cross-reactivity with third-generation agents is <1%; avoid after immediate (IgE [immunoglobulin E]-mediated) penicillin reaction.
- ***C. difficile* colitis**; "collateral damage" (selection of ESBL organisms).
- Positive Coombs test; bleeding with cefotetan and cefamandole.
- Ceftriaxone: biliary sludge; avoid in jaundiced neonates and with intravenous calcium.

### Paediatric use
- Febrile UTI in neonates: **ampicillin + third-generation cephalosporin** (covers enterococcus). Oral cefixime 8 mg/kg/day. A first-generation agent is a good narrow-spectrum choice for many outpatient UTIs.

**Diagram to draw:** β-lactam ring binding a PBP and blocking peptidoglycan cross-links.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Cefazolin = standard surgical prophylaxis.
> - Ceftriaxone misses *Pseudomonas*; ceftazidime and cefepime cover it.
> - No cephalosporin covers enterococci; ESBL hydrolyses third-generation agents.

## Q3d. USG in neonates
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

*Assumed meaning: ultrasonography (USG) of the neonatal urinary tract, including antenatal hydronephrosis follow-up.*

### Why ultrasound first
- No radiation or sedation; repeatable. Shows **anatomy, not function**.
- **Technique:** higher-frequency probes than in adults; kidneys supine and prone; length against age nomograms; bladder full and after voiding; Doppler.

### Normal neonatal kidney (pitfalls)
- Cortex **iso- or hyperechoic** to liver; **prominent hypoechoic pyramids mimic dilated calyces or cysts**.
- **Dilation is under-read in the first 48 hours** (physiological dehydration).

### Antenatal hydronephrosis follow-up
- **First scan after 48 hours**; earlier if solitary kidney or suspected PUV (posterior urethral valves).
- Normal postnatal **APD (anteroposterior pelvic diameter) <10 mm**, no calyceal or ureteric dilation.

| UTD (urinary tract dilation) risk | Findings | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **P1** (SFU [Society for Fetal Urology] 1–2) | APD 10–15 mm, central calyces | Repeat US at 3–6 months; no VCUG or prophylaxis |
| **P2** (SFU 3) | APD ≥15 mm, peripheral calyces or dilated ureter | Repeat US; VCUG (voiding cystourethrogram) and prophylaxis at discretion |
| **P3** (SFU 4) | Abnormal parenchyma or bladder | Repeat US at 1 month; VCUG and prophylaxis |

- **SFU grades:** 0 none; 1 pelvis only; 2 major calyces; 3 major and minor calyces; 4 grade 3 plus parenchymal thinning.

### Other neonatal indications
| Condition | Ultrasound finding |
|---|---|
| PUV | Thick bladder, **keyhole sign**, bilateral hydroureteronephrosis |
| MCDK (multicystic dysplastic kidney) | Non-communicating cysts of varying size, scant parenchyma |
| ARPKD (autosomal recessive polycystic kidney disease) | Large, bilaterally echogenic kidneys |
| Masses | Mesoblastic nephroma; adrenal haemorrhage; renal vein thrombosis (large kidney, hypertension) |
| Duplex anomalies | Ureterocele, ectopic ureter, megaureter |
| Spine | Occult dysraphism before arch ossification (up to 6 months) |
| DSD (differences of sex development) | Müllerian structures, gonads |

**Also know:** hydrometrocolpos, scrotal masses, contrast-enhanced voiding urosonography.

### Limitations
- Normal US (ultrasound) does not exclude VUR (vesicoureteral reflux). Function needs MAG3 (mercaptoacetyltriglycine) renography (after renal maturation) or DMSA (dimercaptosuccinic acid).

**Diagram to draw:** SFU grades 1–4 as kidney outlines.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - First postnatal scan after 48 h; normal APD <10 mm.
> - Echogenic cortex and prominent pyramids are normal in neonates.
> - Keyhole sign + thick bladder = PUV; spinal US only up to 6 months.

## Q3e. Interventional urological treatment in urology
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

*Assumed meaning: interventional radiology (image-guided percutaneous and endovascular) treatment in urology.*

### Principles
- Guidance by ultrasound, fluoroscopy or CT (computed tomography), usually under local anaesthesia. **Seldinger technique:** needle, guidewire, then devices over the wire.
- **Beforehand:** correct coagulopathy, manage anticoagulants, urine culture, antibiotic prophylaxis.

### Non-vascular procedures
| Procedure | Indications and points |
|---|---|
| **PCN (percutaneous nephrostomy)** | Obstructed infected kidney (as effective as retrograde stent), malignant ureteric obstruction, urinary leak or fistula, access for PCNL (percutaneous nephrolithotomy). Puncture a **posterior calyx** through the avascular Brödel plane. Transfusion 0.5–4% |
| **Antegrade stenting** | When retrograde stenting fails: malignant obstruction, ileal conduit, transplant ureter |
| Antegrade pyelography, Whitaker test | Define strictures; pressure–flow test for obstruction |
| **Percutaneous drainage** | Renal or perinephric abscess >3 cm, urinoma, lymphocele |
| Image-guided biopsy | Renal mass biopsy |

### Vascular (endovascular) procedures
| Procedure | Indications |
|---|---|
| **Renal angioembolisation** | Grade IV–V renal trauma; **pseudoaneurysm or arteriovenous fistula** after PCNL or partial nephrectomy (delayed bleed); bleeding AML (angiomyolipoma, first line); palliative embolisation of RCC (renal cell carcinoma) |
| **Internal iliac (anterior division) embolisation** | Intractable bladder haemorrhage; initial control about 90%. Spare the posterior division (gluteal ischaemia) |
| **PAE (prostate artery embolisation)** | LUTS (lower urinary tract symptoms) from BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia); EAU (European Association of Urology) for men accepting lesser outcomes; AUA (American Urological Association) 2023 may offer |
| **Varicocele embolisation** | Coils or liquid sclerosants via femoral or jugular vein |
| **High-flow priapism** | Superselective cavernosal artery embolisation; resolution ≥75%; recurrence 30–40% |
| **PTRA (percutaneous transluminal renal angioplasty)** | Fibromuscular dysplasia; transplant renal artery stenosis |

### Ablation
- **Cryoablation or RFA (radiofrequency ablation)**, percutaneous, for cT1a renal masses <3 cm; biopsy first. Higher local recurrence than partial nephrectomy, salvaged by repeat ablation.

### Complications
- Contrast nephropathy, access-site haematoma, **non-target embolisation**, **post-embolisation syndrome** (fever, pain, leucocytosis; self-limiting).

**Diagram to draw:** prone PCN through a posterior calyx along the Brödel line.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - PCN equals retrograde stent for decompressing an obstructed infected kidney.
> - Embolisation is first line for bleeding AML and post-PCNL pseudoaneurysm or arteriovenous fistula.
> - Internal iliac anterior division for bladder bleeding; spare posterior division.

## Q3f. Familial malignancy in urology
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in 2021-Summer Paper 2, Q3f and Practice Set 2 Paper 1, Q3b)

### Principles
- **Knudson two-hit hypothesis:** every cell carries one inherited mutant tumour-suppressor allele, so one more hit causes cancer. Tumours are **early, bilateral and multifocal**.
- **4–6% of RCC (renal cell carcinoma)** is familial. All familial RCC syndromes are **autosomal dominant**; only HPRC (hereditary papillary renal carcinoma) involves an activated oncogene.

### Hereditary kidney cancer syndromes
| Syndrome | Gene (chromosome) | Kidney tumour | Other features |
|---|---|---|---|
| **VHL** (von Hippel–Lindau) | VHL (3p25) | Clear cell, multifocal | Retinal angioma, CNS (central nervous system) haemangioblastoma, phaeochromocytoma |
| **HPRC** | MET (7q31) | Multiple bilateral papillary type 1 | None |
| **HLRCC** (hereditary leiomyomatosis and RCC) | FH (fumarate hydratase, 1q42–43) | **Aggressive** papillary, collecting duct | Skin and uterine leiomyomas |
| **BHD** (Birt–Hogg–Dubé) | FLCN (17p11.2) | Chromophobe, hybrid oncocytic | Fibrofolliculomas, lung cysts, pneumothorax |
| **TSC** (tuberous sclerosis complex) | TSC1 (9q34), TSC2 (16p13.3) | Multiple AMLs (angiomyolipomas); RCC 2–3% | Angiofibromas, cortical tubers |

- **Also know:** SDH (succinate dehydrogenase)-deficient RCC; BAP1; PTEN (Cowden).

### Kidney management
- **3 cm rule:** watch tumours <3 cm; nephron-sparing surgery at ≥3 cm (VHL, HPRC, BHD), saving function for repeat surgery. **Exception: HLRCC**: excise promptly, wide margins.
- TSC AML: everolimus if >3 cm. **Belzutifan** (HIF-2α [hypoxia-inducible factor 2α] inhibitor) is approved for VHL tumours.

### Other urological cancers
- **Prostate:** BRCA2, BRCA1, ATM, CHEK2, PALB2, HOXB13; father and brother affected: 9-fold risk. Earlier screening; **PARP (poly ADP-ribose polymerase) inhibitors**.
- **UTUC (upper tract urothelial carcinoma): Lynch syndrome** (MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2): about 14-fold risk, 75-fold with MSH2. Screen with MMR (mismatch repair) immunohistochemistry.
- **Testis:** affected brother RR (relative risk) 8–12, father 2–4.
- **Phaeochromocytoma:** about one-third hereditary (MEN-2 [multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2], VHL, NF1 [neurofibromatosis type 1], SDHB).
- **Also know:** Wilms tumour (WT1 in WAGR [Wilms, aniridia, genitourinary anomalies, retardation] and Denys–Drash; Beckwith–Wiedemann).

### Approach
- Young age, multifocality or family history: genetic counselling, germline testing, screen relatives.

**Diagram to draw:** VHL loss → HIF accumulation → VEGF (vascular endothelial growth factor) → angiogenesis.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - All familial RCC is autosomal dominant; 3 cm rule except HLRCC.
> - VHL 3p25, MET 7q31, FH 1q42, FLCN 17p11.
> - Lynch (MSH2) → UTUC; BRCA2 → aggressive prostate cancer.

#### Q1. Describe lymphatic system of urinary tract with reference to etiopathogenesis of chyluria. (LAQ)

- Lymph flows right to left; cisterna chyli at L1–L2, behind the right side of the aorta.
- Bladder drains mainly to external iliac nodes; left kidney to left para-aortic nodes.
- Chyluria = pyelolymphatic fistula from lymphatic obstruction and valve failure, usually *W. bancrofti*.
- Ether test clears milky urine; disconnection surgery for severe or refractory cases.

#### Q2. Pathophysiology of Renovascular hypertension (LAQ)

- Goldblatt 1934: 2K1C = renin-dependent; 1K1C = volume-dependent.
- Chronic phase: contralateral nephrosclerosis makes RVH stenosis-independent.
- Angiotensin II keeps GFR by efferent constriction; ACE inhibitor causes acute kidney injury in bilateral RAS.
- ASTRAL and CORAL: no routine stenting; FMD = angioplasty alone.

#### Q3a. Lymphocele (SAQ)

- Commonest PLND complication; radiological up to 60%, significant 2–5%.
- Lymph creatinine = serum; urinoma creatinine higher.
- Laparoscopic fenestration best; aspiration alone worst.

#### Q3b. Docetaxel (SAQ)

- 75 mg/m² 3-weekly + prednisone; TAX 327 HR 0.76.
- CHAARTED: benefit in high-volume mHSPC (HR 0.61).
- Neutropenia, oedema, neuropathy, nail change; cabazitaxel after failure.

#### Q3c. Cephalsporins (SAQ)

- Cefazolin = standard surgical prophylaxis.
- Ceftriaxone misses *Pseudomonas*; ceftazidime and cefepime cover it.
- No cephalosporin covers enterococci; ESBL hydrolyses third-generation agents.

#### Q3d. USG in neonates (SAQ)

- First postnatal scan after 48 h; normal APD <10 mm.
- Echogenic cortex and prominent pyramids are normal in neonates.
- Keyhole sign + thick bladder = PUV; spinal US only up to 6 months.

#### Q3e. Interventional urological treatment in urology (SAQ)

- PCN equals retrograde stent for decompressing an obstructed infected kidney.
- Embolisation is first line for bleeding AML and post-PCNL pseudoaneurysm or arteriovenous fistula.
- Internal iliac anterior division for bladder bleeding; spare posterior division.
