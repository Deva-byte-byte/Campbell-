## Q1. Describe anatomy of pelvic floor in females. Discuss fertility sparing treatment options for procedentia.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)
(pelvic floor anatomy also answered in 2017-Winter Paper 3, Q2 and 2015-Summer Paper 1, Q2)

*"Procedentia" is read as procidentia (complete uterine prolapse); "fertility sparing" means uterus-preserving treatment for a woman who wants future pregnancy.*

### A. Anatomy of the female pelvic floor
Support comes from the **levator ani**, the **endopelvic fascia and ligaments**, and the **perineal membrane and perineal body**, anchored to the bony pelvis.

**1. Pelvic diaphragm**

| Muscle | Attachment | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Puborectalis | Back of pubis, loops behind anorectal junction | Anorectal angle |
| Pubococcygeus | Pubis and arcus tendineus to viscera and anococcygeal raphe | Slings urethra and vagina |
| Iliococcygeus | Arcus tendineus and ischial spine to coccyx | Horizontal **levator plate** |
| Coccygeus (not levator) | Ischial spine to sacrum and coccyx | Lies on sacrospinous ligament |

- Nerve to levator ani **(S3–S5)**; **pudendal nerve (S2–S4)** to sphincters and perineal muscles.
- Tonic contraction closes the **urogenital hiatus**; the uterus and upper vagina rest on the levator plate.

**2. Perineal membrane and perineal body**
- The membrane spans the pubic arch, pierced by urethra and vagina; above it lies the striated urogenital sphincter. The perineal body anchors the distal vagina.

**3. Ligaments and surgical anchors**
- **Cardinal ligament** (side wall to cervix; carries the uterine artery) and **uterosacral ligament** (sacrum to cervix). The ureter is 4 cm from the uterosacral ligament near the sacrum but 0.9 cm near the cervix.
- Pubocervical fascia attaches to the **ATFP (arcus tendineus fasciae pelvis)**, pubis to ischial spine.
- **Sacrospinous ligament (SSL)**, 7–8 cm, ischial spine to sacrum: the pudendal nerve and vessels pass behind the spine, the sacral plexus just posterior.
- **Sacral promontory**: left common iliac vein and right ureter each about 2.7 cm away.

**4. DeLancey levels (1992)**

| Level | Structures | Loss produces |
|---|---|---|
| I suspension | Cardinal–uterosacral complex | **Uterine or vault prolapse**, enterocele |
| II attachment | Paravaginal attachment to ATFP | Anterior or posterior wall prolapse |
| III fusion | Perineal membrane, perineal body | Urethral hypermobility, distal rectocele |

**5. Why prolapse occurs**
- Muscle fails first (birth trauma, levator avulsion, denervation). The hiatus widens, the ligaments carry the whole load and stretch. Collagen disorders (Ehlers–Danlos, Marfan) add risk; also vaginal birth, forceps, age, obesity, constipation.

**Diagram to draw:** sagittal female pelvis with levator plate and DeLancey levels I–III.

### B. Procidentia: definition and assessment
- **Procidentia**: the uterus lies wholly outside the hymen, **POP-Q (pelvic organ prolapse quantification) stage 4**.
- Assess all compartments, occult SUI (stress urinary incontinence) with prolapse reduced, PVR (post-void residual), hydronephrosis, cervical length and ulceration. Abnormal bleeding needs gynaecological evaluation first.

### C. Fertility-sparing options
**Principle:** restore **level I** support while keeping uterus and cervix. Defer definitive surgery until the family is complete where possible, because pregnancy and delivery cause recurrence.

**1. Conservative (first line)**
- PFMT (pelvic floor muscle training), weight loss, treat constipation.
- **Pessary**: space-filling (Gellhorn, cube) for procidentia; removed for conception; review about 6-monthly for discharge and erosion.

**2. Uterus-preserving surgery**

| Operation | Technique | Fertility points |
|---|---|---|
| **Sacrospinous hysteropexy** (vaginal) | Cervix sutured to the SSL **1.5–3 cm medial to the ischial spine**, into the ligament | Non-inferior to vaginal hysterectomy with uterosacral suspension (SAVE U trial); buttock pain, pudendal injury |
| **Uterosacral hysteropexy** (vaginal or laparoscopic) | Shorten and reattach uterosacral ligaments to cervix, sutures lateral to medial | No mesh; cystoscopy for ureteric kinking |
| **Sacrohysteropexy** (open, laparoscopic, robotic) | Mesh from posterior cervix to anterior longitudinal ligament over the promontory | Most durable apical support; later pregnancy usually by caesarean |
| **Manchester (Fothergill)** | Cervical amputation, cardinal ligaments sutured in front of the stump, anterior colporrhaphy | Suits elongated cervix, but risks **cervical stenosis, incompetence, miscarriage, preterm labour**: avoid if pregnancy wanted |

- **Also know:** Indian abdominal slings for nulliparous prolapse (Shirodkar, Purandare, Khanna, Virkud). **Le Fort colpocleisis** keeps the uterus but not fertility (elderly only).

**3. Choosing**
```
Procidentia, wants pregnancy -> PFMT + pessary
  -> fails -> sacrospinous hysteropexy or
     laparoscopic sacrohysteropexy (avoid Manchester)
  -> recurrence after childbearing -> hysterectomy
     + vault suspension or sacrocolpopexy
```

### D. Complications and follow-up
- Recurrence (mostly posterior), de novo SUI, mesh exposure (3.4–5.4% after sacrocolpopexy), ureteric kinking. The retained uterus needs cervical screening.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Levator ani = puborectalis, pubococcygeus, iliococcygeus; S3–S5.
> - DeLancey I (uterosacral–cardinal) failure causes uterine prolapse.
> - Pessary first; sacrospinous hysteropexy 1.5–3 cm medial to the ischial spine.
> - Manchester amputates the cervix: poor choice for future pregnancy.

## Q2. Describe embryology & clinical implications of IVC anomalous development.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)
(retrocaval ureter also answered in 2017-Winter Paper 3, Q3d)

### Introduction
- The **IVC (inferior vena cava)** forms between the 6th and 8th weeks from three pairs of fetal veins by anastomosis, regression and persistence. The final IVC is **right-sided**, so long left veins must cross the aorta to reach it.
- Most anomalies are silent CT (computed tomography) findings. They **bleed at surgery, mimic lymph nodes, trap the ureter and alter IVC filter placement**.

### Embryology
**Three paired venous systems**

| Vein | Position | Adult derivatives |
|---|---|---|
| **Posterior cardinal** (first) | Dorsolateral | Iliac confluence; mostly regresses |
| **Subcardinal** | Ventromedial | Right: **suprarenal IVC**; intersubcardinal anastomosis: **left renal vein**; left: adrenal and gonadal veins |
| **Supracardinal** (last) | Dorsomedial | Right: **infrarenal IVC**; thoracic parts: **azygos and hemiazygos** |

**Segments of the definitive IVC (top to bottom)**
1. **Hepatic**: right vitelline vein.
2. **Suprarenal**: right subcardinal vein (via the subcardinal–hepatic anastomosis).
3. **Renal**: subcardinal–supracardinal anastomosis.
4. **Infrarenal**: right supracardinal vein.
5. **Iliac confluence**: posterior cardinal veins.

**Renal collar**
- A venous ring encircles the aorta at renal level. The **ventral (pre-aortic) limb** persists as the left renal vein; the **dorsal (retro-aortic) limb** regresses.
- The right supracardinal vein lies **dorsal** to the ureter, so the normal ureter lies in front of the IVC.

```
Left-sided channels regress -> right-sided persist
  -> single right IVC + pre-aortic left renal vein
```

**Diagram to draw:** three stages: paired cardinal veins, anastomoses with renal collar, definitive IVC.

### Anomalies

| Anomaly | Embryological error | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| **Retrocaval ureter** ("preureteral vena cava") | Infrarenal IVC forms from a vein **ventral** to the ureter | Right only; 1 in 1500 autopsies; male 3:1; presents 3rd–4th decade. **Type I** low loop, fishhook, obstructs; **type II** high loop, sickle, mild |
| **Circumaortic left renal vein** | Dorsal limb persists with ventral | Commonest (2–16%); posterior limb runs lower into lumbar IVC |
| **Retroaortic left renal vein** | Ventral limb regresses, dorsal persists | Less common; may be compressed ("posterior nutcracker") |
| **Left IVC** | Left supracardinal persists, right regresses | Crosses to the right at renal level, in front of the aorta |
| **Duplicated IVC** | Both supracardinals persist | Left IVC joins left renal vein; double right cava can trap the ureter |
| **Azygos continuation** | Subcardinal–hepatic anastomosis fails | Large azygos; hepatic veins drain directly to right atrium; polysplenia, heterotaxy |
| **Absent infrarenal IVC** | Supracardinal and posterior cardinal failure | Deep vein thrombosis in the young |

- For retrocaval ureter Campbell names a persistent **lumbar subcardinal** (Chapter 52) or **posterior cardinal** vein (Chapter 93, most texts); both mean a ventral vein forms the IVC.

### Clinical implications
**1. Diagnosis**
- Venous-phase CT or MRI (magnetic resonance imaging). A left or duplicated IVC mimics **para-aortic lymphadenopathy**; a large azygos mimics a mediastinal mass, risking needless biopsy.
- Retrocaval ureter: S-shaped or fishhook ureter at L3–L4 on retrograde pyelography; CT urography confirms; diuretic renography grades obstruction.

**2. RPLND (retroperitoneal lymph node dissection)**
- Preoperative CT must look for circumaortic or retroaortic veins and retrocaval ureter. A left or duplicated IVC lies in the left template; a retroaortic limb tears when lumbar vessels are divided.

**3. Radical nephrectomy**
- The hidden posterior limb of a circumaortic vein bleeds when the artery is approached from behind: ligate both limbs. Caval thrombus in a duplicated IVC needs rerouting of the left IVC.

**4. Living donor nephrectomy**
- The left kidney is preferred for its long vein. A circumaortic or retroaortic vein shortens it and adds bleeding risk; CT angiography is mandatory, and the right kidney may be chosen.

**5. Retrocaval ureter repair**
- Observe non-obstructed type II. For obstruction, **divide the ureter, transpose it in front of the IVC and re-anastomose** over a stent, excising the aperistaltic segment; laparoscopic or robotic. A pyeloplasty that misses it fails.

**6. Interventional and vascular**
- **IVC filter:** with duplication, emboli bypass a single filter, so filter both cavae or place it suprarenally.
- Aortic surgery risks retroaortic vein injury. A retroaortic vein can cause haematuria or left varicocele.
- **Also know:** variable left adrenal vein entry (adrenal vein sampling); right gonadal vein into right renal vein in up to 10%.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Infrarenal IVC = right supracardinal; suprarenal = right subcardinal; hepatic = vitelline.
> - Circumaortic left renal vein is commonest (2–16%): ligate both limbs.
> - Retrocaval ureter: right only, 1 in 1500; transpose anterior to IVC.
> - Duplicated IVC: filter both cavae; do not mistake it for nodes.

## Q3a. Patient positioning in lap urology
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(pneumoperitoneum physiology also answered in 2019-Summer Paper 1, Q2)

### Principles
- Positioning uses **gravity** to move bowel away and gives port or robot access while keeping ventilation safe.
- Injury comes from **stretch, compression or ischaemia**, so position and duration act together; nerve injury occurs in about 2.8%. Shared team responsibility (ASA [American Society of Anesthesiologists] advisory, 2000).
- Secure IV (intravenous) access, oximetry and BP (blood pressure) cuff before final positioning: tucked arms become inaccessible.

### Positions

| Position | Used for | Set-up | Hazards |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Steep Trendelenburg**, low lithotomy or split legs | RARP (robot-assisted radical prostatectomy), cystectomy | Arms tucked and padded; chest strap over egg-crate; **no shoulder braces**; tilt about 22–30° | Brachial plexus; femoral nerve (excess hip flexion); facial and laryngeal oedema; corneal abrasion; tube into right bronchus |
| **Modified flank** (30–45°) | Transperitoneal nephrectomy, pyeloplasty | Back on gel rolls; upper arm across chest; wide tape; tilt to test | Pressure points, slippage |
| **Full lateral (90°)**, table flexed | Retroperitoneoscopy, adrenalectomy | **Axillary roll** 3–4 fingerbreadths below axilla; lower leg flexed 45°, upper straight on pillows | **Rhabdomyolysis**; sciatic stretch |
| **Prone** | Posterior adrenalectomy | Pad torso, elbows, hips | Airway, face and eyes |

### Rhabdomyolysis
- About 0.4% (1% retroperitoneal). Risk: men, BMI (body mass index) ≥25, **kidney rest**, full flexion, long cases. Downside hip pain, brown urine, CK (creatine kinase) >5,000 units. Prevent: limit kidney rest (Campbell gives 20–30 minutes in one place, under 1 hour in another), avoid hypotension, keep cases under 5 hours.

### Prevention and physiology
- Abduction ≤90°; pad ulnar groove and fibular head; recheck after every table move.
- **Head-down** raises cardiac output but splints the diaphragm; restrict fluids.
- Nerve injury on day 1: neurology review, conduction studies, physiotherapy.
- **Also know:** forearm compartment syndrome from infiltrated IV lines; Xi side-docking avoids stirrups.

**Diagram to draw:** full flank position with axillary roll and table break.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Nerve injury about 2.8%; brachial plexus is commonest at risk.
> - Never shoulder braces in steep Trendelenburg.
> - Axillary roll below, not in, the axilla.
> - Rhabdomyolysis: men, BMI ≥25, kidney rest; CK >5,000.

## Q3b. Familial malignancies in Urology.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in 2021-Summer Paper 2, Q3f)

### Principles
- **Knudson two-hit hypothesis:** every cell carries one inherited mutant tumour-suppressor allele, so one more hit causes cancer: tumours are **early, bilateral, multifocal**.
- **4–6% of RCC (renal cell carcinoma)** is familial. All familial RCC syndromes are **autosomal dominant**; only HPRC (hereditary papillary renal carcinoma) involves an activated oncogene.

### Hereditary kidney cancer syndromes

| Syndrome | Gene (chromosome) | Kidney tumour | Other features |
|---|---|---|---|
| **VHL** (von Hippel–Lindau) | VHL (3p25) | Clear cell, multifocal | Retinal angioma, CNS (central nervous system) haemangioblastoma, phaeochromocytoma |
| **HPRC** | MET (7q31) | Multiple bilateral papillary | None |
| **HLRCC** (hereditary leiomyomatosis and RCC) | FH (fumarate hydratase, 1q42–43) | **Aggressive** papillary, collecting duct | Skin and uterine leiomyomas |
| **BHD** (Birt–Hogg–Dubé) | FLCN (17p11.2) | Chromophobe, hybrid oncocytic | Fibrofolliculomas, lung cysts, pneumothorax |
| **TSC** (tuberous sclerosis complex) | TSC1 (9q34), TSC2 (16p13.3) | Multiple AMLs (angiomyolipomas); RCC 2–3% | Angiofibromas, cortical tubers |

- **Also know:** SDH (succinate dehydrogenase)-deficient RCC; BAP1 (uveal melanoma, mesothelioma); PTEN (Cowden).

### Kidney management
- **3 cm rule:** watch tumours <3 cm; nephron-sparing surgery at ≥3 cm (VHL, HPRC, BHD) to save function for repeat surgery. **Exception: HLRCC**: excise promptly, wide margins.
- TSC AML: everolimus at 3–5 cm; embolise or excise ≥5 cm. **Belzutifan** (HIF-2α [hypoxia-inducible factor 2α] inhibitor) approved 2021 for VHL.

### Other urological cancers
- **Prostate:** BRCA2, BRCA1, ATM, CHEK2, PALB2, HOXB13. Father and brother affected: 9-fold risk. Earlier screening, cautious active surveillance, **PARP (poly ADP-ribose polymerase) inhibitors**.
- **UTUC (upper tract urothelial carcinoma): Lynch syndrome** (MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2): about 14-fold risk, 75-fold with MSH2. MMR (mismatch repair) immunohistochemistry, then germline testing.
- **Testis:** affected brother RR (relative risk) 8–12, father 2–4.
- **Phaeochromocytoma:** about one-third hereditary: MEN-2 (multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2; RET), VHL, NF1 (neurofibromatosis type 1), SDHB (highest malignancy).
- **Also know:** Wilms tumour (WT1: WAGR [Wilms, aniridia, genitourinary anomalies, retardation] and Denys–Drash; Beckwith–Wiedemann).

### Approach
- Young age, multifocality or family history: genetic counselling, germline testing, screen relatives.

**Diagram to draw:** VHL loss → HIF accumulation → VEGF (vascular endothelial growth factor) → angiogenesis.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - All familial RCC is autosomal dominant; 3 cm rule except HLRCC.
> - VHL 3p25, MET 7q31, FH 1q42, FLCN 17p11.
> - Lynch (MSH2) → UTUC; BRCA2 → aggressive prostate cancer.

## Q3c. Lymphogranuloma venereum
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition
- **LGV (lymphogranuloma venereum)** is an invasive, lymphotropic STD (sexually transmitted disease) caused by ***Chlamydia trachomatis* serovars L1, L2 and L3**.
- Of 15 serovars, A–C cause trachoma and D–K anogenital infection. The L serovars invade lymphatics, causing lymphadenitis and scarring.

### Epidemiology
- Endemic in the tropics, including India; increasing as **proctitis in MSM (men who have sex with men)**. Not more frequent or virulent in HIV (human immunodeficiency virus).

### Clinical stages

| Stage | Timing | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | 3–30 days | Small painless papule or ulcer, often unnoticed. Rectal inoculation → **proctitis** (bleeding, pain, tenesmus, discharge), now the commonest presentation |
| **Secondary (inguinal syndrome)** | 2–6 weeks later | Fever, malaise; **painful, matted, unilateral inguinal or femoral buboes** that may form fistulae. **Groove sign**: nodes above and below the inguinal ligament |
| **Tertiary** | Untreated | Fibrosis and lymphatic block: **genital elephantiasis** (esthiomene in women), perirectal abscess, fistulae, **rectal stricture** |

- Inguinal disease is commoner in men: the cervix and vagina drain to pelvic nodes.

### Differential diagnosis of genital ulcer
- Syphilis (painless ulcer and nodes); herpes (painful vesicles); chancroid (painful ulcer, suppurative nodes); donovanosis (beefy ulcer, no nodes); **LGV: transient painless lesion, then painful matted nodes**.

### Investigations
- **NAAT (nucleic acid amplification test)** of ulcer, rectal or node aspirate; LGV-specific genotyping confirms where available. Rectal chlamydia NAAT in all proctocolitis.
- Serology not routine. Screen for HIV, syphilis, gonorrhoea; offer PrEP (pre-exposure prophylaxis).

### Treatment
- **Doxycycline 100 mg twice daily for 21 days** (first line).
- Alternatives: erythromycin 500 mg four times daily for 21 days, or azithromycin 1 g weekly for 3 weeks (pregnancy; consider test of cure by NAAT).
- Fluctuant buboes: aspirate through healthy skin to prevent sinus formation.
- **Late disease:** stricture dilatation or resection, fistula repair, reduction of elephantiasis.

**Diagram to draw:** groin with buboes above and below the inguinal ligament (groove sign).

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Serovars L1–L3; invasive and lymphotropic.
> - Proctitis is now the commonest presentation.
> - Groove sign; esthiomene; rectal stricture.
> - Doxycycline 100 mg twice daily for 21 days.

## Q3d. Isotope studies of urology
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(renal scans in depth: see Practice Set 2 Paper 1, Q3e; diuretic renography: see Practice Set 2 Paper 1, Q3f)

### Principles
- Nuclear medicine shows **function, not structure**: a tracer in tiny dose follows a physiological or molecular process.
- **Gamma camera** (sodium iodide crystal, photomultipliers, collimator): planar (about 20 mm resolution) or **SPECT (single-photon emission computed tomography)** with CT (about 10 mm).
- **PET (positron emission tomography):** positron annihilation gives two 511 keV photons 180° apart, detected in coincidence: sharper (about 5 mm), less noise, quantitative SUV (standardised uptake value).
- **⁹⁹ᵐTc (technetium-99m):** 6-hour half-life, 140 keV photon: the workhorse. ¹⁸F: 110 minutes, sharpest PET image.

### Applications

| Area | Tracer | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Kidney** | ⁹⁹ᵐTc-DTPA (diethylenetriaminepentaacetic acid), -MAG3 (mercaptoacetyltriglycine), -DMSA (dimercaptosuccinic acid) | GFR (glomerular filtration rate), split function, obstruction, scars, transplant |
| **Bone** | ⁹⁹ᵐTc-MDP (methylene diphosphonate) | High-risk prostate cancer staging; low specificity |
| **Prostate** | **PSMA (prostate-specific membrane antigen) PET**; choline; fluciclovine | PSMA preferred (NCCN [National Comprehensive Cancer Network]) for staging and recurrence; specificity >90% |
| **Therapy** | ¹⁷⁷Lu-PSMA-617; radium-223 | mCRPC (metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer); VISION trial (2021) |
| **FDG (fluorodeoxyglucose) PET** | Glucose analogue trapped in cells | **Residual seminoma >3 cm**; penile cancer with palpable nodes; distant urothelial disease. Poor in prostate and clear cell RCC (renal cell carcinoma) |
| **Renal mass** | Sestamibi SPECT; girentuximab PET | Oncocytoma "hot" on sestamibi; clear cell RCC "hot" on girentuximab |
| **Adrenal** | ¹²³I-MIBG (metaiodobenzylguanidine), ⁶⁸Ga-DOTATATE, FDG | Metastatic or extra-adrenal phaeochromocytoma; MIBG best in MEN-2 (multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2) |
| **Sentinel node** | ⁹⁹ᵐTc-nanocolloid plus blue dye | Penile cancer, impalpable nodes |
| **Scrotum** | ⁹⁹ᵐTc-pertechnetate | Torsion cold, epididymitis hot |
| **Reflux** | Direct radionuclide cystography | VUR (vesicoureteral reflux) follow-up: more sensitive, lower dose than VCUG (voiding cystourethrogram) |
| **Infection** | ¹¹¹In-labelled leucocytes; ⁶⁷Ga | Occult abscess; not in transplant |

**Diagram to draw:** gamma camera chain: patient, collimator, crystal, photomultiplier, image.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - ⁹⁹ᵐTc: 6 hours, 140 keV; PET: two 511 keV photons.
> - DTPA filtered, MAG3 secreted, DMSA cortical.
> - PSMA PET preferred; FDG for residual seminoma >3 cm.
> - MIBG best in MEN-2; sentinel node with ⁹⁹ᵐTc-nanocolloid.

## Q3e. Renal isotope scans
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in 2017-Winter Paper 2, Q3f and 2021-Summer Paper 1, Q3e; diuretic protocol: see Practice Set 2 Paper 1, Q3f)

### Principle
- Each tracer images the part of the nephron that handles it. Plasma clearance is about 20% filtration and 80% tubular secretion.

### Tracers

| Feature | ⁹⁹ᵐTc-DTPA (diethylenetriaminepentaacetic acid) | ⁹⁹ᵐTc-MAG3 (mercaptoacetyltriglycine) | ⁹⁹ᵐTc-DMSA (dimercaptosuccinic acid) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Handling | Glomerular filtration only; poor images in renal failure | Protein-bound; tubular secretion; good images in renal failure | Filtered, then retained by proximal tubules |
| Study | Dynamic | Dynamic | **Static cortical**, 2 hours |
| Main use | **GFR (glomerular filtration rate)**; captopril study | **Split function, obstruction (first choice)**; transplant | **Pyelonephritis and scars** |
| Dose | 5–10 mCi | 5–10 mCi | 5 mCi adult; 50 µCi/kg child |

### Dynamic renography: method
- Hydrate; catheter for outlet obstruction; cap nephrostomy; IV (intravenous) bolus.
- **Perfusion phase:** frames every 1–2 seconds; kidney appears within seconds of the aorta.
- **Function phase:** regions of interest with background subtraction. **Split function = area under each curve at 1–3 minutes**; normal up to 45:55.
- **Drainage phase:** MAG3 peaks at 3–5 minutes, bladder by 10–15 minutes; a rising curve needs furosemide (see Q3f).
- **GFR:** DTPA camera method (Gates) or plasma-sample clearance.

### DMSA cortical scan

| | Acute pyelonephritis | Scar |
|---|---|---|
| Defect | Ill-defined | Sharp, cortical thinning |
| Kidney | Normal size | Often small |
| At 6 months | Resolves | Persists |

### Special studies
- **Captopril renography (DTPA):** the stenotic kidney keeps GFR by angiotensin II efferent constriction; captopril removes it, so uptake falls. Repeat off ACE (angiotensin-converting enzyme) inhibitors: improvement **≥10%** means renovascular hypertension likely. Largely replaced by CT (computed tomography) angiography.
- **Transplant (MAG3):** acute rejection: perfusion falls and worsens; delayed graft function: perfusion kept, improves. Shows urine leak.

### Advantages and limits
- Quantitative, low radiation, safe in contrast allergy and pregnancy (MAG3 fetal dose <5 mGy); poor anatomy.

**Diagram to draw:** renogram: perfusion spike, peak at 3–5 minutes, washout, 1–3 minute split window.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - DTPA = GFR; MAG3 = obstruction and transplant; DMSA = scars.
> - Split function from 1–3 minute counts; normal 45–55%.
> - Captopril renogram: ≥10% improvement off ACE inhibitors.
> - Scar persists at 6 months; pyelonephritis resolves.

## Q3f. Diureteric renography
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(PUJ obstruction renography also in 2021-Summer Paper 1, Q3e; tracers: see Practice Set 2 Paper 1, Q3e)

*"Diureteric" is read as diuretic renography.*

### Principle
- **Dilatation is not obstruction.** A capacious pelvis retains tracer passively and furosemide washes it out; an obstructed system cannot empty even under diuresis.
- **MAG3 (technetium-99m mercaptoacetyltriglycine)** is the tracer of choice (good curves even with poor function); DTPA (diethylenetriaminepentaacetic acid) is an alternative.

### "Well-tempered" protocol

| Element | How | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Hydration** | Oral; IV (intravenous) fluid in children (about 15 mL/kg) | Dehydration mimics obstruction |
| **Bladder catheter** | Urethral catheter | Removes full-bladder back-pressure and VUR (vesicoureteral reflux) effects |
| **Furosemide** | 0.5 mg/kg, adult maximum 40 mg; 1 mg/kg in children | Maximal diuresis |
| **Timing** | **F+20** (classic, pelvis full); F+15 (Campbell authors); **F0** (with tracer); **F−15** (already in diuresis) | Early diuretic into an unfilled system misleads; always state timing |
| **Regions of interest** | Cortex for split; dilated pelvis for drainage | Wrong placement gives spurious values |

- First study after about 4–6 weeks of age (immature function).

### Outputs
- **DRF (differential renal function)** from 1–3 minute counts: normal 45–55%.
- **T½ (half-clearance time)**, from maximal filling, not from injection:

| T½ after furosemide | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| <10 minutes | Not obstructed |
| 10–20 minutes | Indeterminate |
| >20 minutes | Obstructed |

- **O'Reilly curves:** I normal; II obstructed (rising); IIIa dilated, not obstructed (falls after furosemide); IIIb equivocal. A **biphasic curve** and poor upright (post-void) drainage predict progression.

### Indications
- PUJ (pelviureteric junction) obstruction, antenatal hydronephrosis, megaureter, after pyeloplasty, transplant ureter.
- Consider surgery for **DRF <40%**, falling DRF, T½ >20 minutes or upright retention, with symptoms.

### Pitfalls
- Dehydration, full bladder, poor function, very capacious pelvis, wrong ROI (region of interest), mistimed diuretic.
- No agreed paediatric T½ cut-off; neonatal false-positive rates reach 44%, so **serial trends and curve shape** decide. Equivocal: Whitaker test or MRU (magnetic resonance urography).

**Diagram to draw:** O'Reilly curves I, II, IIIa and IIIb with furosemide arrow.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Well-tempered: hydrate, catheter, standard diuretic dose and timing.
> - Furosemide 0.5 mg/kg (adult maximum 40 mg); 1 mg/kg in children.
> - T½ <10 not obstructed; 10–20 indeterminate; >20 obstructed.
> - Surgery: DRF <40%, falling DRF, or symptoms.

#### Q2. Describe embryology & clinical implications of IVC anomalous development. (LAQ)

#### Q3a. Patient positioning in lap urology (SAQ)

- Axillary roll below, not in, the axilla.
- Rhabdomyolysis: men, BMI ≥25, kidney rest; CK >5,000.
