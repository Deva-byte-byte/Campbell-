## Q1. Discuss principles of hemodialysis and vasular access in details.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

*Assumed meaning: "vasular" is a typo for "vascular" access for haemodialysis.*

### Introduction
- Haemodialysis (HD) removes solutes and water from blood across a semipermeable membrane in an extracorporeal circuit. It is the commonest KRT (kidney replacement therapy) for ESKD (end-stage kidney disease) and severe AKI (acute kidney injury). It needs an access giving about 250–350 mL/min of blood flow.

### Principles of haemodialysis
| Process | Driving force | What it removes |
|---|---|---|
| **Diffusion** | Concentration gradient across the membrane | Small solutes (urea, creatinine, potassium); bicarbonate enters blood |
| **Convection** | Transmembrane pressure; solute is dragged with water | Middle molecules (e.g. β2-microglobulin) |
| **Ultrafiltration** | Transmembrane (hydrostatic) pressure | Excess water, down to "dry weight" |

- **Countercurrent flow:** dialysate runs opposite to blood, keeping a gradient along the whole dialyser. Clearance rises with flows, membrane area and time.
- **Dialyser:** hollow fibres of synthetic membrane; high-flux membranes clear more middle molecules.
- **Dialysate:** ultrapure (reverse-osmosis) water with sodium about 140 mmol/L, potassium about 2 mmol/L, bicarbonate and calcium, adjusted to the patient.
- **Anticoagulation:** heparin; heparin-free or regional citrate if bleeding risk is high (e.g. after surgery).
- **Schedule:** about 4 h three times a week. **Also know:** haemofiltration, haemodiafiltration (HDF), CRRT (continuous renal replacement therapy).

### Adequacy
- **Kt/V** (dialyser urea clearance × time ÷ urea distribution volume): target 1.4, minimum 1.2 per thrice-weekly session (KDOQI [Kidney Disease Outcomes Quality Initiative] 2015). **URR (urea reduction ratio) ≥65%.**

### Indications
- **Acute ("AEIOU"), refractory to medical treatment:** Acidosis; Electrolytes (hyperkalaemia); Ingestions (lithium, salicylates, alcohols); Overload with respiratory distress; Uraemia (encephalopathy, pericarditis, bleeding).
- **Chronic:** symptomatic ESKD, usually at eGFR (estimated glomerular filtration rate) 5–10; IDEAL (2010): early start gave no benefit.

### Complications of haemodialysis
- **During dialysis:** hypotension (commonest), cramps, arrhythmia, air embolism, bleeding. **Disequilibrium syndrome:** rapid urea removal in early sessions shifts water into the brain (cerebral oedema); prevent with short, gentle first sessions.
- **Long term:** cardiovascular disease (main cause of death), mineral–bone disorder, calciphylaxis, β2-microglobulin amyloidosis (carpal tunnel), infection, hepatitis B and C; acquired cystic kidney disease (about 80% in ESKD; RCC [renal cell carcinoma] in 1–2% of these).

### Types of vascular access
| Access | Construction | Ready | Comment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Radiocephalic AVF** (arteriovenous fistula; Brescia–Cimino, 1966) | Radial artery to cephalic vein at the wrist (now usually vein end to artery side) | ≥6–8 weeks | First choice: best patency, fewest infections; highest failure to mature |
| Brachiocephalic AVF | Brachial artery to cephalic vein at the elbow | ≥6–8 weeks | Higher flow; more steal |
| Brachiobasilic AVF | Deep basilic vein transposed superficially | Longer | When the cephalic vein is unusable |
| **AVG** (arteriovenous graft) | PTFE (polytetrafluoroethylene), forearm loop or upper-arm straight | 2–3 weeks | More stenosis, thrombosis, infection |
| **Tunnelled cuffed CVC** (central venous catheter) | Right internal jugular; cuff blocks bacterial migration | At once | Bridge or last resort; highest infection and central stenosis |

- Non-tunnelled 10–12 Fr jugular or femoral catheters are for acute dialysis only.

### Planning the access
- **Hierarchy:** AVF, then AVG, then CVC; distal before proximal; non-dominant arm first (US **Fistula First**, 2003). **KDOQI 2019** asks for an individual "ESKD Life-Plan": the right access for the right patient at the right time.
- **Timing:** refer at eGFR <30; create an AVF at least 6 months before dialysis is expected.
- **Vein preservation:** no venepuncture, cannula or PICC (peripherally inserted central catheter) in the non-dominant arm; avoid subclavian catheters.
- **Assessment:** pulses, **Allen test** (ulnar collateral flow), **Doppler vein mapping** (vessels about ≥2 mm), venography if central stenosis is suspected.
- **Maturity, "rule of 6s":** flow >600 mL/min, diameter >6 mm, depth <6 mm, by 6 weeks.

### Access complications
| Complication | Key point | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Failure to mature | Juxta-anastomotic stenosis; side veins | Angioplasty; ligate branches |
| Stenosis | Neointimal hyperplasia; AVG at venous anastomosis | PTA (percutaneous transluminal angioplasty), stent graft, revision |
| Thrombosis | Usually on an underlying stenosis | Thrombectomy, then treat the stenosis |
| **Steal syndrome** | Hand ischaemia; diabetes, brachial-based access | Banding, DRIL (distal revascularisation–interval ligation), ligation |
| Aneurysm | Repeated needling at one site | Repair if skin is thin |
| Infection | CVC > AVG > AVF | Antibiotics; excise graft; remove catheter |

- **Also know:** venous hypertension (central stenosis); high-output cardiac failure (banding); ischaemic monomelic neuropathy; graft seroma.

### Monitoring
- Each session: thrill and bruit, arm-elevation test (a healthy fistula collapses), prolonged bleeding after needling, rising venous pressure, falling Kt/V. Duplex scan when abnormal; treat a stenosis causing clinical change. Rotate needle sites ("rope-ladder").

### Recent advances
- **Endovascular AVF** (percutaneous WavelinQ, Ellipsys); drug-coated balloons; home and nocturnal HD.
- **CONVINCE (2023):** high-dose HDF lowered all-cause mortality compared with high-flux HD.

**Diagram to draw:** dialyser with countercurrent blood and dialysate, and a radiocephalic end-to-side fistula.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Diffusion clears small solutes, convection middle molecules, ultrafiltration water.
> - Kt/V minimum 1.2 (target 1.4); URR ≥65%.
> - AVF first, distal, non-dominant arm, ≥6 months ahead; rule of 6s.
> - Thrombosis usually sits on a stenosis; steal is treated by banding or DRIL.

## Q2. Discuss evaluation for urolithiasis and management of bilateral renal staghorn calculi
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

### Evaluation for urolithiasis
- **History:** colic, UTI (urinary tract infection), previous stones, fluid and diet, drugs (calcium, vitamins C and D, acetazolamide, steroids), GI (gastrointestinal) disease, gout, family history.
- **Laboratory:** urinalysis (pH, crystals, pyuria) and culture; serum creatinine, electrolytes, calcium, uric acid; PTH (parathyroid hormone) if calcium is high; bicarbonate if RTA (renal tubular acidosis) is suspected.

| Imaging | Role |
|---|---|
| **NCCT** (non-contrast CT) | Gold standard: sensitivity 97%, specificity 95%. Size, site, density (uric acid 200–400 HU [Hounsfield units]; calcium oxalate 600–1200 HU), skin-to-stone distance |
| Low-dose NCCT (≤3 mSv) | Equal accuracy for stones ≥3 mm |
| Ultrasound | First line in pregnancy and children |
| KUB (plain X-ray) | Follow-up of radio-opaque stones only |
| CT urography | Anatomy before PCNL (percutaneous nephrolithotomy) |
| MAG3 (mercaptoacetyltriglycine) renogram or DMSA (dimercaptosuccinic acid) scan | Split function |

- **Stone analysis:** infrared spectroscopy or X-ray diffraction.
- **Metabolic evaluation:** screening (history, serum, urine, stone analysis) for every first-time former. **High-risk formers** (recurrent, children, GI disease, obesity, diabetes, hyperparathyroidism, RTA, gout, family history, solitary kidney, large bilateral stones) need one or two **24-h urines** on a usual diet: volume, pH, calcium, oxalate, uric acid, citrate, sodium, potassium, creatinine (cystine if suspected). Bilateral staghorns are high risk by definition.

### Staghorn calculus
- **Definition:** a branched stone filling the renal pelvis and extending into the calyces. **Partial:** pelvis and some calyces; **complete:** pelvis and all major calyces.
- **Composition:** classically struvite (magnesium ammonium phosphate) with carbonate apatite. Bacterial **urease** (Proteus, Klebsiella, Ureaplasma) splits urea into ammonia, so urine pH rises above 7.2 and struvite crystallises inside a bacterial biofilm. Fewer than half of modern staghorns are infection stones; calcium phosphate, cystine and uric acid also occur.
- **Why treat:** untreated staghorns destroy the kidney and cause sepsis, XGP (xanthogranulomatous pyelonephritis) and death.

### Work-up before surgery
- NCCT with coronal and 3D views: burden, calyceal anatomy, colon and spleen.
- **Renal function:** eGFR (estimated glomerular filtration rate) and split function, because both kidneys are at risk.
- **Infection:** culture-specific antibiotics before surgery; drain an obstructed or infected kidney (nephrostomy through a calyx usable later for PCNL). Culture the stone.
- **Guy's stone score (2011)** predicts stone-free rate:

| Grade | Stone |
|---|---|
| I | Single stone in mid or lower pole or pelvis, simple anatomy |
| II | Single upper-pole stone; multiple stones in simple anatomy; or single stone in abnormal anatomy |
| III | Multiple stones in abnormal anatomy; calyceal diverticulum; partial staghorn |
| IV | Staghorn; or any stone in spina bifida or spinal injury |

- **Also know:** STONE score, CROES nomogram, S-ReSC score.

### Treatment options (AUA [American Urological Association], EAU [European Association of Urology])
- **Goal: complete clearance**, because each fragment is an infected nidus; also clear infection and preserve function.

| Option | Role |
|---|---|
| **PCNL** | **First line.** Multiple tracts if needed; upper-pole access reaches pelvis, lower pole and ureteropelvic junction. Flexible nephroscopy raises clearance; second-look for residue |
| **ECIRS** (endoscopic combined intrarenal surgery) | PCNL (usually supine) plus retrograde flexible ureteroscopy: fewer tracts, less bleeding, higher clearance |
| Staged RIRS (retrograde intrarenal surgery) | Selected partial staghorns; patients who cannot stop anticoagulants |
| SWL (shock wave lithotripsy) | Not as monotherapy; adjunct for residual fragments |
| **Anatrophic nephrolithotomy** (Boyce–Smith) | Incision along Brödel's avascular line, artery clamped, cold ischaemia, infundibula reconstructed. Now rare; when endoscopy cannot clear the stone in a reasonable number of sessions |
| **Nephrectomy** | Poorly functioning kidney (split below about 10–20%) that is infected or painful; XGP |

### Bilateral staghorns: sequence
```
Sepsis or obstruction on either side?
  Yes -> drain + antibiotics -> treat that side once sterile
  No  -> treat the better-functioning (or symptomatic) kidney first
              |
  NCCT: stone-free? -> second look if residue
              |
  Other kidney after recovery
  (selected: same-session bilateral PCNL, or PCNL + contralateral RIRS)
```
- **Why the better kidney first:** it secures renal function early; some start with the simpler side. No trial defines the order.
- **Same-session bilateral surgery:** feasible at experienced centres if the first side was short and uncomplicated; saves an anaesthetic but risks bilateral bleeding or obstruction.
- Check creatinine after each side. Remove a non-functioning kidney only after the other side is cleared.

### Complications and follow-up
- PCNL risks: bleeding (transfusion about 1%; angioembolisation), sepsis, pleural, colon, spleen or liver injury.
- NCCT for stone-free status; treat residual fragments >4 mm. Surveillance cultures.
- 24-h urine even in struvite formers, then targeted therapy; urine ≥2.5 L/day.
- **AHA (acetohydroxamic acid)** 250 mg three times daily, a urease inhibitor, only when surgery is exhausted. Avoid if creatinine >2.5 mg/dL or in pregnancy; causes DVT (deep vein thrombosis), haemolytic anaemia.

**Diagram to draw:** staghorn with an upper-pole PCNL tract and a retrograde flexible scope (ECIRS).

> **Key points to remember:**
> - NCCT gold standard (97%/95%); 24-h urine for high-risk formers.
> - Staghorn: PCNL first line; complete clearance is the goal.
> - Bilateral: drain an infected side first, then the better kidney.
> - AHA only when surgery is not possible.

## Q3a. LASERS in BPH.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Principle
- LASER (light amplification by stimulated emission of radiation): the tissue effect depends on wavelength and **chromophore** (water or haemoglobin), which set **penetration depth**.
- About 60 °C coagulates tissue (delayed sloughing); ≥100 °C vaporises it (immediate removal).

### Lasers used in BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia)
| Laser | Wavelength | Chromophore | Penetration | Procedure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ho:YAG (holmium:yttrium-aluminium-garnet) | ~2120 nm | Water | 0.4 mm | **HoLEP** (holmium laser enucleation of the prostate) |
| Thulium (Tm:YAG, TFL [thulium fibre laser]) | 2013 / 1940 nm | Water (TFL at peak) | 0.2 mm | ThuLEP (thulium laser enucleation), vaporesection, vaporisation |
| KTP (potassium titanyl phosphate) / LBO (lithium triborate) | 532 nm | Haemoglobin | 0.8 mm | **PVP** (photoselective vaporisation), 120–180 W |
| Diode | 940–1470 nm | Water and haemoglobin | 0.5–5 mm | Vaporisation, enucleation |
| Nd:YAG | 1064 nm | Weak | About 1 cm | Coagulation; abandoned (sloughing) |

### Enucleation versus vaporisation
| Feature | Enucleation (HoLEP, ThuLEP) | Vaporisation (PVP) |
|---|---|---|
| Tissue | Whole transition zone to the capsule, then morcellated | Removed layer by layer |
| Histology | Yes | No |
| Gland size | **Any (size-independent)** | Up to 80 cc |
| Retreatment | Rare | Higher, especially >80 cc |

### Evidence
- **HoLEP** (about 2 J × 40–50 Hz): the only endoscopic procedure **superior to TURP** (transurethral resection of prostate) for IPSS (International Prostate Symptom Score) and Qmax. Less bleeding, shorter catheter time and stay.
- **GOLIATH RCT** (randomised controlled trial; 180 W PVP vs TURP, about 270 men, ≤100 cc): PVP non-inferior for IPSS, Qmax and complications at 2 years; shorter catheter time and stay.
- **ThuLEP:** outcomes equal to HoLEP. TFL energy is absorbed about four times more by water than holmium.
- **AUA (American Urological Association):** HoLEP or ThuLEP for any size; PVP for <80 cc; HoLEP, ThuLEP or PVP for men at **higher bleeding risk**.

### Complications
- Storage symptoms and dysuria; transient stress incontinence; retrograde ejaculation (about three-quarters after HoLEP); bladder neck contracture (0–3%); stricture; capsular perforation; morcellator bladder injury.
- **Safety:** wavelength-specific eyewear for all; laser safety officer.

**Diagram to draw:** HoLEP planes: enucleated adenoma pushed into the bladder, then morcellated.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Chromophore sets penetration: thulium 0.2, holmium 0.4, KTP 0.8 mm, Nd:YAG 1 cm.
> - HoLEP is size-independent and superior to TURP.
> - GOLIATH: PVP non-inferior to TURP; best for anticoagulated men.

## Q3b. Interstitial cystitis (Bladder pain syndrome).
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definitions
- **AUA (American Urological Association):** an unpleasant sensation (pain, pressure, discomfort) perceived to be related to the bladder, with LUTS (lower urinary tract symptoms) for **more than 6 weeks**, without infection or another identifiable cause.
- **ESSIC (European Society for the Study of Interstitial Cystitis, 2008):** chronic (>6 months) bladder-related pain with at least one other urinary symptom (persistent urge or frequency), after confusable diseases are excluded.
- NIDDK (National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases, 1987) research criteria missed about 60% of patients.

### Phenotypes
- **Hunner lesion (HL) disease, "IC":** about 20%, mainly over 50; true inflammation (lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate, mast cells).
- **Non-Hunner BPS (bladder pain syndrome):** bladder normal or shows glomerulations.
- **ESSIC type:** cystoscopy 1 normal, 2 glomerulations, 3 Hunner lesion; biopsy A normal, B inconclusive, C positive (e.g. 3C).

### Pathogenesis (theories)
- Leaky GAG (glycosaminoglycan) layer; mast-cell activation; neurogenic inflammation and central sensitisation; stress-axis dysfunction; small-fibre neuropathy. No infection proven. 75–80% have pain beyond the pelvis (IBS [irritable bowel syndrome], fibromyalgia).

### Evaluation (diagnosis of exclusion)
- History (**ask about ketamine**), pelvic-floor palpation, PVR (post-void residual), urinalysis, culture, voiding chart, symptom scores; cytology if risk factors.
- **Office cystoscopy if over 50 or HL suspected;** biopsy to exclude CIS (carcinoma in situ).
- **Local anaesthetic challenge** localises pain to the bladder. Potassium sensitivity test is not recommended.
- **Also know:** urodynamics optional; hydrodistension glomerulations are non-specific.

### Treatment (AUA 2022: phenotype-based, no fixed tiers)
| Group | Options |
|---|---|
| Behavioural | Education, dietary triggers, stress; **pelvic-floor myofascial therapy** if tender |
| Oral | **Amitriptyline** 10 mg rising to about 50 mg; hydroxyzine; cimetidine; **PPS** (pentosan polysulfate) 100 mg three times daily (**pigmentary maculopathy**: eye checks) |
| Intravesical | **DMSO** (dimethyl sulfoxide) 50%, 50 mL, 15–20 min dwell; heparin; alkalinised lidocaine |
| Procedures | **HL: fulguration and/or triamcinolone injection** (75–90% improve); short low-pressure hydrodistension; onabotulinumtoxinA; sacral neuromodulation |
| Last resort | Ciclosporin A for refractory HL; diversion ± cystectomy (<10%) |

- **Do not offer:** long-term antibiotics, intravesical BCG (bacille Calmette–Guérin), long-term systemic steroids.

**Diagram to draw:** stellate Hunner lesion with radiating vessels.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - AUA definition: bladder pain with LUTS >6 weeks, no other cause.
> - Hunner lesion: cystoscopy over 50; fulguration or triamcinolone.
> - Amitriptyline, PPS, DMSO; potassium test obsolete.

## Q3c. Silodosin.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Pharmacology
- The **most α1A-selective** blocker (well over 100-fold vs α1B; about 50-fold vs α1D), used for LUTS (lower urinary tract symptoms) due to BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia).
- Sparing α1B gives **minimal hypotension**. Blocking α1A in the vas and seminal vesicles causes **failure of emission**.
- Onset: flow improves within 2–6 hours, symptoms within 3–4 days. Metabolised by CYP3A4.

### Dose and contraindications
| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Dose | **8 mg once daily with a meal** |
| CrCl (creatinine clearance) 30–50 mL/min | **4 mg daily** |
| Contraindicated | CrCl <30; severe liver impairment; strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (e.g. ketoconazole) |
| Caution | Other α-blockers, PDE5 (phosphodiesterase 5) inhibitors, planned cataract surgery |

### Efficacy
- Better than placebo for IPSS (International Prostate Symptom Score) and flow, including severe LUTS. **Non-inferior to tamsulosin 0.4 mg**, not better (European trial, 2011).
- **Greatest fall in BOOI (bladder outlet obstruction index)** of all α1-blockers.
- Does not shrink the prostate; add a 5-ARI (5α-reductase inhibitor) for large glands.

### Adverse effects
- **Anejaculation:** over a quarter of men in trials (about 14% vs 2% with tamsulosin head-to-head), yet few stop. Men with anejaculation had better responses.
- No excess hypotension in the elderly or with antihypertensives; no QT prolongation.
- **IFIS (intraoperative floppy iris syndrome):** not specifically studied but reported; warn before cataract surgery.

### Other uses
- **MET (medical expulsive therapy)** for distal ureteric stones 5–10 mm. α-blockers reduce ureteric spasm. Silodosin gave faster expulsion than tamsulosin in meta-analyses; **SUSPEND (2015)** found no overall tamsulosin benefit.
- **Also know:** before ureteroscopy (easier access); recent-onset CP/CPPS (chronic prostatitis/chronic pelvic pain syndrome).

| Drug | Selectivity | Dose | Hypotension | Ejaculatory dysfunction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Silodosin** | α1A (highest) | 8 mg | Lowest | **Highest** |
| Tamsulosin | α1A, α1D > α1B | 0.4 mg | Low | Moderate |
| Alfuzosin | Clinically uroselective | 10 mg | Low | Lowest |
| Doxazosin, terazosin | Non-selective; titrate | 1–8 mg, 1–10 mg | Highest | Low |

**Diagram to draw:** α1A in prostate and vas, α1B in vessels, α1D in bladder.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - 8 mg with a meal; 4 mg if CrCl 30–50; avoid if <30.
> - Equal to tamsulosin 0.4 mg; greatest BOOI fall.
> - Anejaculation is the signature effect; least hypotension.

## Q3d. ADPKD (Adult polycystic kidney disease)
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Genetics
- ADPKD (autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease): commonest inherited cystic kidney disease (about 1 in 400–1000); 7–15% of patients on haemodialysis.

| Gene | Chromosome | Share | ESRD (end-stage renal disease) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PKD1** (polycystin-1) | 16 | ~85% | 50s |
| **PKD2** (polycystin-2) | 4 | ~15% | 70s |

- Polycystins (primary cilium) restrain proliferation.

### Clinical features (30–50 years)
- **Pain** (commonest): mass, cyst haemorrhage, infection, stones. **Haematuria** about 50%; **stones** 20–30%.
- **Hypertension:** about 50% at 20–35 years with normal function; stretched vessels release renin.
- **Renal failure:** tracks kidney volume; worse with PKD1, male sex.
- **Extrarenal:** liver cysts (commonest; up to 90% by 50); **intracranial aneurysm 10–30%**; seminal vesicle (40%), pancreatic, arachnoid cysts; mitral valve prolapse; diverticulosis.
- RCC (renal cell carcinoma) is not commoner, but is more often bilateral and sarcomatoid.

### Diagnosis (ultrasound in at-risk relatives)
| Age | Ravine (PKD1 families) | Pei unified (2009) |
|---|---|---|
| 15–29 | ≥2 cysts, one or both kidneys | ≥3 cysts in total (15–39) |
| 30–59 | ≥2 cysts in each kidney | ≥2 in each kidney (40–59) |
| ≥60 | ≥4 cysts in each kidney | ≥4 in each kidney |

- CT or MRI shows TKV (total kidney volume), which predicts progression. Genetic testing if equivocal or for a living donor.

### Management
- **BP <130/80 mmHg** with ACE (angiotensin-converting enzyme) inhibitor or ARB (angiotensin receptor blocker); HALT-PKD: tighter control slowed TKV growth.
- **Tolvaptan** (V2-receptor antagonist; lowers cyclic adenosine monophosphate): **TEMPO 3:4 (2012)** slowed TKV growth and function loss; **REPRISE (2017)** slowed eGFR (estimated glomerular filtration rate) decline in later disease. Causes polyuria and liver toxicity.
- **Pain:** exclude infection, stone, tumour; avoid NSAIDs (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs); aspiration, then laparoscopic unroofing.
- **Infected cyst:** lipid-soluble antibiotics (trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole, fluoroquinolones); drain if no response.
- **Haemorrhage:** usually settles with rest. **Aneurysm screening** (MR angiography) if family history.
- **ESRD:** dialysis or transplant (no recurrence). **Native nephrectomy** before or at transplant for recurrent infection, bleeding, pain, suspected tumour or no room for the graft.

**Diagram to draw:** large cystic kidneys, liver cysts and berry aneurysm.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - PKD1: chromosome 16, 85%, earlier ESRD.
> - Ravine: <30 years ≥2 cysts; 30–59 ≥2 each kidney; ≥60 ≥4 each.
> - Tolvaptan (TEMPO 3:4, REPRISE); ACE inhibitor or ARB.

## Q3e. RIRS (Retrograde intrarenal surgery)
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Introduction
- RIRS (retrograde intrarenal surgery) is flexible ureterorenoscopy (fURS) inside the kidney through the natural route; active deflection and the holmium laser made it practical.

### Instrument
| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Tip | 4.5–7.5 Fr, tapered (digital tips up to about 8.5 Fr) |
| Working channel | 3.6 Fr (laser fibre, basket) |
| Deflection | ≥270° both ways; lower pole needs about 140° (up to 175°) |
| Types | **Fibreoptic** (smallest, image degrades); **digital** (chip on tip, better image, not more durable); **single-use** (7.7 Fr; cheaper below about 99 cases a year) |

### Indications
- **Stones:** renal stones <2 cm; lower pole <1 cm (or >1 cm as an alternative to PCNL [percutaneous nephrolithotomy]); patients who cannot stop anticoagulants; failed SWL (shock wave lithotripsy); obesity; staged treatment of larger stones.
- **Others:** diagnosis and ablation of low-grade UTUC (upper tract urothelial carcinoma); filling defects; endopyelotomy; calyceal diverticulum; essential haematuria.

### Technique
1. Sterile urine; single-dose antibiotic.
2. Cystoscopy, **safety guidewire**, retrograde pyelogram.
3. **Access sheath** (usually 12/14 Fr) over a stiff wire: lowers intrarenal pressure and allows repeated passes, but severe ureteric injury occurs in about 1 in 8.
4. Low-pressure irrigation. **Ho:YAG (holmium:yttrium-aluminium-garnet) or thulium fibre laser**: dusting (low energy, high frequency) or fragment-and-basket. Keep power ≤10 W in the ureter and ≤25 W in the kidney (tissue damage above 43 °C).
5. Inspect the ureter on withdrawal; stent unless uncomplicated.
- **Difficult lower pole:** steep infundibulopelvic angle, long (>10 mm) or narrow (<5 mm) infundibulum. Relocate the stone to an upper calyx with a basket.

### Complications
- Sepsis about 5%; stricture about 3%; bleeding or subcapsular haematoma <1%; perforation; avulsion <1%; stent symptoms; scope damage.

### Recent advances
- Single-use digital scopes; **thulium fibre laser** and pulse-modulated (Moses) holmium (less retropulsion, finer dust); **suction and flexible navigable sheaths** (FANS); intrarenal pressure sensing; robotic flexible ureteroscopy.

**Diagram to draw:** flexible scope deflected into the lower pole calyx through an access sheath.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - 3.6 Fr channel; ≥270° deflection.
> - Access sheath lowers pressure but injures the ureter in about 1 in 8.
> - Laser power ≤10 W ureter, ≤25 W kidney.

## Q3f. Renal isotope scans
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Principle
- A radiotracer follows a physiological process; a **gamma camera** (sodium iodide crystal, photomultipliers, collimator) images the photons. SPECT (single-photon emission computed tomography) gives 3D images.
- **⁹⁹ᵐTc (technetium-99m):** 6-hour half-life, single 140 keV photon, so it is the workhorse.

### Renal tracers
| Tracer | Handling | Study | Main uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **DTPA** (diethylenetriaminepentaacetic acid) | Glomerular filtration only | Dynamic | GFR (glomerular filtration rate); captopril renography; poor images in renal impairment |
| **MAG3** (mercaptoacetyltriglycine) | Tubular secretion (protein-bound), high extraction | Dynamic | **Split function and obstruction (first choice)**; transplant |
| **DMSA** (dimercaptosuccinic acid) | Filtered, then retained by proximal tubules | Static at 2 h | **Pyelonephritis and scars** |

### Diuretic renography
- **Preparation:** good hydration; catheter if outlet obstruction or neurogenic bladder; cap a nephrostomy.
- **Dose:** 5–10 mCi (185–370 MBq) intravenously.
- **Phases:** perfusion (first minute); function: **split function** from the area under each curve at 1–3 min (normal 45:55 to 55:45); drainage.
- **Furosemide** 0.5 mg/kg (adult maximum 40 mg). Timing: F+20 (classic), F+15, F0 or F−15. Always note when it was given.

| Post-furosemide T½ (half-clearance time) | Meaning |
|---|---|
| <10 min | Not obstructed |
| 10–20 min | Indeterminate |
| >20 min | Obstructed |

- **Curve types (O'Reilly):** I normal; II obstructed (rising); IIIa dilated, not obstructed (falls after furosemide); IIIb equivocal.
- **Pitfalls:** poor function, dehydration, full bladder, capacious pelvis.

### Other uses
- **Captopril renography (DTPA):** captopril removes angiotensin II support of GFR in the stenotic kidney. Improvement of ≥10% off ACE (angiotensin-converting enzyme) inhibitors means renovascular hypertension is likely.
- **Transplant:** acute rejection (perfusion falls, worsens) versus delayed graft function (perfusion kept, improves); urine leak.
- **DMSA:** acute pyelonephritis defects resolve; scars persist at about 6 months.
- **Nuclear cystography** for follow-up of reflux (more sensitive, lower radiation than VCUG [voiding cystourethrogram]).
- **Also know:** bone scan (MDP, methylene diphosphonate); labelled leucocyte scans; PSMA (prostate-specific membrane antigen) PET (positron emission tomography). Radiation dose is low.

**Diagram to draw:** renogram curves: normal, obstructed (rising) and dilated non-obstructed (falls after furosemide).

> **Key points to remember:**
> - DTPA = filtration; MAG3 = secretion (best for obstruction); DMSA = cortex.
> - T½ <10 min not obstructed; >20 min obstructed.
> - Normal split 45–55%; furosemide 0.5 mg/kg.

#### Q1. Discuss principles of hemodialysis and vasular access in details. (LAQ)

- Diffusion clears small solutes, convection middle molecules, ultrafiltration water.
- Kt/V minimum 1.2 (target 1.4); URR ≥65%.
- AVF first, distal, non-dominant arm, ≥6 months ahead; rule of 6s.
- Thrombosis usually sits on a stenosis; steal is treated by banding or DRIL.

#### Q2. Discuss evaluation for urolithiasis and management of bilateral renal staghorn calculi (LAQ)

- NCCT gold standard (97%/95%); 24-h urine for high-risk formers.
- Staghorn: PCNL first line; complete clearance is the goal.
- Bilateral: drain an infected side first, then the better kidney.
- AHA only when surgery is not possible.

#### Q3a. LASERS in BPH. (SAQ)

- Chromophore sets penetration: thulium 0.2, holmium 0.4, KTP 0.8 mm, Nd:YAG 1 cm.
- HoLEP is size-independent and superior to TURP.
- GOLIATH: PVP non-inferior to TURP; best for anticoagulated men.

#### Q3b. Interstitial cystitis (Bladder pain syndrome). (SAQ)

- AUA definition: bladder pain with LUTS >6 weeks, no other cause.
- Hunner lesion: cystoscopy over 50; fulguration or triamcinolone.
- Amitriptyline, PPS, DMSO; potassium test obsolete.

#### Q3c. Silodosin. (SAQ)

- 8 mg with a meal; 4 mg if CrCl 30–50; avoid if <30.
- Equal to tamsulosin 0.4 mg; greatest BOOI fall.
- Anejaculation is the signature effect; least hypotension.

#### Q3d. ADPKD (Adult polycystic kidney disease) (SAQ)

- PKD1: chromosome 16, 85%, earlier ESRD.
- Ravine: <30 years ≥2 cysts; 30–59 ≥2 each kidney; ≥60 ≥4 each.
- Tolvaptan (TEMPO 3:4, REPRISE); ACE inhibitor or ARB.

#### Q3e. RIRS (Retrograde intrarenal surgery) (SAQ)

- 3.6 Fr channel; ≥270° deflection.
- Access sheath lowers pressure but injures the ureter in about 1 in 8.
- Laser power ≤10 W ureter, ≤25 W kidney.

#### Q3f. Renal isotope scans (SAQ)
