## I-1. Describe anatomy of pelvicalyceal system in detail and its application in endourological surgeries
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

### Introduction
- Papilla → minor calyx → infundibulum → major calyx → renal pelvis → UPJ (ureteropelvic junction); lined by urothelium. Anatomy is very variable, so map it (CT (computed tomography) urogram or pyelogram) before every endourological procedure.

### Anatomy of the calyces
- **Minor calyx:** a cup around one papilla; its two side projections are the **fornices**. **Compound calyces** (2–3 papillae) are commonest at the poles. Average 7–9 calyces per kidney (range 4–19 or more).
- **Distribution:** upper pole usually 3 calyces, interpolar 3–4, lower pole 2–3.
- **Drainage pattern:** upper pole by a single midline infundibulum; lower pole by a single midline infundibulum or paired calyces; hilar region by **anterior and posterior rows** of paired calyces.
- **Orientation:** the kidney lies on psoas, rotated 30–50° behind the coronal plane, so posterior calyces point posterolaterally (towards the prone surgeon). On a pyelogram anterior and posterior calyces overlap.
- **Brödel vs Hodson pattern (Kaye and Reinke):** in a Brödel-type kidney the posterior calyces lie lateral and the anterior calyces medial; in a Hodson-type kidney the reverse. Brödel type is commoner on the right, Hodson type on the left, so confirm calyx orientation with contrast/air or ultrasound before puncture.

### Renal pelvis
- Pyramidal, apex to the ureter; capacity 3–10 mL. **Intrarenal** (in the sinus), **extrarenal** (larger, longer infundibula, mimics dilatation) or mixed.
- **Duplex (divided) pelvis:** upper and lower parts join at the hilum, with no direct connection between upper and lower calyces. Suspect it when one pole cannot be reached from a puncture.

### Sampaio classification (140 resin endocasts)
| Type | Frequency | Midzone (hilar) drainage |
|---|---|---|
| A-I | 45% | Midzone calyces drain into the superior or inferior calyceal group |
| A-II | 17.2% | **Crossed calyces**: one to the superior and one to the inferior group |
| B-I | 21.4% | Independent major calyceal group from the midzone |
| B-II | 16.4% | 1–4 minor calyces enter the pelvis directly |

- **Perpendicular minor calyx (11.4%):** drains straight into the pelvis, hidden on a pyelogram; narrow neck (≤4 mm) hinders clearance.
- **Crossed calyces:** the limb to the inferior group is **ventral in 87.5%**.

### Vascular relations (the basis of safe access)
- Hilum, anterior to posterior: vein, artery, pelvis, **posterior segmental artery** (the only branch passing behind the pelvis).
- Five segmental **end arteries**; interlobar arteries run in the columns of Bertin, hugging the anterior and posterior faces of the infundibula.
- **Brödel line:** avascular plane between anterior and posterior arterial territories, just posterior to the lateral border.

| Puncture route (Sampaio) | Vascular injury |
|---|---|
| Upper-pole infundibulum | 67% (arterial 26%); posterior segmental artery |
| Mid-pole infundibulum | Arterial 23% |
| Lower-pole infundibulum | Infundibular artery in 38% |
| **Calyceal fornix** | Venous <8%, **no arterial injury** |

- **Relations:** pleura reaches the 12th rib posteriorly, lung the 10th intercostal space; liver and spleen descend in inspiration; **retrorenal colon** is commoner at the lower pole and when prone.

### Applications in endourology
**1. PCNL (percutaneous nephrolithotomy) access**
- Ideal target: a **posterior calyx with a short, wide infundibulum**, punctured **through the papilla and fornix** along the calyx axis, never into the infundibulum or pelvis. This passes through a pyramid and avoids interlobar arteries.
- **Upper-pole posterior access** gives the straightest route to the pelvis, UPJ and most calyces (staghorn). Chest risk (pneumothorax, hydrothorax) rises above the 12th rib, highest above the 11th. Puncture in expiration.
- **Lower-pole access** is chest-safe but risks colon (left) and reaches the UPJ poorly.
- Identifying posterior calyces: rainbowing the C-arm towards the surgeon shortens them; injected air fills non-dependent (posterior, when prone) calyces; ultrasound also shows bowel and pleura.

**2. RIRS (retrograde intrarenal surgery)**
- Entering the lower-pole infundibulum needs about **140° deflection** (104–175°); ≥270° scopes see it, but working there is harder.
- **Unfavourable lower-pole anatomy:** steep infundibulopelvic angle, long calyx (>10 mm), narrow infundibulum (<5 mm). Relocate stones to an upper calyx with a basket.
- Lower-pole stone >1 cm: PCNL preferred (**Lower Pole I, 2001:** stone-free 91% PCNL vs 21% SWL (shock wave lithotripsy)).

**3. Endopyelotomy**
- Incise the UPJ **laterally**, where crossing vessels are absent. A crossing vessel lowers success (42% vs 86%).
- Antegrade route: mid-posterior or superolateral calyx; not lower pole (acute angle to the UPJ).

**4. Infundibulotomy and calyceal diverticulum**
- Incise infundibular stenosis **superiorly or inferiorly**; never anteriorly.
- **Calyceal diverticulum:** cortical cavity joined to the fornix by a narrow neck; treat if symptomatic. Upper or interpolar: ureteroscopy, incise or dilate the neck. Lower pole: direct puncture onto the stone, mini-PCNL; fulguration optional.

**5. Open/anatrophic nephrolithotomy:** incision along the Brödel line.

- **Also know:** horseshoe kidney (medial access, upper pole usually subcostal); pelvic kidney (ultrasound or laparoscopic guidance); megacalycosis mimics obstruction; AV (arteriovenous) fistula or pseudoaneurysm → angioembolisation.

**Diagram to draw:** transverse section of kidney showing anterior and posterior calyces, Brödel line and the needle through the posterior calyceal fornix.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Sampaio: A-I 45%, A-II (crossed) 17.2%, B-I 21.4%, B-II 16.4%.
> - Fornix puncture: no arterial injury; upper-pole infundibulum 67% vascular injury.
> - Lower pole: steep angle, long calyx >10 mm, infundibulum <5 mm unfavourable.
> - Endopyelotomy lateral; infundibulotomy superior or inferior, never anterior.

## I-2. Describe physiology of spermatogenesis and discuss management of azoospermia.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

### Hormonal control: HPG (hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal) axis
- Hypothalamus releases **GnRH (gonadotropin-releasing hormone)** in pulses; pulsatility is essential (constant agonist exposure shuts the axis down). Kisspeptin triggers puberty.
- **LH (luteinising hormone)** → Leydig cells → testosterone (about 5 mg/day). **FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone)** → Sertoli cells.
- Feedback: testosterone (and oestradiol) on LH; **inhibin B** from Sertoli cells on FSH. So FSH and inhibin B report the tubules, LH reports Leydig cells.
- **Intratesticular testosterone** is about 50-fold higher than serum (held by **ABP (androgen-binding protein)**). This is why exogenous testosterone suppresses spermatogenesis.
- Testis is kept 2–4 °C below core temperature by the pampiniform countercurrent exchange.

### Sertoli cells and blood–testis barrier
- Nurse cells: nourish germ cells, secrete ABP, inhibin, fluid; phagocytose residual bodies.
- **Sertoli–Sertoli tight junctions** divide the tubule into a **basal** compartment (spermatogonia) and an **adluminal** compartment (spermatocytes, spermatids). Post-meiotic cells are "foreign" to the immune system; the barrier and local tolerance protect them.

### Phases of spermatogenesis (about 64 days; 42–76 days)
| Phase | Events |
|---|---|
| **1. Proliferation (spermatocytogenesis)** | Dark type A (Ad, reserve stem cells) self-renew; pale type A (Ap) divide every 16 days into type B spermatogonia → primary spermatocytes. Daughter cells stay joined by cytoplasmic bridges |
| **2. Meiosis** | Primary spermatocytes cross into the adluminal compartment. Meiosis I (reduction division; crossing-over at synapsis) → secondary spermatocytes → meiosis II → 4 haploid round spermatids |
| **3. Spermiogenesis** | No division: acrosome from Golgi, flagellum from centriole, nucleus condenses to about 10%, mitochondria form the midpiece, cytoplasm shed |
| **4. Spermiation** | Sertoli cell releases sperm into the lumen |

- **Cycle, stage, wave:** six stages in humans; cycles arranged as a helical wave, so production is continuous (about 123 million sperm a day).
- Defective meiotic recombination causes maturation arrest and sperm aneuploidy.

### Epididymal maturation and transport
- Testicular sperm are immotile. During 2–12 days of epididymal transit they gain **progressive motility** and fertilising ability; stored in the cauda.
- **Capacitation** and the acrosome reaction occur in the female tract.
- **Clinical relevance:** a new semen sample reflects events about 3 months earlier.

### Azoospermia: definition and classification
- No sperm in the **centrifuged** pellet on **two** semen analyses; 10–15% of infertile men.

| Type | Causes | Clues |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-testicular | Kallmann syndrome, pituitary tumour, hyperprolactinaemia, **exogenous testosterone/anabolic steroids** | Low FSH, LH, testosterone |
| Testicular (NOA (non-obstructive azoospermia)) | Klinefelter, Y microdeletion, cryptorchidism, mumps, chemotherapy/radiation | Small testes, high FSH |
| Post-testicular (OA (obstructive azoospermia)) | CBAVD (congenital bilateral absence of the vas), epididymal obstruction, vasectomy, hernia repair, EDO (ejaculatory duct obstruction) | Normal testes and FSH |
| Ejaculatory | Retrograde ejaculation, anejaculation | Low volume |

### Evaluation
```
Azoospermia (2 centrifuged samples)
 ├─ Vasa absent → CBAVD → CFTR (cystic fibrosis gene) testing, both partners; renal scan
 ├─ Volume <1 mL → post-ejaculate urine: sperm = retrograde; none → TRUS (transrectal ultrasound) for EDO
 └─ Normal volume: FSH ≤7.6 IU/L + testis long axis >4.6 cm → obstructive
                   FSH >7.6 + testis ≤4.6 cm → spermatogenic failure
```
- History (testosterone use, surgery, chemotherapy), examination (testes, vasa, epididymis, varicocele), hormones (FSH, LH, testosterone, prolactin).
- **Genetics (AUA/ASRM (American Urological Association/American Society for Reproductive Medicine)):** karyotype and **Y microdeletion** for NOA. AZF (azoospermia factor) **a or b complete deletion: no sperm, do not operate**; AZFc: sperm often found.
- TRUS: seminal vesicle >1.5 cm wide or midline cyst suggests EDO.

### Management
**1. Pre-testicular (hypogonadotropic)**
- Stop exogenous testosterone. **hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin) 1500–5000 IU 2–3 times weekly ± FSH**; pulsatile GnRH in hypothalamic failure; dopamine agonist for prolactinoma.

**2. Obstructive**
- Microsurgical **vasovasostomy** or **vasoepididymostomy** (clear watery fluid or sperm parts in vasal fluid → vasovasostomy; thick toothpaste-like fluid without sperm → vasoepididymostomy).
- **TURED (transurethral resection of the ejaculatory ducts)** for EDO.
- CBAVD or unreconstructable: **MESA (microsurgical epididymal sperm aspiration)** or PESA (percutaneous epididymal sperm aspiration) + **ICSI (intracytoplasmic sperm injection)**; freeze sperm at reconstruction.

**3. Non-obstructive**
- **Micro-TESE (microdissection testicular sperm extraction)** + ICSI. Spermatogenesis in NOA is focal; dilated opaque tubules are chosen under the microscope. Sperm found in about half (Klinefelter 68%, Sertoli-cell-only 30%).
- Before retrieval: repair a clinical varicocele; optimise hormones (clomiphene, anastrozole, hCG; off-label).

**4. Ejaculatory**
- Retrograde: sympathomimetics or sperm from alkalinised urine. Anejaculation: vibratory or electroejaculation.

**5. Failure:** donor sperm or adoption; genetic counselling.

- **Also know:** spermatogonial stem cell renewal by GDNF (glial cell line–derived neurotrophic factor); in-vitro spermatogenesis and stem-cell transplantation are experimental.

**Diagram to draw:** cross-section of a seminiferous tubule showing basal and adluminal compartments with germ cells maturing towards the lumen.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Spermatogenesis about 64 days; Ap divide every 16 days; 6 stages.
> - Intratesticular testosterone 50 × serum; exogenous testosterone causes azoospermia.
> - FSH ≤7.6 IU/L and testis >4.6 cm → obstructive.
> - AZFa/b deletion: do not operate; micro-TESE finds sperm in about half of NOA.

## I-3a. Management of Peyronie's disease
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition
- **PD (Peyronie's disease)** (AUA (American Urological Association) 2015): acquired fibrosis of the tunica albuginea, with pain, deformity, ED (erectile dysfunction) and/or distress. Prevalence 3–20%; up to half worsen untreated.

### Phases
- **Acute (active, first 12–18 months):** pain, worsening deformity; aim to stop progression. **Stable:** pain gone, deformity unchanged for ≥3–6 months; aim to straighten.

### Evaluation
- History: pain, deformity, Dupuytren disease, ability to penetrate. **Key question:** "If your penis were straight, would your current rigidity allow intercourse?" (predicts postoperative function).
- Plaque, SPL (stretched penile length); **office intracavernosal injection ± duplex Doppler ultrasound** before any invasive treatment (curvature by goniometer, calcification, vascular status).
- **Kelami grading:** ≤30° mild, 31–60° moderate, >60° severe; note hourglass or hinge. PDQ (Peyronie's Disease Questionnaire).

### Non-surgical treatment
| Option | Evidence/position |
|---|---|
| NSAIDs (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs) | For pain (only oral agent AUA supports) |
| Oral vitamin E, tamoxifen, potaba, pentoxifylline | No reliable curvature benefit; not recommended |
| **CCH (collagenase *Clostridium histolyticum*)** | Stable disease, curve 30–90°, intact erections. **IMPRESS I/II:** 34% improvement vs 18% placebo. Up to 4 cycles (2 injections of 0.58 mg) with modelling. Risk: corporal fracture |
| Verapamil, interferon α-2b (intralesional) | May offer; stabilise and modestly improve |
| **Traction therapy** | Gains length, reduces curvature; useful in acute phase |
| ESWT (extracorporeal shock wave therapy) | Pain only; no effect on curvature |

### Surgery
- **Indications:** stable disease (≥12 months, stable ≥6 months), deformity preventing intercourse, failed conservative therapy.

```
Erection adequate (± PDE5I (phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitor))?
 ├─ No  → penile prosthesis (inflatable) + manual modelling;
 │        residual >30° → tunical incision; graft if defect >2 cm
 └─ Yes → curve <60–70°, no hourglass/hinge → plication (Nesbit, 16-dot, tunica albuginea plication)
          curve >60–70°, hourglass, hinge, short penis → plaque incision/partial
          excision + graft (pericardium, small intestinal submucosa)
```
- **Plication** shortens the convex side: simple, low ED risk, but loses length.
- **Grafting** restores length and girth but carries higher ED risk (5–54%).

**Diagram to draw:** Nesbit wedge excised opposite the plaque.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Ask the "straight penis" question; duplex before surgery.
> - CCH: IMPRESS, stable 30–90° curve, intact erections.
> - Surgery: stable ≥12 months; plication <60–70°, graft complex curves, prosthesis if ED.

## I-3b. Treatment of renal abscess
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition and types
- **Renal (cortical/corticomedullary) abscess:** pus confined to the renal parenchyma.
  - **Cortical (renal carbuncle):** haematogenous *Staphylococcus aureus* from a skin source; formerly about 80% of cases.
  - **Corticomedullary (now commonest):** ascending Gram-negative infection (*E. coli*, *Klebsiella*, *Proteus*) in a damaged or obstructed kidney (stones, reflux, diabetes).
- **Perinephric abscess:** outside the capsule, within Gerota fascia; paranephric beyond it.

### Diagnosis (needed to plan treatment)
- Fever, flank pain, leucocytosis; urine may be sterile (haematogenous or non-communicating abscess). Blood cultures positive in a quarter to a third.
- **CT (computed tomography): investigation of choice:** round low-attenuation lesion with enhancing "ring sign". Ultrasound: quick, guides drainage. AFBN (acute focal bacterial nephritis) is wedge-shaped without a wall and is treated medically.

### Treatment
**1. Antibiotics immediately (after blood and urine cultures)**
| Presumed source | Empirical therapy |
|---|---|
| Haematogenous (Gram-positive) | Penicillinase-resistant penicillin; vancomycin if allergic or MRSA (methicillin-resistant *S. aureus*) |
| Ascending (Gram-negative) | IV (intravenous) third-generation cephalosporin, antipseudomonal penicillin or aminoglycoside |
- Switch to oral once afebrile; continue until clinical and imaging resolution.

**2. Size-based drainage**
| Size | Management |
|---|---|
| **≤3 cm** | Antibiotics alone (stable patient) |
| **3–5 cm** | Antibiotics alone or drainage; no consistent pathway |
| **>5 cm** | **Percutaneous drainage** (CT/ultrasound-guided) first line; may need several drains |
| Any size: immunocompromised or not responding | Percutaneous drainage |

- Aspiration also distinguishes abscess from a cystic tumour; culture the pus.
- Serial ultrasound or CT until resolution. Worsening suggests perinephric spread, wrong diagnosis or resistant organism.

**3. Treat the cause**
- Relieve obstruction (stent or nephrostomy); treat stones once infection settles; control diabetes.

**4. Surgery**
- Open drainage for loculated or failed percutaneous drainage; **nephrectomy** for a non-functioning, destroyed kidney.

### Perinephric abscess (if present)
- Lower threshold: drain if >3 cm; aminoglycoside + antistaphylococcal penicillin.

**Diagram to draw:** coronal kidney showing renal, perinephric and paranephric abscess sites.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Now mostly ascending Gram-negative abscess in a damaged kidney.
> - ≤3 cm antibiotics; 3–5 cm either; >5 cm percutaneous drainage.
> - CT is the investigation of choice; follow until resolution.

## I-3c. Catheter Associated Urinary Tract Infections (CAUTI)
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition
- **CAUTI (catheter-associated urinary tract infection):** symptomatic UTI (urinary tract infection) with a catheter in place >2 days (or removed within 48 hours). Distinguish from **asymptomatic bacteriuria**, which needs no treatment.

### Epidemiology and risk factors
- Commonest hospital-acquired infection (about 75–80% of hospital UTIs); commonest cause of secondary bacteraemia.
- Bacteriuria develops at about **5–10% per catheter-day**; 100% with long-term catheters. Intermittent catheterisation: 1–3% per catheterisation.
- **Risk factors:** duration (most important), female sex, older age, diabetes, breaks in the closed system, no systemic antibiotics.

### Pathogenesis
- Entry at insertion, then **extraluminal** (along the outer surface; commoner in women) or **intraluminal** (from the drainage bag).
- **Biofilm:** bacteria in a matrix on the catheter surface, formed within minutes; it shields them from antibiotics. Urease-producers (*Proteus*) cause encrustation.
- Organisms: *E. coli*, *Pseudomonas*, *Proteus*, *Enterococcus*; long-term catheters polymicrobial.

### Diagnosis
- Fever, rigors, flank or suprapubic pain, or delirium with no other source. Pyuria does not distinguish infection.
- Culture from a **freshly replaced catheter**, not the bag. **IDSA (Infectious Diseases Society of America): ≥10³ CFU/mL** (colony-forming units) with symptoms; surveillance (CDC (Centers for Disease Control and Prevention)) definition uses ≥10⁵.

### Prevention (bundle)
- **Avoid unnecessary catheters;** review daily, remove early. Aseptic insertion.
- **Closed, dependent drainage**; bag below bladder; no routine irrigation.
- Meatal cleaning with water; no prophylactic antibiotics (delay bacteriuria only 3–4 days, select resistance).
- Alternatives: **clean intermittent catheterisation**, condom or suprapubic catheter.
- Silver-alloy catheters reduce bacteriuria in short-term use; no proven fall in symptomatic CAUTI.
- Do not treat asymptomatic bacteriuria (except pregnancy, before urological surgery).

### Treatment
- **Replace or remove the catheter** before taking the culture and starting antibiotics, if in place >2 weeks (biofilm source).
- Empirical, then culture-directed antibiotics: **7 days** if prompt response; **10–14 days** if delayed.

**Diagram to draw:** catheter in bladder showing extraluminal and intraluminal routes and biofilm.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Bacteriuria 5–10% per catheter-day; duration is the key risk.
> - Diagnosis: symptoms + ≥10³ CFU/mL from a fresh catheter.
> - Remove catheter early; closed drainage; treat only symptomatic CAUTI, 7 or 10–14 days.

## I-3d. Contrast Induced Nephropathy (CIN)
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definitions
- **CIN (contrast-induced nephropathy)** (older term): creatinine rise ≥25% or ≥0.5 mg/dL within 48–72 hours of iodinated contrast, with no other cause.
- Current terms (ACR (American College of Radiology)):
  - **CA-AKI (contrast-associated acute kidney injury):** AKI within 48 hours of contrast, whatever the cause.
  - **CI-AKI (contrast-induced AKI):** the subset actually caused by contrast.
- **KDIGO (Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes) AKI:** creatinine rise ≥0.3 mg/dL in 48 hours, ≥1.5 × baseline in 7 days, or urine <0.5 mL/kg/h for 6 hours.

### Pathophysiology
- **Renal vasoconstriction** causes **medullary hypoxia** and ischaemic tubular injury, plus **direct tubular toxicity**. Usually transient; dialysis rarely needed.

### Risk factors
- **Patient:** CKD (chronic kidney disease, strongest), diabetes, age, dehydration, heart failure, anaemia, NSAIDs (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs).
- **Procedure:** high volume, repeated doses within 48–72 hours, intra-arterial route, high-osmolar agents.
- **eGFR (estimated glomerular filtration rate) thresholds:** real risk mainly at **eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m²** (IV (intravenous) contrast); ESUR (European Society of Urogenital Radiology) uses **<45** for intra-arterial first-pass exposure.
- Older uncontrolled studies overstated the risk; ACR: rare but real.

### Prevention
- Consider ultrasound or non-contrast CT (computed tomography) instead.
- Lowest dose of **low- or iso-osmolar** agent; low kVp (80); avoid repeat doses within 48 hours; stop NSAIDs.
- **IV isotonic saline** (e.g. 1 mL/kg/h before and after) for high-risk patients (eGFR <30 not on dialysis): the only proven measure.
- **PRESERVE (2018):** sodium bicarbonate and **NAC (N-acetylcysteine)** gave no benefit over saline.
- **AMACING (2017):** no prophylaxis was non-inferior to hydration at eGFR 30–59.
- Prophylactic dialysis does not help.

### Metformin
- Risk is lactic acidosis if AKI develops. **eGFR ≥30 and no AKI: continue.** eGFR <30 or AKI: **withhold for 48 hours**, restart when creatinine is stable.

**Diagram to draw:** contrast → vasoconstriction + tubular toxicity → medullary hypoxia → AKI.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - CIN = rise ≥25% or 0.5 mg/dL in 48–72 h; now CA-AKI vs CI-AKI.
> - Main risk: eGFR <30 (IV); <45 intra-arterial.
> - Isotonic saline only proven; PRESERVE: bicarbonate and NAC useless.
> - Metformin: withhold 48 h only if eGFR <30 or AKI.

## I-3e. Transrectal ultrasound study of prostate
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Principle and technique
- **TRUS (transrectal ultrasound):** the prostate lies directly in front of the rectum. Probes: end-fire, side-fire or biplane, **6–10 MHz** (higher frequency: better resolution, less depth). Left lateral (or lithotomy for transperineal work), after DRE (digital rectal examination). Transverse and sagittal planes, base to apex.

### Normal sonographic anatomy
| Zone (McNeal) | Glandular share | TRUS appearance | Cancer share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Peripheral | ~70% | Homogeneous, echogenic (posterior) | ~70% |
| Central | ~25% | Echogenic, at base | ~5–10% |
| Transition | 5–10% (BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia)) | Heterogeneous, anterior | ~20–25% |
| Anterior fibromuscular stroma | Non-glandular | Hypoechoic | – |

- Zones are not seen as such; TRUS separates echogenic posterior PZ (peripheral zone) from heterogeneous TZ (transition zone). **Corpora amylacea** mark the surgical capsule.
- Seminal vesicles 4.5–5.5 × 2 cm; absent seminal vesicle → ipsilateral renal agenesis in 79%.

### Measurements
- **Volume (ellipsoid) = 0.52 (π/6) × transverse × AP (anteroposterior) × length**; 1 mL ≈ 1 g; normal 20–25 g. **Planimetry** (serial sections) for brachytherapy.
- **PSAD (prostate-specific antigen density)** = PSA/volume; **>0.15** suggests cancer.

### Findings
- **Cancer:** classically a hypoechoic PZ lesion, but **39% are isoechoic**; false positives: BPH nodules, prostatitis, infarct.
- **Cysts:** Müllerian (midline), ejaculatory duct cyst (just off midline), seminal vesicle cyst (lateral, renal agenesis).
- Abscess, calculi, extraprostatic extension.
- After ADT (androgen deprivation therapy) or radiotherapy the gland shrinks and becomes hypoechoic.

### Indications
- **Guided biopsy** (12-core systematic ± MRI (magnetic resonance imaging)-targeted fusion).
- Volume before BPH surgery, brachytherapy, cryotherapy; fiducial markers.
- **Azoospermia:** ejaculatory duct obstruction (seminal vesicle >1.5 cm wide, midline cyst).
- Drainage of prostatic abscess or cysts.

### Biopsy notes
- Periprostatic block. Complications: bleeding, **sepsis** (fluoroquinolone-resistant *E. coli*); transperineal route lowers infection.

### Advances
- Colour/power Doppler, contrast-enhanced TRUS, **elastography**, multiparametric ultrasound.
- **Micro-ultrasound (29 MHz;** 70 µm resolution) with PRI-MUS scoring, comparable to MRI.
- MRI-first pathway: PROMIS (2017) mpMRI (multiparametric MRI) sensitivity 93% vs 48% for TRUS biopsy.

**Diagram to draw:** transverse and sagittal TRUS views labelling PZ, TZ, seminal vesicles and urethra.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - 6–10 MHz; volume = 0.52 × T × AP × L; PSAD >0.15.
> - 39% of cancers isoechoic; hypoechoic PZ lesion classic.
> - Seminal vesicle >1.5 cm wide: ejaculatory duct obstruction.

## I-3f. Oncogenes
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition
- **Proto-oncogene:** a normal gene that promotes cell growth and division (an "accelerator").
- **Oncogene:** its mutated or overactive form that drives uncontrolled proliferation. Change is **gain of function** and **dominant**: one altered allele is enough.
- The first human oncogene, **HRAS**, was isolated from the **T24 bladder cancer** cell line.

### Mechanisms of activation
| Mechanism | Effect | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Point (activating) mutation** | Constitutively active protein | RAS (bladder), FGFR3 (fibroblast growth factor receptor 3; low-grade papillary bladder), MET germline (hereditary papillary RCC (renal cell carcinoma)) |
| **Gene amplification** | Increased copy number → overexpression | MYC (prostate, bladder), HER2 (human epidermal growth factor receptor 2), AR (androgen receptor) in castration-resistant prostate cancer |
| **Chromosomal translocation/fusion** | Gene placed under a strong promoter, or fusion protein | **TMPRSS2–ERG** (androgen-driven ERG in about half of prostate cancers); BCR–ABL; TFE3 fusions (translocation RCC) |

### Functional classes
| Class | Example |
|---|---|
| Growth factors | PDGF (platelet-derived growth factor), sis |
| **Receptor tyrosine kinases** | EGFR (epidermal growth factor receptor), HER2, FGFR3, MET, KIT (seminoma) |
| **Signal transducers** | **RAS** (binds GTP (guanosine triphosphate); mutation locks it "on"), BRAF, PI3K |
| **Transcription factors** | **MYC**, ERG, ETS family |
| Cell cycle / apoptosis regulators | Cyclin D1, BCL-2 |

### Oncogene versus tumour suppressor gene
| | Oncogene | Tumour suppressor |
|---|---|---|
| Normal role | Accelerator | Brake, DNA repair |
| Change | Gain of function | Loss of function |
| Alleles needed | One | Usually both (**Knudson two-hit**: retinoblastoma) |
| GU (genitourinary) examples | MYC, MET, FGFR3, ERG | RB1, TP53, VHL, PTEN |

### Urological significance
- **Bladder:** FGFR3 mutations in up to 80% of low-grade Ta tumours (10–20% of MIBC (muscle-invasive bladder cancer)); RAS mutually exclusive with FGFR3; HER2 in 9–12% of MIBC.
- **Kidney:** MET activation in papillary RCC.
- **Prostate:** TMPRSS2–ERG fusion, MYC (8q gain), AR amplification after hormone therapy.
- **Testis:** KIT and KRAS mutations (seminoma).

### Targeted therapy (oncogene addiction)
- Erdafitinib (pan-FGFR inhibitor) for FGFR-altered advanced urothelial cancer; MET-active TKIs (tyrosine kinase inhibitors) in papillary RCC; AR-targeted drugs in prostate cancer; HER2-directed drugs (trastuzumab deruxtecan received a tumour-agnostic FDA approval in 2024 for HER2-positive solid tumours; disitamab vedotin is under study in urothelial cancer).

**Diagram to draw:** growth factor → receptor → RAS → RAF → MEK → ERK → MYC pathway.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Oncogenes: gain of function, one allele; activated by mutation, amplification, translocation.
> - HRAS first found in T24 bladder cancer; FGFR3 in up to 80% low-grade Ta.
> - TMPRSS2–ERG in about half of prostate cancers; MET in papillary RCC.

#### I-1. Describe anatomy of pelvicalyceal system in detail and its application in endourological surgeries (LAQ)

- Sampaio: A-I 45%, A-II (crossed) 17.2%, B-I 21.4%, B-II 16.4%.
- Fornix puncture: no arterial injury; upper-pole infundibulum 67% vascular injury.
- Lower pole: steep angle, long calyx >10 mm, infundibulum <5 mm unfavourable.
- Endopyelotomy lateral; infundibulotomy superior or inferior, never anterior.

#### I-2. Describe physiology of spermatogenesis and discuss management of azoospermia. (LAQ)

- Spermatogenesis about 64 days; Ap divide every 16 days; 6 stages.
- Intratesticular testosterone 50 × serum; exogenous testosterone causes azoospermia.
- FSH ≤7.6 IU/L and testis >4.6 cm → obstructive.
- AZFa/b deletion: do not operate; micro-TESE finds sperm in about half of NOA.

#### I-3a. Management of Peyronie's disease (SAQ)

- Ask the "straight penis" question; duplex before surgery.
- CCH: IMPRESS, stable 30–90° curve, intact erections.
- Surgery: stable ≥12 months; plication <60–70°, graft complex curves, prosthesis if ED.

#### I-3b. Treatment of renal abscess (SAQ)

- Now mostly ascending Gram-negative abscess in a damaged kidney.
- ≤3 cm antibiotics; 3–5 cm either; >5 cm percutaneous drainage.
- CT is the investigation of choice; follow until resolution.

#### I-3c. Catheter Associated Urinary Tract Infections (CAUTI) (SAQ)

- Bacteriuria 5–10% per catheter-day; duration is the key risk.
- Diagnosis: symptoms + ≥10³ CFU/mL from a fresh catheter.
- Remove catheter early; closed drainage; treat only symptomatic CAUTI, 7 or 10–14 days.

#### I-3d. Contrast Induced Nephropathy (CIN) (SAQ)

- CIN = rise ≥25% or 0.5 mg/dL in 48–72 h; now CA-AKI vs CI-AKI.
- Main risk: eGFR <30 (IV); <45 intra-arterial.
- Isotonic saline only proven; PRESERVE: bicarbonate and NAC useless.
- Metformin: withhold 48 h only if eGFR <30 or AKI.

#### I-3e. Transrectal ultrasound study of prostate (SAQ)

- 6–10 MHz; volume = 0.52 × T × AP × L; PSAD >0.15.
- 39% of cancers isoechoic; hypoechoic PZ lesion classic.
- Seminal vesicle >1.5 cm wide: ejaculatory duct obstruction.
