## II-1. Describe medical management of BPH and role of various surgical modalities in its management.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

### Definition
- **BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia)** = histological (stromal + epithelial hyperplasia of transition zone); **BPE (benign prostatic enlargement)**; **BPO (benign prostatic obstruction)** from BPE → **LUTS (lower urinary tract symptoms)**: storage, voiding, post-micturition.
- Goals: relieve bother, prevent progression (AUR (acute urinary retention), surgery).

### Drug targets
- **Static** (bulk, DHT (dihydrotestosterone)) → 5-ARIs (5α-reductase inhibitors), surgery; **dynamic** (α1A tone) → α-blockers; **bladder** → antimuscarinics, β3-agonists.

### Evaluation
- **IPSS (International Prostate Symptom Score)** 0–35: mild 0–7, moderate 8–19, severe 20–35; + quality-of-life question 0–6. FVC (frequency-volume chart), 3 days.
- DRE (digital rectal examination), urinalysis, **PSA (prostate-specific antigen)** (if life expectancy >10 y).
- Optional: creatinine, uroflowmetry (Qmax (maximum flow rate) <10 mL/s suggests BOO (bladder outlet obstruction)), PVR (post-void residual).
- **Before surgery** (AUA (American Urological Association)): prostate size/shape by imaging/cystoscopy; pressure-flow study if diagnosis uncertain.

### Medical management
**1. Watchful waiting** – mild/non-bothersome: fluid timing, ↓ caffeine/alcohol, double voiding.

**2. α1-blockers** – first line, moderate–severe bothersome LUTS; act within days.

| Drug | Dose | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Terazosin | 1→10 mg OD (once daily) | Non-selective; titrate; orthostatic hypotension |
| Doxazosin | 1→8 mg OD | Non-selective; titrate |
| Alfuzosin | 10 mg OD | Least ejaculatory dysfunction |
| Tamsulosin | 0.4 mg OD | α1A/α1D selective |
| Silodosin | 8 mg OD (4 mg renal impairment) | Most α1A selective; highest anejaculation |

- IPSS ↓ ~30–40%; **no ↓ volume or AUR/surgery risk**.
- Side effects: postural hypotension, dizziness, ejaculatory dysfunction.
- **IFIS (intraoperative floppy iris syndrome)** – strongest with tamsulosin; **defer starting α-blocker if cataract surgery planned**; inform ophthalmologist.

**3. 5-ARIs** – finasteride 5 mg (type 2); dutasteride 0.5 mg (types 1 + 2).
- For **enlarged prostate** (AUA: PSA >1.5 ng/mL or volume >30 mL; EAU (European Association of Urology): >40 mL).
- Volume ↓ **~20–25%** over 6–12 months; **PSA halved** (×2 after 6 months); AUR/surgery ↓ ~50%; finasteride for BPH haematuria.
- Side effects: ↓ libido, ED (erectile dysfunction), ejaculatory disorder, gynaecomastia; ↑ high-grade cancer detection → FDA (Food and Drug Administration) warning (2011).

**4. Combination α-blocker + 5-ARI**
- **MTOPS (Medical Therapy of Prostatic Symptoms; 2003)**: doxazosin + finasteride ↓ clinical progression **66%** vs placebo (doxazosin 39%, finasteride 34%).
- **CombAT (Combination of Avodart and Tamsulosin; 4-yr)**: dutasteride + tamsulosin, volume ≥30 mL; AUR/surgery ↓ **~66% vs tamsulosin**.
- Enlarged prostate at risk of progression; consider stopping α-blocker at 6–12 months.

**5. Storage symptoms**
- **Antimuscarinics** (solifenacin, tolterodine, oxybutynin) ± α-blocker for persistent OAB (overactive bladder); caution **PVR >250–300 mL**; cognitive side effects in elderly.
- **β3-agonists**: mirabegron 25–50 mg, vibegron 75 mg; no cognitive burden; monitor blood pressure (mirabegron).

**6. PDE5 (phosphodiesterase-5) inhibitor** – **tadalafil 5 mg OD**: ↓ LUTS (±ED), not Qmax; no nitrates; AUA: not with α-blocker.

**7. Nocturnal polyuria** – low-dose **desmopressin**; check **serum sodium** (baseline, first week, periodically).

**8. Phytotherapy** – AUA: **not recommended**.

```
LUTS → IPSS, DRE, urinalysis, PSA, FVC
  Mild → watchful waiting
  Voiding → α-blocker (± 5-ARI if >30–40 mL)
  Storage persists → antimuscarinic/β3 (check PVR)
  Nocturnal polyuria → desmopressin
  With ED → tadalafil 5 mg
  Failure/complications → surgery (size-based)
```

### Surgical management
**Indications**: refractory retention (failed TWOC (trial without catheter)), recurrent UTI (urinary tract infection), recurrent haematuria refractory to 5-ARI, bladder stones, renal insufficiency/upper tract dilatation from BPO; LUTS failing/declining medical therapy.

| Modality | Size (AUA) | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TURP (transurethral resection of prostate)** – M (monopolar)/B (bipolar) | **30–80 mL** | Reference standard | Bleeding, retrograde ejaculation ~65–75%, TUR (transurethral resection) syndrome (M), stricture, BNC (bladder neck contracture) |
| **TUIP (transurethral incision of prostate)** | **≤30 mL**, no median lobe | Less retrograde ejaculation | Higher retreatment |
| **PVP (photoselective vaporization) 180W** | 30–80 mL | Anticoagulated/high-risk | No histology |
| **HoLEP (holmium laser enucleation)/ThuLEP (thulium laser enucleation)** | **Size-independent** | Lowest retreatment, least bleeding, tissue | Learning curve; transient stress incontinence |
| **Simple prostatectomy** (open/robotic) | **>80–100 mL** | Complete removal; stones/diverticulum | Morbidity, transfusion |
| **Aquablation** | 30–80; consider **80–150 mL** | Heat-free, ejaculation preserved | Bleeding, GA (general anaesthesia) |
| **Rezūm (water vapour thermal therapy)** | 30–80 mL | Day care, treats median lobe | Delayed relief, catheter 3–7 days |
| **PUL (prostatic urethral lift)** | 30–80 mL, **no obstructing median lobe** | Local anaesthesia, sexual function preserved | Lower efficacy, higher retreatment |
| **iTIND** | 25–75 mL | Temporary (5–7 days) | Modest efficacy |
| **PAE (prostatic artery embolisation)** | Larger glands | No GA | Less effective |

**TUR syndrome** (M-TURP, 1.5% glycine): dilutional hyponatraemia, fluid overload, glycine toxicity (confusion, bradycardia, visual disturbance); risk ↑ resection >60–90 min, perforation. Treat: stop surgery, diuretics, 3% saline if severe, slow correction. **B-TURP (saline)** virtually eliminates it.

**Landmark trials**
- **GOLIATH**: PVP **non-inferior** to TURP (IPSS, Qmax); shorter catheter/stay.
- **WATER (Waterjet Ablation Therapy for Endoscopic Resection of prostate tissue)**: Aquablation vs TURP (30–80 mL) non-inferior, less anejaculation; **WATER II**: 80–150 mL.

### Complications
- Early: bleeding, TUR syndrome, sepsis, failed TWOC; late: retrograde ejaculation, transient incontinence, stricture, BNC.

### Follow-up
- IPSS at 4–12 weeks (α-blocker), 3–6 months (5-ARI), then yearly.

### Recent advances
- **Optilume BPH** (paclitaxel-coated balloon, PINNACLE trial) FDA-approved 2023.
- Office MISTs (minimally invasive surgical therapies) preserve ejaculation.

**Diagram to draw:** McNeal zones + size-based algorithm (≤30 TUIP → 30–80 TURP/PVP → >80 enucleation/simple prostatectomy).

> **Key points to remember:**
> - IPSS 0–35: 0–7 mild, 8–19 moderate, 20–35 severe.
> - 5-ARI: ~25% volume ↓, PSA halved, AUR/surgery ↓ ~50%; MTOPS combination ↓ progression 66%.
> - AUA sizes: TUIP ≤30; TURP/PVP/PUL/Rezūm/Aquablation 30–80; HoLEP size-independent; simple prostatectomy >80–100 mL.
> - IFIS with tamsulosin; B-TURP eliminates TUR syndrome; GOLIATH: PVP non-inferior.

## II-2. Discuss metabolic evaluation in urinary calculus disease.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

### Introduction
- Urolithiasis = systemic/metabolic disease; recurrence ~50% at 5–10 years without prevention.
- Aim: identify correctable risk factors → targeted diet + drugs.

### Physicochemical basis
- **Supersaturation** → nucleation → growth → aggregation → retention.
- Zones: undersaturated → metastable (inhibitors act) → unstable (spontaneous nucleation).
- Promoters: Ca, oxalate, uric acid, low volume, abnormal pH. **Inhibitors**: citrate, magnesium, pyrophosphate, Tamm-Horsfall protein.
- **RSS (relative supersaturation)** from 24-h urine for CaOx (calcium oxalate), CaP (calcium phosphate), uric acid – guides/monitors therapy (target <1).

### Who needs it
- **Basic – every stone former** (AUA (American Urological Association), EAU (European Association of Urology)).
- **Comprehensive (24-h urine)** – high-risk, recurrent, interested first-time formers.

**High-risk (EAU)**
- Early onset/children, family history, recurrent/short-interval recurrence, solitary kidney, bilateral/large burden, residual fragments.
- Stones: brushite, uric acid, infection, cystine.
- Disease: **primary hyperparathyroidism**, metabolic syndrome, nephrocalcinosis, **GI (gastrointestinal) disease**/bariatric surgery, sarcoidosis.
- Genetic: cystinuria, primary hyperoxaluria, **RTA (renal tubular acidosis) type 1**, Dent disease.
- Drugs: topiramate, acetazolamide, indinavir, triamterene, high-dose vitamin C/D.
- Anatomy: medullary sponge kidney, PUJO (pelviureteric junction obstruction), calyceal diverticulum, horseshoe kidney.

### Basic evaluation (all)
- **History**: onset, episodes, family history, diet (fluid, salt, animal protein, oxalate), drugs, bowel disease, gout, UTIs (urinary tract infections).
- **Imaging**: NCCT (non-contrast computed tomography) – burden, density (uric acid <500 HU (Hounsfield units)).
- **Serum**: creatinine, **calcium**, **uric acid**, electrolytes, **bicarbonate** (low → RTA, diarrhoea), phosphate; **intact PTH (parathyroid hormone)** if Ca high-normal/raised or hypercalciuria.
- **Urine**: **pH** (>6.5–7 → RTA/infection; <5.5 → uric acid), crystals (hexagonal = cystine; coffin-lid = struvite), culture (urease organisms – Proteus, Klebsiella).
- **Stone analysis** – first episode, and recurrence on therapy: **infrared spectroscopy or X-ray diffraction**.
- **Cystine screen**: cyanide-nitroprusside test → confirm with 24-h cystine.

### Comprehensive – 24-h urine
- **Two collections** (AUA one or two; EAU two), usual diet, ≥2–4 weeks after stone event, stone- and infection-free.
- Adequacy: **urinary creatinine** (men ~20–25, women ~15–20 mg/kg/d).

| Parameter | Desirable | Abnormality |
|---|---|---|
| Volume | **>2.5 L** | <2 L – commonest |
| Calcium | <250 mg (F), <300 mg (M), or **<4 mg/kg** | Hypercalciuria |
| Oxalate | **<40–45 mg** | Hyperoxaluria |
| Citrate | **>320 mg** | Hypocitraturia |
| Uric acid | <750 mg (F), **<800 mg (M)** | Hyperuricosuria |
| Sodium | <100 mmol/d | ↑ Ca excretion |
| pH | 5.8–6.2 | <5.5 uric acid; >6.5–7 CaP/RTA/infection |
| Magnesium | >70 mg | Hypomagnesiuria (GI) |
| Sulphate | – | ↑ = high animal protein |
| Cystine | Very low | Cystinuria (**>400 mg/d**) |

### Diagnostic categories
1. **Hypercalciuria** – absorptive, renal leak (↑ PTH, normal serum Ca), resorptive (primary hyperparathyroidism – ↑ Ca, ↑ PTH). Pak classification of limited therapeutic impact (thiazide works for all idiopathic). Others: sarcoidosis, vitamin D excess, immobilisation.
2. **Hyperoxaluria** – dietary (mild, 45–60 mg/d); **enteric** (fat malabsorption: fatty acids bind Ca → free oxalate absorbed in colon; + hypocitraturia, low volume); **primary** (PH (primary hyperoxaluria) 1–3; often >75–100 mg/d, childhood nephrocalcinosis, ESKD (end-stage kidney disease); genetic testing).
3. **Hypocitraturia** – idiopathic, **dRTA (distal RTA)**, chronic diarrhoea, hypokalaemia, high animal protein, topiramate.
4. **Hyperuricosuria** – purine diet, gout; promotes **CaOx** stones. **Uric acid stones**: main determinant **urine pH <5.5** (metabolic syndrome, diabetes).
5. **dRTA** – normal anion-gap acidosis, urine pH >5.5–6, hypocitraturia, CaP stones. Incomplete dRTA → **ammonium chloride test** (0.1 g/kg; pH fails to fall **<5.5**) or furosemide–fludrocortisone test.
6. **Cystinuria** – AR (autosomal recessive), COLA amino acids (cystine, ornithine, lysine, arginine); solubility ~250 mg/L → aim **<250 mg/L**.
7. **Infection stones** – urease organisms, pH >7, struvite; look for anatomic/neurogenic cause.

```
Stone → basic evaluation (history, serum, urine, stone analysis)
  Low risk → general advice (output >2.5 L, ↓ Na/protein, normal Ca)
  High risk/recurrent → two 24-h urines → specific abnormality
    → targeted diet + drugs → repeat 24-h urine ~6 months → yearly
```

### Treatment by abnormality

| Abnormality | Diet | Drug |
|---|---|---|
| Low volume | Output >2.5 L | – |
| Hypercalciuria | Na <2.3 g/d, normal Ca (1,000–1,200 mg/d), ↓ animal protein | **Thiazide** (HCTZ (hydrochlorothiazide) 25–50 mg, chlorthalidone 25 mg, indapamide 2.5 mg) + K citrate |
| Primary hyperparathyroidism | – | **Parathyroidectomy** |
| Hypocitraturia | ↑ fruit/vegetables | **K citrate** 30–60 mEq/d |
| Enteric hyperoxaluria | Low fat/oxalate, Ca with meals | Ca citrate, K citrate, cholestyramine |
| **PH1** | High fluid | **Pyridoxine**, **lumasiran** (RNAi (ribonucleic acid interference), 2020), nedosiran; liver ± kidney transplant |
| Hyperuricosuria (CaOx) | ↓ purine | **Allopurinol** 100–300 mg |
| Uric acid stones | – | **K citrate** to pH 6.0–6.5 ± allopurinol |
| dRTA | – | **K citrate** |
| Cystinuria | Fluid >3–4 L, ↓ Na/protein | Alkalinise **pH 7.0–7.5**; **tiopronin** if refractory |
| Infection | – | Complete clearance, antibiotics, acetohydroxamic acid |

### Special groups
- **Children**: **all need full metabolic evaluation**; spot urine ratios (Ca/oxalate/citrate to creatinine) if 24-h impractical; screen cystinuria, PH, RTA, Dent.
- **Pregnancy**: defer 24-h urine until **after delivery/lactation** (physiological hypercalciuria); avoid thiazides, allopurinol, tiopronin.

### Follow-up
- **Repeat 24-h urine within 6 months** (AUA), then annually; serum chemistry for drug toxicity (K with thiazide); periodic imaging; repeat stone analysis if no response.

### Recent advances
- **RNAi for PH1** (lumasiran, nedosiran) – marked ↓ urinary oxalate.
- **NOSTONE (2023)**: hydrochlorothiazide did not ↓ recurrence vs placebo.
- Genetic panels for early-onset/monogenic disease.

**Diagram to draw:** Supersaturation zones + basic vs comprehensive evaluation algorithm.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Two 24-h urines on usual diet, ≥4 weeks after event; creatinine for adequacy.
> - Targets: volume >2.5 L, Ca <250/300 mg, oxalate <40 mg, citrate >320 mg, uric acid <750/800 mg.
> - dRTA: NH4Cl load fails to lower pH <5.5; cystinuria: cystine <250 mg/L, pH 7.0–7.5, tiopronin.
> - Repeat 24-h urine within 6 months; lumasiran for PH1.

## II-3a. Management of priapism
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition
- Erection persisting **>4 hours** beyond or unrelated to sexual stimulation (AUA/SMSNA (American Urological Association/Sexual Medicine Society of North America) 2021).

### Classification
| | **Ischaemic (low-flow)** | **Non-ischaemic (high-flow)** | **Stuttering** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | Veno-occlusive, compartment syndrome | Cavernous artery–sinusoidal fistula | Recurrent self-limiting ischaemic |
| Cause | ICI (intracavernosal injection) drugs, SCD (sickle cell disease), antipsychotics, idiopathic | Perineal trauma (straddle) | SCD |
| Penis | Rigid, painful | Partially rigid, painless | Painful |
| Emergency | **Yes** | No | Prevent progression |

### Diagnosis
- History (duration, drugs, trauma, SCD); glans soft in ischaemic.
- **Corporal blood gas** – ischaemic: **pO2 <30, pCO2 >60 mmHg, pH <7.25** (arterial: >90, <40, 7.40).
- **Colour Doppler**: absent flow (ischaemic); high flow/fistula (non-ischaemic).
- CBC (complete blood count), Hb (haemoglobin) electrophoresis, toxicology.

### Ischaemic priapism
```
Dorsal penile block → aspiration (16–18G) ± saline irrigation
→ Phenylephrine 100–500 µg/mL, 1 mL every 3–5 min, up to ~1 h (monitor blood pressure/pulse)
→ Failure → distal shunt → proximal shunt
→ >36 h / failed shunts → immediate penile prosthesis
```
- **Distal shunts**: Winter (Tru-cut), Ebbehoj, T-shunt, Al-Ghorab (open); corporal tunnelling.
- **Proximal**: Quackels (cavernoso-spongiosal), Grayhack (cavernoso-saphenous).
- Oral therapy alone not recommended.
- Ischaemia >24–48 h → fibrosis, ED (erectile dysfunction); **early prosthesis** easier, preserves length (counsel re infection).
- **SCD**: intracavernosal therapy first + concurrent haematology care; do not delay.

### Non-ischaemic
- **Observation** first (many resolve).
- Persistent → **selective embolisation** (gelfoam/autologous clot – lower ED); surgical ligation rarely.

### Stuttering
- Each episode treated as ischaemic.
- Prevention: **daily PDE5i (phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitor)**, pseudoephedrine, self-injection phenylephrine, GnRH (gonadotropin-releasing hormone) agonists/antiandrogens (not if fertility desired); SCD – hydroxyurea.

### Recent advances
- Early prosthesis in refractory cases.

**Diagram to draw:** Penile cross-section – distal vs proximal shunts.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Ischaemic: pO2 <30, pCO2 >60, pH <7.25 – emergency.
> - Phenylephrine 100–500 µg/mL, 1 mL q3–5 min up to 1 h.
> - Distal shunts (Winter, Ebbehoj, T, Al-Ghorab) before proximal (Quackels, Grayhack).
> - Prosthesis for >36 h; high-flow → observe/embolise.

## II-3b. Renal tubular acidosis
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition
- **RTA (renal tubular acidosis)** = **hyperchloraemic, normal AG (anion gap) metabolic acidosis** from impaired HCO3 reabsorption or H+ excretion, with preserved GFR (glomerular filtration rate).

### Types

| | **Type 1 (distal)** | **Type 2 (proximal)** | **Type 4 (hyperkalaemic)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Defect | ↓ H+ secretion, collecting duct | ↓ HCO3 reabsorption, PCT (proximal convoluted tubule) | Aldosterone deficiency/resistance → ↓ NH4+ |
| Urine pH | **>5.5 always** | <5.5 once HCO3 below threshold | <5.5 |
| Serum K | Low | Low | **High** |
| Urine citrate | **Low** | Normal/high | Normal |
| Stones/nephrocalcinosis | **Common (CaP)** | Rare | Absent |
| Causes | Genetic, Sjogren's, SLE (systemic lupus erythematosus), amphotericin, lithium, MSK (medullary sponge kidney), obstruction | Fanconi, myeloma, acetazolamide, tenofovir | Diabetes, **obstructive uropathy**, ACEi (angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors), spironolactone |
| Treatment | **K citrate 1–2 mEq/kg/d** | Alkali 10–15 mEq/kg/d + K; thiazide | Low K diet, loop diuretic, **fludrocortisone** |

- **Type 3**: rare, carbonic anhydrase II deficiency – mixed, osteopetrosis.
- **Incomplete dRTA (distal RTA)**: normal HCO3 but cannot acidify urine – common in recurrent CaP (calcium phosphate) stone formers.

### Diagnosis
- Normal AG acidosis: AG = Na – (Cl + HCO3).
- **UAG (urine anion gap) = (Na + K) – Cl**: **negative** → GI (gastrointestinal) loss/proximal; **positive** → distal/type 4.
- Fresh urine pH (pH meter).
- **Ammonium chloride test** (0.1 g/kg): pH fails to fall **<5.5** = dRTA.
- **Furosemide–fludrocortisone test** (40 mg + 1 mg): better tolerated.
- FE (fractional excretion) HCO3 >15% on bicarbonate loading = proximal.
- US (ultrasound)/CT (computed tomography) for nephrocalcinosis; 24-h urine citrate/calcium.

### Urological relevance
- dRTA → **recurrent CaP stones, nephrocalcinosis**, hypocitraturia.
- **MSK** associated with incomplete dRTA.
- **Obstructive uropathy** (PUV (posterior urethral valves), BPO (benign prostatic obstruction)) → type 4; improves after relief.
- **Topiramate/acetazolamide** → CaP stones.
- **Urinary diversion** → hyperchloraemic acidosis mimicking RTA.
- K citrate preferred over sodium bicarbonate (sodium ↑ Ca excretion).

### Recent advances
- Genetic testing for inherited dRTA; prolonged-release alkali (ADV7103).

**Diagram to draw:** Nephron – PCT (type 2), α-intercalated cell (type 1), aldosterone site (type 4).

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Type 1: urine pH >5.5, hypokalaemia, hypocitraturia, CaP stones/nephrocalcinosis.
> - Type 4: hyperkalaemia, no stones; obstructive uropathy.
> - Positive UAG = distal RTA; NH4Cl 0.1 g/kg – pH fails to fall <5.5.
> - Treat dRTA with potassium citrate.

## II-3c. Millin's prostatectomy
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition
- **Retropubic extravesical simple prostatectomy**: adenoma enucleated through **transverse anterior capsulotomy**, bladder not opened.
- **Terence Millin (Lancet, 1945)**. Contrast **Freyer's transvesical** (through opened bladder).

### Indications
- Gland **>80–100 mL** (AUA (American Urological Association)) when HoLEP (holmium laser enucleation of prostate) unavailable.
- Usual BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia) surgery indications (retention, recurrent infection/haematuria, renal impairment, bladder stones).
- **Best for** large lateral lobes, small/no median lobe; concomitant hernia repair.
- **Relative contraindications**: large median lobe, diverticulum/large stones (transvesical preferred), small fibrous gland, prior pelvic surgery/radiation, prostate cancer.

### Steps
1. Supine; catheter; Pfannenstiel/lower midline.
2. Extraperitoneal – develop **space of Retzius**.
3. **Haemostatic sutures** across anterior capsule proximal and distal to incision (Santorini plexus) ± lateral pedicles.
4. **Transverse capsulotomy** ~1–2 cm distal to bladder neck.
5. Scissors to develop plane → **finger enucleation**.
6. **Divide urethra at apex under vision** (protects sphincter).
7. Inspect ureteric orifices; **posterior bladder neck wedge excision** (prevents contracture).
8. Figure-of-eight sutures at **5 and 7 o'clock**.
9. 20–22 Fr three-way catheter; **watertight capsule closure**; drain.

### Millin vs Freyer

| | **Millin** | **Freyer** |
|---|---|---|
| Bladder | Not opened | Opened |
| Apex | Divided under vision – better continence | Blind finger fracture |
| Haemostasis | Fossa visible | Less visible |
| Stones/diverticulum, large median lobe | Difficult | Ideal |
| Catheter/stay | Shorter | Longer (SP (suprapubic) + urethral) |
| Specific risk | Santorini bleeding, osteitis pubis | Bladder spasm, SP fistula |

### Complications
- Early: **haemorrhage/transfusion**, clot retention, infection, leak, DVT (deep vein thrombosis).
- Late: **retrograde ejaculation** (most), bladder neck contracture, stricture, stress incontinence/ED (erectile dysfunction) (uncommon), **osteitis pubis**.
- Durable outcomes, low retreatment.

### Modern alternatives
- **HoLEP/ThuLEP (thulium laser enucleation of prostate)** – size-independent, less bleeding, shorter stay; EAU (European Association of Urology) first choice >80 mL where available.
- **RASP (robotic-assisted simple prostatectomy)** – transvesical or Millin-type; less blood loss, shorter stay, allows diverticulectomy/stone removal.

**Diagram to draw:** Sagittal section – transverse capsulotomy below bladder neck, enucleation plane.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Millin 1945: retropubic, transverse anterior capsulotomy, bladder not opened.
> - Apical urethral division under vision; 5 and 7 o'clock sutures; bladder neck wedge.
> - Freyer (transvesical) preferred for median lobe, stones, diverticulum.
> - HoLEP and robotic simple prostatectomy are modern alternatives for >80–100 mL.

## II-3d. Energy sources in laparoscopic urology
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Introduction
- Aims: **cutting, coagulation, vessel sealing**.
- ~60 °C – protein denaturation (coagulation); ~100 °C – vaporization (cutting); >200 °C – carbonization.

### Types

| Device | Principle | Use | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Monopolar** | RF (radiofrequency) current from active electrode through patient to return pad; cut (continuous, low-voltage) vs coag (interrupted, high-voltage) | Cheap, versatile (hook, scissors) | Greatest lateral spread; stray current; pacemaker interference |
| **Bipolar** | Current between jaws only | Near nerves/bowel | Slow; no cutting; charring |
| **Advanced bipolar** (LigaSure) | Pressure + impedance-controlled energy fusing collagen | Seals **up to 7 mm**; nephrectomy, RPLND (retroperitoneal lymph node dissection) | Cost; spread ~1–3 mm |
| **Ultrasonic** (Harmonic) | Blade ~55.5 kHz, coaptive coagulation <100 °C | Cut + seal **up to 5 mm**; no current through patient | Hot blade |
| **Hybrid** (Thunderbeat) | Ultrasonic + bipolar | Seals up to 7 mm | Cost |
| **Lasers** | Holmium, thulium, KTP (potassium titanyl phosphate) | Stricture incision, endourology | Smoke, eye safety |
| **Argon beam** | Monopolar via ionized argon, non-contact | Parenchymal bed haemostasis (partial nephrectomy) | **Gas embolism** |
| **Hydro-jet** | Water jet, preserves vessels | Partial nephrectomy | Not haemostatic |
| Ablation | RFA (radiofrequency ablation), microwave, cryo | Small renal masses | – |

### Mechanisms of electrosurgical injury
- Often unrecognised (outside view) → delayed bowel perforation.
1. **Insulation failure** – current leaks to bowel.
2. **Direct coupling** – active electrode touches another metal instrument.
3. **Capacitive coupling** – current induced through intact insulation; **avoid hybrid trocars** (metal cannula + plastic anchor).
4. **Thermal spread** – ureter, nerves, bowel; residual heat of tips.
5. **Return electrode burns** – poor pad contact.
6. Smoke; fire in oxygen-rich setting.

### Safety measures
- **AEM (active electrode monitoring)** – shuts generator on insulation failure/capacitive coupling.
- **REM (return electrode contact quality monitoring)**.
- Lowest power, brief activation; tip in view.
- Bipolar near critical structures; athermal nerve-sparing in prostatectomy.
- Pacemaker/ICD (implantable cardioverter-defibrillator): bipolar preferred.

### Recent advances
- Smart generators with real-time impedance feedback; robotic vessel sealers; ultrasonic sealing up to 7 mm.

**Diagram to draw:** Monopolar vs bipolar circuit; capacitive coupling across hybrid trocar.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - LigaSure: vessels up to 7 mm; Harmonic: up to 5 mm at ~55.5 kHz.
> - Stray current injury: insulation failure, direct coupling, capacitive coupling.
> - Avoid hybrid trocars; use AEM and REM pads.
> - Argon beam – risk of gas embolism.

## II-3e. Role of vascular grafts in urology
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Introduction
- Grafts replace, bypass or extend vessels when primary repair impossible – transplantation, extended oncological resections.

### Types

| Type | Examples | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Autologous vein** | Saphenous, gonadal, iliac/renal vein | Low infection, best small-vessel patency | Limited length |
| **Autologous artery** | Internal iliac, inferior epigastric, Carrel aortic patch | Renal artery reconstruction; grows in children | Limited availability |
| **Synthetic** | **PTFE (polytetrafluoroethylene)**, **Dacron** | Off-the-shelf; IVC (inferior vena cava), aorta | Infection, thrombosis in small vessels; not in contaminated field |
| **Biological** | Cryopreserved cadaveric vessels; bovine pericardium | Infected fields | Availability, degeneration |

### Applications
1. **Renal transplantation** – multiple arteries: bench side-to-side conjoining, end-to-side implantation of polar artery, inferior epigastric to lower-pole artery; short right vein → gonadal/saphenous/IVC extension; Carrel patch; synthetic aorto-iliac bypass for diseased recipient iliacs.
2. **Renovascular** – stenosis (FMD (fibromuscular dysplasia), atherosclerosis), **renal artery aneurysm**: aortorenal bypass (saphenous, hypogastric, PTFE); ex vivo bench repair.
3. **Autotransplantation** – ureteric loss, branch disease.
4. **RCC (renal cell carcinoma) with IVC thrombus** – cavotomy + primary closure; **patch cavoplasty** if lumen narrowed >50%; **PTFE IVC replacement** if wall invaded and collaterals inadequate; infrarenal IVC often ligated if collaterals; level III–IV may need cardiopulmonary bypass.
5. **Post-chemotherapy RPLND (retroperitoneal lymph node dissection)** – aorta encased (GCT (germ cell tumour)) → **Dacron/PTFE interposition graft**.
6. **Dialysis access** – PTFE AV (arteriovenous) graft.
7. **Penile revascularisation** – inferior epigastric to dorsal artery (post-trauma arteriogenic ED (erectile dysfunction)); rare.
8. **Vascular injury** – laparoscopic/pelvic surgery, trauma: saphenous/PTFE interposition.

### Principles
- **Autologous** in contaminated/urine-exposed fields and small vessels; synthetic for large high-flow vessels.
- Tension-free, spatulated, intima-to-intima; polypropylene monofilament.
- Heparin during clamping; antiplatelet/anticoagulation as indicated.
- Omental wrap isolates synthetic graft from urinary tract/bowel; antibiotics; Doppler surveillance.

### Complications
- Thrombosis, stenosis, haemorrhage, pseudoaneurysm, **graft infection** (often explantation), graft-enteric/urinary fistula, limb oedema (IVC ligation).

### Recent advances
- Robotic IVC thrombectomy; human acellular vessel for dialysis access/trauma.

**Diagram to draw:** Bench conjoining/end-to-side reconstruction of two renal arteries.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Autologous (saphenous, hypogastric, gonadal) for small vessels/contaminated fields; PTFE/Dacron for IVC/aorta.
> - Transplant: Carrel patch, bench reconstruction, gonadal/IVC extension of short right vein.
> - IVC: patch cavoplasty if narrowed; PTFE replacement if wall invaded.
> - Post-chemo RPLND may need aortic graft replacement.

## II-3f. Transrectal ultrasonography
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Introduction
- Real-time imaging of prostate/seminal vesicles via rectum; piezoelectric crystals; ↑ frequency = ↑ resolution, ↓ penetration.
- Probe **6–10 MHz (commonly 7.5)**, **biplanar** (transverse + sagittal), end/side-fire; Doppler, elastography.

### Technique
- Left lateral; DRE (digital rectal examination) first; enema; transverse base→apex, then sagittal.

### Zonal anatomy (McNeal)
| Zone | Volume | Echo | Cancers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Peripheral | ~70% | Isoechoic | ~70% |
| Transition | ~5–10% (BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia)) | Hypoechoic, heterogeneous | ~20–25% |
| Central | ~25% | Slightly hyperechoic | ~5–10% |

### Measurements
- **Volume = 0.52 × L × W × H** (length × width × height; 1 mL ≈ 1 g).
- **PSAD (PSA (prostate-specific antigen) density) >0.15** suspicious; used after MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) (PI-RADS (Prostate Imaging Reporting and Data System) 3).
- Cancer: **hypoechoic peripheral lesion** (low specificity).

### Indications
- **Biopsy guidance**; volume for BPH surgery planning.
- **Ejaculatory duct obstruction**: seminal vesicles >1.5 cm AP (anteroposterior), midline cysts; vesiculography.
- Prostatic abscess drainage.
- **Brachytherapy**, **HIFU (high-intensity focused ultrasound)**, cryotherapy, fiducials/rectal spacer.

### TRUS (transrectal ultrasound) biopsy
- **12-core systematic** (base, mid, apex; medial + lateral) ± targeted.
- **Periprostatic nerve block** (lidocaine at base–seminal vesicle junction).
- Complications: haematospermia (commonest), haematuria, rectal bleeding, **sepsis** (FQ (fluoroquinolone)-resistant E. coli), retention.
- Prophylaxis: povidone-iodine rectal cleansing; targeted/augmented prophylaxis; FQ restricted (EMA (European Medicines Agency) 2019).

### Current shift
- **EAU (European Association of Urology) 2024/25: transperineal biopsy recommended** – lower infection; prophylaxis may be omitted.
- **Pre-biopsy mpMRI (multiparametric MRI)** + targeted biopsy:
  - **PROMIS (2017)**: mpMRI sensitivity for csPCa (clinically significant prostate cancer) 93% vs TRUS biopsy 48%.
  - **PRECISION (2018)**: MRI-targeted detected more csPCa (38% vs 26%), less insignificant cancer.

### Recent advances
- **Micro-ultrasound (29 MHz)** – OPTIMUM RCT (randomised controlled trial, 2025): non-inferior to MRI-targeted biopsy.
- Contrast-enhanced ultrasound, shear-wave elastography.

**Diagram to draw:** Transverse/sagittal zonal views; 12-core template.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Biplanar probe 6–10 MHz; volume = 0.52 × L × W × H; PSAD >0.15 suspicious.
> - 12-core systematic biopsy with periprostatic block.
> - EAU: transperineal preferred (lower sepsis); FQ prophylaxis restricted.
> - PROMIS and PRECISION established MRI-first pathway.
