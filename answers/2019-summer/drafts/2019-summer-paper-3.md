## Q1. Discuss differential diagnosis of small renal tumours. Describe techniques to achieve hemostasis during laparoscopic partial nephrectomy.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

### Definition and biology
- **SRM (small renal mass):** an enhancing solid or complex cystic renal mass **≤4 cm (cT1a)**, usually found incidentally.
- **Benign share falls with size:** 20–30% of masses ≤4 cm and about 40% of masses ≤2 cm are benign. At ≥7 cm few are benign and about half are high grade.
- Male sex triples the risk of malignancy. Under 2% of small cancers metastasise.

### Differential diagnosis
| Group | Entities | Pointers |
|---|---|---|
| RCC (renal cell carcinoma) | **Clear cell** (commonest), papillary (10–15%), chromophobe (3–5%), cystic RCC, rare molecular subtypes | Clear cell enhances strongly; papillary enhances weakly |
| Other malignant | Urothelial carcinoma, lymphoma, metastasis, sarcoma, epithelioid AML (angiomyolipoma) | Infiltrative growth |
| Benign solid | Oncocytoma, fat-poor AML, papillary adenoma, metanephric adenoma | See below |
| Cystic | Haemorrhagic or complex cyst, cystic nephroma | Bosniak category |
| Inflammatory, vascular | Abscess, focal pyelonephritis, tuberculosis; aneurysm, AVM (arteriovenous malformation), infarct, pseudotumour | Clinical picture |

- **Oncocytoma:** commonest benign enhancing SRM (up to half of benign SRMs). Central stellate scar and segmental enhancement inversion suggest it but are not specific. A core cannot separate it from chromophobe RCC, which has oncocytic areas. Sestamibi SPECT (single-photon emission computed tomography) is under evaluation.
- **Fat-poor AML:** no visible fat (fat below −20 HU [Hounsfield units] diagnoses classic AML); homogeneous, T2-dark, mimics RCC; biopsy HMB-45 and Melan-A positive.
- **Also know:** metanephric adenoma (hypovascular, polycythaemia, WT1 positive); papillary adenoma (≤15 mm).

### Narrowing the differential
- **Multiphase CT (computed tomography):** enhancement over 15–20 HU means solid tissue. MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) helps below 2 cm.
- **RMB (renal mass biopsy)** if it will change management (AUA [American Urological Association]); seeding almost unreported; non-diagnostic about 14%.
- Then surveillance, ablation or **PN (partial nephrectomy)**, preferred for cT1a.

### Haemostasis in LPN (laparoscopic partial nephrectomy)
**Principle:** a clear field for excision, then secure closure of the bed. Plan with R.E.N.A.L. score and 3D (three-dimensional) CT; intraoperative ultrasound.

**1. Vascular control**
| Technique | Method | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Main artery ± vein clamp | Bulldog clamps, or laparoscopic Satinsky en bloc | Artery-only equals artery–vein. Aim warm ischaemia ≤25 min (trifecta) |
| Selective or super-selective | Clamp the tumour's segmental branch; 3D CT, NIRF (near-infrared fluorescence) | Avoids global ischaemia; no functional gain in an RCT (randomised controlled trial) |
| Off-clamp ("zero ischaemia") | No clamping; small exophytic tumours | More blood loss; same long-term eGFR (estimated glomerular filtration rate) |
| Early unclamping | Release after the inner suture layer | Shorter ischaemia; possibly fewer pseudoaneurysms |
| Cold ischaemia | Ice slush, cold saline via ureteric catheter, cold arterial perfusion | Solitary kidney, CKD (chronic kidney disease), long repair; benefit unproven |

- Function depends mainly on parenchyma preserved. Mannitol: no benefit in an RCT. **Also know:** parenchymal clamp for peripheral tumours.

**2. Excision:** score the capsule, cut with cold scissors, clip visible vessels. Enucleation along the pseudocapsule bleeds less.

**3. Renorrhaphy (most reliable step)**
- **Inner layer:** running 3-0 or 2-0 absorbable suture closes cut vessels and collecting system (opened in about 60%).
- **Outer layer, sliding-clip technique:** mattress sutures anchored by a locking clip; clips slid down to the capsule give tension without knots. Pledgets prevent capsular tears; oxidised cellulose bolster fills the defect.
- **Barbed suture:** holds tension without knots, so ischaemia is shorter.
- Single layer gave better function and fewer pseudoaneurysms than two layers.

**4. Energy:** argon beam or bipolar coagulation of the bed. Argon can destroy collecting-system sutures and adds gas, so vent the ports.

**5. Haemostatic agents (adjuncts, no proven benefit in RCTs):** gelatin–thrombin matrix; fibrin sealant; oxidised cellulose; PEG (polyethylene glycol) hydrogel; cyanoacrylate. Sutureless closure only for selected small tumours.

**6. Final check:** unclamp, inspect, **lower pneumoperitoneum** to unmask venous bleeding; drain.

### Postoperative bleeding
- Early (hypotension, falling haemoglobin): resuscitate, CT, angioembolisation.
- **Delayed haematuria (about day 12):** pseudoaneurysm or arteriovenous fistula; selective angioembolisation preserves function. Endophytic and posterior tumours carry higher risk.

**Diagram to draw:** sliding-clip renorrhaphy over a bolster, with bulldog clamp on the artery.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - SRM ≤4 cm: 20–30% benign; ≤2 cm about 40%.
> - Oncocytoma is the commonest benign mimic; biopsy cannot exclude chromophobe RCC.
> - Trifecta: negative margin, no major complication, warm ischaemia ≤25 min.
> - Delayed haematuria after PN means pseudoaneurysm: angioembolise.

## Q2. Describe evaluation of SUI in females. Discuss surgical therapy of SUI in Males.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

### Part A: Evaluation of SUI (stress urinary incontinence) in women
- **Definition (ICS [International Continence Society]):** involuntary leakage on effort, exertion, sneezing or coughing.
- **Mechanisms:** urethral **hypermobility** (loss of suburethral support, DeLancey hammock) and **ISD (intrinsic sphincter deficiency)**. Most women have some ISD.

**History**
- Leakage with cough or activity means SUI; after urgency, UUI (urgency urinary incontinence); both, MUI (mixed). A positive SUI history predicts urodynamic SUI in about three-quarters.
- Severity (pads/day), bother; voiding symptoms, prolapse, obstetric history, prior surgery or radiation, neurological disease, drugs.
- **Questionnaires:** ICIQ-UI SF (International Consultation on Incontinence Questionnaire–UI Short Form); UDI-6 (Urogenital Distress Inventory: bother); IIQ-7 (Incontinence Impact Questionnaire: impact). **Bladder diary** (3 days).

**Examination (comfortably full bladder)**
- Neurological (S2–S4 sensation, anal tone, bulbocavernosus reflex); atrophy; prolapse by **POP-Q (Pelvic Organ Prolapse Quantification)**.
- **Cough stress test:** cough supine, then standing if negative. Leakage synchronous with cough confirms SUI. Reduce prolapse to unmask **occult SUI**.
- **Q-tip test:** swab to bladder neck, Valsalva; rotation **>30°** = hypermobility. Visual mobility examination is now routine.

**Tests**
| Test | Method and use |
|---|---|
| Urinalysis | Required; exclude infection, haematuria |
| PVR (post-void residual) | Required before surgery; ultrasound; repeat if high (no agreed cut-off) |
| 1-hour pad test (ICS) | 500 mL fluid, standard exercises; >1.4 g positive; 1–10 g mild, 11–50 g moderate, >50 g severe |
| 24-hour pad test | Normal activities; more reproducible |
| Cystoscopy | Haematuria, irritative symptoms, previous mesh |

**Urodynamics (UDS): not routine.** VALUE trial (2012): office evaluation was non-inferior to UDS before surgery for uncomplicated SUI.
- **Indications (AUA [American Urological Association]/SUFU [Society of Urodynamics, Female Pelvic Medicine and Urogenital Reconstruction] 2023):** unclear diagnosis or SUI not shown; urgency-predominant MUI; neurogenic bladder; high PVR or voiding dysfunction; prolapse stage ≥3 without demonstrable SUI; failed previous surgery.
- **ALPP/VLPP (abdominal/Valsalva leak point pressure; McGuire 1993):** <60 cmH2O = ISD; 60–90 equivocal; ≥90 = little ISD. A low MUCP (maximum urethral closure pressure, <20 cmH2O) also suggests ISD.

### Part B: Surgical therapy of male SUI
- **Aetiology:** commonest after **radical prostatectomy (RP)**, which removes the internal sphincter, leaving continence to the rhabdosphincter. Also TURP (transurethral resection of the prostate), radiation, pelvic fracture.
- **Work-up:** 24-h pad weight (mild <150 g, moderate <400 g, severe above); standing cough test; **cystoscopy** (anastomotic stricture, sphincter coaptation); UDS if mixed or poor contractility.
- **Timing:** PFMT (pelvic floor muscle training) first; continence improves for up to 2 years, but do not delay surgery beyond 6 months for severe leak.

**Devices**
| Device | Mechanism | Use and results |
|---|---|---|
| **Transobturator sling (AdVance XP)** | Repositions the bulb 3–4 cm proximally; needs residual sphincter | Mild–moderate, no radiation; cure about 60% |
| Quadratic sling (Virtue) | Compression plus elevation | Mild; limited data |
| Adjustable sling (ATOMS, adjustable transobturator male system) | Cushion adjusted via scrotal port | Mild–moderate; about two-thirds dry |
| ProACT balloons | Bladder-neck compression | More erosion |
| Bulking agents | Submucosal bulk | Small leaks, unfit men; rarely durable |
| **AUS (artificial urinary sphincter)** | Circumferential compression | **Gold standard**; all severities, after radiation |

**AMS 800 AUS**
- **Components:** urethral **cuff** (bulbar, usually 4.0–4.5 cm), **PRB (pressure-regulating balloon) 61–70 cmH2O** (51–60 high risk; 71–80 bladder neck) in a preperitoneal pocket, scrotal **pump**. Filled with 23 mL.
- **Technique:** perineal incision; cuff on proximal bulbar urethra; **deactivate for 4–6 weeks**.
- **Needs** dexterity and cognition. Social continence (0–1 pad) 55–77%; satisfaction about 90%; lifespan 7–10 years.
- **Complications:** infection 1–3% (explant; reimplant after 3–6 months); **erosion** 2–15% (explant, catheter about 3 weeks); urethral **atrophy** (late recurrent leak); mechanical failure; retention.
- **Erosion risks:** radiation, prior erosion, urethroplasty; use **transcorporal cuff** (tunica adds bulk) or lower PRB. Atrophy: smaller or tandem cuff.

**Algorithm (AUA/SUFU 2019)**
```
Severe SUI or any radiotherapy -> AUS
Moderate, no radiotherapy      -> AUS or male sling
Mild, no radiotherapy          -> sling (or PFMT, AUS)
Sling failure                  -> AUS
```
- **MASTER RCT (randomised controlled trial):** sling was **non-inferior** to AUS for continence at 12 months; AUS remains preferred for heavy leak (>250 g/day) or radiation.

**Diagram to draw:** AMS 800 (cuff, pump, PRB) and transobturator sling path.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Q-tip >30° = hypermobility; VLPP <60 = ISD.
> - VALUE trial: UDS not needed in uncomplicated SUI.
> - AUS PRB 61–70 cmH2O; deactivate 4–6 weeks.
> - Radiation or severe SUI: AUS first; MASTER: sling non-inferior.

## Q3a. Prepucioplasty
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

*Read as: preputioplasty, a foreskin-preserving operation for phimosis.*

### Definition
- An operation that widens the tight preputial ring but keeps the foreskin. It is an alternative to circumcision.

### Background
- **Physiological phimosis** at birth is due to glans–preputial adhesions. Smegma and erections separate them. About 90% retract by 3 years; fewer than 1% are phimotic at 17 years.
- **Secondary phimosis** follows scarring: forced retraction, balanoposthitis or BXO (balanitis xerotica obliterans).

### Indications
- Symptomatic phimosis: recurrent balanoposthitis, UTI (urinary tract infection), paraphimosis, painful erection.
- Failed **topical steroid** (betamethasone 0.05% twice daily for about 4 weeks with gentle retraction). About 1 in 6 fail.
- Wish to keep the foreskin (cultural reasons, cosmesis).

### Contraindications
- **BXO** with scarring: circumcision is preferred. Triradiate incision with triamcinolone has been reported in selected boys.
- Physiological non-retractility needs only reassurance. Treat active infection first.

### Techniques
| Technique | Method |
|---|---|
| Dorsal slit with transverse closure | Longitudinal incision through the ring, closed transversely (Heineke–Mikulicz principle) |
| Triple incision | Three longitudinal cuts over the ring, each closed transversely |
| Y–V plasty | V-flap of outer skin advanced into a Y-cut |

- **Principle:** cutting the ring lengthwise and closing it crosswise widens its circumference.
- General anaesthesia or penile block; incise only the band; confirm retraction; absorbable sutures.

### Postoperative care
- **Daily retraction from the first week**, continued for weeks, prevents re-stenosis. A topical steroid may help.

### Outcomes and complications
- Most achieve a retractile foreskin. It is quicker and less painful than circumcision.
- Complications: re-phimosis (needs circumcision), bleeding, infection, poor cosmesis, paraphimosis.
- **Also know:** preputial reconstruction during hypospadias repair is also called preputioplasty; dehiscence is its risk.

**Diagram to draw:** dorsal incision through the ring, closed transversely.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Steroid first; about 1 in 6 fail.
> - Cut lengthwise, close crosswise; daily retraction after.
> - BXO: circumcision preferred.

## Q3b. Baceriuria and use of antibiotic policy in pregnancy
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

*Read as: bacteriuria in pregnancy and the antibiotic policy for it.*

### Definition
- **ASB (asymptomatic bacteriuria):** ≥10⁵ CFU (colony-forming units)/mL of one organism without urinary symptoms.

### Why pregnancy matters
- Prevalence is **not raised** (about 2–7%), but ASB **persists and ascends**.
- **Changes:** progesterone relaxes ureteric muscle and the uterus compresses the ureters at the pelvic brim (right more), causing stasis and hydroureteronephrosis. GFR (glomerular filtration rate) rises 30–50%, lowering drug levels.
- Untreated ASB led to **pyelonephritis** in 15–45% (historical), mostly in the third trimester, causing **preterm birth and low birth weight**.
- **Cochrane 2019:** treatment reduces pyelonephritis, preterm delivery and low birth weight.

### Screening
- **Urine culture at 12–16 weeks** or first visit (USPSTF [US Preventive Services Task Force]); dipstick misses cases.

### Antibiotic policy
| Safe | Restricted or avoided |
|---|---|
| Penicillins (amoxicillin), cephalosporins: throughout | **Nitrofurantoin:** stop near term (from 35 weeks): neonatal haemolysis (G6PD [glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase] deficiency) |
| Fosfomycin: cystitis only | **TMP-SMX (trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole):** avoid 1st trimester (folate, neural tube); caution near term (kernicterus) |
| Gentamicin: pyelonephritis | **Fluoroquinolones** (cartilage), **tetracyclines** (teeth, bone), chloramphenicol: avoid |

- ACOG (American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists) 2017: nitrofurantoin and sulfonamides first-line in 2nd–3rd trimester; 1st only if no alternative.
- **ASB or cystitis:** culture-directed, **4–7 days** (cures more than single dose); **repeat culture** after treatment.

### Pyelonephritis
- **Admit**; IV (intravenous) ampicillin plus gentamicin, ceftriaxone or aztreonam, then oral cephalexin; 10–14 days. No improvement by 72 h: renal ultrasound.

### Prophylaxis
- After one pyelonephritis or two cystitis episodes: nightly nitrofurantoin or cephalexin until delivery (Cochrane 2015: close surveillance equally effective).
- **Also know:** diversion, augmentation, SCI (spinal cord injury): treat ASB, consider prophylaxis.

**Diagram to draw:** right hydroureter at the pelvic brim.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Screen by culture at 12–16 weeks; treat all ASB.
> - β-lactams safe throughout; no fluoroquinolones or tetracyclines.
> - Nitrofurantoin not near term; TMP not in 1st trimester.

## Q3c. Accessories for RIRS
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Introduction
- RIRS (retrograde intrarenal surgery) is flexible ureteroscopy in the kidney. Its single **3.6 Fr channel** is shared by accessories and irrigation.

### Guidewires (0.018–0.038 inch, 145–260 cm)
| Type | Construction | Use | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hydrophilic | Nitinol core, slippery when wet | Bypassing impacted stones, tortuous ureter | Displaces easily |
| PTFE (polytetrafluoroethylene)-coated | Steel or nitinol | Safety and working wire | Damages scope channel on backloading |
| **Hybrid** | Floppy hydrophilic tip, stiff PTFE shaft | Both roles | — |

- **Safety wire** keeps access and allows stenting after injury (AUA [American Urological Association], EAU [European Association of Urology]).
- **Also know:** open-ended or dual-lumen catheter (pyelogram, second wire); stiff wire for the sheath; balloon dilators.

### UAS (ureteral access sheath)
- Hydrophilic sheath with tapered obturator; usually **12/14 Fr** (inner/outer); tip below the PUJ (pelviureteric junction).
- **Pros:** lower intrarenal pressure and temperature; repeated passes; less scope wear.
- **Cons:** ureteric injury in nearly half, severe in about **1 in 8** (less if pre-stented); no proven fall in infection.
- **Suction sheaths and FANS (flexible and navigable suction access sheath):** aspirate dust and keep pressure low.

### Retrieval devices
- **Nitinol tipless baskets** (about 1.5–2.4 Fr): kink-resistant, open flush in calyces; relocate lower-pole stones. Small baskets allow more flow but are fragile. Graspers release easily.

### Laser and fibres
- **Ho:YAG (holmium:yttrium-aluminium-garnet)** or **thulium fibre laser**; small-core fibres (about 200 μm) keep deflection and flow; **≤10 W in the ureter**.

### Irrigation and antiretropulsion
- Gravity, pressure bag, hand or foot pumps (hand pumps: more fever); pressure sensors emerging.
- Stone Cone, NTrap, BackStop gel: mainly ureteric stones.

### Stents
- **Double-J**; AUA: may omit after uncomplicated URS (ureteroscopy).

**Diagram to draw:** sheath over wire, tipless basket in lower calyx.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Hydrophilic wire passes; PTFE wire stays.
> - 12/14 Fr sheath lowers pressure; severe injury about 1 in 8.
> - Nitinol tipless basket for calyces; ≤10 W in ureter.

## Q3d. Immediate post TURBT intravesical Therapy for TCC
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Introduction
- After TURBT (transurethral resection of bladder tumour), NMIBC (non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer) recurs in 40–60%. Many early recurrences come from **floating cells implanting** on the raw bed.
- A single chemotherapy instillation (SI) kills them before implantation. TCC (transitional cell carcinoma) is now called urothelial carcinoma.

### Rationale and timing
- Implantation occurs within hours, so instil **as early as possible, ideally within 2–6 h** (theatre or recovery). Guidelines allow up to 24 h, but MMC given at 24 h lost its benefit.

### Agents
| Agent | Dose | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **MMC (mitomycin C)** | 40 mg in 20–40 mL | Alkylating; skin rash |
| **Gemcitabine** | 2 g in 100 mL, 1 h | Standard in USA; mild toxicity |
| Epirubicin | 50 mg | Chemical cystitis |
- **Also know:** pirarubicin works; thiotepa does not.

### Technique
- Exclude perforation; instil via catheter; retain 1–2 h; drain.

### Evidence
- **Sylvester 2016 individual-patient meta-analysis:** about 35% relative reduction in recurrence; **no benefit** with >1 recurrence/year or EORTC (European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer) recurrence score ≥5.
- **SWOG (Southwest Oncology Group) S0337:** gemcitabine versus saline in suspected low-grade NMIBC: 4-year recurrence 35% vs 47%; no excess toxicity.
- No effect on progression.

### Who benefits
- **Low risk** (primary solitary low-grade Ta ≤3 cm): SI alone; greatest benefit.
- Intermediate risk: SI then adjuvant course. High risk: BCG (bacille Calmette-Guérin) follows.

### Contraindications
- **Suspected perforation**, extensive resection, bleeding needing irrigation.
- **Never BCG immediately**: intravasation of live bacteria causes sepsis and death.

### Complications
- Irritative symptoms (commonest), chemical cystitis, skin desquamation (MMC), contracted bladder; serious harm mainly after perforation.
- **Also know:** continuous saline irrigation may also reduce recurrence.

**Diagram to draw:** floating cells implanting on resection bed → killed by instillation.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Instil within 6 h (at most 24 h); never BCG.
> - SWOG S0337: 35% vs 47% recurrence with gemcitabine 2 g.
> - Withhold if perforation; best for low-risk solitary tumours.

## Q3e. Endoscopic correction of VUR : indications and complications
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Introduction
- A **bulking agent** injected under the ureteric orifice supports and lengthens the intramural tunnel, restoring the flap-valve in VUR (vesicoureteral reflux).
- First done with Teflon (1981). **Dx/HA (dextranomer/hyaluronic acid, Deflux)** is now standard.

### Agents
| Agent | Property | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| Teflon (PTFE [polytetrafluoroethylene] paste) | Permanent, particles <40 μm | Migration; never FDA (US Food and Drug Administration) approved |
| **Dx/HA** | Microspheres 80–250 μm; gel resorbed | Volume loss about 23%; late recurrence |
| Polyacrylate (Vantris) | Permanent, 300 μm | Late ureteric obstruction |

### Technique
| Method | Needle entry |
|---|---|
| STING (subureteric Teflon injection) | 2–3 mm below orifice, at 6 o'clock |
| **HIT (hydrodistension implantation)** | Inside the distended orifice; better than STING |
| Double HIT | Two intraureteric sites; about 90% resolution |
- Aim for a **"volcano" mound** with a slit-like orifice.

### Indications
- Persistent primary VUR (grades II–IV) with **breakthrough febrile UTI** (urinary tract infection) on prophylaxis, **new scars**, poor adherence or parental preference.
- Older girls; residual VUR after reimplantation; duplex or secondary VUR (lower success).
- **Relative contraindications:** grade V, **UVJ (ureterovesical junction) obstruction**, untreated BBD (bladder and bowel dysfunction), large diverticulum.

### Success
- **Elder 2006 meta-analysis** (one injection): grade I–II about 79%, III 72%, IV 63%, V 51%; repeat injections raise success.
- **Swedish Reflux Trial:** 20% recurrence at 2 years after initial success.

### Complications
- **Failure or recurrence** (commonest): repeat injection (1–2), then reimplantation.
- **New contralateral VUR** after unilateral injection.
- **Ureteric obstruction:** early or **late-onset**, so long-term ultrasound.
- Febrile UTI despite negative VCUG (voiding cystourethrogram).
- **Calcified mound** (over half) mimics a ureteric stone. Teflon migration.

### Follow-up
- Day case; antibiotics 3 months; ultrasound at 2–6 weeks; ultrasound ± VCUG at 3 months.

**Diagram to draw:** STING versus HIT needle positions with volcano mound.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Dx/HA 80–250 μm; HIT better than STING.
> - Elder 2006: grade IV 63% after one injection.
> - Watch for late obstruction and calcified mound.

## Q3f. Management of DSD
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

*Read as: disorders/differences of sex development. Detrusor–sphincter dyssynergia is covered in 2016-Summer Paper 1, Q3a.*

### Definition and classification (Chicago consensus 2006)
- Congenital conditions with atypical chromosomal, gonadal or anatomical sex.

| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| **46,XX DSD** | **CAH (congenital adrenal hyperplasia)**, 95% 21-hydroxylase; ovotesticular; testicular (SRY+ [sex-determining region Y]); maternal androgens |
| **46,XY DSD** | Gonadal dysgenesis; androgen synthesis defects (5α-reductase); androgen insensitivity (CAIS/PAIS, complete/partial) |
| **Sex chromosome DSD** | 45,X/46,XY mixed gonadal dysgenesis; 45,X Turner; 47,XXY Klinefelter |

### Neonatal evaluation (first 24–48 h)
- **Emergency:** exclude **salt-wasting CAH** (low sodium, high potassium, shock; usually after day 5–7).
- **MDT (multidisciplinary team):** urologist, endocrinologist, geneticist, psychologist, ethicist; gender-neutral language.
- **Examination:** stretched phallic length, meatus, labioscrotal fusion (Prader stage), **gonads**. Impalpable gonads suggest CAH; a palpable gonad means testicular tissue.
- **Tests:**
  - **Karyotype** (FISH [fluorescence in situ hybridisation] for SRY in hours).
  - **17-OHP (17-hydroxyprogesterone)** after 36 h; electrolytes, glucose.
  - LH (luteinising hormone), FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone), testosterone, AMH (anti-Müllerian hormone); testosterone:DHT (dihydrotestosterone) >20:1 = 5α-reductase deficiency; hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin) stimulation.
  - **Pelvic ultrasound** for uterus; then genitography, endoscopy, laparoscopy ± biopsy, genetic panels. No gonadectomy while diagnosing.

### Medical management
- **CAH:** hydrocortisone, fludrocortisone, salt; stress dosing. **Puberty:** oestrogen or testosterone induction if gonads fail.

### Gender assignment
- Weighs diagnosis, androgen exposure, fertility, sexual function, family wishes. Usually female in CAH and CAIS; male trend in 5α-reductase deficiency.

### Surgery: timing and consent
- **Feminising** surgery (clitoroplasty, vaginoplasty, labioplasty) is irreversible, so deferral until the child can consent is favoured unless obstructed; severe CAH is debated.
- **Masculinising** (proximal hypospadias repair, orchidopexy) is often done in infancy.

### Gonadal tumour risk
| Risk | Condition | Management |
|---|---|---|
| High | Dysgenesis with Y, intra-abdominal (15–35%); PAIS non-scrotal (50%) | Gonadectomy |
| Intermediate | Turner with Y (12%) | Gonadectomy |
| Low | CAIS (2%); ovotesticular (3%) | CAIS: keep until after puberty; ovotesticular: remove discordant tissue |

**Diagram to draw:** karyotype-based DSD algorithm.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - 17-OHP after 36 h; salt crisis after day 5–7.
> - Palpable gonad = testicular tissue.
> - Defer irreversible surgery; CAIS gonads kept till puberty.
