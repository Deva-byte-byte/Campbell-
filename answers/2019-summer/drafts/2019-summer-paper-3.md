## Q1. Discuss differential diagnosis of small renal tumours. Describe techniques to achieve hemostasis during laparoscopic partial nephrectomy.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

### Definition and biology
- **SRM (small renal mass):** an enhancing solid or complex cystic renal mass **≤4 cm (cT1a)**, usually found incidentally.
- **Benign share falls with size:** 20–30% of masses ≤4 cm and about 40% of masses ≤2 cm are benign. At ≥7 cm few are benign and about half are high grade.
- Male sex carries about three times the risk of malignancy. Most small cancers are indolent: under 2% metastasise.

### Differential diagnosis
| Group | Entities | Pointers |
|---|---|---|
| RCC (renal cell carcinoma) | **Clear cell** (commonest), papillary (10–15%), chromophobe (3–5%), cystic RCC, rare molecular subtypes | Clear cell enhances strongly; papillary enhances weakly |
| Other malignant | Urothelial carcinoma, lymphoma, metastasis, sarcoma, epithelioid AML | Infiltrative growth |
| Benign solid | Oncocytoma, fat-poor AML (angiomyolipoma), papillary adenoma, metanephric adenoma | See below |
| Cystic | Haemorrhagic or complex cyst, cystic nephroma | Bosniak category |
| Inflammatory, vascular | Abscess, focal pyelonephritis, XGP (xanthogranulomatous pyelonephritis), TB; aneurysm, AVM (arteriovenous malformation), infarct, pseudotumour | Clinical picture |

- **Oncocytoma:** the commonest benign enhancing SRM (up to half of benign SRMs). A central stellate scar and segmental enhancement inversion suggest it but are not specific. A needle core cannot separate it from chromophobe RCC, which contains oncocytic areas. Sestamibi SPECT/CT (single-photon emission CT) shows uptake in mitochondria-rich tumours (under evaluation).
- **Fat-poor AML:** no visible fat (fat below −20 HU diagnoses classic AML). It looks homogeneous and T2-dark and mimics RCC; biopsy is HMB-45 and Melan-A positive.
- **Metanephric adenoma:** hypovascular, mild enhancement, sometimes polycythaemia; WT1 and CD57 positive.
- **Papillary adenoma:** papillary tumour ≤15 mm (WHO [World Health Organization] 2022).

### Narrowing the differential
- **Multiphase CT (computed tomography):** enhancement over 15–20 HU means solid tissue. **MRI (magnetic resonance imaging)** helps below 2 cm. Ultrasound only separates cyst from solid.
- **RMB (renal mass biopsy)** when the result will change management (AUA [American Urological Association]). Haematoma about 5%; seeding almost unreported; non-diagnostic about 14%.
- Then active surveillance, ablation or **PN (partial nephrectomy)**, which is preferred for cT1a.

### Haemostasis in LPN (laparoscopic partial nephrectomy)
**Principle:** a clear field for excision, then secure closure of the bed. Plan with nephrometry (R.E.N.A.L.) and 3D (three-dimensional) CT; map the tumour with intraoperative ultrasound.

**1. Vascular control**
| Technique | Method | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Main artery ± vein clamp | Bulldog clamps, or laparoscopic Satinsky en bloc | Artery-only equals artery–vein. Aim warm ischaemia ≤25 min (trifecta) |
| Selective or super-selective | Clamp the tumour's segmental branch; 3D CT, NIRF (near-infrared fluorescence) | Avoids global ischaemia; no functional gain in an RCT (randomised controlled trial) |
| Off-clamp ("zero ischaemia") | No clamping; small exophytic tumours | More blood loss; same long-term eGFR (estimated glomerular filtration rate) |
| Early unclamping | Release after the inner suture layer | Shorter ischaemia; possibly fewer pseudoaneurysms |
| Cold ischaemia | Ice slush, cold saline via ureteric catheter, cold arterial perfusion | Solitary kidney, CKD (chronic kidney disease), long repair; benefit unproven |
| Parenchymal compression | Laparoscopic parenchymal clamp | Peripheral tumours |

- Function depends mainly on parenchyma preserved, not minutes of clamp. Mannitol showed no benefit in an RCT.

**2. Excision:** score the capsule, cut with cold scissors, clip visible vessels. Enucleation along the pseudocapsule bleeds less.

**3. Renorrhaphy (most reliable step)**
- **Inner layer:** running 3-0 or 2-0 absorbable suture closes cut vessels and the collecting system (opened in about 60%). Ligate large vessels individually.
- **Outer (cortical) layer, sliding-clip technique:** horizontal mattress sutures with a locking clip at the tail; clips are slid down to the capsule, giving tension without knot tying. Pledgets prevent capsular tears; an oxidised cellulose bolster fills the defect.
- **Barbed suture:** holds tension without knots, so ischaemia is shorter.
- **Single versus two layers:** single layer gave better function and fewer pseudoaneurysms. Near the hilum, take superficial bites.

**4. Energy:** argon beam or bipolar coagulation of the bed. Argon can destroy collecting-system sutures and adds gas, so vent the ports.

**5. Haemostatic agents (adjuncts, no proven benefit in RCTs):** gelatin–thrombin matrix; fibrin sealant; oxidised cellulose; PEG (polyethylene glycol) hydrogel; cyanoacrylate. Sutureless closure only for selected small tumours.

**6. Final check:** unclamp, inspect, **lower pneumoperitoneum** to unmask venous bleeding; drain.

### Postoperative bleeding
- Early (hypotension, falling haemoglobin): resuscitate, CT, angioembolisation; re-exploration is rarely needed.
- **Delayed haematuria (about day 12):** pseudoaneurysm or arteriovenous fistula. Selective angioembolisation preserves function. Endophytic and posterior tumours carry higher risk.

**Diagram to draw:** sliding-clip renorrhaphy over a bolster, with bulldog clamp on the artery.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - SRM ≤4 cm: 20–30% benign; ≤2 cm about 40%.
> - Oncocytoma is the commonest benign mimic; biopsy cannot exclude chromophobe RCC.
> - Trifecta: negative margin, no major complication, warm ischaemia ≤25 min.
> - Delayed haematuria after PN means pseudoaneurysm: angioembolise.

## Q2. Describe evaluation of SUI in females. Discuss surgical therapy of SUI in Males.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

### Part A: Evaluation of SUI (stress urinary incontinence) in women
- **Definition (ICS [International Continence Society]):** involuntary leakage on effort, exertion, sneezing or coughing.
- **Mechanisms:** urethral **hypermobility** (loss of suburethral support, DeLancey hammock) and **ISD (intrinsic sphincter deficiency)**. Most women have some ISD.
- **Aims:** confirm SUI, exclude urgency and voiding dysfunction, grade severity and bother, plan surgery.

**History**
- Leakage with cough or activity means SUI; leakage after urgency means UUI (urgency urinary incontinence); both means MUI (mixed). Positive SUI history predicts urodynamic SUI in about three-quarters.
- Severity: pads/day, volume, bother. Voiding symptoms, prolapse bulge, obstetric history, prior pelvic surgery or radiation, neurological disease, drugs, bowel function.
- **Questionnaires:** ICIQ-UI SF (International Consultation on Incontinence Questionnaire–UI Short Form); UDI-6 (bother); IIQ-7 (impact). **Bladder diary** (3 days).

**Examination (comfortably full bladder)**
- Abdomen; neurological (S2–S4 perineal sensation, anal tone, bulbocavernosus reflex); vaginal atrophy; prolapse by **POP-Q (Pelvic Organ Prolapse Quantification)**.
- **Cough stress test:** cough supine, then standing if negative. Leakage synchronous with cough confirms SUI. Reduce prolapse to unmask **occult SUI**.
- **Urethral mobility:** **Q-tip test** (swab to bladder neck, Valsalva; rotation **>30°** = hypermobility); visual mobility examination is now routine.

**Tests**
| Test | Method and use |
|---|---|
| Urinalysis | Required; exclude infection, haematuria |
| PVR (post-void residual) | Required before surgery; ultrasound; repeat if high (no agreed cut-off) |
| 1-hour pad test (ICS) | 500 mL fluid, standard exercises; >1.4 g positive; 1–10 g mild, 11–50 g moderate, >50 g severe |
| 24-hour pad test | Normal activities; more reproducible |
| Cystoscopy | Haematuria, irritative symptoms, previous mesh |
| Imaging | Translabial ultrasound for mesh; VCUG (voiding cystourethrogram) for diverticulum |

**Urodynamics (UDS): not routine.** VALUE trial (2012): office evaluation was non-inferior to UDS before surgery for uncomplicated SUI.
- **Indications (AUA/SUFU [Society of Urodynamics, Female Pelvic Medicine and Urogenital Reconstruction] 2023):** unclear diagnosis or SUI not shown; urgency-predominant MUI; neurogenic bladder; high PVR or voiding dysfunction; prolapse stage ≥3 without demonstrable SUI; failed previous surgery.
- **ALPP/VLPP (abdominal/Valsalva leak point pressure; McGuire 1993):** <60 cmH2O = ISD; 60–90 equivocal; ≥90 = little ISD. A low MUCP (maximum urethral closure pressure, <20 cmH2O) also suggests ISD.

### Part B: Surgical therapy of male SUI
- **Aetiology:** commonest after **radical prostatectomy (RP)**. RP removes the internal sphincter, so continence rests on the rhabdosphincter. Also TURP (transurethral resection of the prostate), radiation, pelvic fracture, neurological.
- **Work-up:** 24-h pad weight (mild <150 g, moderate <400 g, severe above); standing cough test; **cystoscopy** (anastomotic stricture, sphincter coaptation); UDS if mixed or poor contractility.
- **Timing:** PFMT (pelvic floor muscle training) first; continence improves up to 2 years. Do not delay surgery beyond 6 months for severe leak.

**Devices**
| Device | Mechanism | Use and results |
|---|---|---|
| **Transobturator sling (AdVance XP)** | Repositions the bulb 3–4 cm proximally; needs residual sphincter | Mild–moderate, no radiation; cure about 60% |
| Quadratic sling (Virtue) | Compression plus elevation | Mild; limited data |
| Adjustable sling (ATOMS) | Cushion adjusted via scrotal port | Mild–moderate; about two-thirds dry |
| ProACT balloons | Bladder-neck compression | More erosion |
| Bulking agents | Submucosal bulk | Small leaks, unfit men; rarely durable |
| **AUS (artificial urinary sphincter)** | Circumferential compression | **Gold standard**; all severities, after radiation |

**AMS 800 AUS**
- **Components:** urethral **cuff** (bulbar, usually 4.0–4.5 cm), **PRB (pressure-regulating balloon) 61–70 cmH2O** (51–60 high risk; 71–80 bladder neck) in a preperitoneal pocket, scrotal **pump**. Filled with 23 mL.
- **Technique:** perineal incision; cuff on proximal bulbar urethra; **deactivate for 4–6 weeks**.
- **Requirements:** manual dexterity, cognition, adequate capacity and compliance.
- **Outcomes:** social continence (0–1 pad) 55–77%; satisfaction about 90%; lifespan 7–10 years.
- **Complications:** infection 1–3% (explant; reimplant after 3–6 months); **erosion** 2–15% (explant, catheter about 3 weeks); urethral **atrophy** (late recurrent leak); mechanical failure; retention.
- **Erosion risks:** radiation, prior erosion, urethroplasty. Options: **transcorporal cuff** (tunica albuginea adds dorsal bulk), lower PRB, tandem cuff for atrophy.

**Algorithm (AUA/SUFU 2019)**
```
Severe SUI or any radiotherapy -> AUS
Moderate, no radiotherapy      -> AUS or male sling
Mild, no radiotherapy          -> sling (or PFMT, AUS)
Sling failure                  -> AUS
```
- **MASTER RCT (randomised controlled trial):** sling was **non-inferior** to AUS for continence at 12 months; AUS remains preferred for heavy leak (>250 g/day) or radiation.

**Diagram to draw:** AMS 800 (cuff, pump, PRB) and transobturator sling path.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Q-tip >30° = hypermobility; VLPP <60 = ISD.
> - VALUE trial: UDS not needed in uncomplicated SUI.
> - AUS PRB 61–70 cmH2O; deactivate 4–6 weeks.
> - Radiation or severe SUI: AUS first; MASTER: sling non-inferior.

## Q3a. Prepucioplasty
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

*Read as: preputioplasty, a foreskin-preserving operation for phimosis.*

### Definition
- An operation that widens the tight preputial ring but keeps the foreskin. It is an alternative to circumcision.

### Background
- **Physiological phimosis** at birth is due to glans–preputial adhesions. Smegma and erections separate them. About 90% retract by 3 years; fewer than 1% are phimotic at 17 years.
- **Secondary (pathological) phimosis** follows scarring: forced retraction, balanoposthitis or BXO (balanitis xerotica obliterans, lichen sclerosus).

### Indications
- Persistent symptomatic phimosis: recurrent balanoposthitis, UTI (urinary tract infection), ballooning with obstruction, paraphimosis, painful erection.
- Failed **topical steroid** (betamethasone 0.05% twice daily for about 4 weeks with gentle retraction). About 1 in 6 fail.
- Wish to keep the foreskin (cultural reasons, cosmesis).

### Contraindications
- **BXO** with scarring: circumcision is preferred. Triradiate incision with triamcinolone has been reported in selected boys.
- Physiological non-retractility needs only reassurance. Treat active infection first.

### Techniques
| Technique | Method |
|---|---|
| Dorsal slit with transverse closure | Longitudinal incision through the ring, closed transversely (Heineke–Mikulicz principle) |
| Triple incision | Three longitudinal cuts over the ring, each closed transversely |
| Y–V plasty | V-flap of outer skin advanced into a Y-cut |

- **Principle:** cutting the ring lengthwise and closing it crosswise widens its circumference.
- General anaesthesia or penile block; incise only the constricting band; confirm full retraction; absorbable sutures.

### Postoperative care
- **Daily retraction from the first week**, continued for weeks, prevents re-stenosis. A topical steroid may help.

### Outcomes and complications
- Most achieve a retractile foreskin. It is quicker and less painful than circumcision.
- Complications: re-phimosis (needs circumcision), bleeding, infection, poor cosmesis, paraphimosis if retraction is neglected.
- **Also know:** preputial reconstruction during hypospadias repair is also called preputioplasty; dehiscence is its risk.

**Diagram to draw:** dorsal incision through the ring, closed transversely.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Steroid first; about 1 in 6 fail.
> - Cut lengthwise, close crosswise; daily retraction after.
> - BXO: circumcision preferred.

## Q3b. Baceriuria and use of antibiotic policy in pregnancy
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

*Read as: bacteriuria in pregnancy and the antibiotic policy for it.*

### Definition
- **ASB (asymptomatic bacteriuria):** ≥10⁵ CFU (colony-forming units)/mL of one organism without urinary symptoms (IDSA [Infectious Diseases Society of America]: two consecutive specimens in women).

### Why pregnancy matters
- Prevalence is **not raised** (about 2–7%), but ASB **persists and ascends**.
- **Changes:** progesterone relaxes ureteric muscle; the uterus compresses the ureters at the pelvic brim (right more); bladder capacity rises. Stasis and hydroureteronephrosis follow.
- GFR (glomerular filtration rate) rises 30–50%, so creatinine falls; drug levels may be lower.
- Untreated ASB led to **pyelonephritis** in 15–45% (historical), mostly in the third trimester. This causes **preterm birth and low birth weight**.
- **Cochrane 2019:** treating ASB reduces pyelonephritis, preterm delivery and low birth weight.

### Screening
- **Urine culture at 12–16 weeks** or the first visit (USPSTF [US Preventive Services Task Force]). Dipstick misses cases. A negative early culture makes later bacteriuria unlikely.

### Antibiotic policy
| Safe | Restricted or avoided |
|---|---|
| Penicillins (amoxicillin), cephalosporins: throughout | **Nitrofurantoin:** stop near term (35 weeks+), as it causes neonatal haemolysis (G6PD [glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase] deficiency) |
| Fosfomycin: lower UTI (urinary tract infection) | **TMP-SMX (trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole):** avoid 1st trimester (folate, neural tube); caution near term (kernicterus) |
| Gentamicin: pyelonephritis only | **Fluoroquinolones** (cartilage), **tetracyclines** (teeth, bone), chloramphenicol (grey baby): avoid |

- ACOG (American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists) 2017: nitrofurantoin and sulfonamides are first-line in 2nd–3rd trimester; in 1st only if no alternative.
- **ASB or cystitis:** culture-directed, **4–7 days** (longer courses cure more than single dose). **Repeat culture** after treatment and periodically.

### Pyelonephritis
- **Admit**; IV (intravenous) ampicillin plus gentamicin, ceftriaxone or aztreonam; oral cephalexin after afebrile; total 10–14 days. No fluoroquinolones.
- No improvement by 72 h: renal ultrasound (stone, obstruction, abscess).

### Prophylaxis
- After one pyelonephritis or two cystitis episodes: nightly nitrofurantoin or cephalexin until delivery. Cochrane 2015: close surveillance alone was as effective.
- **Also know:** treat ASB after diversion, augmentation or SCI (spinal cord injury) in pregnancy.

**Diagram to draw:** right hydroureter at the pelvic brim.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Screen by culture at 12–16 weeks; treat all ASB.
> - β-lactams safe throughout; no fluoroquinolones or tetracyclines.
> - Nitrofurantoin not near term; TMP not in 1st trimester.

## Q3c. Accessories for RIRS
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Introduction
- RIRS (retrograde intrarenal surgery) is flexible ureteroscopy inside the kidney. The scope has a single **3.6 Fr channel**, so accessories must be small and still allow irrigation.

### Guidewires (0.018–0.038 inch, 145–260 cm)
| Type | Construction | Use | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hydrophilic | Nitinol core, slippery when wet | Bypassing impacted stones, tortuous ureter | Displaces easily |
| PTFE (polytetrafluoroethylene)-coated | Steel or nitinol | Safety and working wire | Damages scope channel on backloading |
| **Hybrid** | Floppy hydrophilic tip, stiff PTFE shaft | Both roles | — |
| Stiff (super-stiff) | Rigid shaft | Carrying access sheath | Perforation if forced |

- **Safety wire** keeps access and allows a stent after injury (AUA and EAU [European Association of Urology] recommend it).
- **Also know:** open-ended or dual-lumen catheter for retrograde pyelogram and second wire; balloon dilators.

### UAS (ureteral access sheath)
- Hydrophilic sheath with tapered obturator; common sizes **10/12, 12/14 Fr** (inner/outer); length by sex and height; tip below the PUJ (pelviureteric junction).
- **Pros:** lower intrarenal pressure and temperature; repeated passes for basketing; better vision; less scope wear.
- **Cons:** ureteric injury in nearly half, severe in about **1 in 8**; possible stricture. Pre-stenting reduces injury. No proven fall in infection.
- **Suction sheaths and FANS (flexible and navigable suction access sheath):** aspirate dust and keep pressure low.

### Retrieval devices
- **Nitinol tipless baskets** (1.5–2.4 Fr): kink-resistant, open flush in calyces; relocate lower-pole stones.
- Graspers (three-prong) for releasable grip. Small baskets permit more irrigation but are fragile.

### Laser and fibres
- **Ho:YAG (holmium:yttrium-aluminium-garnet)** and **thulium fibre laser**. Small-core fibres (about 200 μm) preserve deflection and flow. Keep power **≤10 W in the ureter**.

### Irrigation
- Gravity, pressure bag, hand or foot pumps. Hand pumps link to more fever. **Intrarenal pressure** sensors (wire or scope) are emerging.

### Antiretropulsion devices
- Stone Cone, NTrap (nitinol), Accordion, BackStop gel. Mainly for ureteric stones.

### Stents
- **Double-J** at the end. AUA: may omit after uncomplicated URS (normal anatomy and renal function, no injury, no second stage).

**Diagram to draw:** sheath over wire, tipless basket in lower calyx.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Hydrophilic wire passes; PTFE wire stays.
> - 12/14 Fr sheath lowers pressure; severe injury about 1 in 8.
> - Nitinol tipless basket for calyces; ≤10 W in ureter.

## Q3d. Immediate post TURBT intravesical Therapy for TCC
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Introduction
- After TURBT (transurethral resection of bladder tumour), NMIBC (non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer) recurs in 40–60%. Many early recurrences come from **floating tumour cells implanting** on the raw resection bed.
- A single chemotherapy instillation (SI) **immediately after TURBT** kills these cells before they implant. "Urothelial carcinoma" now replaces "TCC (transitional cell carcinoma)".

### Rationale and timing
- Implantation happens within hours, so instil **within 24 h, ideally within 6 h** (in theatre or recovery). MMC at 24 h gave no benefit.

### Agents
| Agent | Dose | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **MMC (mitomycin C)** | 40 mg in 20–40 mL | Alkylating; skin rash |
| **Gemcitabine** | 2 g in 100 mL, 1 h | Standard in USA; mild toxicity |
| Epirubicin | 50 mg | Chemical cystitis |
| Pirarubicin | — | Effective |
- Thiotepa is ineffective.

### Technique
- Confirm no perforation; catheter; drain bladder; instil; clamp 1–2 h; drain. Avoid irrigation during dwell.

### Evidence
- **Sylvester 2004 meta-analysis:** about 39% fewer recurrences (absolute about 12%).
- **Sylvester 2016 individual-patient meta-analysis:** about 35% relative reduction in recurrence risk; **no benefit** with >1 recurrence/year or EORTC (European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer) recurrence score ≥5.
- **SWOG (Southwest Oncology Group) S0337:** gemcitabine versus saline in suspected low-grade NMIBC: 4-year recurrence 35% vs 47%; low grade 34% vs 54%; no excess toxicity.
- No effect on progression.

### Who benefits
- **Low risk** (solitary, primary, low-grade Ta ≤3 cm): SI alone is the adjuvant treatment.
- Intermediate risk: SI followed by an adjuvant course (still benefits).
- High risk: limited added benefit; BCG (bacille Calmette-Guérin) follows.

### Contraindications
- **Suspected perforation** or extensive resection (extravasation causes pelvic necrosis).
- Severe haematuria needing irrigation.
- **Never give BCG immediately**: intravasation of live bacteria causes sepsis and death.

### Complications
- Irritative symptoms (commonest), chemical cystitis, skin desquamation (MMC), contracted bladder, calcified eschar; serious reactions mainly after perforation.
- **Also know:** continuous saline or sterile-water irrigation may reduce recurrence; neoadjuvant MMC before TURBT is investigational.

**Diagram to draw:** floating cells implanting on resection bed → killed by instillation.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Instil within 6 h (at most 24 h); never BCG.
> - SWOG S0337: 35% vs 47% recurrence with gemcitabine 2 g.
> - Withhold if perforation; best for low-risk solitary tumours.

## Q3e. Endoscopic correction of VUR : indications and complications
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Introduction
- VUR (vesicoureteral reflux) can be corrected by injecting a **bulking agent** under the ureteric orifice. The mound lengthens and supports the intramural tunnel, restoring the flap-valve.
- First done with Teflon (Matouschek 1981; STING, O'Donnell and Puri 1986). **Dx/HA (dextranomer/hyaluronic acid, Deflux)** is now standard (FDA [US Food and Drug Administration] approved 2001).

### Agents
| Agent | Property | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| Teflon (PTFE paste) | Permanent, particles <40 μm | Migration; never FDA approved |
| Macroplastique | Silicone | Seldom used |
| **Dx/HA** | Microspheres 80–250 μm; gel resorbed | Volume loss about 23%; late recurrence |
| Polyacrylate (Vantris) | Permanent, 300 μm | Late ureteric obstruction |

### Technique
| Method | Needle entry |
|---|---|
| STING (subureteric Teflon injection) | 2–3 mm below orifice, at 6 o'clock |
| **HIT (hydrodistension implantation)** | Inside the distended orifice, into the tunnel; better than STING |
| Double HIT | Two intraureteric sites; about 90% resolution |
- Aim for a **"volcano" mound** with a slit-like orifice. Day case, no catheter.

### Indications
- Persistent primary VUR (grades II–IV) with **breakthrough febrile UTI** (urinary tract infection) on prophylaxis, **new scars**, poor adherence or parental preference over long prophylaxis.
- Persistent VUR in older children, especially girls; low-grade VUR after reimplantation failure.
- Selected secondary VUR, duplex systems and paraureteral diverticulum (lower success).
- **Relative contraindications:** grade V, **UVJ (ureterovesical junction) obstruction**, uncontrolled BBD (bladder and bowel dysfunction) or neurogenic bladder, large diverticulum, active infection.

### Success
- **Elder 2006 meta-analysis** (one injection): grade I–II about 79%, III 72%, IV 63%, V 51%. Repeat injections raise overall success.
- **Swedish Reflux Trial:** 20% recurrence at 2 years after initial success.

### Complications
- **Failure or recurrence** (commonest); needs repeat injection (1–2) or reimplantation.
- **New contralateral VUR** after unilateral injection.
- **Ureteric obstruction:** early (oedema, mound) or **late-onset**, so long-term ultrasound is needed.
- Febrile UTI despite negative VCUG (voiding cystourethrogram), especially with BBD.
- **Calcification** of the mound (over half) mimics a distal ureteric stone.
- Migration (Teflon); puncture bleeding (do not cauterise).

### Follow-up
- Antibiotics 3 months; ultrasound at 2–6 weeks; ultrasound ± VCUG at 3 months.

**Diagram to draw:** STING versus HIT needle positions with volcano mound.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Dx/HA 80–250 μm; HIT better than STING.
> - Elder 2006: grade IV 63% after one injection.
> - Watch for late obstruction and calcified mound.

## Q3f. Management of DSD
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

*Read as: disorders/differences of sex development. Detrusor–sphincter dyssynergia is covered in 2016-Summer Paper 1, Q3a.*

### Definition and classification (Chicago consensus 2006)
- DSD (differences of sex development): congenital conditions where chromosomal, gonadal or anatomical sex is atypical.

| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| **46,XX DSD** | **CAH (congenital adrenal hyperplasia)**, mostly 21-hydroxylase (95%); ovotesticular; testicular (SRY+) DSD; maternal androgens |
| **46,XY DSD** | Gonadal dysgenesis (complete/partial); androgen synthesis defects (5α-reductase, 17β-HSD3 [17β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase]); androgen insensitivity (CAIS/PAIS, complete/partial) |
| **Sex chromosome DSD** | 45,X/46,XY mixed gonadal dysgenesis; 45,X Turner; 47,XXY Klinefelter; chimerism |

### Neonatal evaluation (first 24–48 h)
- **Emergency:** exclude **salt-wasting CAH**; crisis (low sodium, high potassium, shock) usually after day 5–7.
- **MDT (multidisciplinary team):** urologist, endocrinologist, geneticist, psychologist, neonatologist, ethicist. Gender-neutral language.
- **Examination:** stretched phallic length, meatus, labioscrotal fusion (Prader stage), **gonads**. Impalpable gonads suggest CAH; a palpable gonad means testicular tissue.
- **Tests:**
  - **Karyotype** (FISH [fluorescence in situ hybridisation] for SRY in hours).
  - **17-OHP (17-hydroxyprogesterone)** after 36 h; electrolytes, glucose.
  - LH (luteinising hormone), FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone), testosterone, AMH (anti-Müllerian hormone), DHT (dihydrotestosterone): testosterone:DHT >20:1 means 5α-reductase deficiency; hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin) stimulation.
  - **Pelvic ultrasound** for uterus; genitography, endoscopy, laparoscopy ± gonadal biopsy if unclear; genetic panels.
- No gonadectomy during the diagnostic phase.

### Medical management
- **CAH:** hydrocortisone, fludrocortisone and salt; stress dosing.
- **Puberty:** sex steroid induction (oestrogen or testosterone) when gonads fail.

### Gender assignment
- Based on diagnosis, androgen exposure, fertility, sexual function and family wishes. Usually female in 46,XX CAH and CAIS; male trend in PAIS and 5α-reductase deficiency (dysphoria highest if raised female).

### Surgery: timing and consent
- **Feminising** (clitoroplasty, vaginoplasty, labioplasty) is irreversible, so deferral until the child can consent is now favoured, except for urinary obstruction. Severe CAH (Prader III–IV) remains debated.
- **Masculinising:** proximal hypospadias repair, orchidopexy; often in infancy.

### Gonadal tumour risk
