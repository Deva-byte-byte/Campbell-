## Q1. Describe the embryology and anatomy of male Urethra. Describe different endoscopic management techniques of treatment of urethral strictures.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

### Embryology
- **Cloaca** is divided by the **urorectal septum** (weeks 4–7) into the anterior **UGS (urogenital sinus)** and the posterior anorectal canal.
- The UGS has three parts: **vesical** (bladder), **pelvic** (prostatic and membranous urethra) and **phallic** (urethral plate of the penis).
- **Posterior urethra:** from the pelvic UGS. Prostatic buds appear at 10 weeks; the **utricle** (Müllerian remnant) and ejaculatory ducts (Wolffian) open on the verumontanum.
- **Anterior urethra:** Leydig cells make testosterone from about 8 weeks; type 2 5α-reductase converts it to DHT (dihydrotestosterone), which masculinises the genital tubercle. Germ layers: ectoderm gives skin and prepuce, mesoderm the corpora, endoderm the **urethral plate**.
- **Penile urethra: "double zipper"**
  1. **Opening zipper:** the urethral plate canalises proximal to distal into a urethral groove between the urethral folds.
  2. **Closing zipper:** the folds fuse in the ventral midline, forming the tube and the median raphe. Folds fused by 16 weeks; urethra complete by about 18 weeks.

| Glanular urethra | Ectodermal ingrowth theory (Glenister) | Endodermal theory (current) |
|---|---|---|
| Formed by | Surface ectoderm grows into the glans to meet the endodermal urethra | Direct canalisation of the urethral plate inside the glans |
| Epithelium | Mixed ectoderm and endoderm | Endoderm (UGS) along the whole urethra |

- **Clinical link:** the shaft–glans junction (change of mechanism) is the commonest hypospadias site. **Also know:** urethral duplication; PUV (posterior urethral valves); utricular cyst.

### Anatomy
- About 18–20 cm long in adults; normal diameter 8–9 mm. Posterior urethra runs from the bladder neck to the **perineal membrane**; anterior urethra from there to the meatus.

| Segment | Length / features | Lining |
|---|---|---|
| Prostatic | Length of prostate; urethral crest, verumontanum, utricle, ejaculatory ducts | Urothelium |
| Membranous | 2–2.5 cm; shortest and narrowest; striated sphincter | Pseudostratified columnar |
| Bulbar | Widens as the bulb; Cowper ducts at 3 and 9 o'clock; **eccentric, close to the dorsum** of spongiosum | Pseudostratified columnar |
| Penile | About 15 cm; central, uniform calibre; glands of Littré | Pseudostratified columnar |
| Fossa navicularis | Glanular; lacuna magna on the roof | Stratified squamous |

- **Sphincters:** (1) **internal (preprostatic) smooth muscle sphincter** at the bladder neck, autonomic; (2) **external striated rhabdosphincter**, horseshoe-shaped around the membranous urethra, pudendal nerve (mainly S3, some S2; Onuf nucleus), plus its intrinsic smooth muscle.
- **Blood supply:** internal pudendal → common penile artery → artery to the bulb (proximal) and **retrograde flow via the dorsal arteries through the glans**. This dual supply allows bulbar transection in anastomotic urethroplasty.
- **Venous** to pudendal and prostatic plexuses; **lymph** to internal and external iliac nodes.

### Endoscopic management of stricture
**Principle:** healing after incision or dilation is by **secondary intention**, a race between epithelialisation and wound contraction. No endoscopic method removes **spongiofibrosis**, so cure is limited to short, superficial bulbar strictures.

| Favourable | Unfavourable |
|---|---|
| Bulbar, **<1.5–2 cm** | Penile, fossa, longer |
| No dense spongiofibrosis | Straddle injury, LS (lichen sclerosus) |
| First procedure | Repeat; luminal narrowing >74% |

**1. Dilation**
- Stretches the scar without tearing it (bleeding means tearing).
- **Safest: balloon dilation over a guidewire** under vision; blind filiforms condemned. Sounds or self-dilation for palliation.

**2. DVIU (direct vision internal urethrotomy)**
1. RUG (retrograde urethrogram) and VCUG (voiding cystourethrogram) define site and length.
2. Guidewire across; **normal saline** irrigation.
3. **Sachse cold knife** at 12 o'clock, through scar to healthy tissue; avoid deep cuts (corpora: ED (erectile dysfunction), fistula).
4. Catheter **3–7 days**; longer adds nothing.
- **Laser urethrotomy** (holmium, others): **no advantage over cold knife**.
- Results: **Steenkamp and Heyns** (randomised): dilation equals urethrotomy; a second attempt rarely and a third never succeeds. **Santucci (2010):** about 8% stricture-free long term.

**3. Adjuncts against recurrence**
- **CISC (clean intermittent self-catheterisation)** tapering over 3–6 months: delays recurrence, which returns on stopping.
- **Mitomycin C** injection reduces recurrence; steroid injection, colchicine: weak data.

**4. Urethral stents**
- **UroLume** (permanent): **withdrawn** (ingrowth). **Memokath**, removable stents: small series only.

**5. Optilume (paclitaxel-coated balloon)**
- FDA (Food and Drug Administration) 2023. **ROBUST III:** 74.6% vs 26.8% stricture-free at 1 year after failed endoscopy.

**Complications:** recurrence (commonest), bleeding with erections, irrigant extravasation, UTI (urinary tract infection) and sepsis, ED, spongio-cavernous fistula, incontinence.

**Guideline (AUA (American Urological Association) 2016):** urethrotomy or dilation for short (<2 cm) bulbar strictures; **urethroplasty, not repeat endoscopy**, after failure. The reconstructive ladder is abandoned.

**Diagram to draw:** sagittal male urethra with segments, sphincters and a 12 o'clock urethrotomy.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Double zipper: canalisation, then fold fusion; glanular urethra endodermal.
> - Bulbar urethra eccentric (dorsal); dual retrograde glanular blood supply.
> - DVIU: bulbar, <1.5–2 cm, first attempt; Santucci about 8% long-term.
> - Optilume ROBUST III 74.6% vs 26.8%.

## Q2. Describe physiological and hemodynamic changes due to creation of pneumoperitoneum during laparoscopy. Describe complications of Retroperitoneoscopy.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

### Introduction
- **Pneumoperitoneum** is insufflation of gas, usually CO₂ (carbon dioxide), to 10–15 mmHg to create working space.
- Its effects have **two drivers**: **pressure** (compression of cava, kidneys, diaphragm) and **absorbed CO₂** (acidosis, sympathetic drive). Position modifies both.
- **CO₂** is standard: non-combustible, cheap, highly soluble (embolism less lethal). Helium or argon in severe lung disease; N₂O (nitrous oxide) supports combustion.

### Effects by pressure (supine)
| Effect | 10 mmHg | 20 mmHg | 40 mmHg |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heart rate, MAP (mean arterial pressure), SVR (systemic vascular resistance) | ↑ | ↑ | MAP, SVR ↑; HR ↓ |
| Venous return, cardiac output | Variable | Variable | ↓ sharply |
| GFR (glomerular filtration rate), urine | ↓ | ↓↓ | ↓↓ |
| Arterial PCO₂ / pH | ↑ / ↔ | ↑ / ↓ | ↑ / ↓ |

### Cardiovascular
- **Afterload rises** at every pressure (aortic compression, catecholamines, vasopressin).
- **Preload:** in hypovolaemia the cava collapses and venous return falls; in hypervolaemia it may rise. Above 40 mmHg output collapses.
- **Arrhythmia:** hypercapnia causes tachycardia and ectopics; peritoneal stretch causes **vagal bradycardia** or asystole. Arrhythmia may herald pneumothorax, hypoxia or embolism.
- **CVP (central venous pressure) is falsely high** (raised intrathoracic pressure).
- Leg venous stasis: theoretical DVT (deep vein thrombosis) risk.

### Respiratory
- Diaphragm splinted: **FRC (functional residual capacity) and compliance fall**, peak airway pressure rises; atelectasis, V/Q mismatch. PEEP (positive end-expiratory pressure) helps.
- Steep Trendelenburg adds endobronchial tube slip and facial oedema.

### Acid–base (CO₂ absorption)
- CO₂ is absorbed across the peritoneum (and through fat and lymphatics extraperitoneally); rises over 30–60 minutes, then plateaus.
- **Hypercarbia and respiratory acidosis**; managed by raising minute ventilation and lowering pressure.
- **COPD (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease)** patients cannot blow it off: arterial gases if >1 hour; consider helium. Stored CO₂ causes **peak hypercarbia after extubation**.

### Renal
- **Oliguria** from **renal vein and parenchymal compression** (not ureteric or hormonal); cortical flow falls.
- Keep pressure ≤10–12 mmHg; avoid fluid overload; mannitol or furosemide if needed. Reversible.

### Other systems
- **Splanchnic** flow falls (rare mesenteric thrombosis); less ileus than open surgery.
- **ICP (intracranial pressure)** and **IOP (intraocular pressure)** rise with hypercarbia and head-down tilt: caution in head injury, shunts, glaucoma.
- **Hormonal:** catecholamines, cortisol and **vasopressin** rise; renin–angiotensin activation adds to afterload.
- **Stress and immune response** smaller than open surgery (less hyperglycaemia and catabolism), which may explain faster recovery.
- Shoulder-tip pain from diaphragmatic CO₂ irritation; hypothermia from dry cold gas.

### Position effects
| Position | Effect |
|---|---|
| Head-down | Venous return and output ↑; lung volumes ↓, ICP and IOP ↑ |
| Head-up | Output ↓, hypotension |
| Lateral flank | Lung V/Q mismatch; rhabdomyolysis with kidney rest |

- **Practical rule:** work at 10–12 mmHg; never exceed 20 mmHg for long. Retroperitoneal insufflation causes smaller haemodynamic swings.

### Complications of retroperitoneoscopy
**Why different:** small space with no natural cavity, created by finger and **balloon dissection** (commercial or Gaur glove balloon) behind Gerota's fascia, in front of psoas; fewer landmarks; gas tracks along tissue planes; CO₂ absorbed through fat and disrupted lymphatics, so hypercarbia appears early if the port leaks.

**1. Access and balloon-related**
- Balloon in the wrong plane (intramuscular → hernia); **balloon rupture** (latex fragments).
- **Peritoneal tear:** CO₂ enters the peritoneum and collapses the working space. Fix by an extra port to vent, or convert to transperitoneal.

**2. Gas-related**
- **Subcutaneous emphysema** (commonest): crepitus over flank, chest and neck, pneumoscrotum; prevent with a **balloon-tip port**, tight incisions, 12 rather than 15 mmHg.
- **Hypercarbia**, especially with emphysema.
- **Pneumothorax** (1.6–4%, commoner retroperitoneally), pneumomediastinum, pneumopericardium: needle decompression, chest drain.
- **Gas embolism** (renal vein injury): collapse, end-tidal CO₂ rises then falls, mill-wheel murmur. Stop gas, **left lateral head-down**, 100% oxygen, aspirate through a central line.

**3. Vascular injury**
- Commoner in upper-tract (retroperitoneal) work: renal vessels, IVC (inferior vena cava), lumbar veins, gonadal vessels, aorta.
- Increase pressure temporarily, compress, add ports, suture or clip; convert if uncontrolled.

**4. Visceral injury**
- Pleura, peritoneum, colon, duodenum, pancreas, spleen, liver, ureter; usually by thermal spread.

**5. Postoperative**
- Port-site hernia or haematoma; flank bulge; neuropraxia (lateral femoral cutaneous, genitofemoral); **rhabdomyolysis** (about 1%; limit kidney rest); urinoma, lymphocele; delayed bowel injury; conversion to open or transperitoneal surgery.

**Diagram to draw:** flank port positions for retroperitoneoscopy with balloon in the space between psoas and Gerota's fascia.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Two drivers: pressure and CO₂; work at 10–12 mmHg.
> - Afterload rises; oliguria from renal vein compression; peak hypercarbia after extubation.
> - Gas embolism: left lateral, head down, oxygen, aspirate.
> - Retroperitoneoscopy: emphysema and peritoneal tear; balloon-tip port prevents leak.

## Q3a. Describe methods to estimate GFR in a prospective renal donor
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Why it matters
- Donor loses about half the nephron mass. **KDIGO (Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes) 2017** sets GFR (glomerular filtration rate) thresholds and asks for **measurement, not just estimation**, at a decision point.

### Methods
**1. Serum creatinine and eGFR (estimated GFR)**
- Creatinine is freely filtered, but generation depends on **muscle mass, diet and age**; it is also secreted, so it overestimates GFR.
- **CKD-EPI (Chronic Kidney Disease Epidemiology Collaboration) 2021 creatinine equation:** age and sex, **no race term**; currently recommended. MDRD (Modification of Diet in Renal Disease) and Cockcroft–Gault are older.
- Less accurate at GFR >60, where donors lie: a screening test only.

**2. Cystatin C**
- 13-kDa protein made by all nucleated cells; freely filtered, fully reabsorbed and broken down. Independent of muscle mass; affected by thyroid state, steroids, inflammation.
- **Combined creatinine–cystatin C equation** is more accurate than either alone.

**3. Measured 24-hour creatinine clearance**
- (Urine creatinine × volume) ÷ serum creatinine. Prone to collection errors; secretion overestimates GFR.

**4. mGFR (measured GFR): reference**
| Marker | Method |
|---|---|
| **Inulin** | Urinary clearance; gold standard, research only |
| **Iohexol** | Plasma clearance; safe, accurate |
| **Iothalamate** | Urinary or plasma clearance |
| **⁵¹Cr-EDTA (ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid)** | Plasma clearance |
| **⁹⁹ᵐTc-DTPA (diethylenetriaminepentaacetic acid)** | Plasma sampling, or gamma camera (Gates method) |

**5. Split function**
- DTPA renogram gives differential function; MAG3 or DMSA alternatives. A difference >10% (beyond 45:55) matters: **leave the better kidney with the donor**.

### KDIGO 2017 approach
- Initial **eGFRcr**; confirm with **mGFR, measured creatinine clearance or eGFRcr-cys**.

| GFR (mL/min/1.73 m²) | Decision |
|---|---|
| **≥90** | Accept |
| **60–89** | Individualise (age, lifetime risk) |
| **<60** | Decline |

- Indian practice: **DTPA GFR** with split function plus CT (computed tomography) angiography is standard.

**Diagram to draw:** plasma tracer clearance curve after a single injection.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - CKD-EPI 2021 race-free; cystatin C adds accuracy.
> - Inulin is gold standard; iohexol or DTPA in practice.
> - KDIGO 2017: ≥90 accept, 60–89 individualise, <60 decline.
> - Better kidney stays with the donor.

## Q3b. VLPP in SUI
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition
- **VLPP (Valsalva leak point pressure):** the **intravesical pressure** at which urine leaks during Valsalva, **without a detrusor contraction**.
- It is a type of **ALPP (abdominal leak point pressure)**, which measures the urethra's ability to resist abdominal pressure. Lower value = weaker sphincter.
- Seen only in SUI (stress urinary incontinence); **no normal value** (continent women never leak).

### Technique
1. Small dual-lumen urethral catheter plus rectal catheter; zero to atmosphere.
2. Patient **standing or sitting**; fill to **150 mL** (original method; some use 200 mL).
3. Ask for a **graded Valsalva**; record the **lowest total Pves (vesical pressure)** at visible leak (include baseline, not the change).
4. No leak: repeat every 50 mL to capacity; then **remove the urethral catheter** and read pressure from the rectal line.
5. Report volume, catheter size and provocation (Valsalva or cough).

### Interpretation (McGuire 1993)
| VLPP (cmH₂O) | Inference |
|---|---|
| **<60** | **ISD (intrinsic sphincter deficiency)** |
| **60–90** | Equivocal; mixed |
| **>90** | **Urethral hypermobility**, little ISD |

### Limitations
- Not standardised: falls with **larger catheters** and **higher volumes**.
- **VLPP is lower than CLPP (cough leak point pressure)** in the same woman; state the method.
- Invalid during DO (detrusor overactivity) or poor compliance; prolapse raises it (reduce it).
- ISD and hypermobility coexist; VLPP **does not predict surgical outcome**.
- **VALUE trial (Nager 2012):** for uncomplicated SUI, office evaluation was non-inferior to urodynamics before surgery. AUA/SUFU (Society of Urodynamics, Female Pelvic Medicine and Urogenital Reconstruction) 2023: urodynamics optional in the index patient.

### VLPP vs DLPP
| VLPP | DLPP (detrusor leak point pressure) |
|---|---|
| Sphincter vs abdominal pressure | Bladder pressure vs outlet resistance |
| **Low = bad** | **High (>40 cmH₂O) = bad**: upper-tract risk (McGuire 1981) |
| SUI | Neurogenic, low-compliance bladder |

**Also know:** MUCP (maximum urethral closure pressure) ≤20 cmH₂O suggests ISD.

**Diagram to draw:** Pves, Pabd, Pdet tracing with leak marked on Valsalva.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Pves at leak with Valsalva, no detrusor contraction; 150 mL.
> - <60 ISD; 60–90 equivocal; >90 hypermobility.
> - VLPP < CLPP; catheter size and volume alter it.
> - DLPP >40: upper-tract danger.

## Q3c. HoLEP in BPH
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Principle
- **HoLEP (holmium laser enucleation of the prostate)** removes the whole adenoma along the **surgical capsule**, as in open simple prostatectomy, but endoscopically.
- **Ho:YAG (holmium:yttrium–aluminium–garnet), 2120 nm**: absorbed by water, pulsed, **0.4 mm penetration**, coagulates vessels. Normal saline irrigation (no TUR (transurethral resection) syndrome).
- Evolution: HoLAP → HoLRP → **HoLEP (1998)**, made practical by the **morcellator**.

### Indications
- **Size-independent** (<30, 30–80, >80 mL); median lobe; **anticoagulated or high bleeding risk** (AUA (American Urological Association) recommendation); alternative to open prostatectomy for large glands.

### Steps
- 60–150 W laser, 550 µm end-firing fibre, 24–28 Fr resectoscope; about 2 J × 40–50 Hz to enucleate.
1. Bladder-neck incisions at 5 and 7 o'clock to capsule (median lobe) or one at 6 o'clock.
2. Perimontanal incision; **early apical release** to spare the sphincter.
3. Develop the capsular plane round to 12 o'clock; detach lobes into the bladder (**en bloc, 2-lobe or 3-lobe**).
4. Haemostasis with a defocused beam.
5. **Morcellation** with a full bladder; 22 Fr three-way catheter.

### Outcomes
- Versus **TURP (transurethral resection of the prostate)** (trials, meta-analyses): **better IPSS (International Prostate Symptom Score) and Qmax (maximum flow rate)**, less bleeding, shorter catheter time and stay; longer operation. The **only endoscopic method superior to TURP**.
- Versus **open prostatectomy** for large glands (**Kuntz, 5-year**): equal function with less blood loss and shorter stay.
- **Durable**: whole transition zone removed; **retreatment rare**.

### Complications
- **Retrograde ejaculation** about 75%; transient storage symptoms and early SUI (stress urinary incontinence).
- BNC (bladder neck contracture) 0–3.2%; stricture; capsular perforation; ureteric orifice injury; **morcellator bladder injury**; retained adenoma.
- Erectile function unchanged.

### Limitation
- **Steep learning curve**: efficiency plateaus after about 50 cases.

### Recent advances
- **Moses pulse modulation**; thulium enucleation; ejaculation-sparing HoLEP; same-day discharge. EAU (European Association of Urology): enucleation is an alternative to TURP, and to open surgery for >80 mL.

**Diagram to draw:** sagittal prostate showing enucleation along the capsule.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Ho:YAG 2120 nm, water chromophore, 0.4 mm.
> - Size-independent; best for anticoagulated men.
> - Better IPSS and Qmax than TURP; durable; retreatment rare.
> - Retrograde ejaculation about 75%; learning curve about 50 cases.

## Q3d. Non obstructive azoospermia
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition
- **NOA (non-obstructive azoospermia):** no sperm in the **centrifuged** ejaculate on two samples, due to **failure of spermatogenesis** (testicular failure). About 60% of azoospermic men.

### Causes
- **Genetic:** **Klinefelter (47,XXY)**, the commonest; **Y microdeletion** (AZF (azoospermia factor) a, b, c); other karyotype anomalies.
- **Acquired:** cryptorchidism, mumps orchitis, torsion, **chemotherapy or radiation**, trauma, varicocele.
- **Idiopathic**: commonest overall.
- *Note:* hypogonadotropic hypogonadism is pre-testicular and treatable medically (hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin) ± FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone)).

### Evaluation
- Small soft testes, palpable vasa, normal ejaculate volume.
- **FSH >7.6 IU/L and testis long axis ≤4.6 cm** point to NOA (FSH ≤7.6 with normal testes suggests obstruction).
- LH (luteinising hormone), testosterone, oestradiol, prolactin.
- **Genetics (AUA/ASRM (American Urological Association/American Society for Reproductive Medicine)):** karyotype and Y microdeletion in all NOA; counselling.
- **Histology patterns:**

| Pattern | Meaning | Micro-TESE retrieval |
|---|---|---|
| Hypospermatogenesis | All stages, fewer cells | Best |
| Maturation arrest | Halts at a germ-cell stage | About 40% |
| SCO (Sertoli cell–only) | No germ cells | About 30% |

### Management
**1. Optimisation (before retrieval)**
- Stop exogenous testosterone; repair clinical varicocele (no definitive evidence in NOA).
- Hormonal therapy off-label: clomiphene, anastrozole, hCG for low testosterone.

**2. Micro-TESE (microdissection testicular sperm extraction)**
- **Principle:** spermatogenesis in NOA is **focal**. Under ×20–25 magnification, **larger, opaque tubules** are chosen; least tissue removed, blood supply preserved.
- Sperm found in **about 50%**; better than conventional TESE.

| Predictor | Retrieval (%) |
|---|---|
| Post-orchidopexy | 74 |
| AZFc deletion | 70 |
| Klinefelter | 68 |
| Post-chemotherapy | 53 |
| **AZFa or AZFb complete deletion** | **0: do not operate** |

- FSH and testis size **do not** reliably predict retrieval.

**3. ICSI (intracytoplasmic sperm injection)**
- Fresh or frozen testicular sperm. Live birth per cycle modest; miscarriage higher (sperm DNA damage). Male offspring of AZF deletion carriers inherit it.

**4. No sperm:** donor sperm or adoption.

**Also know:** in-vitro spermatogenesis and stem-cell transplantation are experimental.

**Diagram to draw:** opened testis under microscope: plump tubules versus thin Sertoli cell–only tubules.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - FSH >7.6 and testis ≤4.6 cm suggest NOA.
> - Karyotype and Y microdeletion in all.
> - Micro-TESE: sperm in about half; SCO 30%.
> - AZFa/b deletion: no retrieval.

## Q3e. Acetylcysteine
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

*Answered in 2018-Summer Paper 1, Q3b; core answer repeated here.*

### Introduction
- **NAC (N-acetylcysteine):** acetylated cysteine, a **thiol (–SH) compound**; oral, intravenous or nebulised.

### Mechanisms of action
- **Glutathione precursor:** supplies cysteine, the rate-limiting substrate for **glutathione**.
- **Antioxidant:** the free –SH group scavenges reactive oxygen species.
- **Mucolytic:** breaks **disulfide bonds** in mucoproteins.
- **Thiol–disulfide exchange:** in theory forms a soluble mixed disulfide with cystine.

### Clinical uses
**1. Paracetamol (acetaminophen) overdose: main indication**
- Overdose saturates normal pathways; the toxic metabolite **NAPQI (N-acetyl-p-benzoquinone imine)** builds up once glutathione is exhausted, causing liver necrosis.
- NAC restores glutathione; most effective **within 8 hours**, but still given later.
- **IV (intravenous) 21-hour regimen:** 150 mg/kg, then 50 mg/kg over 4 h, then 100 mg/kg over 16 h. Shorter two-bag regimens are used in some countries.

**2. Mucolytic:** COPD (chronic obstructive pulmonary disease), cystic fibrosis.

### Urological relevance
- **Contrast-induced AKI (acute kidney injury):** once widely used. **PRESERVE (2018):** no benefit over saline. **No longer recommended**; isotonic hydration is the proven measure.
- **Cystinuria:** thiol action theoretical; standard thiols are **tiopronin** and D-penicillamine.
- **Cyclophosphamide cystitis:** **mesna** (another thiol) is used.
- **Mucus after enterocystoplasty or neobladder:** oral or irrigation; weak evidence.
- **Male infertility:** antioxidant for oxidative stress (high ROS (reactive oxygen species), sperm DNA fragmentation); limited evidence.
- **Ischaemia–reperfusion** (renal surgery, transplant): antioxidant protection studied; not established.

### Adverse effects
- **Anaphylactoid reactions** with IV loading (flushing, rash, bronchospasm): slow the infusion, antihistamine.
- Nausea, vomiting, sulphur smell; bronchospasm when nebulised in asthma.
- Safe in pregnancy and renal impairment for paracetamol poisoning; benefit outweighs risk.

**Diagram to draw:** paracetamol → NAPQI → glutathione conjugation, with NAC replenishing glutathione.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Thiol; glutathione precursor, antioxidant, mucolytic.
> - Antidote for paracetamol overdose; best within 8 h.
> - PRESERVE 2018: no benefit in contrast AKI.
> - Cystinuria uses tiopronin, not NAC.

## Q3f. ESBL producing enterobacterial UTI
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition
- **ESBLs (extended-spectrum β-lactamases):** **plasmid-encoded** enzymes that hydrolyse penicillins, **third-generation cephalosporins** and aztreonam. They are **inhibited by clavulanate** and spare **carbapenems** and cephamycins.
- Mainly *E. coli* and *Klebsiella* (Enterobacterales); first reported 1983.

### Mechanism and spread
- Older types **TEM** and **SHV**; now **CTX-M** dominates worldwide. Spread through the community is linked to *E. coli* **ST131** (fluoroquinolone resistance is its hallmark).
- Plasmids carry co-resistance genes: **fluoroquinolones, aminoglycosides, TMP-SMX (trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole)**. So oral options are few.
- India has one of the highest prevalence rates.

### Risk factors
- Prior antibiotics (especially cephalosporins, quinolones); recent hospital stay; **indwelling catheter**, urological instrumentation; recurrent UTI; diabetes; nursing-home residence; travel to high-prevalence areas.

### Detection
- Screening: reduced susceptibility to ceftriaxone, cefotaxime or ceftazidime.
- **Phenotypic confirmation:** clavulanate synergy (**combination disc** or double-disc synergy test); automated systems.
- Genotypic PCR (polymerase chain reaction) for CTX-M.

### Treatment
| Infection | Options |
|---|---|
| **Cystitis** | **Nitrofurantoin** 5 days, **fosfomycin** 3 g single dose (E. coli), **pivmecillinam**; TMP-SMX or quinolone if susceptible; single-dose aminoglycoside |
| **Pyelonephritis, complicated UTI** | Quinolone or TMP-SMX if susceptible; else **aminoglycoside** |
| **Urosepsis, bacteraemia** | **Carbapenem** (meropenem, ertapenem), then oral step-down by culture |

- **MERINO trial (2018):** in ceftriaxone-resistant *E. coli*/*Klebsiella* bacteraemia, **piperacillin–tazobactam** had higher 30-day mortality than meropenem (12.3% vs 3.7%). Avoid pip-taz for serious ESBL infection.
- **Ceftolozane–tazobactam, ceftazidime–avibactam:** active but **reserved** for carbapenem-resistant organisms (stewardship).
- Remove or change catheters; drain obstruction (source control).
- Treat **asymptomatic bacteriuria** only before urological procedures or in pregnancy.

### Prevention
- **Antibiotic stewardship:** avoid broad-spectrum cephalosporins and quinolones; shortest effective course; culture before treatment.
- Hand hygiene, contact precautions, catheter care.
- Pre-biopsy rectal swab culture for targeted prophylaxis; transperineal biopsy.
