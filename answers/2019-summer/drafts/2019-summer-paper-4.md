## Q1. Discuss neuromodulation for voiding dysfunction.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

### Definition and history
- **Neuromodulation:** electrical stimulation of a peripheral nerve that alters the activity of central pathways controlling the bladder. It acts on the nervous system, not directly on muscle.
- Direct muscle stimulation failed (painful, local). Stimulating **afferents** needs less current and affects the whole organ.
- **Tanagho and Schmidt** (about 1985): SNM (sacral neuromodulation). PTNS (percutaneous tibial nerve stimulation): FDA (Food and Drug Administration) approval 2000.
- **Rule:** exclude anatomical causes first (obstruction, stones, cancer, fistula). Stimulation cannot correct anatomy or restore muscle.

### Mechanism
- Sacral roots are mixed nerves. Current excites **afferents → spinal cord and forebrain** (areas of awareness and attention) → altered PMC (pontine micturition centre) output.
- **OAB (overactive bladder):** heightened central control damps PMC firing → fewer involuntary contractions.
- **Non-obstructive retention:** restores awareness of filling and pelvic floor tone → PMC switches on → **EUS (external urethral sphincter) relaxes**. Hence SNM treats opposite disorders.
- **Tibial:** nerve carries **S2–S3** fibres; inhibition of OAB-type (C-fibre) reflexes is **opioid-mediated** (naloxone blocks it); long-term cortical plasticity.

### Modalities
| Modality | Target and method | Main use |
|---|---|---|
| **SNM** | Tined lead in **S3** foramen + implanted generator | Refractory OAB, NOUR (non-obstructive urinary retention) |
| **PTNS** | 34-gauge needle above medial malleolus | Refractory OAB |
| TTNS (transcutaneous tibial) | Surface patch, home use | OAB (less effective) |
| Implantable tibial | Subcutaneous device over tibial nerve | OAB, avoids clinic visits |
| Pudendal | Tined lead in Alcock's canal | IDO (idiopathic detrusor overactivity), NDO (neurogenic DO), SNM failure |
| DGNS (dorsal genital nerve stimulation) | Dorsal penile/clitoral nerve | Experimental; NDO |
| IVES (intravesical electrical stimulation) | Intravesical cathode | Underactive bladder in children |
| **SARS (sacral anterior root stimulator)** | Anterior roots + dorsal rhizotomy | Complete SCI (spinal cord injury) |

### A. Sacral neuromodulation
- **Third line** for OAB (AUA (American Urological Association) and EAU (European Association of Urology) agree). **FDA approved:** UUI (urgency urinary incontinence) 1997; urgency-frequency and NOUR 1999. **Not approved:** bladder pain, pelvic pain, neurogenic bladder.
- **Test phase** (bladder diary): **PNE (percutaneous nerve evaluation)**: office, temporary lead, 5–7 days; or **staged implant**: permanent tined lead under fluoroscopy, test up to 4 weeks, then generator in the buttock (preferred for NOUR).
- **Landmark:** S3 about 9 cm above the coccyx tip, 1–2 cm lateral. **S3 response: perineal "bellows" + great-toe flexion** (S2: calf/foot rotation; S4: bellows only).
- **Implant if ≥50% improvement** in a diary parameter (retention: ≥50% fewer catheterisations).
- **Evidence:** **InSite**: success 61% vs 42% with medical therapy. **ROSETTA**: onabotulinumtoxinA 200 U reduced UUI slightly more (−3.9 vs −3.3 episodes/day) but caused more UTI (urinary tract infection) (24% vs 10%) and CIC (clean intermittent catheterisation). NOUR: most stop catheterising.
- **Problems:** pain, lead migration, loss of effect; **revision >30% at 5 years**; costly.
- **Advances:** rechargeable (≥15 years) devices; full-body **MRI (magnetic resonance imaging)-conditional**.

### B. Tibial nerve stimulation
- **PTNS technique:** 34-gauge needle **3–4 cm (three fingerbreadths) above the medial malleolus**, behind the tibia; ground pad on same leg; 20 Hz, 0–10 mA. Confirm by **toe flexion/fanning** or sole tingling. **30 min weekly × 12 weeks**, then maintenance.
- **SUmiT** (n = 220, validated sham): response **55% vs 21%**. Extension: 77% relapse-free at 3 years with tapering maintenance.
- **OrBIT:** PTNS comparable to extended-release tolterodine, with fewer side effects.
- **TTNS:** home self-treatment; skin impedance limits amplitude, so less effective.
- **Implantable devices** remove clinic visits: **BlueWind** (miniature, external control; benefit sustained at 3 years); **eCoin** (coin-sized, battery-powered, office insertion under local anaesthesia).

### C. Other targets
- **Pudendal nerve stimulation:** tined lead via posterior approach. Better than SNM in a blinded crossover; **salvage after failed SNM**. Off-label.
- **DGNS:** purely sensory; acute DO suppression in SCI; **conditional (on-demand)** stimulation possible. Electrode fixation is difficult.
- **IVES (Katona):** intravesical cathode in saline activates Aδ afferents → detrusor contraction. Needs some afferent and muscle function; daily sessions; the sham-controlled trial was negative.
- **Brindley SARS:** stimulates anterior S2–S4 roots → voiding, defecation, erection. **Dorsal rhizotomy** abolishes reflex incontinence and DSD (detrusor–sphincter dyssynergia). Sphincter relaxes faster than detrusor between bursts → **post-stimulus voiding**. Needs intact efferents.

### Comparison (third-line OAB)
| | PTNS | OnabotulinumtoxinA 100 U | SNM |
|---|---|---|---|
| Invasiveness | Lowest | Moderate | Implant |
| Drawback | 12 weekly visits | UTI, retention/CIC | Revision, cost |
| Retention | No role | Worsens | **Treats** |

- **Also know:** intravaginal stimulation (SUI, stress urinary incontinence); sacral dermatome stimulation; bilateral SNM no better; closed-loop SNM.

**Diagram to draw:** S3 lead with buttock generator, and PTNS needle above the medial malleolus.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Afferent modulation explains SNM benefit in both OAB and retention.
> - S3: bellows + great-toe flexion; implant if ≥50% improvement.
> - SUmiT: PTNS 55% vs sham 21%; 12 weekly sessions.
> - ROSETTA: Botox 200 U slightly better for UUI, more UTI and CIC.

## Q2. Discuss bladder preservation surgery for TCC of urinary bladder.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

*Read as: bladder-preserving treatment of muscle-invasive TCC (transitional cell carcinoma, urothelial carcinoma), including radical TURBT (transurethral resection of bladder tumour), partial cystectomy and trimodality therapy.*

### Introduction
- Standard for MIBC (muscle-invasive bladder cancer): cisplatin NAC (neoadjuvant chemotherapy) + RC (radical cystectomy) with PLND (pelvic lymph node dissection).
- RC is morbid and patients are often elderly. Preservation suits **(1) unfit patients** and **(2) fit, motivated patients with limited disease**.
- **Why single modalities fail:** they leave either the remaining urothelium or occult nodes untreated (cT2 understaged to pT3 in up to about 40%; occult nodes about 25%).

### Options
| Option | Status |
|---|---|
| Radical TURBT alone | Not a primary treatment |
| Partial cystectomy + PLND | Highly selected solitary tumours; urachal cancer |
| RT (radiotherapy) alone | Inferior; quasi-palliative |
| Chemotherapy alone | Not recommended |
| **TMT (trimodality therapy)** | **Best-studied, guideline-accepted** |

### A. Radical TURBT
- Rationale: about 10% are pT0 at cystectomy. Only for tumour limited to superficial muscle, with negative re-resection.
- About a third progress to MIBC on long follow-up; selected series report 5-year CSS (cancer-specific survival) about 82%.

### B. Partial cystectomy
**Ideal candidate:**
- Small (**<3 cm**), **solitary** tumour in a mobile part of bladder (dome).
- **No CIS (carcinoma in situ)**, no hydronephrosis.
- 1–2 cm margin achievable away from the ureteric orifice and trigone; adequate residual capacity.
- **Treatment of choice for urachal adenocarcinoma** (en bloc dome, urachus and umbilicus).

**Contraindications:** CIS, multifocal tumour, trigone/bladder neck tumour, small bladder, prostatic urethral involvement.

**Technique:** NAC if cisplatin-eligible → PLND → cystotomy away from tumour → full-thickness excision with perivesical fat, **frozen-section margins** → reimplant ureter if needed → watertight closure → irrigation (prevents seeding) → drain; cystogram before catheter removal.

**Outcomes:** 5-year OS (overall survival) 67–70%, intact bladder 65–74%; salvage RC up to a quarter; late recurrence → lifelong follow-up.

### C. Trimodality therapy
**Components:** (1) **maximal, visibly complete TURBT**; (2) **RT** to bladder ± pelvic nodes; (3) concurrent **radiosensitising chemotherapy**.

**Selection criteria:**
- cT2 (selected cT3–4a), small solitary tumour.
- **No hydronephrosis, no extensive CIS**, visibly complete TURBT.
- Good bladder function; motivated, compliant patient.
- Only 6–19% of MIBC meet strict criteria.

**Radiosensitising regimens:**
| Regimen | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Mitomycin C + 5-FU (fluorouracil)** | **BC2001**: 2-year locoregional DFS (disease-free survival) **67% vs 48%** vs RT alone, no excess late toxicity |
| Cisplatin (± 5-FU or paclitaxel) | MGH (Massachusetts General Hospital)/RTOG (Radiation Therapy Oncology Group) protocols |
| Gemcitabine | Cisplatin-ineligible |
| Carbogen + nicotinamide (BCON) | Hypoxia modification |

**Radiation schedules:**
```
Split course (MGH/RTOG):  TURBT → chemoRT ~40 Gy → cystoscopy + biopsy
                           CR → consolidation to 60–66 Gy
                           Residual tumour → immediate cystectomy
Continuous (current):      TURBT → chemoRT 64 Gy/32 fr or 55 Gy/20 fr
                           → cystoscopy and imaging at 3–4 months
```

**Response assessment:** cystoscopy with biopsy of tumour site, cytology, CT (computed tomography)/MRI (magnetic resonance imaging). **CR (complete response)** → surveillance. Non-response → salvage RC.

**Salvage cystectomy:**
- Needed in **10–30%** for persistent or recurrent muscle-invasive disease; it is the designed safety net.
- NMIBC (non-muscle-invasive) recurrence → TURBT + intravesical BCG (bacillus Calmette–Guérin).

**Outcomes:**
- 5-year CSS 50–84%, OS 36–74%.
- MGH/RTOG: CR 69%; 5-year DSS (disease-specific survival) 71%; 80% of survivors keep the bladder.
- Propensity-matched RC vs TMT: equal 5-year metastasis-free survival (about 75%) and CSS (81% vs 84%).
- Low late toxicity; good quality of life.

### Comparison with radical cystectomy
| | Radical cystectomy | TMT |
|---|---|---|
| Staging | Pathological | Clinical only |
| Nodes | Removed | Irradiated or not treated |
| Function | Diversion; sexual dysfunction | Native bladder; bowel/bladder toxicity |
| Follow-up | Upper tract, urethra | **Intensive cystoscopy for 10 years** |
| Evidence | Standard | No completed RCT (SPARE closed for poor accrual) |

### Follow-up after preservation
- Cystoscopy every 3–4 months for 2 years, 6-monthly years 3–4, then yearly to 10 years; CT urogram and chest CT every 3–6 months for 2 years, then yearly to 5 years; cytology.

### Recent advances
- **Immunotherapy + chemoradiation:** PD-1/PD-L1 (programmed death-1 / ligand-1) inhibitors with TMT under trial (e.g. SWOG/NRG S1806 with atezolizumab; KEYNOTE-992 with pembrolizumab); results awaited.
- Hypofractionation 55 Gy/20 non-inferior (BC2001–BCON meta-analysis: same toxicity).
- Bladder sparing after complete response to NAC (± checkpoint inhibitor) with biomarker selection (DNA repair gene mutations, circulating tumour DNA); RETAIN: about half recurred.
- **Also know:** robotic partial cystectomy; molecular subtypes.

**Diagram to draw:** TMT flowchart from TURBT to chemoradiation, response check, surveillance or salvage cystectomy.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - TMT = complete TURBT + RT + radiosensitiser; select T2, no hydronephrosis, no CIS.
> - BC2001: MMC/5-FU + RT, 2-year locoregional DFS 67% vs 48%.
> - Salvage cystectomy 10–30%; follow up the bladder for 10 years.
> - Partial cystectomy: solitary <3 cm, no CIS, frozen-section margins, always PLND.

## Q3a. PSMA PET Ct scan
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Principle
- **PSMA (prostate-specific membrane antigen):** transmembrane glycoprotein over-expressed by prostate cancer, rising with grade.
- **PET/CT (positron emission tomography/computed tomography):** positron-labelled ligand binds PSMA; uptake as SUV (standardised uptake value).
- **Tracers:** ⁶⁸Ga-PSMA-11 (half-life 68 min); ¹⁸F-DCFPyL, ¹⁸F-rhPSMA-7.3, ¹⁸F-PSMA-1007 (110 min, better images; PSMA-1007 little urinary excretion).
- **Specificity >90%**; beats choline and fluciclovine.

### Indications
**1. Primary staging (high risk):**
- **proPSMA (2020):** accuracy **92% vs 65%** vs CT + bone scan.
- Nodal sensitivity only 30–40% against PLND (pelvic lymph node dissection): a negative scan does not exclude micrometastases.
- NCCN (National Comprehensive Cancer Network): may **replace** conventional imaging; AUA (American Urological Association): add-on.

**2. BCR (biochemical recurrence):**
- Detection rises with PSA (prostate-specific antigen): about **half positive below 0.5 ng/mL**, nearly all above 2.
- **Calais trial:** PSMA 56% vs fluciclovine 26%. **CONDOR:** management changed in 63.9%.
- Guides salvage radiotherapy (disease outside field in about 1 in 5).

**3. Intraprostatic detection:** **PRIMARY** trial: adding PSMA PET to MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) improved negative predictive value; 5-point PRIMARY score.

**4. Theranostics:** selects patients for **¹⁷⁷Lu-PSMA-617**. **VISION (2021):** mCRPC (metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer) after ARPI (androgen receptor pathway inhibitor) and taxane: OS 15.3 vs 11.3 months.

### Reporting
- **PROMISE miTNM** (molecular imaging TNM): miT, miN, miM staging plus expression score (vs blood pool, liver, parotid). PSMA-RADS also used.

### Pitfalls
| False positive | False negative |
|---|---|
| Coeliac/stellate ganglia, fractures, Paget's disease, fibrous dysplasia | Small nodes (<5 mm) |
| Other tumours (RCC (renal cell carcinoma), HCC (hepatocellular carcinoma)) | PSMA-negative tumours (about 5–10%) |
| ¹⁸F-PSMA-1007 benign bone uptake | Neuroendocrine transformation; masking by bladder activity |

- **Also know:** no proof yet that PSMA-guided treatment improves survival (stage migration).

**Diagram to draw:** PSMA ligand binding the cell-surface antigen with fused PET/CT image of a nodal lesion.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - proPSMA: accuracy 92% vs 65%.
> - BCR: about half positive below PSA 0.5 ng/mL.
> - Nodal sensitivity 30–40%: negative scan does not exclude nodes.
> - VISION: ¹⁷⁷Lu-PSMA-617 improves OS in mCRPC.

## Q3b. LHRH agonist
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Physiology
- **LHRH (luteinising hormone-releasing hormone, GnRH (gonadotrophin-releasing hormone))** from the hypothalamus is released in **pulses**. It drives pituitary LH (luteinising hormone) and FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone). LH → Leydig cells → testosterone (about 95% testicular).
- **Continuous** exposure **desensitises and down-regulates** the pituitary receptor. This is the basis of agonist therapy.

### Drugs
| Drug | Formulation |
|---|---|
| Leuprolide | Depot 1, 3, 6 monthly |
| Goserelin | SC (subcutaneous) implant 1 or 3 monthly |
| Triptorelin | IM (intramuscular) depot |
| Histrelin | Implant, 1 year |
| Buserelin | Nasal or SC |

- Substitution at **position 6** of the decapeptide raises potency and half-life.

### Mechanism
```
Agonist → initial LH surge (flare, testosterone up)
        → receptor down-regulation (1–2 weeks)
        → LH falls → castrate testosterone in 2–4 weeks
```
- Survival **equals orchidectomy**. Castrate: **<50 ng/dL** (traditional); aim **<20 ng/dL** (orchidectomy median about 15).

### Flare phenomenon
- LH rises up to **10-fold for 10–20 days** → bone pain, **spinal cord compression**, urinary retention.
- Dangerous with spinal or weight-bearing metastases.
- **Prevent:** antiandrogen (bicalutamide, flutamide) started with or before the agonist, for **21–28 days**.
- Impending cord compression → **antagonist** (degarelix) or orchidectomy instead.

### Indications
- Metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (with ARPI (androgen receptor pathway inhibitor) or docetaxel).
- With radiotherapy for intermediate/high-risk and locally advanced disease.
- BCR (biochemical recurrence) with short doubling time; continued in CRPC (castration-resistant prostate cancer).
- **Also know:** central precocious puberty, endometriosis; cryptorchidism (low success); intermittent ADT (androgen deprivation therapy) acceptable (PR.7).

### Side effects
- Hot flushes (50–80%), loss of libido, ED (erectile dysfunction).
- **Osteoporosis** (DEXA (dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry); calcium, vitamin D, denosumab or zoledronic acid).
- Diabetes, cardiovascular events, sarcopenia, anaemia, gynaecomastia (≤5%), cognitive change.
- Slow testosterone recovery after stopping.

### Agonist vs antagonist
| Agonist | Antagonist |
|---|---|
| Flare; needs antiandrogen | No flare; castrate by day 3 (degarelix) |
| FSH rises | FSH falls |
| Depot to yearly | Monthly SC or oral (relugolix; HERO) |

**Diagram to draw:** hypothalamic–pituitary–testis axis showing the agonist site and flare.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Continuous stimulation → down-regulation → castration in 2–4 weeks.
> - Flare 10–20 days; antiandrogen for 21–28 days.
> - Castrate <50 ng/dL; aim <20 ng/dL.

## Q3c. Digital flexible ureteroscopes
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Introduction
- **Fibreoptic flexible URS (ureteroscopy):** coherent glass-fibre bundle carries the image; fibres break → black spots; honeycomb (moiré) pattern.
- **Digital scopes** (first 2006): a sensor at the tip (**"chip-on-tip"**) converts light to an electrical signal, so the image does not degrade.

### Sensor technology
| | CCD (charge-coupled device) | CMOS (complementary metal-oxide semiconductor) |
|---|---|---|
| Heat and power | More | Less (runs cooler) |
| Cost, speed | Higher, slower | Cheaper, faster |
| Status | Phased out | Current standard |

- **LED (light-emitting diode)** at the tip; no camera head or light cable → better ergonomics.

### Fibreoptic vs digital
| Feature | Fibreoptic | Digital |
|---|---|---|
| Image | Degrades | Sharper, magnified, stable |
| Tip | Smallest, tapered | Larger (about 8.5 Fr), flatter |
| Durability | Repair about every 15 cases | **Not better** in later comparisons |
| Operative time | Longer | Shorter; similar SFR (stone-free rate) |
| Processor | Platform-agnostic | Proprietary |

- Larger tip → more access-sheath use. Keep a fibreoptic scope for narrow UPJ (pelviureteric junction) or infundibulum.

### Deflection and channel
- Active deflection **≥270° both directions**; enhanced primary deflection eases lower-pole access.
- Working channel **3.6 Fr**; dual-channel scopes exist.

### Single-use vs reusable
- **LithoVue (2016):** first single-use digital scope; CMOS, 7.7 Fr tip, 9.5 Fr shaft, 270°.
- Single-use advantages: maximal deflection each case, sterile, no reprocessing or repair; cost **lower below about 99 cases/year**.
- Reusable: cheaper in high-volume centres; needs reprocessing (peracetic acid, glutaraldehyde, ethylene oxide).
- Most centres use a **hybrid fleet**; environmental impact similar.

### Recent advances
- **Intrarenal pressure sensor** in the scope (LithoVue Elite): high pressure causes fever, SIRS (systemic inflammatory response syndrome), sepsis. Temperature sensing next.
- **AI (artificial intelligence):** stone recognition, laser setting adjustment, navigation.
- **Robotic flexible URS:** ergonomics, less radiation to surgeon.
- **Also know:** suction sheaths, further miniaturisation.

**Diagram to draw:** scope tip cross-section showing CMOS chip, LED and 3.6 Fr channel.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Chip-on-tip CMOS: better image, larger tip, durability not better.
> - Single-use cheaper below about 99 cases/year.
> - Pressure sensors and AI are the newest developments.

## Q3d. Videoendoscopic inguinal lymphadenectomy
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition
- **VEIL (video-endoscopic inguinal lymphadenectomy):** ILND (inguinal lymph node dissection) through thigh ports, beneath Scarpa's fascia; laparoscopic (Tobias-Machado) or robotic (**RVEIL**).
- **Aim:** open-surgery template without the groin incision that causes flap necrosis.

### Indications
- Penile SCC (squamous cell carcinoma), **impalpable nodes**, intermediate/high risk (pT1b+, G3, LVI (lymphovascular invasion)).
- **Mobile, non-bulky palpable nodes.**
- **Not for** bulky, fixed (cN3) or skin-involving nodes → chemotherapy, then open surgery.
- Also vulval cancer, melanoma.

### Boundaries (femoral triangle)
- Superior: **inguinal ligament**; lateral: **sartorius**; medial: **adductor longus**; floor: fascia lata; apex: muscles crossing.
- Mark an inverted triangle on the skin **before insufflation**, because gas distorts landmarks.

### Technique
```
Position: supine frog-leg / low lithotomy
→ 2 cm camera incision below the femoral triangle apex
→ finger dissection beneath Scarpa's fascia
→ two lateral and medial working ports (+ assistant port)
→ CO2 at 15 mmHg (higher briefly to create space)
→ develop space up to the inguinal ligament
→ remove nodal packet off fascia lata, apex to ligament
→ preserve saphenous vein if possible; clip tributaries
→ frozen section → deep dissection medial to femoral vein if positive
→ bag retrieval, suction drain in dependent part
```
- **Template:** superficial ± deep; Daseler's five zones; Cloquet's node is the highest deep node.
- **Never dissect lateral to the femoral artery** (femoral nerve).

### Outcomes vs open
| | Open ILND | VEIL/RVEIL |
|---|---|---|
| Wound infection, necrosis, dehiscence | Higher | **Lower** |
| Hospital stay, drain time | Longer | Shorter |
| Operative time | Shorter | About 2 h (slightly longer per side) |
| Nodal yield, recurrence | Reference | **Similar** |
| Evidence | Retrospective | Retrospective, selection-biased |

- Commonest complication: **lymphocele**, then lymphoedema and cellulitis.

### Robotic VEIL
- 3D vision, wristed instruments, ICG (indocyanine green) for lymphatics; adequate dissection on open re-exploration (phase I). No VEIL vs RVEIL comparison.

- **Also know:** DSNB (dynamic sentinel node biopsy) first for impalpable nodes (EAU-ASCO, European Association of Urology–American Society of Clinical Oncology); modified (Catalona) template; complete nodal management within 3 months.

**Diagram to draw:** femoral triangle with port sites below the apex.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Same template as open: ligament, sartorius, adductor longus.
> - Fewer wound complications; equal nodal yield.
> - Not for bulky, fixed or skin-involving nodes.

## Q3e. Methods to predict prognosis of patient of Cancer prostate
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### 1. Clinical and pathological factors
- **PSA (prostate-specific antigen):** organ-confined about 80% at <4 but <50% at >10 ng/mL; density and velocity add information.
- **Clinical stage (DRE (digital rectal examination), TNM).**
- **Grade group (ISUP (International Society of Urological Pathology)) – strongest single factor:**

| Grade group | Gleason | 5-year BCR (biochemical recurrence)-free |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ≤6 | 96% |
| 2 | 3+4 | 88% |
| 3 | 4+3 | 63% |
| 4 | 8 | 48% |
| 5 | 9–10 | 26% |

- Biopsy: % positive cores, **cribriform and intraductal** patterns, perineural invasion.
- Prostatectomy: margin (extent, grade), EPE (extraprostatic extension), seminal vesicle and node status.

### 2. Risk groups
| D'Amico | Low | Intermediate | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| PSA (ng/mL) | ≤10 | 10–20 | >20 |
| Gleason | ≤6 | 7 | 8–10 |
| Stage | T1–T2a | T2b | T2c |

- **NCCN (National Comprehensive Cancer Network):** very low, low, favourable/unfavourable intermediate, high, very high.
- **CAPRA (Cancer of the Prostate Risk Assessment)** score 0–10 (age, PSA, Gleason, stage, % cores); **CAPRA-S** after surgery.

### 3. Nomograms
- **Partin tables:** organ-confined disease, EPE, seminal vesicle, node involvement.
- **MSKCC (Memorial Sloan Kettering) nomograms:** pre- and post-treatment BCR and 15-year cancer-specific survival.
- **Briganti nomogram:** risk of nodal invasion → decides PLND (pelvic lymph node dissection).

### 4. Imaging
- mpMRI (multiparametric MRI): PI-RADS (Prostate Imaging Reporting and Data System) score, tumour contact length, EPE.
- PSMA PET (prostate-specific membrane antigen PET) for metastatic burden.

### 5. Tissue biomarkers
| Test | Basis | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Decipher** | 22-RNA classifier | Metastasis risk; post-op radiotherapy |
| **Oncotype DX GPS** | 17 genes | Adverse pathology; active surveillance |
| **Prolaris** | Cell-cycle progression genes | Progression, cancer death |

- Germline BRCA2 (BReast CAncer gene 2): aggressive disease.

### 6. After treatment
- **PSA doubling time (PSADT):** <3 months highest risk; Pound: time to BCR <2 years, PSADT <10 months, Gleason 8–10 predict metastasis.
- **EAU (European Association of Urology) BCR risk groups:** low risk = PSADT >1 year and grade group <4.
- PSA nadir on ADT (androgen deprivation therapy); **Phoenix** failure (nadir + 2) after radiotherapy.

**Diagram to draw:** flowchart from risk group to nomogram, biomarker and treatment decision.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Grade group is the strongest predictor.
> - D'Amico, NCCN and CAPRA combine PSA, grade and stage.
> - Decipher, Oncotype, Prolaris add genomic risk.

## Q3f. Male menopause
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

*Read as: late-onset hypogonadism (testosterone deficiency of ageing men, "andropause"). Same topic as 2017-Summer Paper 4, Q3e and 2017-Winter Paper 4, Q3e; full answer given here.*

### Definition
- Symptoms plus **consistently low serum testosterone** in an ageing man.
- "Menopause" is a misnomer: the fall is gradual and partial. Preferred terms: PADAM (partial androgen deficiency of the ageing male), LOH (late-onset hypogonadism).

### Pathophysiology
- Testosterone falls slowly from middle age.
- **Testis:** fewer Leydig cells. **Centre:** blunted feedback, irregular GnRH (gonadotrophin-releasing hormone) pulses.
- **SHBG (sex hormone-binding globulin) rises** with age, so free testosterone falls more.
- Worse with obesity, diabetes and **opioids**.

### Clinical features
- **Sexual (most specific):** low libido, ED (erectile dysfunction), fewer morning erections.
- Fatigue, low muscle mass, osteoporosis, anaemia, low mood.
- **ADAM (Androgen Deficiency in the Aging Male)** questionnaire: sensitive, not specific.

### Diagnosis
- **Morning (7–11 am) total testosterone, twice.** AUA (American Urological Association): **<300 ng/dL**. EAU (European Association of Urology): about **12 nmol/L**; calculated free testosterone if borderline.
- **LH (luteinising hormone):** high in primary, low or normal in secondary hypogonadism. Check **prolactin** if LH is low; pituitary MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) if testosterone <150 ng/dL.
- Baseline: PSA (prostate-specific antigen), DRE (digital rectal examination), haematocrit.

### Treatment
- Lifestyle first (weight loss, exercise).
- **TRT (testosterone replacement therapy)** for symptoms + confirmed low level: gels, injections (enanthate every 2–3 weeks; undecanoate every 10–14 weeks), oral undecanoate. Stop if no benefit at 3–6 months.
- **Contraindications:**
  - Prostate or breast cancer.
  - **Wish for fertility** (TRT stops sperm production; use hCG (human chorionic gonadotrophin) or clomiphene).
  - Haematocrit >50%.
  - Untreated severe sleep apnoea; recent MI (myocardial infarction).

### Monitoring
- At 3–6 months, then yearly: testosterone, **haematocrit** (stop if >54%), PSA.

### Safety evidence
- **TRAVERSE (2023):** 5,246 men aged 45–80 at high cardiovascular risk. TRT was **non-inferior for major cardiac events**; more atrial fibrillation and pulmonary embolism.
