## Q1. Various uses of intestines in Uro
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)
(also answered in 2023-Winter Paper 2, Q2)

*Read as: the various uses of intestinal segments in urology.*

### Introduction
- Bowel is used to store urine, carry it to the skin, replace the ureter, make catheterisable channels and line a neovagina.
- Choice depends on renal function, previous surgery or radiation, bowel disease, mesenteric length and the reconstruction needed.

### Uses at a glance
| Use | Segment and operation |
|---|---|
| **Incontinent diversion** | **Ileal conduit** (10–15 cm ileum, 10–15 cm from the ileocaecal valve); colon conduit (transverse) after pelvic radiation; jejunal conduit only if nothing else (avoid) |
| **Continent cutaneous pouch** | Indiana (right colon, plicated ileum as channel), Mainz I, Kock (ileal nipple valves); Mitrofanoff channel |
| **Orthotopic neobladder** | Studer (40–44 cm ileum plus afferent limb), Hautmann (~70 cm ileum, W shape), joined to the urethra |
| **Rectal reservoir** | Ureterosigmoidostomy, Mainz II: now rare (acidosis, cancer) |
| **Augmentation cystoplasty** | "Clam" ileocystoplasty (20–40 cm ileum), sigmoid, caecum; gastrocystoplasty in renal failure. For refractory neurogenic or small contracted bladder; needs CIC (clean intermittent catheterisation) |
| **Ureteric replacement** | **Ileal ureter**, isoperistaltic, for long defects; Yang–Monti tubes |
| **Catheterisable channels** | **Mitrofanoff** appendicovesicostomy; **Yang–Monti** (1–2 cm ileum retubularised transversely); MACE (Malone antegrade continence enema) for bowel |
| **Neovagina** | **Sigmoid** preferred (wide lumen, mobile mesentery, less stenosis); ileum if pelvis irradiated; MRKH (Mayer–Rokitansky–Küster–Hauser), cloaca, gender-affirming vaginoplasty |

- Ileal ureter contraindications: creatinine >2 mg/dL, bladder outlet obstruction, inflammatory bowel disease, radiation enteritis.

### Choice of segment
| Segment | Electrolyte disturbance | Points |
|---|---|---|
| **Stomach** | Hypochloraemic, hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis | Less mucus; usable in renal failure; **haematuria–dysuria syndrome** |
| **Jejunum** | Hyponatraemic, hypochloraemic, **hyperkalaemic acidosis** | Severe salt loss; avoid |
| **Ileum** | **Hyperchloraemic metabolic acidosis** | Mobile, reliable supply; B12 and bile-salt loss |
| **Colon** | Hyperchloraemic metabolic acidosis | Easy antireflux tunnel; diarrhoea if ileocaecal valve used |

### Technical principles
- **Preparation:** ERAS (enhanced recovery after surgery); routine mechanical bowel preparation not needed; mark the stoma through the rectus.
- **Bowel anastomosis:** good blood supply, no tension, no spillage; close the mesenteric window.
- **Mesentery:** transilluminate, keep a major arcade; avoid watershed areas (Griffiths point, Sudeck point).
- **Detubularisation:** opening the bowel on its antimesenteric border and refolding it into a sphere **abolishes coordinated contractions** and gives more volume for the same surface area, so pressure stays low (Laplace's law).
- **Ureterointestinal anastomosis:** spatulated, tension-free, mucosa to mucosa, stented. **Wallace** (ureters joined, refluxing) has the lowest stricture rate (~3%); **Bricker** end-to-side 4–22%; antireflux tunnels (Leadbetter–Clarke) stricture more (8–14%). Reflux with normal ureters has not been shown to harm the adult kidney.
- **Stoma:** protruding "rosebud" for appliances; flush for catheterised pouches; loop-end (Turnbull) in obese patients.

### Selecting conduit versus continent diversion
- **Renal function for continent diversion:** creatinine <2 mg/dL; GFR (glomerular filtration rate) >35 mL/min; urine pH ≤5.8 after ammonium chloride load; osmolality ≥600 mOsm/kg after water deprivation; minimal proteinuria.
- **Contraindications to continent diversion:** poor renal or hepatic function, inability or unwillingness to self-catheterise, inflammatory bowel disease or short gut. A neobladder also needs a tumour-free urethra and a competent sphincter.
- A conduit is simplest and fastest; continent options improve body image but carry more reoperations and metabolic problems.

### Complications
**Metabolic** (worse with longer segment, longer contact time, poorer renal function)
- **Hyperchloraemic acidosis:** ammonium replaces sodium on the Na⁺/H⁺ exchanger, coupled with Cl⁻/HCO₃⁻ exchange, so ammonium chloride is absorbed. Treat with sodium bicarbonate or citrate.
- **Hypokalaemia** (colon): replace potassium before correcting acidosis.
- **Altered sensorium:** hyperammonaemia (drain the pouch, lactulose). **Drug reabsorption:** phenytoin, methotrexate.
- **Osteomalacia**, growth delay in children; **B12 deficiency** (parenteral B12 after 5–7 years); bile-salt diarrhoea.

**Other**
- Bacteriuria (common; treat only symptoms or urea-splitting organisms); **stones** (reservoirs ~20%, mucus and staples); **augment perforation** (emergency; regular CIC prevents it); ureterointestinal stricture; bowel obstruction; stomal stenosis, parastomal hernia; mucus.
- **Cancer:** ureterosigmoidostomy 6–29% after 10–20 years (endoscopy from 5 years); augments (endoscopy from 10 years). Neovagina: stenosis, mucus discharge, adenocarcinoma reported.

### Follow-up
- Lifelong electrolytes, bicarbonate, renal function, B12, upper-tract imaging, and endoscopy of colonic or augmented segments.

### Recent advances
- Robotic intracorporeal diversion; tissue-engineered bladder and alternatives to bowel (ureterocystoplasty, autoaugmentation) remain limited.

**Diagram to draw:** ileal conduit with mesenteric arcade and Wallace anastomosis.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Ileum and colon: hyperchloraemic acidosis; jejunum: hyperkalaemia; stomach: alkalosis.
> - Continent diversion needs GFR >35, pH ≤5.8, osmolality ≥600.
> - Detubularisation gives low pressure; Wallace has the lowest stricture rate.
> - Sigmoid is the default neovagina; Yang–Monti when the appendix is absent.

## Q2. Describe management of LAPC
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)
(related: Practice Set 10 Paper 2, Q1)

*LAPC is read as locally advanced prostate cancer.*

### Definition and introduction
- **cT3–4, N0 or N1, M0:** extracapsular extension (T3a), seminal vesicle invasion (T3b), fixed or invading adjacent organs (T4), ± pelvic nodes.
- At least 10% of new cases. Any single treatment carries a high recurrence risk, so **multimodal therapy** is the rule. In the PSA (prostate-specific antigen) era, high risk is defined more by grade and PSA than by palpation.
- Untreated, 15-year disease-specific survival is 57% for T3–T4 vs 81% for T1–T2 (Swedish cohort).

### Evaluation
- **DRE (digital rectal examination)**, PSA, biopsy ISUP (International Society of Urological Pathology) grade group.
- **mpMRI (multiparametric MRI):** separates T2 from T3 (PI-RADS v2 reporting).
- **Bone scan + CT/MRI**, or **PSMA (prostate-specific membrane antigen) PET-CT**. proPSMA trial: accuracy 92% vs 65% for conventional imaging.
- NCCN (National Comprehensive Cancer Network) very high risk: cT3b–T4, primary Gleason pattern 5, or >4 cores grade group 4–5. **Also know:** CAPRA (Cancer of the Prostate Risk Assessment) score; Decipher.
- Assess life expectancy: a fit man in his 70s with high-grade disease will live long enough to die of it.

### A. Radiotherapy (RT) + long-term ADT (androgen deprivation therapy): standard
- Dose-escalated IMRT (intensity-modulated RT, ≥78–81 Gy), or EBRT (external-beam RT) with brachytherapy boost (**ASCENDE-RT:** LDR (low-dose-rate) boost halved biochemical failure; overall survival (OS) equal).
- **Why add ADT:** it shrinks the target and is synergistic with radiation.
- **EORTC 22863:** 3 years of goserelin with RT: 5-year OS 78–79% vs 62%.
- **RTOG 92-02:** 28 vs 4 months ADT better on all endpoints except overall OS; OS gain in Gleason 8–10.
- **Duration:** NCCN 18–36 months with EBRT (12–36 with brachytherapy boost); 18 vs 36 months gave similar OS.
- **RT is needed with ADT:** SPCG-7 (cancer death RR (relative risk) 0.44) and MRC PR07 (OS HR (hazard ratio) 0.77).
- Pelvic nodal RT: RTOG 94-13 inconclusive; POP-RT favoured it in high-risk N0.

### B. Systemic intensification
- **STAMPEDE (M0):** for N1, or two of T3–4, Gleason ≥8, PSA ≥40, **abiraterone + prednisolone for 2 years** with ADT + RT: metastasis-free survival HR 0.53, OS HR 0.60. NCCN lists it for very high risk.
- Docetaxel (RTOG 0521 OS HR 0.69): not routine.

### C. Radical prostatectomy (RP) + ePLND (extended pelvic lymph node dissection)
- For fit men with a resectable tumour, as part of multimodal therapy. cT3 is overstaged in 7–27%. RP alone gives disease-free survival in at least half at 8–10 years, and 10-year disease-specific survival ~80–90%.
- **Advantages:** accurate pathological staging; removes the source of local morbidity.
- **Neoadjuvant ADT before RP:** clinical downstaging and fewer positive margins, but **no survival gain**.
- **Post-RP RT:** EORTC 22911, SWOG 8794 (metastasis-free survival and OS improved) and ARO 96-02 favour adjuvant RT over observation. **RADICALS-RT, RAVES, GETUG-AFU 17:** **early salvage RT** is preferred for most men. Adjuvant RT is still considered for very high risk (pT3b, Gleason 8–10, positive margin). Dose 64–66 Gy; adding short-term ADT to salvage RT improved progression-free survival (GETUG-AFU 16).

### D. ADT alone
- Only for men unfit for or refusing local treatment. Consider immediate ADT for PSA doubling time <12 months, PSA >50 ng/mL or symptoms; otherwise deferred ADT is acceptable.
- **Also know:** bicalutamide 150 mg (spares sexual interest; harmful in localised disease); intermittent ADT.

### Node-positive disease
| Setting | Management |
|---|---|
| **cN1 M0** | RT to prostate and pelvis + ADT 2–3 years + abiraterone 2 years; RP + ePLND in selected men |
| **pN1 after RP** | **Immediate ADT** (ECOG 7887: OS benefit) **or ADT + adjuvant RT** (better survival than ADT alone); observation if PSA undetectable and ≤2 nodes |

### Algorithm
```
cT3–4 / cN1, M0 (PSMA PET or CT + bone scan)
   ├─ Fit, resectable  → RP + ePLND → early salvage RT ± ADT
   ├─ Most             → RT + ADT 18–36 months (+ abiraterone if N1 / very high risk)
   └─ Unfit / refuses  → ADT alone (immediate or deferred)
```

### Complications and local sequelae
- ADT: hot flushes, osteoporosis, metabolic syndrome, cardiovascular risk, loss of libido; give bone protection.
- RT: proctitis, cystitis. RP: incontinence, erectile dysfunction.
- A prostate left in place can cause outflow obstruction, haematuria and ureteric obstruction.
- **Also know:** cryotherapy and HIFU (high-intensity focused ultrasound) are not standard.

### Follow-up and recent advances
- PSA every 3–6 months; bone and metabolic health.
- PSMA PET staging; trials adding apalutamide or enzalutamide (ATLAS, ENZARAD); SPCG-15 (RP vs RT + ADT).

**Diagram to draw:** T-stage diagram (T3a extracapsular, T3b seminal vesicle, T4 adjacent organs).

> **Key points to remember:**
> - RT + long-term ADT (18–36 months) is standard; ADT alone is inferior (SPCG-7).
> - STAMPEDE: abiraterone 2 years for N1 or very high-risk M0.
> - Neoadjuvant ADT before RP: no survival gain.
> - Early salvage RT preferred over adjuvant RT for most men after RP.

## Q3a. Drug therapy in OAB
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in Practice Set 1 Paper 1, Q3e)

### Place and mechanisms
- In OAB (overactive bladder), drugs are second line, after behavioural therapy. Two main classes: **antimuscarinics** and **β3-adrenoceptor agonists**.
- **Antimuscarinics:** acetylcholine acts on **M3** receptors (raising intracellular calcium) to contract the detrusor; the commoner M2 enhances it. Blockade reduces involuntary contractions and afferent signalling.
- **β3 agonists:** activation raises **cAMP (cyclic adenosine monophosphate)** and relaxes the detrusor during storage.

### Agents
| Drug | Feature | Usual dose |
|---|---|---|
| Oxybutynin | Non-selective; most dry mouth; patch, gel | 5 mg two to three times daily; ER (extended-release) |
| Tolterodine | Bladder-selective; QT prolongation at high dose | ER 4 mg daily |
| Fesoterodine | Prodrug of tolterodine's active metabolite | 4–8 mg daily |
| Solifenacin | M3-selective; long half-life | 5–10 mg daily |
| Darifenacin | About 30-fold M3-selective | 7.5–15 mg daily |
| Trospium | Quaternary; little brain entry | 20 mg twice daily |
| Mirabegron (β3) | Inhibits CYP2D6 (a liver enzyme): metoprolol interaction; small blood-pressure rise | 25–50 mg daily |
| Vibegron (β3) | No CYP2D6 interaction | 75 mg daily |

### Comparison
| Feature | Antimuscarinics | β3 agonists |
|---|---|---|
| Efficacy | Similar | Similar |
| Side effects | Dry mouth, constipation, blurred vision | Near placebo |
| Cognition | **Dementia risk 10–65% higher with use >3 months** | No anticholinergic burden |
| Contraindications | Retention, uncontrolled narrow-angle glaucoma, gastric retention | Uncontrolled severe hypertension |

- **Choice:** elderly → β3 agonist, or ER trospium, darifenacin or fesoterodine. ER forms cause less dry mouth. Poor persistence is the main problem; switch dose or drug before third-line therapy.

### Combination and special groups
- **Solifenacin + mirabegron** beats monotherapy (SYNERGY; BESIDE in non-responders).
- **Men:** add an antimuscarinic or β3 agonist to an α-blocker; retention up to 3%, so check PVR (post-void residual).
- **Also know:** vaginal oestrogen in atrophy; desmopressin for nocturnal polyuria; imipramine (children, mixed incontinence); intravesical oxybutynin; third line onabotulinumtoxinA 100 U and neuromodulation.

**Diagram to draw:** detrusor cell: acetylcholine → M3 → contraction; noradrenaline → β3 → cAMP → relaxation.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - M3 mediates contraction; β3 raises cAMP to relax.
> - Equal efficacy; β3 avoids dry mouth and dementia risk.
> - Solifenacin + mirabegron combination works (SYNERGY).

## Q3b. Techniques of chordee correction
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(related: Practice Set 10 Paper 1, Q3d)

### Definition and causes
- Chordee: **ventral penile curvature**, with or without hypospadias. It, not meatal position, decides one-stage versus staged repair.
- Causes: tethered ventral skin, fibrous dysplastic dartos and Buck's fascia, short urethral plate, **corporal disproportion**.
- Devine–Horton (1973) graded the dysplastic layers: I spongiosum, II Buck's and dartos, III dartos only, IV corporal disproportion.

### Assessment
- Parents' photographs; **artificial erection** in theatre: Gittes–McLaughlin (tourniquet at the base plus saline), corporal compression, or prostaglandin. A tourniquet can hide basal curvature.
- Grade: mild <30°, moderate 30–45°, severe ≥45° (goniometer; eye underestimates).

### Stepwise correction
```
Deglove to penoscrotal junction + excise dysplastic dartos → erection test
  ├─ Straight        → urethroplasty
  ├─ Residual <30°   → dorsal plication
  └─ >30°            → divide urethral plate → still >30° → ventral lengthening (stage)
```

| | **Dorsal shortening (plication)** | **Ventral lengthening (corporotomy ± graft)** |
|---|---|---|
| Methods | **Nesbit:** excise dorsal tunical ellipses, close transversely. **Baskin:** single midline plication in the **12 o'clock nerve-free zone**. Heineke–Mikulicz dorsal incision closed transversely; 16-dot plication | 1–3 transverse **full-thickness corporotomies** (STAG, staged tubularised autograft); single **9-to-3 o'clock corporotomy** with graft: **dermis, tunica vaginalis**, SIS (small intestinal submucosa); dermal graft 20% larger than defect |
| Use | ≤15–30° | Moderate–severe after plate division |
| Recurrence | 28% (one series) | 9% |
| Risk | Shortening; dorsal nerve injury off midline | Longer, staged; theoretical erectile dysfunction |

- **Plate division** (transection) releases a short plate; not dividing it is an independent risk factor for recurrence. Repair is then staged.
- **Also know:** "fairy cuts" (superficial tunical incisions); lateral or dorsal curves need multipoint plication; correct **torsion** by dartos flap rotation.
- **Rule:** correct fully the first time and repeat the erection test before urethroplasty. Curvature may recur at puberty.

**Diagram to draw:** penile cross-section with dorsal nerves at 11 and 1 o'clock and midline plication at 12.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Artificial erection after degloving decides the technique.
> - <30°: dorsal plication; >30°: divide plate ± ventral graft and stage.
> - Plication recurrence 28% vs grafting 9%.

## Q3c. Anterior urethral valve
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition and epidemiology
- AUV (anterior urethral valve): a congenital obstructing fold or cusp in the anterior urethra (bulbar, penoscrotal or penile). It is the **commonest congenital obstructive lesion of the anterior urethra** but **25–30 times less common than PUV (posterior urethral valves)**.
- Often linked to an **anterior urethral diverticulum**: the distal lip of the diverticulum fills and acts as a valve, or a semilunar fold hangs from the roof.

### Embryology (unclear)
- Hypoplastic corpus spongiosum over the segment suggests a place on the **hypospadias spectrum**, or faulty union of urethral mucosa and fossa navicularis epithelium; a ruptured dilated Cowper (bulbourethral) gland duct is also proposed.

### Clinical features (vary with severity and age)
- Antenatal hydronephrosis, LUTO (lower urinary tract obstruction) in severe cases.
- **Ventral penile bulge** during voiding, with urine expressed by compressing the shaft; post-void dribbling, weak stream, incontinence.
- Palpable bladder, UTI (urinary tract infection), renal insufficiency.

### Evaluation
- **VCUG (voiding cystourethrogram):** dilated anterior urethra proximal to the valve, diverticulum, trabeculated bladder, bladder diverticula, often VUR (vesicoureteric reflux).
- Ultrasound, creatinine; urethroscopy confirms.
- **Differential:** Cowper syringocele, megalourethra, acquired diverticulum, stricture.

### Management
- **Premature or small infant:** vesicostomy until the urethra takes a cystoscope.
- **Most cases:** **endoscopic valve ablation** (Bugbee electrode or laser), usually definitive.
- **Large diverticulum:** open excision and urethroplasty over a catheter.
- Then manage VUR, infection and bladder dysfunction.

### AUV versus PUV
| Feature | AUV | PUV |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 25–30 times rarer | Commonest infant outlet obstruction |
| Site | Bulbar, penoscrotal, penile | From the verumontanum |
| Sign | Penile bulge | Weak stream, palpable bladder |
| Bladder dysfunction | Up to 80% | Near universal |
| Renal outcome | 78% normal function | ESRD (end-stage renal disease) 20–50% |

### Prognosis
- Depends on pre-treatment creatinine and GFR (glomerular filtration rate). Azotaemia, VUR and UTI together raise the risk of poor renal outcome 25-fold.

**Diagram to draw:** VCUG sketch: dilated bulbar urethra with diverticulum ending at a valve lip.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - 25–30 times rarer than PUV; diverticulum common.
> - Penile bulge on voiding; diagnosis by VCUG.
> - Endoscopic ablation usually definitive; 78% keep normal renal function.

## Q3d. Vaginal prolapse repair.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(related: Practice Set 12 Paper 1, Q3d)

*Read as: surgical repair of vaginal wall and vault prolapse (pelvic organ prolapse).*

### Principles
- POP (pelvic organ prolapse) is a quality-of-life condition: operate for bother, not anatomy. The first operation gives the best chance.
- Identify each compartment by POP-Q (Pelvic Organ Prolapse Quantification). **The apex is the cornerstone**: failing to support it greatly raises recurrence.
- **Three categories:** **restorative** (native tissue), **compensatory** (graft or mesh, for weak tissue or failed repair), **obliterative**.
- Vaginal route: fewer complications, quicker recovery. Abdominal route: more durable.
- Screen for **occult SUI (stress urinary incontinence)** with prolapse reduced; consider a concomitant sling. Finish with **cystoscopy** to confirm ureteric efflux.

### Operations by compartment
| Compartment | Operations | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| **Anterior** (cystocele) | **Anterior colporrhaphy** (midline plication of pubocervical fascia); **paravaginal repair** (fascia reattached to the ATFP (arcus tendineus fasciae pelvis)) | Central vs lateral defect; does not support the apex |
| **Apical** (uterus, vault) | **Uterosacral suspension** (ureter injury 1–11%); **sacrospinous fixation** (suture 1.5–3 cm medial to the ischial spine; gluteal pain 15%); iliococcygeus suspension; McCall culdoplasty; vaginal hysterectomy; hysteropexy; Manchester | OPTIMAL trial: uterosacral = sacrospinous at 2 years |
| **Apical, abdominal** | **Sacrocolpopexy** (open, laparoscopic, robotic): macroporous polypropylene mesh from vaginal apex to sacral promontory | Most durable (>90%); mesh exposure ~10% long term (CARE) |
| **Posterior** (rectocele) | **Midline fascial plication** without levatorplasty; site-specific repair; perineorrhaphy | Levator plication causes dyspareunia; constipation may persist |
| **Obliterative** | **Le Fort** (partial, uterus in situ, lateral channels) or total colpocleisis | Elderly, not sexually active; success >90%; coital regret ≤13% |

- Cochrane 2016: vaginal apical repair has more recurrence and dyspareunia than sacrocolpopexy, but suits many older women.
- Transvaginal synthetic mesh for POP was withdrawn in the USA (FDA (Food and Drug Administration), 2019).

### Complications
- Bladder, ureter or rectal injury; retention; de novo SUI; dyspareunia; recurrence (anterior after sacrospinous fixation); mesh exposure.

**Diagram to draw:** DeLancey levels of support with sacrospinous and sacrocolpopexy fixation points.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Always restore apical support.
> - Sacrocolpopexy most durable; colpocleisis for frail elderly.
> - Cystoscopy after repair; screen for occult SUI.

## Q3e. Perineo-scrotal hypospadias
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(related: 2017-Winter Paper 3, Q1)

### Definition
- A severe **proximal hypospadias**: meatus at the penoscrotal junction, scrotum or perineum, with severe ventral curvature, a short penis, bifid scrotum and often **penoscrotal transposition**.
- Cause: failed androgen-dependent urethral fold fusion (8–16 weeks).

### Evaluation
- Examine stretched penile length, glans width, transposition and **both testes**.
- **DSD (disorder of sex development) work-up** if there is an undescended testis (especially non-palpable), a perineal meatus or ambiguity: karyotype, ultrasound for Müllerian remnants, testosterone, LH (luteinising hormone), FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone), AMH (anti-Müllerian hormone). DSD risk is 64% in proximal vs 7% in distal cases.
- Prostatic utricle is commoner; cystoscopy in severe cases. No routine VCUG (voiding cystourethrogram) or renal ultrasound.

### Preparation
- **Timing:** 6–18 months.
- **Preoperative androgen** if glans width <14–15 mm: testosterone 2 mg/kg IM (intramuscular) at 5 and 2 weeks before surgery; evidence mixed.

### Surgery
**1. Curvature correction:** deglove, artificial erection, plication if <30°, plate division and ventral corporotomy ± graft if >30° (see Practice Set 17 Paper 2, Q3b).

**2. Urethroplasty: staged repair is now preferred**
| Repair | Technique | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| **Two-stage Bracka / STAG** (staged tubularised autograft) | Stage 1: correct curvature, divide plate, quilt inner-preputial or **buccal graft**. Stage 2 (≥6 months): tubularise, glansplasty, barrier layer | Preferred for most |
| **Two-stage Byars** | Dorsal preputial flaps moved ventrally, tubularised later | Supple flap bed |
| One-stage TPIF (transverse preputial island flap, Duckett tube) or onlay island flap | Flap urethroplasty | Higher complications |
| Proximal TIP (tubularised incised plate) | Plate kept | Stricture, recurrent curvature: abandoned for severe cases |

- **Why stage?** A urethroplasty over a fresh corporal graft fails more often.
- Principles: magnification, tension-free closure, **vascularised barrier layer** (dartos, tunica vaginalis); **scrotoplasty** for transposition.

### Complications
- **23–68%**; 25–75% need further surgery. **Fistula** commonest (3–45%), glans dehiscence, meatal stenosis, stricture, **diverticulum** (long rigid neourethra raises resistance: Poiseuille's law), recurrent curvature, hair-bearing urethra.

**Diagram to draw:** two-stage graft repair: stage 1 graft bed, stage 2 tubularisation.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Perineoscrotal hypospadias with undescended testis → DSD work-up.
> - Curvature >30°: plate division ± graft and staging.
> - STAG/Bracka staged repair preferred; fistula commonest complication.

## Q3f. Surgical management of bilateral varicocele
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(related: Practice Set 6 Paper 3, Q3e)

### Introduction
- Dilated pampiniform veins: ~15% of men, 35% of primary and 75–81% of secondary infertility. Mostly left (left vein enters the renal vein 8–10 cm higher). With sensitive tests most are bilateral; clinical significance of subclinical ones is uncertain.
- Harm: heat (lost countercurrent exchange), oxidative stress, DNA fragmentation, Leydig dysfunction; progressive.
- Grades: subclinical (Doppler only), I (palpable on Valsalva), II (palpable), III (visible).

### Indications for repair
- Infertile couple, **palpable** varicocele, abnormal semen, normal female partner.
- Pain (dull ache on standing), hypogonadism, high DNA fragmentation; adolescent testicular asymmetry >20%.
- **Do not repair subclinical** sides. Repair each palpable side.

### Techniques
| Technique | Recurrence | Hydrocele | Bilateral use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Microsurgical subinguinal / inguinal** | **0.5–1%** | **~0** | **Preferred**: two small incisions, local or regional anaesthesia |
| Laparoscopic | 3–15% | 12% | Both sides through same ports; general anaesthesia, serious complications possible |
| Retroperitoneal (Palomo) | 15–25% | 7% | Children; artery ligation acceptable |
| Embolisation / sclerotherapy | 15–25% | 0 | Femoral (left) or jugular (right) access; technical failure |

### Microsurgical steps (each side)
- Subinguinal incision over the external ring; deliver the cord.
- Microscope and **micro-Doppler** to map the testicular artery; papaverine for spasm.
- Ligate all internal and external spermatic veins; preserve **artery, lymphatics, vas and one set of vasal veins**.
- Deliver the testis to ligate gubernacular veins (lower recurrence).
- **Impulse test** confirms no missed vein.

### Bilateral-specific points
- **Never ligate both testicular arteries**: in adults this has caused azoospermia and atrophy. So artery-sparing microsurgery is safest bilaterally.
- An isolated right varicocele, or one that does not decompress lying down: image for a renal or retroperitoneal mass.
- Repair any coexisting hydrocele.

### Complications
- Hydrocele (lymphatic ligation), testicular artery injury (atrophy), recurrence (upright colour Doppler shows reflux).

**Diagram to draw:** spermatic cord cross-section: artery, lymphatics and vas preserved, veins ligated.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Repair only palpable sides with abnormal semen.
> - Microsurgical subinguinal: recurrence <1%, hydrocele nil.
> - Never ligate both testicular arteries.

*All the key points from this paper in one place. Read this in the last 30 minutes before the exam.*


#### Q1. Various uses of intestines in Uro (LAQ)

- Ileum and colon: hyperchloraemic acidosis; jejunum: hyperkalaemia; stomach: alkalosis.
- Continent diversion needs GFR >35, pH ≤5.8, osmolality ≥600.
- Detubularisation gives low pressure; Wallace has the lowest stricture rate.
- Sigmoid is the default neovagina; Yang–Monti when the appendix is absent.

#### Q2. Describe management of LAPC (LAQ)

- RT + long-term ADT (18–36 months) is standard; ADT alone is inferior (SPCG-7).
- STAMPEDE: abiraterone 2 years for N1 or very high-risk M0.
- Neoadjuvant ADT before RP: no survival gain.
- Early salvage RT preferred over adjuvant RT for most men after RP.

#### Q3a. Drug therapy in OAB (SAQ)

- M3 mediates contraction; β3 raises cAMP to relax.
- Equal efficacy; β3 avoids dry mouth and dementia risk.
- Solifenacin + mirabegron combination works (SYNERGY).

#### Q3b. Techniques of chordee correction (SAQ)

- Artificial erection after degloving decides the technique.
- <30°: dorsal plication; >30°: divide plate ± ventral graft and stage.
- Plication recurrence 28% vs grafting 9%.

#### Q3c. Anterior urethral valve (SAQ)

- 25–30 times rarer than PUV; diverticulum common.
- Penile bulge on voiding; diagnosis by VCUG.
- Endoscopic ablation usually definitive; 78% keep normal renal function.

#### Q3d. Vaginal prolapse repair. (SAQ)

- Always restore apical support.
- Sacrocolpopexy most durable; colpocleisis for frail elderly.
- Cystoscopy after repair; screen for occult SUI.

#### Q3e. Perineo-scrotal hypospadias (SAQ)

- Perineoscrotal hypospadias with undescended testis → DSD work-up.
- Curvature >30°: plate division ± graft and staging.
- STAG/Bracka staged repair preferred; fistula commonest complication.

#### Q3f. Surgical management of bilateral varicocele (SAQ)

- Repair only palpable sides with abnormal semen.
- Microsurgical subinguinal: recurrence <1%, hydrocele nil.
- Never ligate both testicular arteries.
