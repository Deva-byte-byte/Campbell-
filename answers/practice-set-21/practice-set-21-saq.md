## Q3a. Cystinuria
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition and genetics
- **Autosomal recessive** defect of proximal tubular (and jejunal) reabsorption of the dibasic amino acids **COLA: cystine, ornithine, lysine, arginine**. Only cystine is insoluble, so only cystine forms stones.
- Transporter subunits and ICC (International Cystinuria Consortium) types:

| Gene | Subunit | Chromosome | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| SLC3A1 | rBAT (heavy) | 2p | A |
| SLC7A9 | b⁰,⁺AT (light) | 19q | B |
| Both | — | 2 and 19 | AB |

- Fractional excretion of cystine rises from about 0.4% to up to 100%. Incidence 1 in 1000 to 1 in 17,000; **1–3% of adult stones, up to 10% of paediatric stones**; males more severe.

### Pathophysiology
- **Pure supersaturation disease**: no specific inhibitor. Solubility is pH dependent: about 300 mg/L at pH 5, 400 mg/L at pH 7, 1000 mg/L at pH 9.
- Coexisting hypocitraturia (commonest), hyperuricosuria or hypercalciuria cause mixed calcium–cystine stones.

### Clinical features
- First stone at mean about 12 years; recurrent, bilateral, staghorn stones; CKD (chronic kidney disease) from repeated surgery (about a quarter of adults have eGFR [estimated glomerular filtration rate] <60).

### Diagnosis
- **Hexagonal crystals** in urine (always abnormal); cyanide–nitroprusside screen; 24-hour urinary cystine (normal <60 mg/day/1.73 m²; homozygotes often >400).
- Stone: yellow, waxy, faintly radiopaque (sulphur); **resists SWL (shock wave lithotripsy)**.

### Management
- **Fluids:** urine output **≥3 L/day**, including at night.
- **Diet:** sodium ≤2300 mg/day; limit animal protein (not in children).
- **Alkalinise to pH 7.0–7.5** with potassium citrate 30–60 mEq/day; avoid >7.5 (calcium phosphate stones) and sodium alkali (raises cystine excretion).
- **Thiols** if cystine stays >250 mg/L: thiol–cystine mixed disulfide is soluble.
  - **Tiopronin** (choice): 300 mg twice daily, up to 1200 mg/day.
  - **D-penicillamine:** 250 mg/day, titrated; rash, proteinuria, nephrotic syndrome, pancytopenia.
  - Captopril: weak evidence.
- Monitor blood count, liver tests, urine protein.
- **Surgery:** ureteroscopy or PCNL (percutaneous nephrolithotomy) with laser; nephron-sparing approach.

**Diagram to draw:** proximal tubule cell with rBAT/b⁰,⁺AT transporter and hexagonal crystal.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Autosomal recessive; SLC3A1 (type A), SLC7A9 (type B).
> - Hexagonal crystals; target cystine <250 mg/L.
> - Urine ≥3 L, pH 7.0–7.5, then tiopronin.

## Q3b. TURP techniques
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(irrigation fluids answered in 2017-Summer Paper 2, Q3a; complications in Practice Set 9 Paper 2, Q3b)

### Introduction
- TURP (transurethral resection of the prostate): wire loop removes transition-zone adenoma **from bladder neck to verumontanum, down to the surgical capsule**. Gold standard for glands up to 80 mL (AUA [American Urological Association] 2023); larger glands need enucleation or simple prostatectomy.

### Preparation
- Sterile urine; spinal or general anaesthesia; lithotomy with peroneal padding.
- Irrigant at body temperature, lowest height giving vision.
- **Inspect bladder first** (tumour, diverticula); identify ureteric orifices, verumontanum, external sphincter.

### Resection techniques
- **Median lobe first**, then lateral lobes.
- **Modified Nesbit "encirclement":** start at 11–9 and 1–3 o'clock; expose but do not resect bladder neck fibres.
- **Floor-first:** channel at 5 or 7 o'clock to the capsule (sets depth early); widen laterally; lateral lobes "fall" into the fossa.
- **Anterior start:** 12 o'clock bladder neck, then quadrants down to the apex.
- **Chips:** long "canoe-like" chips with synchronised rocking.
- **Anterior tissue and apex last** in a bloodless field; leaving a little tissue is better than incontinence.
- Haemostasis: arterial bleeders must be fulgurated; venous ooze settles with catheter traction.
- Evacuate chips (Ellik evacuator); three-way catheter.

### Energy variants

| | Monopolar | Bipolar |
|---|---|---|
| Current | Loop → patient → pad | Stays at loop |
| Irrigant | Glycine, sorbitol, water | Saline |
| TUR (transurethral resection) syndrome | Yes; keep resection <90 min | Eliminated |
| Bleeding | More | Less transfusion, clot retention |
| Efficacy | Equal | Equal |

- Plasma loop (platinum–iridium) cuts at lower temperature and seals vessels.
- **Also know:** TUVP (transurethral vaporisation, button electrode; ≤80 mL); TUIP (transurethral incision, <30 mL, no median lobe); bipolar enucleation; high bleeding risk: AUA prefers HoLEP (holmium laser enucleation) or PVP (photoselective vaporisation).

### Outcomes
- About 80% symptom improvement; low retreatment; retrograde ejaculation about two-thirds.

**Diagram to draw:** resection field with clock positions, bladder neck, verumontanum and sphincter.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Bladder neck to verumontanum, depth to capsule.
> - Median lobe first; apex last.
> - Bipolar saline TURP: no TUR syndrome.

## Q3c. Amyloidosis in urology
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition
- Extracellular deposits of misfolded protein in **β-pleated sheet fibrils**. **Congo red stain gives apple-green birefringence** under polarised light.

### Classification

| Type | Protein | Cause |
|---|---|---|
| **AL** (amyloid light-chain; primary) | Immunoglobulin light chain | Plasma cell dyscrasia, myeloma |
| **AA** (amyloid A; secondary) | Serum amyloid A | Chronic inflammation: TB (tuberculosis), rheumatoid arthritis, osteomyelitis, pressure sores, hidradenitis |
| **ATTR** (hereditary transthyretin) | Transthyretin | Autosomal dominant neuropathy |
| Dialysis-related | β2-microglobulin | Long-term dialysis |
| **Localised** | Usually AL | Deposits in one organ only |

### Urological sites
- **Kidney (systemic):** nephrotic proteinuria, CKD (chronic kidney disease), enlarged kidneys, renal vein thrombosis, proximal tubular defects.
- **Bladder (usually localised):** **painless gross haematuria**; yellowish plaques or nodules that **mimic carcinoma**. Secondary bladder amyloid can bleed severely; Campbell lists amyloidosis among systemic causes of haemorrhagic cystitis.
- **Ureter and renal pelvis:** stricture or filling defect, often calcified; mimics urothelial cancer; obstruction.
- **Urethra:** rare; mass, obstructive voiding, haematuria.
- **Seminal vesicles:** senile deposits in older men; can mimic cancer invasion on MRI (magnetic resonance imaging).
- **Also know:** ATTR gives neurogenic bladder (underactive detrusor, LUTS [lower urinary tract symptoms] >80%) and erectile dysfunction; adrenal insufficiency; papillary necrosis.

### Evaluation
- **Biopsy is diagnostic** (avoids unnecessary nephroureterectomy or cystectomy).
- Exclude systemic disease: serum and urine protein electrophoresis, immunofixation, free light chains, echocardiogram, renal function.

### Management
- **Localised bladder:** TUR (transurethral resection) or fulguration; intravesical DMSO (dimethyl sulfoxide) reported; recurrence is common, so follow-up cystoscopy is needed.
- **Ureter:** ureteroscopic resection or segmental excision; stent for obstruction.
- **Urethra:** observe if mild; dilatation or urethroplasty if obstructive.
- **Refractory bleeding:** irrigation, embolisation, cystectomy.
- **Systemic:** AL: chemotherapy (daratumumab with bortezomib regimen, ANDROMEDA trial) or stem cell transplant; AA: treat inflammation (colchicine in familial Mediterranean fever); ATTR: tafamidis, patisiran. AA amyloid can recur in a renal transplant.

**Diagram to draw:** Congo red stained fibrils with apple-green birefringence.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Congo red, apple-green birefringence.
> - Localised bladder amyloid mimics cancer; biopsy then TUR.
> - Always exclude systemic AL disease.

## Q3d. Management of CAPD peritonitis
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(CAPD as a whole answered in 2018-Summer Paper 2, Q3e and Practice Set 1 Paper 2, Q3a)

### Introduction
- CAPD (continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis) peritonitis is the leading cause of technique failure and transfer to haemodialysis. Commonest organism: **coagulase-negative *Staphylococcus*** (touch contamination).
- ISPD (International Society for Peritoneal Dialysis) 2022 target: centre rate **≤0.40 episodes per patient-year**.

### Diagnosis (any 2 of 3)
1. Abdominal pain and/or **cloudy effluent**.
2. Effluent **WCC (white cell count) >100/µL with >50% neutrophils** (dwell ≥2 hours).
3. Positive effluent culture.

### Initial steps
- Effluent cell count, Gram stain, **culture in blood-culture bottles** (culture-negative rate should be <15%).
- Examine exit site and tunnel; analgesia; heparin in bags if fibrin.

### Empirical antibiotics
- **Intraperitoneal route preferred** (intravenous if septic); start immediately after samples.
- Cover **Gram-positives** (vancomycin or first-generation cephalosporin, e.g. cefazolin) **and Gram-negatives** (third-generation cephalosporin, e.g. ceftazidime, or aminoglycoside). Choice follows centre sensitivities.
- Intermittent (once daily in long dwell) or continuous dosing; avoid prolonged aminoglycoside (residual function, ototoxicity).
- **Antifungal prophylaxis** (nystatin or fluconazole) during antibiotics.

### Organism-specific duration

| Organism | Duration |
|---|---|
| Coagulase-negative *Staphylococcus*, streptococci, culture-negative | 2 weeks |
| *S. aureus*, enterococcus, Gram-negatives | 3 weeks |
| *Pseudomonas* | 3 weeks, two antipseudomonal drugs |
| Polymicrobial enteric | Add metronidazole; surgical review (bowel perforation) |
| **Fungal** | **Immediate catheter removal** + antifungal |

### Catheter removal
- **Refractory** (effluent not clear after 5 days of right antibiotics), relapsing, fungal, refractory exit-site or tunnel infection.
- Haemodialysis meanwhile; new catheter after at least 2 weeks.

### Definitions
- **Relapse:** same organism (or sterile) within 4 weeks of finishing therapy. **Recurrent:** different organism within 4 weeks. **Repeat:** same organism after 4 weeks.

### Prevention
- Training, exit-site mupirocin or gentamicin, prophylaxis before colonoscopy or gynaecological procedures.

**Diagram to draw:** management flowchart from cloudy bag to catheter removal.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Two of: cloudy fluid or pain, WCC >100/µL with >50% neutrophils, positive culture.
> - Intraperitoneal Gram-positive plus Gram-negative cover.
> - Remove catheter for fungal or refractory (5 days) peritonitis.

## Q3e. Avanafil
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Introduction
- Second-generation oral **PDE5I (phosphodiesterase type 5 inhibitor)**; **FDA (US Food and Drug Administration) approved 2012**; Europe 2013. Structure differs most from the other PDE5Is.

### Mechanism
- Sexual stimulation releases NO (nitric oxide), which raises cGMP (cyclic guanosine monophosphate) and relaxes cavernosal smooth muscle. PDE5 breaks down cGMP; avanafil blocks this.
- **Enhances, does not initiate, erection**: stimulation is needed.
- **More selective for PDE5** over PDE6 (retina) and PDE1 than sildenafil, so **no visual disturbance** in the comparison table.

### Pharmacokinetics (Campbell Table 72.7)

| Feature | Avanafil | Sildenafil | Tadalafil |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time to peak | **0.3–0.5 h (fastest)** | 0.8 h | 2 h |
| Half-life | 3–5 h | 3–5 h | 17.5 h |
| Fatty food | Reduces absorption | Reduces | No effect |
| Doses | **50, 100, 200 mg** | 25–100 mg | 5–20 mg |
| Blue vision | No | Yes | Rare |

- Taken about 15–30 minutes before sex; start 100 mg; maximum once daily. Metabolised by CYP3A4 (cytochrome P450 3A4).

### Efficacy
- Effective in general ED (erectile dysfunction), **diabetes** and **after nerve-sparing radical prostatectomy** (REVIVE trial programme): improves IIEF (International Index of Erectile Function) erectile scores and successful-intercourse rates, some within 15 minutes. Efficacy equivalent across PDE5Is; no head-to-head superiority.

### Adverse effects
- Headache, flushing, nasal congestion, dyspepsia; back pain rare.

### Contraindications and interactions
- **Nitrates in any form**: absolute; no nitrate within 12 hours of a dose.
- Avoid strong CYP3A4 inhibitors (ketoconazole, ritonavir); maximum 50 mg with moderate inhibitors.
- **α-blockers:** start 50 mg once stable (additive hypotension).
- Caution: aortic stenosis, recent myocardial infarction or stroke, hypotension, retinitis pigmentosa. Not recommended in severe renal impairment, dialysis or severe hepatic impairment.
- **Also know:** proposed prophylaxis for recurrent ischaemic priapism; can cause priapism rarely.

### Advantages
- **Fastest onset**, fewer visual and muscle effects; suits on-demand spontaneous use.

**Diagram to draw:** NO–cGMP pathway in cavernosal smooth muscle with the PDE5 block.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Peak 0.3–0.5 h; doses 50, 100, 200 mg.
> - Highly PDE5-selective: no blue vision.
> - Nitrates absolutely contraindicated.

## Q3f. Methods of orchidopexy
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(treatment algorithm of undescended testis answered in 2018-Summer Paper 3, Q3a)

### Principles
- Operate **after 6 months, ideally before 18 months** (Kollin RCT [randomised controlled trial]: surgery at 9 months gave better testicular growth than at 3 years). No hormones.
- Goals: tension-free **dependent scrotal** testis with intact blood supply; ligate the patent processus.

### Palpable testis
- **Inguinal orchidopexy:** groin incision → open external oblique (spare ilioinguinal nerve) → free cord, divide gubernaculum → separate and **ligate sac at internal ring** → retroperitoneal mobilisation, divide lateral bands → **Prentiss manoeuvre** (medial transposition under inferior epigastric vessels) → **subdartos pouch**. Transalbugineal sutures unnecessary.
- **Trans-scrotal (Bianchi) orchidopexy:** high scrotal incision; sac ligated through it; inguinal conversion 3.5–4.4%; atrophy 0.3–0.6%; less pain.

### Non-palpable testis: examination under anaesthesia, then laparoscopy

| Method | Principle | Success |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary laparoscopic** | Mobilise vessels, preserve vasal collaterals, new hiatus medial to epigastric vessels | >90% (97%) |
| **Open transabdominal (Jones and Bagley)** | Preperitoneal high mobilisation | 95% |
| **Fowler–Stephens** (one- or two-stage) | Divide testicular vessels high; testis survives on **vasal and gubernacular collaterals**; second stage after 6 months | 60–97% (one-stage 63%, two-stage 68%) |
| **Shehata traction** | Fix testis near opposite anterior superior iliac spine; vessels lengthen; orchidopexy at 12 weeks | 84–85%; atrophy 10% |
| **Microvascular autotransplantation** | Testicular vessels to inferior epigastric vessels | 96% |
| **Orchidectomy** | Postpubertal, dysgenetic or atrophic testis | — |

- Fowler–Stephens for testes **not within 2–4 cm of the internal ring**.
- **Also know:** staged (two-stage) inguinal orchidopexy; Dessanti PTFE (polytetrafluoroethylene) sleeve traction; gubernaculum-sparing technique; redo approaches (Redman lateral, Cartwright fascial patch).

### Complications
- **Atrophy** (highest after Fowler–Stephens), retraction or reascent, vas or vessel injury, haematoma, infection; torsion very rare after subdartos fixation.

### Follow-up
- Size and position at 6 months; counsel on fertility and cancer risk; self-examination after puberty. Offer prosthesis for an atrophic testis.

**Diagram to draw:** Fowler–Stephens collateral supply: testicular artery clipped, deferential and cremasteric arteries intact.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Orchidopexy at 6–18 months; subdartos pouch.
> - Prentiss manoeuvre gives extra length.
> - Fowler–Stephens relies on vasal collaterals.
