## III-1. Classify urethral strictures. Discuss in brief, surgical management of stricture after failed VIU
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

### Definition
- A **urethral stricture** is a fixed narrowing of the **anterior** urethra (inside the corpus spongiosum) that will not accept instruments without tearing the mucosa. It is an epithelial scar with variable **spongiofibrosis**.
- **VIU (visual internal urethrotomy)**, also called DVIU (direct vision internal urethrotomy), cuts the scar and lets it heal by **secondary intention**: a race between epithelialisation and wound contraction. It fails when contraction wins, which is usual when spongiofibrosis is deep.

### Classification
**1. By site (WHO (World Health Organization) 2004 / ICUD (International Consultation on Urological Diseases) 2010 terminology)**
| Segment | Term |
|---|---|
| Anterior: meatus, fossa navicularis, penile (pendulous), bulbar | **Stricture** |
| Membranous urethra after pelvic fracture | **PFUI (pelvic fracture urethral injury)** / distraction defect |
| Posterior urethra after prostatectomy or radiotherapy; bladder neck | **Stenosis or contracture** |

**2. By aetiology**
| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Idiopathic (commonest, with iatrogenic) | No cause found |
| Iatrogenic | Catheters, TURP (transurethral resection of prostate), cystoscopy, hypospadias repair, radiotherapy |
| Traumatic | Straddle injury (bulbar); pelvic fracture (PFUI) |
| Inflammatory | **Lichen sclerosus (LS)**; gonorrhoea (now rare) |
| Congenital | Rarest; infants only |

**3. By length:** short (up to about 2 cm), long, multifocal, **panurethral** (whole anterior urethra).

**4. By depth (Jordan):** (A) mucosal fold, (B) iris constriction, (C) full-thickness with minimal spongiofibrosis, (D) full-thickness spongiofibrosis, (E) fibrosis beyond the spongiosum, (F) complex stricture with fistula or abscess.

**5. By complexity:** simple (short, bulbar, first treatment) versus complex (long, multiple, LS, radiation, failed previous treatment, fistula).
- **PFUI** (Ch 125): major distraction, minor distraction, partial injury.
- **Also know:** **LSE system** (Lumen calibre, Segment involved, Etiology) for standard reporting.

### Why repeat VIU is futile after failure
- VIU succeeds only in **bulbar strictures <1.5 cm without dense spongiofibrosis, at the first attempt**. Recurrence shows deep spongiofibrosis, which an incision cannot remove.
- **A second urethrotomy adds virtually nothing.** Each dilation or urethrotomy lengthens the scar and lowers later urethroplasty success. The "reconstructive ladder" is abandoned.
- AUA (American Urological Association) guideline (2016): offer **urethroplasty, not repeated endoscopy**, after failed dilation or DVIU.

### Pre-operative evaluation
- Define the **4 Ls: location, length, depth, density**.
- **Dynamic RUG (retrograde urethrogram) + VCUG (voiding cystourethrogram)**, flexible urethroscopy, sonourethrography; MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) for PFUI.
- **Urethral rest**: suprapubic catheter for 6–8 weeks lets the true length show. Avoid instrumentation before surgery.
- Treat infection; look for LS at the meatus; examine the mouth; record erectile function.

### Surgical options after failed VIU
| Stricture | Operation | Principle |
|---|---|---|
| **Bulbar, short (1–2 cm, up to 3–5 cm proximally)** | **EPA (excision and primary anastomosis)**: gold standard, about 90–95% success | Excise all scar, spatulate both ends widely, tension-free anastomosis. **Non-transecting** or **vessel-sparing** variants keep bulbar arteries |
| **Bulbar, longer** | **BMG (buccal mucosal graft) onlay**: dorsal (**Barbagli**), ventral, dorsal inlay (**Asopa**); Kulkarni one-sided | Graft spread-fixed on a well-vascular bed (corpora or spongiosum) |
| Bulbar with an obliterated segment | **Augmented anastomosis** | Excise the narrowest part, anastomose the floor, graft the roof |
| **Penile** | One-stage BMG inlay or onlay; **penile skin flap** (Orandi, Quartey, circular) | Avoid EPA here (chordee) |
| **LS, failed hypospadias, infection** | **Staged repair** (Johanson): graft, then tubularise after about 6 months | Oral mucosa, not genital skin, in LS |
| **Panurethral** | Kulkarni one-stage dorsal graft, or staged | — |
| **Elderly, unfit, multiple failures** | **Perineal urethrostomy** (BMG-augmented) | Simple, durable bypass |
| **PFUI after failed VIU** | Delayed **perineal anastomotic urethroplasty** | Bulbar mobilisation, then intracrural separation, inferior pubectomy, rerouting |

- **Onlay is better than tube.** Graft and flap results are equal; grafts are simpler.
- **Not fit or declines surgery:** **Optilume** paclitaxel-coated balloon (**ROBUST III**: stricture-free 74.6% vs 26.8% at 1 year after repeated failed endoscopy); intermittent self-dilation; long-term suprapubic catheter.
- **Urethral stents** (UroLume) are withdrawn.

### Complications
- Recurrence, chordee, ED (erectile dysfunction; similar to circumcision overall), post-void dribble, graft-site pain and numbness.

### Follow-up
- Voiding cystourethrogram at catheter removal (about 3 weeks); then uroflow, PVR (post-void residual) and symptom score; urethroscopy if flow falls. EPA recurrences occur mostly in the first 6 months; graft results decline with time.

### Recent advances
- Tissue-engineered oral mucosa; robotic posterior repair.

**Diagram to draw:** dorsal versus ventral onlay cross-section; EPA with spatulated ends.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - VIU cures only short bulbar strictures at first attempt; do not repeat it.
> - EPA is the gold standard (about 90–95%); BMG onlay for longer bulbar strictures.
> - Staged oral mucosa for LS; perineal urethrostomy for the unfit or multiply failed.
> - ROBUST III: Optilume 74.6% vs 26.8% stricture-free at 1 year.

## III-2. Discuss continence mechanism in females . classify SUI and principles of different surgical options
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

### Introduction
- **SUI (stress urinary incontinence)**: involuntary leakage on effort, coughing or sneezing.
- Continence needs **urethral pressure greater than bladder pressure** at rest and during stress.

### A. Continence mechanism in the female
**1. Intrinsic urethral factors**
- **Mucosal seal**: folded urothelium coapts. The **oestrogen-sensitive submucosal vascular plexus** provides up to **30%** of closure force.
- **Smooth muscle** (internal sphincter, bladder neck and proximal urethra): involuntary tone. The female bladder neck is weak and often open in continent women.
- **Striated urogenital sphincter**: the rhabdosphincter, mostly **slow-twitch** fibres, gives tonic mid-urethral closure. Distal parts are the **compressor urethrae** and **urethrovaginal sphincter**.
- The **continence zone is the proximal and mid-urethra.**

**2. Support (extrinsic)**
- The urethra lies on the **anterior vaginal wall and endopelvic fascia**, attached laterally to the **ATFP (arcus tendineus fasciae pelvis)** and levator ani.
- **Pubourethral ligaments** anchor the mid-urethra; **levator ani** (pubococcygeus) tone keeps the backing firm.

**3. Neural**
- **Pudendal nerve (S2–4)** drives the sphincter (**guarding reflex**). Urethral pressure rises **just before** a cough reaches it, so closure is active, not just transmitted.

**4. Theories of continence and SUI**
| Theory | Mechanism | Surgical implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Pressure transmission (Enhorning 1961)** | Hypermobile urethra drops out of the abdominal pressure zone | Restore position (Burch) |
| **Hammock (DeLancey 1994)** | Abdominal pressure compresses the urethra against a firm suburethral layer; a lax layer fails | Restore backing |
| **Integral theory (Petros & Ulmsten 1990)** | Pubourethral ligament laxity at the mid-urethra | Tension-free MUS (mid-urethral sling) |
| **ISD (intrinsic sphincter deficiency; McGuire & Lytton 1978)** | Urethra itself fails to close | Sling, bulking, sphincter |
- Most SUI has some ISD. **Hypermobility alone never causes SUI.**

### B. Classification of SUI
**1. Blaivas & Olsson (1988), on video-urodynamics**
| Type | Bladder neck at rest | On stress |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Closed, above lower border of symphysis | Descends and opens, but no leak seen (history of SUI) |
| **I** | Closed, above symphysis | Descends **<2 cm**; leaks; little or no cystocele |
| **IIA** | Closed, above symphysis | Descends **>2 cm** with cystourethrocele; leaks |
| **IIB** | Closed but **at or below** lower border of symphysis | Proximal urethra opens; leaks with or without further descent |
| **III** | **Open at rest** without detrusor contraction | Leaks with minimal stress = **ISD** |

**2. Functional: hypermobility versus ISD**
| | Urethral hypermobility | ISD |
|---|---|---|
| Q-tip test | Angle change **>30°** | Often fixed urethra |
| VLPP (Valsalva leak point pressure) | **≥90 cmH₂O** | **<60 cmH₂O** (60–90 equivocal) |
| MUCP (maximum urethral closure pressure) | Normal | **≤20 cmH₂O** |
| Cause | Childbirth, ageing, obesity | Previous surgery, radiation, neuropathy, ageing |

**3. Other**
- **Severity (Stamey):** grade 1 leak on cough or sneeze; grade 2 on minimal effort (walking, rising); grade 3 at rest.
- **Index (uncomplicated)** versus **complicated** SUI (recurrent, prolapse, neurogenic, high PVR (post-void residual), radiation); pure versus mixed incontinence.

### C. Principles of surgical options
| Operation | How it works | Best for | Key facts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Burch colposuspension** (open, laparoscopic or robotic) | Paravaginal fascia beside the bladder neck sutured to **Cooper's ligament**; lifts the bladder neck back into the pressure zone and gives a stable backing | Hypermobility; women declining mesh; at abdominal surgery | 85–90% at 1 year, about 70% at 5 years; poor in ISD |
| **MMK (Marshall–Marchetti–Krantz)** | Bladder neck to pubic periosteum | Historical | Osteitis pubis; abandoned |
| **Autologous pubovaginal sling** (rectus fascia) | At the **bladder neck**, minimal tension; restores the hammock and compresses | **All types, including ISD**, recurrent SUI, neurogenic SUI | Gold standard. **SISTEr**: sling beat Burch (47% vs 38%) but more voiding dysfunction |
| **MUS**: retropubic (TVT, tension-free vaginal tape, Ulmsten 1995), transobturator (Delorme 2001), single-incision | Loose tape under the **mid-urethra** acts as a new pubourethral ligament; urethra kinks against it on stress | First-line index SUI | **TOMUS**: retropubic 80.8% vs transobturator 77.7% (equal); retropubic often preferred in ISD |
| **Bulking agents** | Submucosal injection at bladder neck or proximal urethra improves coaptation | ISD with fixed urethra; frail; minimal surgery | Low durability; repeat injections |
| **AUS (artificial urinary sphincter)** | Cuff round the bladder neck; constant circumferential closure | Severe recurrent ISD, fixed urethra | Second-line; erosion, infection, revision |
- **Also know:** needle suspensions abandoned (sutures pull through); anterior colporrhaphy is not an SUI operation; mesh complications (exposure, erosion into bladder or urethra, pain) need excision; FDA (Food and Drug Administration) 2019 stopped transvaginal mesh for prolapse, not slings.
- Before surgery: PFMT (pelvic floor muscle training), weight loss, pessary.

**Diagram to draw:** DeLancey hammock cross-section; sites of Burch sutures, bladder-neck sling and mid-urethral tape.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Continence zone is the mid-urethra; submucosal plexus gives up to 30% of closure.
> - Blaivas types 0, I, IIA, IIB, III; type III = ISD (VLPP <60, MUCP ≤20 cmH₂O).
> - Burch corrects position; slings and AUS correct function.
> - SISTEr (sling > Burch); TOMUS (retropubic = transobturator).

## III-3a. Bladder myeloplsty
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

*Assumed to mean "bladder myoplasty" (a muscle procedure on the bladder wall).*

### Definition
- **Detrusor myoplasty**: wrapping a **vascularised, innervated skeletal muscle flap** round the bladder so that voluntary contraction of that muscle empties an **acontractile bladder**.

### Indications
- **Acontractile or underactive detrusor** with catheter dependence: lower motor neuron lesions (spina bifida, cauda equina injury), myogenic failure.
- Needs good bladder capacity and compliance, and a competent but not obstructing outlet.

### Latissimus dorsi detrusor myoplasty (LDDM; Stenzl)
- **Free latissimus dorsi flap**: artery and vein joined to the **inferior epigastric vessels**; motor nerve joined to an **intercostal nerve**.
- Muscle wrapped **spirally over more than three-quarters of the bladder** and anchored to pelvic floor fascia and ligaments.
- Patient voids by contracting the transferred muscle (abdominal effort).
- **Results:** most voided with low residual; bladder contractility index rose to the strong range.
- **Complications** in about one-third: thromboembolism, abscess, wound infection; none long term.
- **Rectus abdominis detrusor myoplasty**: simpler, less morbid; small spinal cord injury series with spontaneous voiding.
- **Status: investigational.** Standard care stays **clean intermittent catheterisation**.

### Other "myoplasty" procedure: detrusor myotomy/myectomy (autoaugmentation)
- Purpose is the opposite: **increase capacity and compliance** in a small, poorly compliant or overactive bladder.
- **Technique (Cartwright and Snow, 1989):** strip and excise the detrusor over the dome, leave the mucosa intact, and anchor the detrusor edges (psoas hitch) so the mucosa bulges as a **wide-mouthed diverticulum**.
- **Pros:** urothelial lining, no mucus, no metabolic change, no bowel.
- **Cons:** seldom adds significant capacity; compliance gain may not last; **not recommended for adult detrusor overactivity**.
- **Also know:** seromuscular enterocystoplasty covers the bulge with demucosalised sigmoid.

### Alternatives
- **Also know:** sacral neuromodulation, sacral anterior root stimulation, reduction cystoplasty (abandoned), muscle-derived stem cells (research).

**Diagram to draw:** latissimus flap spiralled round the bladder with vascular and nerve anastomoses.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - LDDM: free latissimus to inferior epigastric vessels and intercostal nerve; for acontractile bladder.
> - Investigational; clean intermittent catheterisation remains standard.
> - Autoaugmentation: detrusor excised, mucosa bulges; little lasting gain.

## III-3b. Pathogenesis and management of uretero-vaginal fistula
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition
- **UVF (uretero-vaginal fistula)**: abnormal communication between the ureter and vagina, causing continuous leakage.

### Pathogenesis
- **Unrecognised injury to the distal third of the ureter** during pelvic surgery. About **three-quarters follow abdominal hysterectomy**, often uneventful (ureteric injury 0.2–2.4%).
- Others: obstetric, cancer, radiation, endometriosis, foreign body.
- **Sites of injury:** pelvic brim (near the infundibulopelvic ligament); **under the uterine artery** in the cardinal ligament; lateral to the uterosacral ligament; anterolateral vaginal fornix near the UVJ (ureterovesical junction). Most in the **distal 6 cm**.
- **Mechanisms:** ligation, crush, laceration, thermal injury, **devascularisation**. The injured segment necroses and leaks into the vaginal cuff, which is the weakest point.
- **Delayed recognition raises fistula risk about fourfold.**

### Clinical features
- **Continuous leakage with normal voiding**: the other kidney fills the bladder.
- Appears **within 4 weeks**, often with fever, flank pain or ileus.

| | VVF (vesicovaginal fistula) | UVF |
|---|---|---|
| Voiding | Reduced | Normal |
| Upper tract | Normal | Hydronephrosis common |
| First line | Catheter, repair | Stent |

### Investigations
- **Double dye test**: oral phenazopyridine (orange urine), blue dye into the bladder, tampon, walk. **Orange = UVF**; blue = VVF; both = both.
- **CT (computed tomography) urography**: hydronephrosis, extravasation.
- **Cystoscopy and RGP (retrograde pyelogram)**, stent at the same sitting.
- **Exclude coexisting VVF** (up to 25% of VVF have UVF) and bilateral injury.

### Management
- **Stent first**: retrograde double-J if a wire passes; otherwise **PCN (percutaneous nephrostomy)** and antegrade stent. **Stenting within 2 weeks** works best. Remove at **4–6 weeks** and re-image.
- **Surgery** if stenting fails: **ureteroneocystostomy** with **psoas hitch** or **Boari flap**; nephropexy or ileal ureter for long gaps; open, laparoscopic or robotic. Early repair equals delayed. Success **≥90%**.
- Follow-up ultrasound: stricture may appear up to 30 months later.

### Prevention
- Know the danger sites; cystoscopy at hysterectomy; selective prophylactic stents.

**Diagram to draw:** ureter under the uterine artery; double dye test.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Distal ureteric injury at hysterectomy; leak within 4 weeks, normal voiding.
> - Orange tampon = UVF; exclude coexisting VVF.
> - Stent early; reimplant with psoas hitch if it fails.

## III-3c. DD of frequency and urgency in 3 yr male child and treatment
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Introduction
- **Frequency**: ≥8 voids a day (ICCS, International Children's Continence Society). **Urgency**: sudden, compelling need to void.
- At 3 years toilet training may be ongoing; decide first whether the pattern is abnormal.

### Differential diagnosis
| Group | Causes and clues |
|---|---|
| **Infection** | **UTI (urinary tract infection)**: fever, dysuria; balanoposthitis; viral haemorrhagic cystitis |
| **Functional** | **BBD (bladder and bowel dysfunction)**/OAB (overactive bladder); voiding postponement; **extraordinary daytime urinary frequency** (sudden very frequent small voids, no nocturia or wetting, often after stress; self-limiting) |
| **Bowel** | **Constipation**: rectal mass presses on bladder; commonest associated cause |
| **Obstruction** | **PUV (posterior urethral valves)**, mild late form; **meatal stenosis** (circumcised boys; thin deflected stream); severe phimosis |
| **Neurogenic** | Occult spinal dysraphism, tethered cord (sacral dimple, hair tuft, gait or foot abnormality) |
| **Bladder irritants** | Stone, foreign body, hypercalciuria, pinworms, rarely **rhabdomyosarcoma** |
| **Polyuria** | Diabetes mellitus, diabetes insipidus, chronic kidney disease |
| **Behavioural** | Anxiety, family stress, developmental disorders |

### Evaluation
- **History:** onset, nocturia, wetting, stream, fever, **bowel habit (Bristol chart)**, thirst, stressors.
- **Examination:** abdomen, meatus and foreskin, **lumbosacral spine**, lower-limb neurology.
- **Urinalysis and culture**; glucose; specific gravity >1.005 makes diabetes insipidus unlikely.
- **Ultrasound** of kidneys and bladder: wall thickness, residual, dilation, stones.
- **Bladder and bowel diary**; uroflow if the child cooperates.
- **Selective:** VCUG (voiding cystourethrogram) for suspected PUV or febrile UTI with abnormal ultrasound; spinal MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) for neurological signs; urine calcium/creatinine ratio.

### Treatment (cause-directed)
- **UTI:** antibiotics per culture.
- **Constipation first:** disimpaction, then maintenance **polyethylene glycol**, diet and toilet routine.
- **Urotherapy:** timed voiding every 2–3 hours, relaxed posture, adequate fluids, avoid caffeine.
- **OAB persisting after urotherapy:** **oxybutynin** 0.3–0.6 mg/kg/day in 2–3 doses (maximum 15 mg/day); watch for constipation.
- **Extraordinary daytime frequency:** reassurance; resolves in weeks to months.
- **Meatal stenosis:** meatotomy. **PUV:** endoscopic valve ablation. **Tethered cord:** neurosurgical release.

**Diagram to draw:** flowchart: urinalysis → ultrasound with residual → targeted tests.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Always ask about bowels; treat constipation first.
> - Rule out UTI, PUV, meatal stenosis and occult dysraphism.
> - Extraordinary daytime frequency: no nocturia, normal tests, self-limiting.

## III-3d. Genetic causes of male infertility. Enumerate and classify.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Introduction
- Genetic defects underlie many cases of **azoospermia** and **severe oligozoospermia**. They decide **sperm retrieval odds** and **transmission to offspring** through ICSI (intracytoplasmic sperm injection).

### Classification
| Group | Condition | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Chromosomal, numerical** | **Klinefelter syndrome (47,XXY)** | **Commonest genetic cause**; 1 in 500–1000; small testes, high FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone), low testosterone, usually azoospermia. Non-mosaic in 80–90%. Testicular sperm in about 50% (micro-TESE, microdissection testicular sperm extraction) |
| | 47,XYY; **46,XX male** (SRY gene translocated to X) | XX male: azoospermia, no sperm retrievable |
| **2. Chromosomal, structural** | **Robertsonian and reciprocal translocations**, inversions, ring Y | Oligozoospermia; recurrent miscarriage |
| **3. Y chromosome microdeletions (Yq, AZF (azoospermia factor))** | **AZFa** (USP9Y, DDX3Y), **AZFb**, **AZFc** (DAZ genes) | Complete AZFa or AZFb deletion: **no sperm at retrieval**. AZFc: sperm often in ejaculate or testis; sons inherit the deletion |
| **4. Single-gene: excurrent ducts** | **CFTR (cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator) mutations**: CBAVD (congenital bilateral absence of vas deferens); idiopathic ejaculatory duct obstruction | Low-volume azoospermia, normal spermatogenesis; **test both partners** (ΔF508) |
| **5. Single-gene: hypothalamic–pituitary** | **Kallmann syndrome** (KAL1, anosmia); GnRH (gonadotropin-releasing hormone) receptor mutations | Low LH (luteinising hormone) and FSH; treat with hCG ± FSH |
| **6. Androgen action** | **Androgen receptor** mutation (long CAG repeat); 5α-reductase type 2 deficiency | High testosterone and LH, poor sperm |
| **7. Sperm structure and motility** | **Globozoospermia** (SPATA16, DPY19L2: no acrosome); macrocephalic sperm (AURKC); **PCD (primary ciliary dyskinesia)**/Kartagener (DNAH5, DNAI1: immotile sperm) | ICSI; macrocephalic sperm: ICSI not advised (aneuploidy) |

### Genetic testing indications
- **Karyotype and Y microdeletion:** non-obstructive azoospermia and severe oligozoospermia (**<5 million/mL**, ASRM/AUA (American Society for Reproductive Medicine/American Urological Association) 2021; EAU (European Association of Urology) uses <10 million/mL for karyotype).
- **CFTR:** CBAVD, idiopathic obstructive azoospermia; screen the partner; counsel before ICSI.
- **Also know:** Noonan syndrome, myotonic dystrophy.

**Diagram to draw:** Y chromosome long arm showing AZFa, b, c.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Klinefelter is the commonest genetic cause; micro-TESE finds sperm in about half.
> - AZFa or AZFb complete deletion: do not attempt retrieval.
> - CBAVD = genital form of cystic fibrosis; test both partners.

## III-3e. Urinary bladder compliance
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition
- **Compliance** is the change in volume for a change in detrusor pressure: **C = ΔV / ΔPdet**, in **mL/cmH₂O**.
- It reflects **low-pressure storage** during filling.

### Physiological basis
- **Viscoelastic wall**: smooth muscle, collagen (about 50% of the wall) and elastin (about 2%). Coiled collagen straightens as the bladder fills.
- More collagen or less elastin makes the wall stiff.

### Measurement (filling cystometry)
- ICS (International Continence Society) points: Pdet at **start of filling** and at **cystometric capacity** (or before a leak-causing contraction), **excluding detrusor contractions**.
- **No agreed normal value**; compliance depends on capacity. Below **20 mL/cmH₂O** (some use 10–20) suggests poor accommodation. In children, normal is often taken as **>15 mL/cmH₂O**.

### Pitfalls
| Pitfall | Effect | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Fast filling | False low compliance | Slow or stop filling; pressure falls |
| Sustained low-amplitude detrusor contraction | Mimics low compliance | Stop filling: pressure returns to baseline |
| **Pop-off**: VUR (vesicoureteric reflux), diverticulum, leaking outlet | Falsely good compliance | Video-urodynamics; occlude outlet |
- A small bladder can give a low number without danger (**compliance paradox**). **Absolute storage pressure matters more than the ratio.**

### Causes of low compliance
- **Neurogenic**: spina bifida, spinal cord injury, lower motor neuron and decentralisation injury (radical pelvic surgery).
- **Outlet obstruction**: PUV (posterior urethral valves), long-standing BPO (benign prostatic obstruction).
- **Structural**: radiation cystitis, tuberculosis, schistosomiasis, multiple bladder operations.

### Consequences and DLPP
- **DLPP (detrusor leak point pressure)**: lowest Pdet at which leakage occurs without contraction or strain.
- **McGuire (1981)**: DLPP **>40 cmH₂O** in myelodysplastic children led to hydronephrosis or VUR in 85%. Not a validated safe limit (20 cmH₂O is more sensitive); aim as low as possible.
- A strong outlet keeps the patient dry but transmits high pressure to the kidneys.

### Management
- **Clean intermittent catheterisation + antimuscarinics** (first line).
- **Intradetrusor botulinum toxin**.
- **Augmentation cystoplasty** if refractory; vesicostomy in infants.
- Repeat urodynamics to confirm safety.

**Diagram to draw:** cystometrogram: normal flat versus steep rising filling curve.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - C = ΔV/ΔPdet; <20 mL/cmH₂O suggests poor compliance.
> - McGuire: DLPP >40 cmH₂O, 85% upper-tract damage.
> - Pop-offs (VUR, diverticulum) hide true pressure; use video-urodynamics.

## III-3f. Congenital ureteral anomalies
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Embryology basis
- The **ureteric bud** arises from the **mesonephric (wolffian) duct** at about week 5 and induces the metanephric blastema.
- Anomalies arise from **abnormal budding**, **failed distal canalisation**, **abnormal muscle maturation** or **persistent embryonic vessels**.

### Classification
| Category | Anomaly | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| **Number** | **Duplication**: incomplete (bifid, Y-ureter) or complete | About 1 in 125; unilateral 6× bilateral. Lower pole: VUR (vesicoureteric reflux), PUJ (pelviureteric junction) obstruction. Upper pole: ectopia, ureterocele |
| **Termination (orifice)** | **Ectopic ureter** | Opens at or below the bladder neck. **Females**: bladder neck to vagina, uterus; **continuous dribbling with normal voiding**. **Males**: always above external sphincter (posterior urethra, seminal vesicle, vas); **never incontinent**; epididymitis. Zinner syndrome |
| **Structure** | **Ureterocele** | Cystic dilation of distal submucosal ureter (Chwalle membrane). Intravesical vs ectopic (Glassberg); cecoureterocele (Stephens) |
| | **PUJ obstruction** | Intrinsic aperistaltic segment (infants) or crossing lower-pole vessel (older children) |
| | **Megaureter** (≥7 mm) | Obstructed, refluxing, non-refluxing non-obstructed, refluxing-obstructed; primary obstructive = adynamic distal segment |
| | **Primary VUR** | Short submucosal tunnel, lateral orifice |
| **Position** | **Preureteral (retrocaval) vena cava** | Persistent subcardinal vein traps the right ureter; fishhook (S) shape at L3–L4; type I obstructs, type II does not |
| | Retroiliac ureter; herniation | Rare |

### Weigert–Meyer law
- In complete duplication the **upper-pole ureter opens lower and more medial** (ectopic, obstructs, ureterocele); the **lower-pole ureter opens higher and lateral** (refluxes).
- Reason: the upper-pole bud arises more cephalad on the wolffian duct, is incorporated later and ends more caudally.
- **Also know:** blind-ending ureter, inverted-Y ureter, triplication, congenital stricture, ureteral valves, fibroepithelial polyp.

### Evaluation
- Ultrasound, VCUG (voiding cystourethrogram), **DMSA (dimercaptosuccinic acid)** scan, MR (magnetic resonance) urography.

### Management principles
- **Ureterocele:** endoscopic incision, upper-pole heminephrectomy or ureteroureterostomy.
- **Ectopic ureter:** heminephroureterectomy if non-functioning; otherwise ureteroureterostomy or reimplantation.
- **PUJ obstruction:** pyeloplasty. **Megaureter:** observe most; taper and reimplant (5:1) if function falls.
- **Retrocaval ureter:** divide, bring anterior to the cava, excise the aperistaltic segment, reanastomose.

**Diagram to draw:** complete duplication showing Weigert–Meyer orifice positions.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Upper pole obstructs (ectopia, ureterocele); lower pole refluxes.
> - Female ectopic ureter: continuous dribbling with normal voiding; male never incontinent.
> - Retrocaval ureter is a venous anomaly; always right-sided.
