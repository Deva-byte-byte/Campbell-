## II-1. Importance and management of Inguinal lymph nodes in CA penis.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

### Importance
- Presence and extent of inguinal metastasis is the **strongest prognostic factor** in penile SCC (squamous cell carcinoma).
- A long **locoregional phase** precedes distant spread, so the groin is a **curable station**; lymphadenectomy alone can cure.
- **Kroon (2005)**: early ILND (inguinal lymph node dissection) for impalpable nodes gave 3-year disease-specific survival about 84% vs about 35% when delayed until nodes were palpable (ENE (extranodal extension) about 20% vs 95%).
- Manage both groins **within 3 months** of diagnosis; aim for **at least 8 nodes per side**.
- About 80% 5-year survival with 2 or fewer unilateral nodes, no ENE, no pelvic nodes.

### Lymphatic drainage
- **Superficial system** (prepuce, shaft skin) → superficial inguinal nodes. **Deep system** (glans, frenulum) → corona → within Buck's fascia → presymphyseal plexus → femoral triangle nodes.
- Presymphyseal crossover makes drainage **bilateral** and unpredictable in side; with one palpable groin, the other has micrometastasis in about 30%.
- **Stepwise, no skip**: superficial → deep inguinal → external iliac; so superficial status gates deep dissection and inguinal status gates pelvic dissection.
- **Superficial nodes** (4–25, above fascia lata) lie in **Daseler's five zones**:

| Zone | Related vessel |
|---|---|
| Central | Saphenofemoral junction |
| Superolateral | Superficial circumflex iliac vein |
| Inferolateral | Lateral femoral cutaneous and superficial circumflex veins |
| **Superomedial** (sentinel nodes mostly here) | Superficial external pudendal and superficial epigastric veins |
| Inferomedial | Great saphenous vein |

- **Deep nodes** (1–3) medial to the femoral vein; **Cloquet's node** is the highest.

### Staging (TNM (tumour–node–metastasis) 8th edition)
| N | Clinical | Pathological |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | One mobile unilateral node | 2 or fewer unilateral, no ENE |
| 2 | Mobile, 2 or more unilateral or bilateral | 3 or more (unilateral or bilateral) |
| 3 | Fixed mass or pelvic nodes | ENE or pelvic nodes |
- **T1a**: no LVI (lymphovascular invasion), no PNI (perineural invasion), not high grade. **T1b**: LVI or PNI or high grade.

### Evaluation
- **Careful bilateral palpation**: cN0 or cN+ (number, side, mobile or fixed). Imaging (US (ultrasound), CT (computed tomography), MRI) only if examination is unreliable (obesity, previous groin surgery).
- **cN+ or high risk**: FDG-PET/CT (fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography–CT) or CT chest–abdomen–pelvis.
- About half of palpable nodes are reactive, yet an antibiotic trial is **no longer recommended** (delays treatment). US-guided **FNAC (fine-needle aspiration cytology)** is specific; a negative result never ends the pathway.

### Management of the impalpable groin (cN0): risk-based
| Risk (EAU-ASCO (European Association of Urology–American Society of Clinical Oncology) 2023) | Primary | Groin |
|---|---|---|
| Low | pTis, pTa, pT1a G1 | Surveillance |
| Intermediate | pT1a G2 | Surveillance (compliant) or surgical staging |
| High | **pT1b or more**, G3 | **DSNB (dynamic sentinel node biopsy)**; ILND if unavailable |
- NCCN (National Comprehensive Cancer Network): pT1b G1–2 is intermediate risk, staged surgically (ILND first).
- **DSNB**: US ± FNAC first (a tumour-replaced node diverts flow → false negative); then 99mTc (technetium-99m) nanocolloid, blue dye and gamma probe; serial sectioning. False-negative about 7% at expert centres.
- **Modified ILND (Catalona)**: smaller field (nothing lateral to the femoral artery or below the fossa ovalis), **saphenous vein preserved**, no sartorius transposition; positive frozen section → complete ILND.
- **Also know:** superficial ILND; fascia-sparing ILND (FS-ILND); **VEIL (video-endoscopic inguinal lymphadenectomy)**/robotic: same yield, fewer wound complications, non-bulky nodes only; Cabanas anatomical sentinel biopsy abandoned.

### Management of palpable nodes (cN+)
```
Palpable node → US-guided FNAC
   positive → radical ILND same side + stage other side
               (superficial ILND, FS-ILND or DSNB)
   negative → excisional biopsy → negative: observe
Bulky (≥4 cm), fixed, bilateral or pelvic nodes
   → neoadjuvant TIP × 4 → responders: ilioinguinal resection
   → cisplatin-unfit: chemoradiation
```
- **Radical ILND** (cN2 or more; cN1 may have FS-ILND): incision 2 cm below and parallel to the inguinal ligament; flaps deep to **Camper's fascia**; boundaries inguinal ligament, sartorius, adductor longus; fascia lata opened; stay medial to the femoral artery (femoral nerve); **sartorius transposition** over the vessels; suction drains.
- **PLND (pelvic lymph node dissection)**: if **more than 2 inguinal nodes or ENE** (pN2–3); ipsilateral; external iliac commonest, then obturator.
- **Neoadjuvant TIP** (paclitaxel, ifosfamide, cisplatin), **Pagliaro (2010)**: objective response 50%; survival mainly in responders.
- **Adjuvant**: chemotherapy for pN2–3 (weak evidence); radiotherapy for multiple nodes or ENE (at least 50 Gy; 54 Gy for ENE).

### Complications
- Wound infection, **flap necrosis**, dehiscence, seroma, **lymphocele**, **lymphoedema**, DVT/PE (deep vein thrombosis/pulmonary embolism).
- Reduced by transverse incision, saphenous sparing, drain removal by volume (<30 mL/day), thromboprophylaxis, minimally invasive access; sartorius transposition increases them.

### Follow-up
- Examination 3-monthly for 2 years, then 6-monthly to 5 years (recurrence mostly within 2 years); then self-examination.

**Diagram to draw:** femoral triangle with Daseler's zones and Cloquet's node.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Nodal status is the strongest prognostic factor; ILND can cure.
> - pT1b or higher: stage groins surgically (DSNB preferred) within 3 months.
> - Kroon 2005: early vs delayed ILND, 84% vs 35% 3-year survival.
> - PLND if more than 2 nodes or ENE; bulky → neoadjuvant TIP (InPACT (International Penile Advanced Cancer Trial) testing the sequence).

## II-2. Traumatic renal vascular parenchymal injuries : diagnosis and management.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

*Assumed meaning: diagnosis and management of traumatic renal parenchymal and renal vascular injuries.*

### Introduction
- Kidney is the **commonest urological organ injured** (up to 5% of trauma); **blunt 80–90%**.
- **Deceleration** stretches the hilum and UPJ (ureteropelvic junction): arterial intimal tear and thrombosis, vein disruption, pedicle avulsion.
- Main renal artery injury is rare (about 0.05% of blunt trauma).

### Classification: AAST (American Association for the Surgery of Trauma) 2018
| Grade | Injury |
|---|---|
| I | Subcapsular haematoma and/or contusion, no laceration |
| II | Perirenal haematoma within Gerota's fascia; laceration ≤1 cm, no extravasation |
| III | Laceration >1 cm without collecting-system injury; **any vascular injury or active bleeding contained within Gerota's fascia** |
| IV | Laceration into collecting system with extravasation; renal pelvis laceration or complete UPJ disruption; **segmental artery or vein injury**; active bleeding beyond Gerota's fascia; segmental or complete infarction (thrombosis) |
| V | **Main renal artery or vein laceration or hilar avulsion**; devascularised kidney with active bleeding; shattered kidney |
- Advance one grade for multiple injuries up to grade III. Change from 1989: main vessel injury moved from IV to V; pseudoaneurysm and AVF (arteriovenous fistula) now grade IV.

### Clinical diagnosis
- Mechanism is the most important history. Signs: flank bruise, tenderness, **fractured ribs 9–12**, flank or low chest wound.
- **Haematuria** (first sample): gross, or microscopic (>5 RBC (red blood cells)/hpf (high-power field)) with shock. Degree does **not** match severity; it may be **absent in pedicle injury** and UPJ avulsion.

### Indications for imaging (AUA (American Urological Association) and EAU (European Association of Urology))
1. Blunt trauma with gross haematuria.
2. Blunt trauma with microhaematuria and **SBP (systolic blood pressure) <90 mmHg** at any time.
3. Rapid deceleration (high-speed collision, fall), even without haematuria.
4. Penetrating trauma with likely renal injury, any haematuria.
5. Children: image liberally (hypotension appears late).
- Microhaematuria without shock or deceleration: observe without imaging.

### Imaging
- **Contrast-enhanced CT (computed tomography)** is the gold standard. Arterial/nephrographic phase (~80 s) shows bleeding; **delayed excretory phase** (5–10 min) shows urinary extravasation.
- **CT red flags:**

| Finding | Suggests |
|---|---|
| Medial haematoma | Pedicle (vascular) injury |
| Medial extravasation, no contrast in distal ureter | Renal pelvis or UPJ avulsion |
| Absent enhancement, cortical rim sign | Main renal artery occlusion |
| Active contrast blush | Arterial bleeding needing AE (angioembolisation) |
| Haematoma rim >4 cm, medial laceration | High risk of intervention |
- **One-shot IVP (intravenous pyelography)** on table (2 mL/kg, film at 10 min) in the unstable patient: confirms a functioning contralateral kidney. FAST (focused assessment with sonography for trauma) is non-specific. Renal vein injury is poorly seen on CT.

### Management
```
Unstable despite resuscitation → laparotomy (or AE if team ready)
Stable → stage with CT
   Grade I–III → non-operative
   Grade IV–V  → non-operative in selected; AE for active bleeding
   Extravasation → observe; stent/nephrostomy if urinoma enlarges,
                   fever, or UPJ/pelvis injury
```
- **Non-operative**: bed rest until gross haematuria clears, serial haemoglobin; success >90% in blunt injury. Failure: instability, or falling haemoglobin despite 2–3 units of blood.
- **Angioembolisation**: superselective coils for active bleeding, pseudoaneurysm, AVF or delayed bleeding; repeat AE often succeeds.
- **Reimaging** (~48 h) for grade IV–V with deep laceration, or new fever, pain or falling haematocrit.

### Indications for exploration
| Absolute | Relative |
|---|---|
| Haemodynamic instability, no or transient response | Extravasation with devitalised parenchyma (>25%) |
| Expanding or pulsatile retroperitoneal haematoma | Arterial thrombosis |
| Suspected pedicle avulsion | Associated colon or pancreatic injury |
| UPJ avulsion | Incomplete staging |

### Operative technique
- Midline transabdominal; life-threatening injuries first.
- **Vascular control before opening Gerota's fascia**: lift small bowel; incise retroperitoneum over the aorta above the inferior mesenteric artery → left renal vein crossing the aorta → loop vein and both arteries; then reflect colon, open Gerota's fascia.
- **Renorrhaphy**: debride → suture-ligate vessels → watertight collecting-system closure → close defect over bolster → omental cover → drain. Polar injury: partial nephrectomy.
- **Nephrectomy** if unreconstructable or unstable. **Damage control**: pack, ICU (intensive care unit), relook at 24–48 h.

### Renovascular injury
- **Main artery thrombosis** (intimal tear): unilateral with normal other kidney → observe (revascularisation rarely succeeds). **Solitary or bilateral → revascularise** (warm ischaemia ideally <120 min); endovascular stent for intimal flap.
- **Vein**: left renal vein can be ligated near the IVC (inferior vena cava) (gonadal, adrenal collaterals); right cannot. Repair with 5-0 vascular suture; segmental veins may be ligated.

### Complications
- Delayed bleeding (within 3 weeks; AE), urinoma, abscess, **hypertension** (Goldblatt, **Page kidney**, AVF), hydronephrosis, loss of function.

### Follow-up
- Blood pressure, urinalysis, creatinine; DMSA (dimercaptosuccinic acid) for grade III–V; imaging if new symptoms.

**Diagram to draw:** left renal vein crossing the aorta, with vessel loops.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - AAST 2018: segmental vessel injury grade IV; main vessel grade V.
> - Image if gross haematuria, microhaematuria with SBP <90, deceleration or penetrating injury.
> - Stable patients, even grade IV–V: non-operative ± AE.
> - Revascularise only solitary or bilateral injuries.

## II-3a. Irrigation fluids for TURP.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Ideal irrigant
- Transparent, **non-conducting** (monopolar), isotonic, non-toxic, non-haemolytic, cheap.
- **Why non-conducting?** In monopolar TURP (transurethral resection of the prostate), current flows loop → patient → pad; ionic saline would disperse it. **Bipolar** TURP completes the circuit in the instrument, so it uses **normal saline**.

### Fluids
| Fluid | Osmolality (approx.) | Points |
|---|---|---|
| Sterile water | 0 | Best vision, cheap; haemolysis, hyponatraemia; now avoided for TURP |
| **Glycine 1.5%** | ~200 mOsm/kg | Commonest (UK, North America); glycine → ammonia, serine; transient blindness (retinal inhibitory neurotransmitter), encephalopathy |
| Sorbitol 3% | ~165 | Metabolised to fructose and glucose → hyperglycaemia; lactic acidosis |
| Mannitol 5% | ~275 | Near iso-osmolar; not metabolised; osmotic diuresis; volume overload in renal failure |
| Sorbitol 2.7% + mannitol 0.54% | ~180 | Continental Europe |
| **Normal saline 0.9%** | ~308 | Bipolar TURP, lasers (HoLEP, PVP); no TUR syndrome |
- Plasma is about 290 mOsm/kg, so monopolar fluids are **hypo-osmolar**.

### TUR (transurethral resection) syndrome
- **Mechanism**: prostatic venous pressure is ~10 mmHg; irrigant at higher pressure enters open sinuses → volume overload, **dilutional hyponatraemia** and hypo-osmolality → cerebral oedema; plus glycine toxicity.
- **Features**: confusion, nausea, vomiting, visual disturbance, hypertension then hypotension, bradycardia, seizures, coma.
- **Risk factors**: gland >45 mL, resection >90 minutes (VA (Veterans Affairs) study), high irrigation pressure, open sinuses or capsular perforation.
- **Prevention**: bag height ~60 cm (each extra 10 cm more than doubles absorption), continuous-flow resectoscope, time limit, spinal anaesthesia (awake patient), bipolar TURP. Ethanol 1% tagging can monitor absorption.
- **Treatment**: stop surgery, check sodium; Na <120 mEq/L serious. Furosemide for overload; **3% saline** 100 mL boluses (max 300 mL) for neurological signs; raise sodium by 4–6 mEq/L initially, not over 12 mEq/L in 24 h (risk of central pontine myelinolysis).

### Bipolar advantage
- Meta-analyses: no TUR syndrome, less transfusion and clot retention; NNT (number needed to treat) about 50 to prevent one TUR syndrome.

**Diagram to draw:** monopolar vs bipolar current pathways.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Monopolar: non-ionic hypo-osmolar fluid; bipolar: saline.
> - Glycine 1.5%: ~200 mOsm/kg; transient blindness.
> - Risk: gland >45 mL, time >90 min; bag height 60 cm.
> - 3% saline; ≤12 mEq/L rise in 24 h.

## II-3b. Pre operative investigations for renal donor.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

*Assumed meaning: evaluation of a living kidney donor.*

### Principles
- Donor safety first. **KDIGO (Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes) 2017** is **risk-based**: the donor's lifetime ESKD (end-stage kidney disease) risk is compared with the centre's threshold. Team includes an **independent donor advocate**.
- India: **THOA (Transplantation of Human Organs Act) 1994, amended 2011**: near relatives, or authorisation committee approval for others; swap donation allowed.

### Sequence of evaluation
**1. Compatibility**
- ABO group, HLA (human leukocyte antigen) typing, **crossmatch** (CDC (complement-dependent cytotoxicity) and flow), DSA (donor-specific antibody); incompatible pairs → paired exchange.

**2. Kidney function and urine**

| Test | Accept | Individualise | Decline |
|---|---|---|---|
| GFR (glomerular filtration rate), mL/min/1.73 m² | ≥90 | 60–89 | <60 |
| Albuminuria (ACR (albumin–creatinine ratio), mg/g) | <30 | 30–100 | >100 |
- GFR by two methods, one measured (DTPA (diethylenetriamine pentaacetic acid) or creatinine clearance).
- Urinalysis, culture; persistent microscopic haematuria → cystoscopy, imaging, ± kidney biopsy.

**3. Medical and metabolic**
- Blood pressure (ambulatory if borderline), fasting glucose, **HbA1c (glycated haemoglobin)**, OGTT (oral glucose tolerance test) if at risk; lipids, liver tests, BMI (body mass index).
- **Type 1 diabetes excludes**; prediabetes, controlled hypertension and obesity: individual risk.
- **Stones**: metabolic work-up; bilateral or recurrent usually exclude.

**4. Infection screen**
- HIV (human immunodeficiency virus), HBsAg (hepatitis B surface antigen), anti-HBc, HCV (hepatitis C virus), CMV (cytomegalovirus), syphilis; **tuberculosis** screen.

**5. Cardiopulmonary**: ECG (electrocardiogram), chest X-ray, echo; stress test if indicated.

**6. Cancer screening** by age: Pap smear, mammogram, PSA (prostate-specific antigen), stool test or colonoscopy.

**7. Anatomy**
- **CT (computed tomography) angiography**: number of vessels, early branching, stones, masses.
- **Split function** (DTPA or DMSA (dimercaptosuccinic acid)) if asymmetric: the **better kidney stays with the donor**; left preferred (longer vein).

**8. Psychosocial**: voluntary, no coercion or payment.

### Counselling figures (Muzaale 2014)
- Lifetime ESKD 90 per 10,000 donors vs 14 in matched non-donors; mortality 3 per 10,000.

**Also know:** APOL1 (apolipoprotein L1) testing in African ancestry; healthy older (marginal) donors acceptable.

**Diagram to draw:** donor work-up flowchart.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - KDIGO 2017: risk-based; decline GFR <60 or ACR >100.
> - ABO and crossmatch first; CT angiography for anatomy.
> - Leave the better kidney with the donor.
> - THOA: near relatives or authorisation committee.

## II-3c. UW solution.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Introduction
- **University of Wisconsin solution** (Belzer, late 1980s): the **gold standard** cold-storage solution for kidney, liver and pancreas.

### Principles of cold preservation
- Cooling to **4 °C** cuts metabolism to roughly a tenth, but does not stop it.
- Ischaemia → ATP (adenosine triphosphate) loss → **Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pump failure** → cell swelling; anaerobic glycolysis → acidosis; on reperfusion, **oxygen free radicals** (xanthine oxidase) → ischaemia–reperfusion injury, seen clinically as DGF (delayed graft function).
- A solution must therefore prevent swelling and oedema, buffer acid, scavenge radicals and supply ATP precursors.

### Composition (per litre)
| Component | Amount | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Lactobionate** (K⁺ salt) | 100 mmol | Impermeant anion; prevents cell swelling; chelates calcium |
| **Raffinose** | 30 mmol | Impermeant trisaccharide |
| **Hydroxyethyl starch** | 50 g | Colloid; prevents interstitial oedema |
| KH₂PO₄ | 25 mmol | Buffer |
| MgSO₄ | 5 mmol | Membrane stability |
| **Adenosine** | 5 mmol | ATP precursor |
| **Glutathione** | 3 mmol | Antioxidant |
| **Allopurinol** | 1 mmol | Xanthine oxidase inhibitor; fewer free radicals |
| Insulin, dexamethasone, antibiotic | Small | Additives |
- **Intracellular-type**: K⁺ about 125 mmol/L, Na⁺ about 30 mmol/L; osmolality ~320 mOsm/kg; pH 7.4.

### Drawbacks
- **Viscous** (starch): slow flush. **High K⁺**: flush before reperfusion (hyperkalaemia, vasospasm). May crystallise (filter).

### Comparison
| Feature | UW | HTK (Custodiol) | Euro-Collins |
|---|---|---|---|
| Type | Intracellular (high K⁺) | Low K⁺, low Na⁺ | Intracellular |
| Impermeant | Lactobionate, raffinose | Mannitol | Glucose (enters cells; weaker) |
| Buffer | Phosphate | **Histidine** (strong) | Phosphate |
| Colloid | HES (hydroxyethyl starch) | None (low viscosity) | None |
| Others | Antioxidants, adenosine | Tryptophan, ketoglutarate | — |
- Kidney outcomes with UW and HTK (histidine–tryptophan–ketoglutarate) are similar; Euro-Collins largely abandoned. **Also know:** Celsior, IGL-1, Marshall's citrate.

### Practical use
- Living donor, very short cold ischaemia: cold lactated Ringer's suffices; deceased donor or exchange: UW or HTK.
- Keep cold ischaemia short (ideally <24 h): DGF and thrombosis rise with time.
- **Hypothermic machine perfusion** (Belzer MPS (machine perfusion solution), gluconate-based) reduces DGF vs static storage (**Moers 2009**) without long-term survival gain; normothermic perfusion experimental.

**Diagram to draw:** ischaemia cascade with the UW component acting at each step.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Lactobionate and raffinose (impermeants), HES (colloid).
> - Allopurinol, glutathione (antioxidants); adenosine (ATP).
> - High K⁺: flush before reperfusion.
> - HTK: histidine buffer, low viscosity, low K⁺.

## II-3d. Uro Dynamic Studies.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition and principles
- UDS (urodynamic studies) test bladder and urethral function in storage and voiding.
- ICS (International Continence Society): **decide the question first**, **reproduce the symptom**; a normal study does not exclude disease.
- Before invasive UDS: symptom score, 3-day **frequency–volume chart**, free uroflow and PVR (post-void residual); treat UTI (urinary tract infection) first.

### Components
| Test | Method | Main values |
|---|---|---|
| **Uroflowmetry** | Voided volume ≥150 mL | Bell curve normal; Qmax <10 mL/s (men) suggests obstruction or weak detrusor; plateau = stricture |
| **PVR** | Bladder scan or catheter | Emptying |
| **Filling cystometry** | Bladder and rectal lines, fill with saline 30–100 mL/min (adults); **Pdet = Pves − Pabd**; zero at atmosphere, transducer at symphysis | Sensation, capacity, **DO (detrusor overactivity)**, **compliance** = ΔV/ΔPdet (<20 mL/cmH₂O impaired) |
| **Leak point pressures** | ALPP (abdominal LPP): cough or Valsalva at 150 mL | Low ALPP = weak sphincter (ISD (intrinsic sphincter deficiency)) |
| | DLPP (detrusor LPP) | **>40 cmH₂O** risks upper tracts (McGuire) |
| **Pressure–flow study** | Void with lines in | High pressure–low flow = obstruction; low–low = underactivity |
| **EMG (electromyography)** | Perineal surface electrodes | DESD (detrusor–external sphincter dyssynergia) |
| **UPP (urethral pressure profile)** | Catheter withdrawn | Limited value |
| **Videourodynamics** | Above with contrast and fluoroscopy | VUR (vesicoureteric reflux), bladder neck, site of obstruction |

### Indices (men)
- **BOOI (bladder outlet obstruction index)** = PdetQmax − 2Qmax: **>40 obstructed**, 20–40 equivocal, <20 unobstructed.
- **BCI (bladder contractility index)** = PdetQmax + 5Qmax: **>150 strong**, 100–150 normal, **<100 weak**.
- Women: no validated nomogram (Nitti video criteria).

### Indications
- Male LUTS (lower urinary tract symptoms) before surgery when unclear (young men, equivocal flow, failed TURP (transurethral resection of the prostate)); complex, mixed or recurrent incontinence.
- **Neurogenic bladder** (spinal injury, MS (multiple sclerosis), spina bifida), radiation cystitis, before augmentation.
- Children: neurogenic bladder, PUV (posterior urethral valves); fill at **5–10% of expected capacity per minute**; expected capacity = (age + 2) × 30 mL.

### Urodynamic risk factors
- Impaired compliance, DESD, DISD (detrusor–internal sphincter dyssynergia), high-pressure DO throughout filling.

**Also know:** ambulatory UDS; bethanechol test; antibiotic prophylaxis for risk groups.

**Diagram to draw:** normal trace: Pves, Pabd, Pdet, flow, EMG.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Pdet = Pves − Pabd.
> - BOOI >40 obstructed; BCI <100 weak.
> - DLPP >40 cmH₂O: upper-tract risk.
> - Ask a question first; reproduce the symptom.

## II-3e. Azospermia
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

*Assumed meaning: azoospermia.*

### Definition
- **No sperm in the ejaculate** after **centrifugation** of the sample, on **at least two** semen analyses; 10–15% of infertile men.

### Classification
| Type | Cause | Clues |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-testicular** (hypogonadotropic) | Kallmann syndrome, pituitary tumour, hyperprolactinaemia, **exogenous testosterone or anabolic steroids** | Low FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone), LH (luteinising hormone), testosterone |
| **Testicular (NOA (non-obstructive azoospermia), spermatogenic failure)** | Klinefelter, Y microdeletion, cryptorchidism, mumps, chemotherapy or radiation, idiopathic | Small soft testes, **FSH >7.6 IU/L**, long axis ≤4.6 cm |
| **Post-testicular (OA (obstructive azoospermia))** | CBAVD (congenital bilateral absence of the vas deferens), epididymal obstruction, vasectomy, hernia repair, EDO (ejaculatory duct obstruction) | Normal testes, **FSH ≤7.6**, dilated epididymis |
| Ejaculatory | Retrograde ejaculation, anejaculation | Low volume; sperm in post-ejaculate urine |

### Evaluation
- History (mumps, surgery, drugs, testosterone); examination (testis size, **vasa**, epididymis, varicocele).
- **Hormones**: FSH (reflects germ-cell mass), LH, testosterone, prolactin.
```
Azoospermia → vasa absent? → CBAVD → CFTR testing (both partners), renal US
            → volume <1 mL? → sperm in post-ejaculate urine → retrograde
                            → none → TRUS for EDO
            → normal volume: FSH ≤7.6 + testis >4.6 cm → obstructive
                             FSH >7.6 + small testes → spermatogenic failure
```
- **Genetics** (AUA/ASRM (American Society for Reproductive Medicine) guideline): **karyotype** and **Y microdeletion** in NOA; Klinefelter (47,XXY) commonest. **AZF (azoospermia factor) a or b deletion: no sperm, do not operate**; AZFc: sperm often found. **CFTR (cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator)** in CBAVD.
- **TRUS (transrectal ultrasound)**: seminal vesicle >1.5 cm, midline cyst → EDO.
- **Testis biopsy**: combined with sperm extraction and freezing.

### Management
- **Hypogonadotropic**: stop testosterone; **hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin) ± FSH**; dopamine agonist for prolactinoma.
- **Obstructive**: **vasovasostomy** or **vasoepididymostomy** (microsurgical); **TURED (transurethral resection of ejaculatory ducts)** for EDO; or sperm retrieval (MESA (microsurgical epididymal sperm aspiration), PESA, TESA) + ICSI (intracytoplasmic sperm injection).
- **Non-obstructive**: **micro-TESE (microdissection testicular sperm extraction)** + ICSI; sperm in about half. Repair clinical varicocele; hormonal optimisation (off-label).
- Retrograde ejaculation: sympathomimetics, or sperm from alkalinised urine.
- No sperm: donor sperm or adoption; genetic counselling.

**Diagram to draw:** hypothalamic–pituitary–testis axis with the three levels of failure.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Confirm on two centrifuged samples.
> - FSH ≤7.6 and testis >4.6 cm → obstructive.
> - Karyotype and Y microdeletion in NOA; AZFa/b: do not retrieve.
> - NOA: micro-TESE + ICSI.

## II-3f. Radiotherapy for CA prostate.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Radiobiology
- Prostate cancer has a **low α/β ratio (~1.5 Gy)**, lower than the rectum, so larger fractions favour tumour kill over late toxicity: the basis of **hypofractionation**.

### EBRT (external beam radiotherapy)
- **IMRT (intensity-modulated radiotherapy)**/VMAT (volumetric modulated arc therapy) with **IGRT (image-guided radiotherapy)** (fiducials, cone-beam CT (computed tomography)) is standard, allowing dose escalation with less toxicity.

| Schedule | Dose per fraction | Example | Trial |
|---|---|---|---|

### Brachytherapy
- **LDR (low dose rate)**: permanent ¹²⁵I (140–160 Gy) or ¹⁰³Pd seeds via TRUS (transrectal ultrasound)-guided perineal template; monotherapy for low and favourable intermediate risk.
- **HDR (high dose rate)**: temporary ¹⁹²Ir afterloading (e.g. 27 Gy in 2).
- **Boost** (EBRT 45 Gy + implant) for unfavourable intermediate or high risk: **ASCENDE-RT**: LDR boost halved biochemical failure, with more urinary toxicity.
- **Contraindications**: IPSS (International Prostate Symptom Score) >20, Qmax <10 mL/s, recent TURP (transurethral resection of the prostate), pubic arch interference, prior pelvic RT (radiotherapy), inflammatory bowel disease.

### ADT (androgen deprivation therapy) with RT, by risk
| Risk | ADT |
|---|---|
| Low, favourable intermediate | None |
| Unfavourable intermediate | **4–6 months** |
| High / locally advanced | **24–36 months** (EORTC 22863); abiraterone if very high risk (STAMPEDE) |
- RT added to ADT improves survival in locally advanced disease (**SPCG-7, NCIC PR.3/MRC PR07**).

### Post-prostatectomy RT
- **Adjuvant RT** (pT3, positive margin) improves control (EORTC 22911, ARO 96-02) and survival (SWOG 8794).
- **RADICALS-RT, RAVES, GETUG-AFU 17** (ARTISTIC meta-analysis): **early salvage at PSA (prostate-specific antigen) 0.1–0.2 ng/mL** equals adjuvant RT and spares most men RT.
- Salvage 64–66 Gy + ADT (**RTOG 9601**: 2 years bicalutamide improved survival) ± pelvic nodes (SPPORT).

### Outcomes
- **ProtecT** (2016; 15-year 2023): equally low prostate-cancer mortality with RT, surgery or active monitoring; RT more bowel effects, surgery more incontinence and ED (erectile dysfunction).
- Failure after RT: **Phoenix definition** = nadir + 2 ng/mL.

### Toxicity
- Acute: frequency, dysuria, diarrhoea. Late: **proctitis** with bleeding (6–18 months; sucralfate, argon plasma coagulation), cystitis, stricture, ED, second cancers.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - α/β ~1.5 Gy: basis of hypofractionation (CHHiP 60 Gy in 20).
> - ADT: 4–6 months unfavourable intermediate, 24–36 months high risk.
> - Early salvage (PSA 0.1–0.2) = adjuvant (RADICALS, RAVES, GETUG-AFU 17).
> - Phoenix: nadir + 2 ng/mL.

#### II-1. Importance and management of Inguinal lymph nodes in CA penis. (LAQ)

- Nodal status is the strongest prognostic factor; ILND can cure.
- pT1b or higher: stage groins surgically (DSNB preferred) within 3 months.
- Kroon 2005: early vs delayed ILND, 84% vs 35% 3-year survival.
- PLND if more than 2 nodes or ENE; bulky → neoadjuvant TIP (InPACT (International Penile Advanced Cancer Trial) testing the sequence).

#### II-2. Traumatic renal vascular parenchymal injuries : diagnosis and management. (LAQ)

- AAST 2018: segmental vessel injury grade IV; main vessel grade V.
- Image if gross haematuria, microhaematuria with SBP <90, deceleration or penetrating injury.
- Stable patients, even grade IV–V: non-operative ± AE.
- Revascularise only solitary or bilateral injuries.

#### II-3a. Irrigation fluids for TURP. (SAQ)

- Monopolar: non-ionic hypo-osmolar fluid; bipolar: saline.
- Glycine 1.5%: ~200 mOsm/kg; transient blindness.
- Risk: gland >45 mL, time >90 min; bag height 60 cm.
- 3% saline; ≤12 mEq/L rise in 24 h.

#### II-3b. Pre operative investigations for renal donor. (SAQ)

- KDIGO 2017: risk-based; decline GFR <60 or ACR >100.
- ABO and crossmatch first; CT angiography for anatomy.
- Leave the better kidney with the donor.
- THOA: near relatives or authorisation committee.

#### II-3c. UW solution. (SAQ)

- Lactobionate and raffinose (impermeants), HES (colloid).
- Allopurinol, glutathione (antioxidants); adenosine (ATP).
- High K⁺: flush before reperfusion.
- HTK: histidine buffer, low viscosity, low K⁺.

#### II-3d. Uro Dynamic Studies. (SAQ)
