## I-1. Describe in details basic patho-physiology of urine production and transport
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

### Introduction
- Urine is formed by filtration, reabsorption, secretion and concentration, then moved by peristalsis to a low-pressure bladder.
- Kidney roles: excretion of waste and drugs; volume, water, electrolyte and acid–base balance.

### A. Functional anatomy
- Glomerular barrier (fenestrated endothelium, basement membrane, podocytes) restricts by size and negative charge, so albumin is excluded.
- **Juxtamedullary nephrons** (long loops) build the medullary gradient. Juxtaglomerular apparatus: macula densa, arterioles, renin-secreting cells.

### B. Renal blood flow (RBF) and autoregulation
- RBF ~25% of cardiac output. Medullary oxygen tension is only 10–20 mmHg, so the thick ascending limb and S3 segment are hypoxia-prone.
- **Autoregulation** keeps RBF and GFR (glomerular filtration rate) constant at mean arterial pressure 80–180 mmHg:
  - **Myogenic:** the afferent arteriole constricts when stretched and dilates (prostaglandins) when pressure falls; NSAIDs (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs) abolish this.
  - **Tubuloglomerular feedback:** low NaCl at the macula densa releases renin; angiotensin II constricts the efferent arteriole. Angiotensin blockade abolishes this.

### C. Glomerular filtration
- GFR ~125 mL/min (180 L/day); filtration fraction ~20%.
- **Net filtration pressure** ≈ glomerular hydrostatic 60 – Bowman's space 18 – oncotic 32 = **~10 mmHg**.
- Efferent constriction lowers RBF but **raises** GFR; afferent constriction lowers both. Obstruction raises Bowman's space pressure, so GFR falls.
- **Also know:** GFR estimated from creatinine or cystatin C (race-free 2021 equations).

### D. Tubular handling
| Segment | Handling | Drug link |
|---|---|---|
| Proximal tubule | ~**65%** Na⁺ and water; all glucose (SGLT2, sodium–glucose cotransporter 2); bicarbonate | SGLT2 inhibitors |
| Thick ascending limb | ~**25%** NaCl (NKCC2); water-impermeable "diluting segment" | Loop diuretics |
| Distal convoluted tubule | 5–7% Na⁺; Ca²⁺ | Thiazides |
| Collecting duct | Principal cells: Na⁺/K⁺ (aldosterone), water; intercalated cells: acid | Spironolactone; distal RTA (renal tubular acidosis) |

### E. Concentration
- **Countercurrent multiplication:** ascending limbs absorb NaCl without water, raising medullary osmolality from 300 to ~**1200 mOsm/kg**; urea recycling adds about half. Vasa recta exchange without washing out the gradient.
- **Antidiuretic hormone (ADH)**, released by high osmolality or low volume, acts on V2 receptors to insert aquaporin-2 (AQP2) into principal cells. Concentration needs both a hypertonic medulla and AQP2.
- Aldosterone retains Na⁺; atrial natriuretic peptide excretes it.

### F. Ureteric transport
- **Pacemaker:** atypical smooth muscle cells at the minor calyces/pelvicalyceal border.
- Peristalsis is **myogenic**: the excised or denervated ureter keeps contracting. Nerves (T11–L1, S2–4) carry pain referred to flank, groin and genitalia.
- Calcium entry triggers action potentials spread by gap junctions; calcium–calmodulin then activates myosin light-chain kinase.
- Walls coapt behind each bolus: 2–6 waves/min at 20–80 cmH₂O. Very high flow makes boluses coalesce, dilating the ureter **without obstruction**.
- Ureterovesical tunnel length:diameter 5:1 prevents reflux; bladder pressure >40 cmH₂O stops efflux.
- Endotoxin inhibits and α1-blockers relax the ureter.

### G. Bladder storage and voiding
- **Storage** below ~10 cmH₂O: compliance (collagen, elastin), sympathetic β3 detrusor relaxation, α1 bladder-neck tone, pudendal sphincter tone.
- **Voiding:** pelvic parasympathetic (S2–4) acetylcholine on M3 receptors contracts the detrusor; nitric oxide relaxes the urethra. Aδ afferents sense fullness.

### H. Pathophysiology of obstruction (unilateral: triphasic)
```
Phase 1 (0–1.5 h): RBF up,   pressure up    (afferent dilatation)
Phase 2 (1.5–5 h): RBF down, pressure up    (efferent constriction)
Phase 3 (>5 h):    RBF down, pressure down  (afferent constriction)
```
- Ischaemic injury starts **within 2 hours** of complete obstruction.
- **Tubules:** down-regulated aquaporins and Na⁺ transporters cause a concentrating defect, salt wasting and distal RTA; K⁺ is wasted early, retained late.
- **Fibrosis** (final path to failure): stretch, inflammation, TGF-β (transforming growth factor-β), angiotensin II, microRNAs.
- Immature kidneys fail to develop; mature kidneys proliferate, then scar.
- **Clinical:** hypertension (~77% bilateral, ~20% unilateral); contralateral hypertrophy; post-obstructive diuresis ~2% unilateral vs ~50% bilateral.
- **Lower tract:** detrusor hypertrophy, then decompensation; collagen lowers compliance.
- **Also know:** diuretic MAG3 (mercaptoacetyltriglycine) renogram (half-time >40 min = obstructed); Whitaker test (>15–20 cmH₂O = obstructed); SGLT2 inhibitors and tolvaptan as recent renal drugs.

**Diagram to draw:** Nephron with transporters; triphasic graph.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - RBF 25% of cardiac output; GFR 125 mL/min; net filtration pressure ~10 mmHg.
> - Proximal 65%, loop 25%, distal 5–7% of Na⁺; ADH → V2 → AQP2.
> - Myogenic peristalsis from calyceal pacemaker; post-obstructive diuresis 2% vs 50%.

## I-2. Describe female pelvic anatomy in relation to prolapse and SUI
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

### Introduction
- Support comes from the **levator ani, endopelvic fascia and ligaments, perineal membrane and bony pelvis**. Failure causes **pelvic organ prolapse (POP)** and **stress urinary incontinence (SUI)**, effort leakage from failed urethral support or closure.

### A. Bony pelvis and perineum
- Fixation points and dangers: **sacrospinous ligament** (pudendal bundle), **Cooper's ligament** (bleeding), **sacral promontory** (left common iliac vein, right ureter).
- **Perineal membrane** and **perineal body** give distal support.

### B. Pelvic floor muscles
| Muscle | Attachment | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Pubococcygeus | Pubis to vagina, perineal body, anus (coccygeus is separate) | Closes hiatus, lifts urethra |
| Puborectalis | Sling behind anorectal junction | Anorectal angle |
| Iliococcygeus | Arcus tendineus levator ani to coccyx | Horizontal **levator plate** |
- Levator tone closes the **urogenital hiatus**, through which all POP passes.
- **Muscle fails first, fascia second:** once injury opens the hiatus, ligaments carry the load and fail.
- Up to **20% of primiparous women** have levator defects on imaging; avulsion predicts advanced prolapse.

### C. Endopelvic fascia and ligaments
- **Cardinal and uterosacral ligaments** suspend the apex; the uterosacral is the main anchor (ureter close by at the cervix).
- Pubocervical fascia attaches laterally to the **arcus tendineus fasciae pelvis (ATFP, "white line")**, pubis to just above the ischial spine.
- **Rectovaginal fascia** supports the posterior wall; **pubourethral ligaments** anchor the mid-urethra.

### D. DeLancey's levels of support (1992)
| Level | Region | Structures | Defect causes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I – suspension** | Cervix/upper third | Cardinal–uterosacral complex | Uterine/vault prolapse, enterocele |
| **II – attachment** | Middle third | ATFP, levator fascia | Cystocele, rectocele |
| **III – fusion** | Distal third | Perineal membrane, perineal body | Urethral hypermobility, perineal descent |
- **Central cystocele:** midline weakness, rugae lost. **Paravaginal cystocele:** ATFP detachment (commonest at the ischial spine, Richardson 1981), rugae preserved.
- Level I is the keystone: apical loss underlies most anterior prolapse, so correct the apex in any repair.

### E. Urethra and continence
- Continence zone is the **proximal and mid-urethra** (bladder neck weak): striated sphincter (with compressor urethrae and urethrovaginal sphincter) plus oestrogen-sensitive submucosa (~30% of closure).

| Theory | Mechanism | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Pressure transmission (Enhorning 1961)** | Hypermobile urethra leaves the abdominal pressure zone | Restore position (Burch) |
| **Hammock (DeLancey 1994)** | Urethra compressed against firm vaginal backing | Restore backing |
| **Integral theory (Petros & Ulmsten 1990)** | Pubourethral ligament, suburethral hammock, pubococcygeus at mid-urethra | Mid-urethral sling |
| **Intrinsic sphincter deficiency (ISD) (McGuire 1978)** | Urethral function fails | Sling, bulking |
- ISD: Valsalva leak point pressure <60 or maximal urethral closure pressure <20 cmH₂O. Most SUI has some ISD, so slings work in hypermobility but Burch does poorly in severe ISD.

### F. Innervation
- **Pudendal nerve (S2–4):** sphincters; near the ischial spine. **Nerve to levator ani (S3–5):** on the muscle's pelvic surface; neuropathy in about a quarter of primiparous women.

### G. Risk factors (Bump & Norton)
- Predisposing: genetics, collagen disorders. Inciting: **vaginal delivery** (strongest; forceps worse), hysterectomy. Promoting: obesity, constipation, cough. Decompensating: ageing.

### H. Staging and examination
| POP-Q (Pelvic Organ Prolapse Quantification, Bump 1996) stage | Most distal point |
|---|---|
| 0 | No prolapse |
| I | >1 cm above hymen |
| II | Within ±1 cm of hymen |
| III | >1 cm below hymen, < (total vaginal length – 2) cm |
| IV | Complete eversion |
- **Also know:** Baden–Walker grades 0–4; stage I–II often normal (POSST study); cough stress test with prolapse reduced (occult SUI); Q-tip >30° = hypermobility.

### I. How anatomy guides surgery
| Defect | Operation | Principle |
|---|---|---|
| SUI | **Mid-urethral sling**: retropubic (Ulmsten 1995, TVT [tension-free vaginal tape]) or transobturator (Delorme 2001) | Mid-urethral support |
| SUI | **Burch colposuspension** | Fascia to Cooper's ligament |
| SUI – ISD | Autologous sling at bladder neck, bulking | Compression |
| Paravaginal cystocele | Paravaginal repair | Reattach to ATFP |
| Apical | **Sacrocolpopexy** (most durable) | Mesh to ligament over S1 |
| Apical (vaginal) | **Sacrospinous fixation** 1.5–3 cm medial to spine | Avoids pudendal bundle |
- **April 2019:** the US FDA (Food and Drug Administration) stopped transvaginal mesh for anterior POP; slings unaffected.

**Diagram to draw:** DeLancey levels I–III; hammock cross-section.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - DeLancey: I suspension, II attachment (ATFP), III fusion; level I is the keystone.
> - Enhorning 1961, DeLancey 1994, Petros & Ulmsten 1990, McGuire ISD 1978.
> - Central cystocele rugae lost, paravaginal preserved; POP-Q II = ±1 cm of hymen.

## I-3a. Newer generation CT scan
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Introduction
- Computed tomography (CT) is the reference imaging for stones, masses and trauma. Attenuation is expressed in Hounsfield units (HU): water 0, air −1000.

### Evolution
| Generation | Feature |
|---|---|
| Helical (~1989) | Continuous table movement, volumetric data |
| Multidetector (1998 → 320-row) | Sub-millimetre slices, 3D reconstruction |
| Dual-source/dual-energy (2005) | Two energies: material analysis |
| Photon-counting (US approval 2021) | Energy-sorts each photon: less noise, dose and metal artefact |

### Dual-energy CT (DECT)
- Two energies (e.g. 80 and 140 kVp) compared pixel by pixel: **uric acid vs non-uric-acid stones**, **virtual unenhanced** images (one phase fewer) and **iodine maps** (hyperdense cyst vs enhancing mass).

### Low-dose CT
| Protocol | Dose |
|---|---|
| Conventional abdomen/pelvis | 10–20 mSv |
| Low-dose | <3–4 mSv |
| Ultra-low-dose | 1–2 mSv |
- Stone sensitivity stays ~95%; use standard dose if body mass index >30. EAU (European Association of Urology): ultrasound first; AUA (American Urological Association): CT first.

### Reconstruction
- Filtered back projection is noisy; iterative reconstruction halves dose; **deep learning reconstruction** gives good images at ~1 mSv.

### Protocols
- **CT urography:** non-contrast, nephrographic and excretory phases (~20–30 mSv). **Split-bolus** merges phases, lowering dose but may miss subtle urothelial lesions.
- **Renal mass:** enhancement ≥20 HU = solid, 10–20 equivocal; nephrographic phase most sensitive; Bosniak 2019 for cysts.
- **Also know:** stone HU (uric acid 200–400, calcium oxalate 600–1200; indinavir invisible); bone window for fragments; CT angiography for donors; adrenal washout protocol; partial volume causes pseudoenhancement of small cysts; ALARA (as low as reasonably achievable) dosing, since radiation risk has no threshold.

**Diagram to draw:** Timeline: axial → helical → multidetector → dual-source → photon-counting.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Doses: conventional 10–20, low-dose <3–4, ultra-low 1–2 mSv.
> - DECT: uric acid stones, virtual unenhanced, iodine maps.
> - Enhancement ≥20 HU = solid; deep learning enables ~1 mSv CT.

## I-3b. History of Laparoscopy
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Introduction
- Laparoscopy is endoscopic viewing of the peritoneal cavity through a pneumoperitoneum; it grew from optics to robotic surgery.

### Optics and access
| Year | Pioneer | Milestone |
|---|---|---|
| **1806** | **Bozzini** | **Lichtleiter**, candle-lit light conductor |
| 1877 | Nitze | Distal electric light and lenses |
| **1901** | **Kelling** | Insufflated abdomen of a **dog** |
| **1910** | **Jacobaeus** | First human series; "laparoscopy" |
| **1938** | **Veress** | Spring-loaded needle |
| **1966** | **Hopkins** | **Rod-lens system** |
| 1970s–80s | **Semm** | Automatic insufflator; operative laparoscopy |
| 1970s | Hasson | Open access |
| 1987 | Mouret | Video cholecystectomy |
- **Also know:** Desormeaux 1853, first therapeutic endoscopy; fibreoptics (Tyndall's principle) for flexible scopes; CO₂ became the standard insufflant.

### Urological milestones
| Year | Pioneer | Milestone |
|---|---|---|
| **1976** | **Cortesi** | Impalpable testis, first urological use |
| **1990–91** | **Clayman** | **First laparoscopic nephrectomy**; nephroureterectomy 1991 |
| **1992** | **Gaur (India)** | **Balloon retroperitoneal access** |
| **1995** | **Ratner** | Live-donor nephrectomy |
| 1997 | Schuessler | Laparoscopic radical prostatectomy (slow, not adopted) |
| 2000 | Guillonneau & Vallancien | Standardised prostatectomy |
| **2000** | **da Vinci**; **Binder** | Robot cleared in US; first robotic prostatectomy |
| 2000s | **Menon** | Vattikuti Institute Prostatectomy |
| 2007 | Raman; Rane | Single-port nephrectomy |

### Why the robot spread
- Laparoscopic prostatectomy stalled because intracorporeal suturing was hard. The robot gave wristed instruments and a 3D magnified view.
- da Vinci generations: S 2006 (fourth arm), Si 2009 (dual console), Xi 2014 (flexible docking), SP 2018 (single port).

### Indian contribution and recent advances
- Gaur's balloon made retroperitoneoscopy practical; indigenous robot SSI Mantra with telesurgery.
- New robots (Versius, Hugo, Hinotori), telesurgery, haptic feedback.

**Diagram to draw:** Timeline 1806 → 1901 → 1910 → 1938 → 1990 → 2000.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Bozzini 1806; Kelling 1901 (dog); Jacobaeus 1910 (human); Veress 1938; Hopkins rod-lens.
> - Urology: Cortesi 1976; Clayman 1990; Gaur 1992; Ratner 1995.
> - da Vinci 2000; Binder first robotic prostatectomy; robot solved suturing barrier.

## I-3c. Renin-angiotensin system
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition
- Hormonal cascade controlling **blood pressure, extracellular volume, Na⁺/K⁺ balance and GFR (glomerular filtration rate)**.

### Cascade
```
Angiotensinogen (liver)
   ↓ renin (juxtaglomerular cells) – rate-limiting
Angiotensin I
   ↓ ACE (angiotensin-converting enzyme), mainly lung
Angiotensin II → AT1 / AT2 receptors
   ↓ ACE2
Angiotensin (1-7) → Mas receptor (protective)
```

### Juxtaglomerular apparatus and renin release
- Granular cells in the afferent arteriole secrete renin and act as baroreceptors; the **macula densa** senses tubular NaCl.
- **Stimuli:** low perfusion pressure, low macula densa NaCl, sympathetic β1 activity. **Inhibitors:** angiotensin II feedback, atrial natriuretic peptide, β-blockers.

### Actions of angiotensin II
- **AT1 receptor:** vasoconstriction; **aldosterone** (distal Na⁺ retention, K⁺ loss); direct proximal Na⁺ reabsorption; thirst and ADH (antidiuretic hormone); **fibrosis**.
- **AT2 receptor:** vasodilatation; fetal urinary tract development.
- **Intrarenal:** efferent constriction maintains GFR when perfusion falls, so ACE inhibitors and ARBs (angiotensin receptor blockers) drop GFR in bilateral or solitary-kidney renal artery stenosis.

### Urological relevance
- **Renovascular hypertension (Goldblatt kidney):** atherosclerosis or fibromuscular dysplasia; **renal vein renin ratio >1.5** lateralises.
- **Page kidney:** subcapsular haematoma compresses parenchyma; ACE inhibitor/ARB, then drainage.
- **Reninoma (juxtaglomerular cell tumour):** young women, ≤3 cm mass, refractory hypertension, **hypokalaemia**; excision cures most.
- **Obstruction:** hypertension in ~77% bilateral vs ~20% unilateral; angiotensin II drives fibrosis.
- **Also know:** post-traumatic hypertension (arteriovenous fistula, scarring); reflux nephropathy (renin-guided segmental resection); angiotensin blockade and hyperkalaemia in CKD (chronic kidney disease); ACE inhibitors/ARBs contraindicated in pregnancy.

**Diagram to draw:** Juxtaglomerular apparatus and cascade.

### Recent advances
- Renal denervation (US approval 2023) for resistant hypertension.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Renin is rate-limiting; released by low pressure, low NaCl, β1.
> - Efferent constriction maintains GFR, so ACE inhibitors risk renal failure in bilateral stenosis.
> - Renin ratio >1.5 lateralises; reninoma excision cures most.

## I-3d. Patho-physiology of Brain stem death
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition
- **Irreversible loss of the capacity for consciousness and to breathe** from permanent brainstem failure; legally death. Donation after brain death (DBD) keeps circulation until procurement, minimising warm ischaemia.

### Pathophysiology
```
Brain injury → oedema → raised intracranial pressure (ICP)
 → falling cerebral perfusion → herniation ("coning")
 → Pons: Cushing reflex (hypertension, bradycardia)
 → Medulla: catecholamine storm (hypertension, arrhythmia)
 → Loss of sympathetic tone → vasodilatation, hypotension
```
- The storm injures myocardium and causes pulmonary oedema. Loss of antidiuretic hormone causes **diabetes insipidus (DI)**: polyuria, hypernatraemia; thyroid hormone and cortisol fall.
- **Hypothermia** (hypothalamic loss) and **disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC)** from brain thromboplastin.

### Diagnosis
- **Preconditions:** apnoeic coma of known irreversible cause; exclude sedatives, relaxants, hypothermia (35–36 °C by protocol), metabolic causes.
- **Brainstem reflexes, all must be absent:**

| Test | How it is done | Cranial nerves |
|---|---|---|
| Pupillary light reflex | Pupils fixed, no response to bright light | II, III |
| Corneal reflex | Touch cornea; no blink | V, VII |
| Oculovestibular (caloric) | ~50 mL ice-cold water into each ear (drum visible); no eye movement | VIII, III, VI |
| Oculocephalic (doll's eye) | Turn head; eyes move with the head | VIII, III, VI |
| Motor response | Central painful stimulus (supraorbital pressure); no facial grimace | V, VII |
| Gag and cough | Stimulate pharynx; pass suction catheter down trachea; no response | IX, X |

- **Apnoea test:** pre-oxygenate with 100% oxygen, disconnect the ventilator with oxygen insufflation, and watch for breathing effort until **arterial CO₂ (PaCO₂) ≥60 mmHg**. No effort = positive. Abort if hypotension, desaturation or arrhythmia.
- **Ancillary tests** (electroencephalography (EEG), cerebral angiography, perfusion scan, transcranial Doppler) are not mandatory; used when clinical testing cannot be completed.

### Indian law
- **THOA (Transplantation of Human Organs Act) 1994**, amended 2011, Rules 2014: **4-member board** (hospital in-charge, independent specialist, neurologist/neurosurgeon, treating doctor; no transplant team member); **two tests at least 6 hours apart**.

### Donor management
- **Rule of 100s:** systolic pressure >100 mmHg, urine >100 mL/h, PaO₂ (arterial oxygen tension) >100 mmHg, haemoglobin >100 g/L.
- **Vasopressin** preferred (also treats DI); thyroid hormone, methylprednisolone, insulin.
- **Also know:** cold storage at 4 °C (University of Wisconsin solution); donor exclusions (metastatic cancer, melanoma, untreatable viral infection).

### Recent advances
- Hypothermic machine perfusion reduces delayed graft function.

**Diagram to draw:** Herniation sequence leading to storm, DI, hypothermia, DIC.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Raised ICP → Cushing reflex → storm → hypotension, DI, hypothermia, DIC.
> - Apnoea test: no breathing at PaCO₂ ≥60 mmHg.
> - THOA: 4-member board, two tests ≥6 h apart; Rule of 100s.

## I-3e. Male reproductive immunology
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Introduction
- Haploid germ cells appear at puberty, after self-tolerance is established, so their antigens are "foreign". The testis is therefore **immune-privileged**.

### Immune privilege
**Anatomical barrier: blood–testis barrier (BTB)**
- **Tight junctions between Sertoli cells**, formed at puberty, divide a **basal compartment** (spermatogonia, plasma-like fluid) from an **adluminal compartment** (later spermatocytes, spermatids). 

**Functional barrier**
- A small antigen leak at the **rete testis, efferent ductules and epididymis** induces suppressor T cells and tolerance; a large load provokes immunity.
- Sertoli and local mediators: galectin-1, TGF-β (transforming growth factor-β), interferon-γ, soluble Fc receptor, **androgens**.
- Insult before puberty does not induce antibodies; the barrier also limits chemotherapy (isolated testicular relapse).

### Antisperm antibodies (ASA)
- **Causes:** obstruction (**vasectomy** commonest), torsion, trauma, biopsy, infection, heat (varicocele, cryptorchidism), genetic (human leukocyte antigen HLA-B28).
- **Effects:** agglutination, poor motility, failure to cross cervical mucus and bind the zona; head-bound antibody matters most.
- **Tests:** mixed antiglobulin reaction or immunobead test; **≥50% of motile sperm bound** is significant (WHO [World Health Organization] 2010).
- **Treatment:** corticosteroid trials conflict; washing removes only unbound antibody; **IVF/ICSI (in vitro fertilisation/intracytoplasmic sperm injection)** is usual.

### Infection and inflammation
- **Leukocytospermia:** >1 × 10⁶ peroxidase-positive leukocytes/mL. Reactive oxygen species and cytokines damage sperm.
- Organisms: gonococcus, *Chlamydia*, *E. coli*; mumps orchitis after puberty; tuberculosis scars ducts.
- **Also know:** sperm DNA fragmentation testing; prostatitis harms sperm via oxidative stress and ASA; epididymal macrophages ingest sperm after vasectomy; seminal plasma suppresses female tract immunity and is antioxidant.

**Diagram to draw:** Seminiferous tubule with Sertoli tight junctions and compartments.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - BTB = Sertoli tight junctions; plus active tolerance (galectin-1, TGF-β, androgens).
> - ASA: vasectomy commonest; ≥50% bound significant; IVF/ICSI.
> - Leukocytospermia >1 million/mL.

## I-3f. Principles of antimicrobial therapy in UTI
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definitions
- **Uncomplicated** urinary tract infection (UTI): healthy non-pregnant women, normal tract. **Complicated:** men, pregnancy, abnormal tract, catheter, immunosuppression.
- **Recurrent:** ≥2 in 6 months or ≥3 in 12 months (reinfection or persistence).

### Principles
- **Site decides the level:** urine in cystitis; tissue in pyelonephritis, prostatitis, bacteraemia. Nitrofurantoin and fosfomycin lack tissue levels: **cystitis only**.
- β-lactams are time-dependent; aminoglycosides and fluoroquinolones (FQs) concentration-dependent (once-daily gentamicin).
- **Resistance thresholds:** trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX) if local resistance ≤20%; oral FQ for pyelonephritis if ≤10%.
- **Collateral damage:** FQs and cephalosporins select resistance in bowel flora and *C. difficile*, so avoid them in cystitis.
- Culture all except simple cystitis; **drain** obstructed infected kidneys; shortest effective course.

### Empirical choices
| Scenario | Drug | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Cystitis (women) | Nitrofurantoin; fosfomycin 3 g; pivmecillinam; TMP-SMX | 5 d; single; 3–5 d; 3 d |
| Cystitis (men) | TMP-SMX or per culture | 7 d (= 14 d, Drekonja 2021) |
| Pyelonephritis (oral) | Ciprofloxacin; levofloxacin 750 mg | 7 d; 5 d |
| Complicated | Aminoglycoside ± ampicillin, or 3rd-generation cephalosporin | 7–14 d |
| Urosepsis | Broad-spectrum **within 1 h**, source control | – |

### Asymptomatic bacteriuria
- Treat **only in pregnancy and before mucosa-breaching urological procedures**.

### Prophylaxis
- Recurrent: vaginal oestrogen, **methenamine hippurate** (non-inferior to antibiotics, ALTAR 2022), cranberry, post-coital antibiotics.
- Surgical: single dose within 1 hour; ≤24 h for percutaneous nephrolithotomy and prostheses.
- **Also know:** ESBL (extended-spectrum β-lactamase) producers; nitrofurantoin avoided in renal failure; new agents pivmecillinam (US 2024) and gepotidacin (2025).

**Diagram to draw:** Concentration–time curve showing peak:MIC (minimum inhibitory concentration).

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Recurrent = ≥2 in 6 months or ≥3 in 12 months.
> - Cystitis: nitrofurantoin 5 d, fosfomycin single, TMP-SMX 3 d (resistance ≤20%).
> - Pyelonephritis: ciprofloxacin 7 d or levofloxacin 5 d (FQ resistance ≤10%).

#### I-3c. Renin-angiotensin system (SAQ)

- Renin is rate-limiting; released by low pressure, low NaCl, β1.
- Efferent constriction maintains GFR, so ACE inhibitors risk renal failure in bilateral stenosis.
- Renin ratio >1.5 lateralises; reninoma excision cures most.

#### I-3d. Patho-physiology of Brain stem death (SAQ)

- Raised ICP → Cushing reflex → storm → hypotension, DI, hypothermia, DIC.
- Apnoea test: no breathing at PaCO₂ ≥60 mmHg.
- THOA: 4-member board, two tests ≥6 h apart; Rule of 100s.

#### I-3e. Male reproductive immunology (SAQ)

- BTB = Sertoli tight junctions; plus active tolerance (galectin-1, TGF-β, androgens).
- ASA: vasectomy commonest; ≥50% bound significant; IVF/ICSI.
- Leukocytospermia >1 million/mL.

#### I-3f. Principles of antimicrobial therapy in UTI (SAQ)

- Recurrent = ≥2 in 6 months or ≥3 in 12 months.
- Cystitis: nitrofurantoin 5 d, fosfomycin single, TMP-SMX 3 d (resistance ≤20%).
- Pyelonephritis: ciprofloxacin 7 d or levofloxacin 5 d (FQ resistance ≤10%).
