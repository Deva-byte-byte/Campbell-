## I-1. Describe in details basic patho-physiology of urine production and transport
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks) | ~1500 words

### Introduction
- Urine formation = **glomerular filtration → tubular reabsorption → tubular secretion → concentration/dilution**; transport = active **peristalsis** of pelvis/ureter to a low-pressure, compliant bladder.
- Sources: Campbell: Renal Physiology and Pathophysiology chapters; Physiology and Pharmacology of the Renal Pelvis and Ureter; Pathophysiology of Urinary Tract Obstruction.

### A. Renal blood flow (RBF)
- RBF ≈ **20–25% of cardiac output** (~1–1.2 L/min); RPF ~600 mL/min; cortex receives ~90%, medulla <10% (protects medullary gradient but makes it hypoxia-prone).
- Serial arterioles: **afferent** (regulates inflow) → glomerulus → **efferent** (maintains glomerular pressure) → peritubular capillaries / **vasa recta**.
- **Autoregulation** keeps RBF and GFR constant over MAP ~**80–180 mmHg**:
  - **Myogenic** – afferent smooth muscle contracts on stretch.
  - **Tubuloglomerular feedback (TGF)** – ↑NaCl at **macula densa** → adenosine/ATP → afferent constriction (and ↓renin).
- Extrinsic: sympathetic (α1 vasoconstriction, β1 renin), Ang II (efferent > afferent), endothelin, thromboxane (constrictors); PGE2/PGI2, NO, bradykinin, ANP (dilators). NSAIDs remove PG protection in low-flow states.

### B. Glomerular filtration
- GFR ~**125 mL/min (~180 L/day)**; filtration fraction (GFR/RPF) ≈ **20%**.
- **Starling equation:** GFR = Kf × [(P_GC – P_BS) – (π_GC – π_BS)].
- Determinants: glomerular capillary hydrostatic pressure (main), Bowman's space pressure (↑ in **obstruction**), plasma oncotic pressure, Kf (surface area – mesangial contraction; permeability).
- Filtration barrier: fenestrated endothelium → GBM (negative charge, heparan sulphate) → podocyte slit diaphragm (nephrin, podocin). Restricts by size (>~ 60–70 kDa) and charge (albumin excluded).
- Measurement: inulin (gold standard), creatinine clearance, eGFR (CKD-EPI), DTPA renography (split function).
- Tubular secretion (PCT organic anion/cation transporters) eliminates drugs, contrast and creatinine – basis of MAG3 renography (secreted) vs DTPA (filtered).

### C. Tubular handling – segment-wise
| Segment | Key transporters | Handling | Clinical/drug link |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PCT** | Na⁺/H⁺ (NHE3), SGLT2/1, Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase, AQP1, carbonic anhydrase | ~**65%** Na⁺/water (iso-osmotic), ~100% glucose & amino acids, ~80–90% HCO₃⁻, most phosphate (PTH ↓), secretes organic acids/bases (drugs, creatinine) | Acetazolamide, SGLT2i; Fanconi syndrome; cystinuria (dibasic AA) |
| **Thin descending limb** | AQP1 | Water-permeable, solute-impermeable → tubular fluid concentrates | |
| **Thin ascending limb** | – | Water-impermeable; passive NaCl exit | |
| **Thick ascending limb (TAL)** | **NKCC2**, ROMK, Ca/Mg paracellular (claudin-16/19) | ~**25%** NaCl; **impermeable to water → "diluting segment"**; generates medullary gradient | Loop diuretics; Bartter; secretes Tamm–Horsfall (uromodulin) |
| **DCT** | **NCC**, TRPV5 (Ca²⁺) | ~5–7% Na⁺; active Ca²⁺ reabsorption (PTH) | Thiazides ↓ urinary Ca (hypercalciuric stones); Gitelman |
| **Collecting duct – principal cells** | **ENaC**, ROMK, **AQP2** (apical), AQP3/4 (basolateral) | Na⁺ reabsorption/K⁺ secretion (aldosterone); water (ADH) | Amiloride, spironolactone; Liddle |
| **Intercalated cells** | Type A: H⁺-ATPase; Type B: pendrin | Acid/base | Distal RTA (type 1) → CaP stones, nephrocalcinosis |

### D. Concentration and dilution
- **Countercurrent multiplication (loop of Henle):** TAL pumps NaCl out without water → single effect (~200 mOsm gradient at each level) multiplied by countercurrent flow → medullary interstitium up to ~**1200 mOsm/kg** at papilla.
- **Urea recycling:** ADH-stimulated urea transporters (UT-A1/A3) in inner medullary collecting duct contribute ~half of inner-medullary osmolality.
- **Countercurrent exchange (vasa recta):** hairpin capillaries, slow flow – preserve the gradient (washout in osmotic diuresis/high flow).
- **ADH (vasopressin):** released from posterior pituitary for ↑plasma osmolality (~1–2% change sensed) or ↓volume → **V2 receptor** on basolateral principal cell → Gs → cAMP → PKA → **phosphorylation and insertion of AQP2** vesicles into apical membrane → water reabsorption along gradient. Long-term ↑AQP2 synthesis.
  - V1a – vasoconstriction; V2 also on endothelium (vWF/factor VIII release – basis of **desmopressin** in nocturia/enuresis/bleeding).
  - Urine osmolality range ~**50–1200 mOsm/kg**.
  - Nephrogenic DI: V2/AQP2 mutations, lithium, hypercalcaemia, hypokalaemia, **post-obstructive** (↓AQP2).

### E. Hormonal control (summary)
| Hormone | Site | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Ang II / aldosterone | PCT, efferent; CD (ENaC) | Na⁺ retention, K⁺/H⁺ loss, maintains GFR |
| ADH | CD | Free-water reabsorption |
| ANP/BNP | Afferent dilatation, CD | Natriuresis, ↑GFR, ↓renin/aldosterone |
| PTH | PCT (↓PO₄), DCT (↑Ca) | Phosphaturia, Ca retention; 1α-hydroxylase |
| Prostaglandins, NO, dopamine | Vessels, tubule | Vasodilatation, natriuresis |

### F. Upper urinary tract transport
**1. Pacemaker activity**
- Site: **proximal renal pelvis/pelvicalyceal border** (minor calyces in multicalyceal kidneys).
- **Atypical smooth muscle cells** – primary pacemakers (spontaneous depolarisation; HCN/T-type Ca²⁺ currents implicated).
- **Interstitial cells of Cajal-like cells (c-Kit⁺)** – in pelvis and ureter; thought to act as secondary pacemakers/conductors coupling atypical SMCs to typical SMCs; may drive activity distally if proximal pacemaker lost (relevance to **UPJ obstruction** – reduced ICC-like cells reported).
- Activity is **myogenic**: peristalsis persists after denervation and in transplanted kidneys; autonomic nerves only modulate.

**2. Electromechanical coupling**
- Typical ureteric SMC resting potential approx. –33 to –70 mV.
- Action potential: rapid upstroke (**L-type Ca²⁺** influx) → **plateau** with oscillations → repolarisation (K⁺ efflux).
- Spread cell-to-cell via **gap junctions** (functional syncytium) at ~2–6 cm/s.
- Ca²⁺ + calmodulin → **myosin light-chain kinase** → phosphorylation → actin–myosin cross-bridging → contraction. Relaxation via cAMP/cGMP (β-agonists, PDE inhibitors, NO).

**3. Bolus propagation and pressures**
- Pelvis fills → pelvic pressure rises → urine pushed through UPJ → ureter contracts proximally behind a **bolus**; walls **coapt** fully so urine moves as discrete boluses.
- Peristalsis ~**2–6/min**; resting ureteral pressure ~0–5 cmH₂O; contraction waves ~20–80 cmH₂O.
- At low flow, pelvis contracts more often than ureter (**"pyeloureteric block"** – not every pelvic contraction propagates).

**4. Effect of flow rate**
- ↑Flow → ↑frequency first, then ↑bolus volume; at very high flow boluses **coalesce** → walls no longer coapt → continuous **fluid column**, ureter dilates – transport becomes inefficient.
- Hence dilatation can occur **without obstruction** (diuresis, DI, high-flow states – relevant to diuretic renogram interpretation).

**5. Ureterovesical junction (UVJ)**
- Oblique intramural tunnel, Waldeyer sheath; **tunnel length:diameter ≈ 5:1** (Paquin) prevents reflux.
- Ureteric bolus pressure must exceed intravesical pressure; with sustained high bladder pressure (>~40 cmH₂O – neurogenic bladder) efflux fails → **secondary hydronephrosis**/upper tract damage.

**6. Factors affecting transport**
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Obstruction** | Acute: ↑pressure & ↑contraction amplitude; chronic: dilatation, ↓coaptation, decompensation, fibrosis |
| **Infection** | Bacterial endotoxin (Gram-negative) inhibits peristalsis → dilatation without obstruction |
| **Pregnancy** | Dilatation (R > L) mainly mechanical compression at pelvic brim; progesterone contributes |
| **Drugs** | α-agonists stimulate; **α1-blockers** (tamsulosin – MET), β-agonists, **Ca-channel blockers** (nifedipine), PDE5i relax; NSAIDs ↓PG-mediated pelvic pressure/diuresis (renal colic); opioids variable |
| **Age/VUR** | Fetal/infant ureter compliant; reflux → dilatation |

### G. Bladder storage (brief)
- Accommodation with low pressure: **high compliance** (viscoelastic wall, collagen/elastin) + **sympathetic** β3 relaxation of detrusor, α1 contraction of bladder neck (T10–L2, hypogastric); **pudendal** (Onuf's nucleus, S2–4) external sphincter tone (guarding reflex); pontine micturition centre inhibited.
- Normal capacity ~400–500 mL; storage pressure normally low (<~15 cmH₂O at capacity).

### H. Pathophysiology of obstruction
**Unilateral ureteral obstruction (UUO) – triphasic response** (Campbell; Vaughan/Moe canine studies):
```
Phase 1 (0 – ~1.5 h): RBF ↑ , ureteral pressure ↑
   - afferent vasodilatation (PGE2, NO)
Phase 2 (~1.5 – 5 h): RBF ↓ , ureteral pressure still ↑ (keeps rising)
   - efferent vasoconstriction begins
Phase 3 (> 5 h, persists): RBF ↓ , ureteral pressure ↓
   - afferent (preglomerular) vasoconstriction: Ang II, thromboxane A2, endothelin
```
- GFR falls because of ↓RBF and ↑tubular pressure; contralateral kidney shows compensatory ↑ function.
- **Bilateral obstruction/solitary kidney:** RBF rises only modestly then falls; **ureteral pressure stays high longer**; accumulation of natriuretic substances (ANP, urea) → **post-obstructive diuresis** after relief.
- **Tubular effects:** ↓concentrating ability (↓AQP2, washout), ↓Na reabsorption, **distal RTA**, ↓K⁺ secretion.
- **Chronic:** Ang II and TGF-β1 → macrophage infiltration, tubular apoptosis, interstitial fibrosis, **tubular atrophy**. Recovery depends on duration, degree, compliance of pelvis (intrarenal pelvis worse), infection.
- **Pyelorenal backflow** (pyelotubular, pyelovenous, pyelolymphatic, pyelointerstitial) acts as pop-off and allows partial function under obstruction; forniceal rupture → urinoma.

### Recent advances
- SGLT2 inhibitors – restore TGF (afferent constriction) → nephroprotection (also increase glycosuria; UTI/genital infection signal).
- Vaptans (V2 antagonists: tolvaptan) – aquaresis; used in ADPKD.
- Biomarkers of obstructive injury: urinary NGAL, KIM-1, MCP-1 (research/paediatric UPJO follow-up).
- ICC-like cell and HCN channel research in pathogenesis of congenital UPJO.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - RBF ~20–25% CO; GFR 125 mL/min; FF 20%; autoregulation 80–180 mmHg (myogenic + TGF).
> - PCT 65%, TAL 25% (water-impermeable diluting segment), DCT 5–7%, CD fine-tunes via aldosterone/ADH (V2 → cAMP → AQP2).
> - Pacemaker = atypical SMCs at pelvicalyceal border; ICC-like cells modulate; peristalsis is myogenic, 2–6/min.
> - High flow → bolus coalescence → dilatation without obstruction.
> - UUO triphasic: ↑/↑, ↓/↑, ↓/↓ (RBF/pressure).

## I-2. Describe female pelvic anatomy in relation to prolapse and SUI
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks) | ~1500 words

### Introduction
- Pelvic organ support depends on an **interaction of muscles (levator ani), connective tissue (endopelvic fascia and ligaments) and bony pelvis**. Failure → **POP** (descent of anterior/apical/posterior vaginal wall) and **SUI** (involuntary leakage on effort when urethral support/closure fails).
- Campbell: Anatomy of the lower urinary tract/female pelvic floor; Pelvic organ prolapse; Stress incontinence chapters.

### A. Bony pelvis
- Female pelvis wider, shallower, gynaecoid inlet; in standing posture the pubic rami and ischial spines lie almost in the same horizontal plane → pelvic organs rest on the **levator plate** rather than the hiatus.
- Key landmarks for surgery: **ischial spine** (ATFP, ATLA and sacrospinous ligament attach), **sacrospinous ligament**, **Cooper's (iliopectineal) ligament**, **sacral promontory/anterior longitudinal ligament**, obturator foramen.

### B. Pelvic floor muscles (pelvic diaphragm)
| Component | Origin → insertion | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Pubococcygeus/pubovisceral** (pubovaginalis, puboperinealis, puboanalis) | Pubis → vagina, perineal body, anus | Closes hiatus, lifts urethra/vagina forward |
| **Puborectalis** | Pubis → sling behind anorectal junction | Anorectal angle; closes urogenital hiatus |
| **Iliococcygeus** | Arcus tendineus levator ani (ATLA) on obturator fascia → anococcygeal raphe/coccyx | Forms horizontal **levator plate** |
| (Coccygeus/ischiococcygeus) | Ischial spine → sacrum/coccyx | Posterior support; overlies sacrospinous ligament |
- Constant **basal tone** (type I fibres) keeps the **levator hiatus** closed; reflex contraction on cough.
- **Levator avulsion** (pubovisceral detachment at vaginal delivery, best seen on 3D/4D translabial USG or MRI) and ↑hiatal area are strong risk factors for POP and recurrence.
- **Perineal membrane (urogenital diaphragm)** and perineal body – distal support (level III).

### C. Endopelvic fascia and ligaments
- Fibromuscular connective tissue (collagen, elastin, smooth muscle) enveloping viscera:
  - **Cardinal (Mackenrodt's) & uterosacral ligaments** – apex suspension.
  - **Pubocervical fascia** – anterior vaginal wall "fascia" attached laterally to **arcus tendineus fasciae pelvis (ATFP, white line)** (pubis → ischial spine).
  - **Rectovaginal fascia (Denonvilliers)** – posterior, attached to perineal body below and uterosacral complex above.
  - **Pubourethral ligaments** – mid-urethra to pubis (Petros; Zacharin).
- Histologically the vaginal "fascia" is the fibromuscular layer of the vaginal wall – no true separate fascia (important when describing "fascial" repairs).

### D. DeLancey's three levels of vaginal support (DeLancey 1992)
| Level | Region | Structures | Defect → |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I – suspension** | Upper vagina/cervix | Cardinal–uterosacral complex | **Uterine/vault prolapse, enterocele** |
| **II – attachment** | Mid vagina | Lateral attachment to **ATFP** (anterior) and ATLA/rectovaginal fascia (posterior) | **Cystocele (paravaginal), rectocele** |
| **III – fusion** | Distal ~2–3 cm | Fusion to perineal membrane, perineal body, levator | **Urethral hypermobility, perineal descent, low rectocele** |

### E. Urethral support and continence mechanisms
**1. Hammock hypothesis (DeLancey 1994)**
- Urethra lies on a supportive layer of **anterior vaginal wall + endopelvic fascia**, attached laterally to ATFP and levator ani.
- Rise in abdominal pressure **compresses urethra against this firm hammock** → closure. Loss of the hammock (laxity/detachment) → urethra not compressed → SUI. (Replaces Enhörning's older "pressure transmission" theory.)

**2. Integral theory (Petros & Ulmsten, 1990)**
- Stress and urge symptoms both arise from **laxity of the vagina or its supporting ligaments** (pubourethral, uterosacral) due to altered connective tissue.
- Three directional muscle forces (anterior pubococcygeus, levator plate, longitudinal muscle of anus) act around ligaments; **mid-urethral pubourethral ligament** is the key – basis of the **tension-free vaginal tape (TVT, Ulmsten 1996)** placed at mid-urethra.

**3. Intrinsic urethral closure (sphincter)**
- Mucosal coaptation (oestrogen-dependent vascular submucosal plexus), smooth muscle, and striated **rhabdosphincter**: sphincter urethrae (middle third) + **compressor urethrae and urethrovaginal sphincter** (distal arching fibres).
- Maximal urethral closure pressure falls with age; **intrinsic sphincter deficiency (ISD)** → low VLPP (<60 cmH₂O)/MUCP (<20 cmH₂O) – poorer results with colposuspension; prefer retropubic sling/autologous sling/bulking.
- SUI = **hypermobility** (support failure) ± **ISD** – a continuum, not separate entities.

### F. Compartments and defects
| Compartment | Defect | Anatomical basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Anterior** | **Central cystocele** (distension) | Midline attenuation of pubocervical fascia – rugae lost |
| | **Lateral (paravaginal) cystocele** (displacement) | Detachment from ATFP – rugae preserved, lateral sulci lost |
| | Transverse/apical | Separation from pericervical ring |
| **Apical** | Uterine/vault prolapse, enterocele | Level I – uterosacral/cardinal failure; enterocele = peritoneum herniation between vaginal fascia layers (post-hysterectomy, after colposuspension) |
| **Posterior** | Rectocele, perineal descent | Rectovaginal fascia defect, perineal body disruption |
- Coexistence common: **apical support underpins anterior** – anterior repair fails if apex not addressed.

### G. Innervation
- **Pudendal nerve (S2–4)** – external urethral/anal sphincters, perineal muscles; traverses Alcock's canal; stretch/compression injury in delivery.
- **Levator ani nerve (S3–5)** – runs on the **pelvic (superior) surface** of levator, separate from pudendal (DeLancey/Barber).
- Autonomic: pelvic splanchnic (S2–4, parasympathetic), hypogastric (T10–L2) – injured in radical pelvic surgery.
- Neuropathy (denervation of pelvic floor) after childbirth contributes to POP/SUI.

### H. Pathophysiology – risk factors (Bump & Norton)
- **Predisposing:** genetics, race, connective tissue disorders (Ehlers–Danlos, Marfan), collagen type III > I.
- **Inciting:** **vaginal childbirth** (strongest; forceps, macrosomia, prolonged 2nd stage, levator avulsion), hysterectomy, pelvic surgery.
- **Promoting:** obesity, chronic cough/COPD, constipation, heavy lifting, smoking.
- **Decompensating:** ageing, **menopause (oestrogen loss)**, neuropathy, myopathy.

### I. POP-Q staging (ICS, Bump et al 1996)
- Reference = **hymen (0)**; above = negative, below = positive (cm). Nine measurements:
| Point | Description | Range |
|---|---|---|
| **Aa** | Anterior wall 3 cm proximal to hymen | –3 to +3 |
| **Ba** | Most dependent anterior wall (Aa–C) | –3 to +tvl |
| **C** | Cervix/vault | |
| **D** | Posterior fornix (omitted post-hysterectomy) | |
| **Ap / Bp** | Posterior equivalents of Aa/Ba | |
| **gh** | Genital hiatus (urethral meatus → posterior hymen) | |
| **pb** | Perineal body | |
| **tvl** | Total vaginal length | |

| Stage | Definition |
|---|---|
| 0 | No prolapse (Aa, Ap, Ba, Bp = –3; C/D ≤ –(tvl–2)) |
| I | Most distal point > 1 cm above hymen (< –1) |
| II | Most distal point within ±1 cm of hymen |
| III | > +1 cm below hymen but < (tvl – 2) cm |
| IV | Complete eversion (≥ tvl – 2) |

### Clinical assessment linked to anatomy
- Examination supine and standing with Valsalva; Sims speculum to isolate each compartment; **cough stress test** for SUI (also after reducing prolapse – **occult SUI**).
- **Q-tip test**: straining angle change >30° = urethral hypermobility.
- Lateral sulci preserved vs lost (paravaginal vs central defect); rectovaginal examination for enterocele/rectocele; levator strength (Oxford scale) and palpable avulsion.
- Urodynamics (VLPP/MUCP) when ISD or mixed symptoms suspected; translabial USG/dynamic MRI in complex/recurrent cases.

### J. How anatomy guides surgery
| Defect/aim | Operation | Anatomical principle |
|---|---|---|
| SUI – hypermobility | **Mid-urethral sling** (retropubic TVT / transobturator TOT) | Integral theory – recreates pubourethral ligament support at mid-urethra |
| SUI | **Burch colposuspension** | Paravaginal/periurethral fascia at bladder neck level → **Cooper's ligament** (restores hammock) |
| SUI – ISD | Autologous fascial (pubovaginal) sling at bladder neck, bulking, AUS | Compression/coaptation |
| Paravaginal cystocele | **Paravaginal repair** | Reattach to ATFP (level II) |
| Central cystocele | Anterior colporrhaphy | Midline plication |
| Apical (level I) | **Sacrocolpopexy** (open/lap/robotic – gold standard durability) | Mesh vault/vagina → anterior longitudinal ligament over **S1 promontory** (beware middle sacral vessels, left common iliac vein) |
| Apical vaginal route | **Sacrospinous ligament fixation** (usually right; suture ~1.5–2 cm medial to ischial spine to avoid pudendal vessels/nerve), uterosacral ligament suspension (ureteric injury risk – check cystoscopy) | Level I restoration |
| Rectocele | Posterior colporrhaphy/site-specific repair, perineorrhaphy | Level II/III |
| Elderly, not sexually active | Colpocleisis | Obliteration |

### K. Mesh regulation
- **FDA, April 2019:** ordered manufacturers (Boston Scientific, Coloplast – last remaining) to **stop selling and distributing transvaginal mesh for anterior-compartment POP repair** (lack of proven benefit–risk). Did **not** apply to mid-urethral slings or sacrocolpopexy mesh.
- UK (2018) paused vaginal mesh including MUS pending safeguards (Cumberlege review 2020). Mesh complications: exposure/erosion, pain, dyspareunia – counsel and document.

**Diagram to draw:** (1) DeLancey levels I–III on a lateral/3D vaginal sketch with ATFP and uterosacral ligaments. (2) Hammock: cross-section of urethra on anterior vaginal wall attached to ATFP, with abdominal pressure arrow.

### Recent advances
- 3D/4D translabial ultrasound and dynamic MRI – levator avulsion, hiatal ballooning (predict recurrence).
- Robotic sacrocolpopexy and hysteropexy (uterine-sparing) increasing.
- Single-incision mini-slings – comparable short-term outcomes to standard MUS (UK SIMS RCT, 2022).
- Autologous fascial sling and colposuspension resurgence after mesh concerns; laser therapies for SUI remain experimental (FDA warning 2018 on energy-based vaginal devices).

> **Key points to remember:**
> - DeLancey levels: I suspension (cardinal–uterosacral), II attachment (ATFP), III fusion (perineal membrane/body).
> - Hammock (DeLancey 1994) vs Integral theory (Petros & Ulmsten 1990 → TVT 1996, mid-urethra).
> - Central (rugae lost) vs paravaginal (rugae preserved) cystocele.
> - POP-Q: hymen = 0; Stage II = ±1 cm of hymen; Stage IV ≥ tvl–2.
> - FDA 2019 – transvaginal mesh for anterior POP withdrawn; MUS and sacrocolpopexy mesh unaffected.

## I-3a. Newer generation CT scan
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks) | ~550 words

### Introduction
- CT is the reference imaging for stones, renal masses, haematuria (CTU) and trauma. Campbell: Urinary tract imaging chapter; EAU Urolithiasis and Upper tract UC guidelines.

### Evolution
| Generation | Feature |
|---|---|
| Conventional (Hounsfield, 1971–72) | Step-and-shoot axial |
| **Helical/spiral CT** (~1989) | Continuous table movement + slip-ring gantry → volumetric data, no misregistration |
| **MDCT** (4-slice 1998 → 16/64 → 256/320-row) | Sub-millimetre isotropic voxels, multiplanar/3D reconstruction, single breath-hold |
| **Dual-source CT** (2005) | Two tube–detector pairs: speed, dual-energy |
| **Photon-counting CT (PCCT)** | First FDA clearance **2021** (Siemens NAEOTOM Alpha) |

### Dual-energy CT (DECT)
- Acquires at two energy spectra (e.g. 80/140 kVp) via dual-source, rapid kV switching, dual-layer detector or split filter.
- Material decomposition based on attenuation ratio:
  - **Uric acid vs non-uric-acid stones** (accuracy high) → selects **oral chemolysis** (alkalinisation) candidates; also cystine, struvite partial differentiation.
  - **Virtual non-contrast (VNC)** images → drop the true unenhanced phase (dose reduction).
  - **Iodine maps** – enhancement of small/hyperdense cysts vs solid masses; stones visible within contrast-filled collecting system.
  - Reduced metal/beam-hardening artefact (virtual monoenergetic images).

### Low/ultra-low dose CT KUB
- Standard NCCT abdomen-pelvis ~**8–10 mSv**; **low-dose** CT KUB **<3–4 mSv**; **ultra-low-dose <1 mSv** (comparable to plain KUB+).
- Sensitivity/specificity for stones remain ~95% (lower for small stones <3 mm and BMI >30). EAU recommends **low-dose NCCT** for acute flank pain (after USG) and follow-up; avoid repeated standard-dose CT in young/recurrent stone formers.

### CT urography (CTU)
- Indicated for **visible haematuria/UTUC** evaluation (EAU).
- **Triple-phase**: unenhanced → nephrographic (~90–100 s) → excretory (~10–15 min) – dose ~15–25 mSv or more.
- **Split-bolus**: contrast in 2 aliquots; single post-contrast acquisition gives combined nephrographic + excretory phase → lower dose (with DECT VNC, a single acquisition possible).
- Adjuncts: IV furosemide/saline for distension; prone imaging.

### Dose reduction technology
- Automatic tube current modulation, low kVp.
- **Iterative reconstruction** (statistical/model-based) – dose reduction while maintaining image quality.
- **Deep-learning reconstruction** (AI-based denoising, commercially available) – further noise reduction at low dose.

### Photon-counting CT
- Detector (CdTe) counts individual photons and sorts energy → inherent spectral data, **no electronic noise**, higher spatial resolution, lower dose, better stone composition and small-structure imaging.

### Other advances
- **4D/perfusion CT** – time-resolved enhancement; renal tumour characterisation, parathyroid (4D-CT), renal perfusion.
- **CT angiography** – donor nephrectomy planning, renal artery anatomy, AVM; 3D models for partial nephrectomy/PCNL planning (3D printing, virtual reality).
- **Radiomics/AI** – lesion segmentation, stone burden volumetry, prediction of SFR.
- Cone-beam CT – intraoperative in hybrid theatres for PCNL access.

### Uses in urology
- Stones (size, HU >1000 suggests hard calcium oxalate monohydrate/brushite; skin-to-stone distance >10 cm → SWL failure), renal masses (Bosniak 2019 – CT/MRI based), staging (RCC, UTUC, bladder, testis), trauma (AAST grading with delayed phase), abscess/emphysematous pyelonephritis, PUJ crossing vessels.

> - Helical CT ~1989; MDCT 1998; dual-source 2005; PCCT FDA-cleared 2021.
> - DECT: uric acid vs non-UA, VNC, iodine maps.
> - Low-dose KUB <3–4 mSv, ultra-low <1 mSv – EAU preferred NCCT.
> - Split-bolus CTU reduces phases and dose.

## I-3c. Renin-angiotensin system
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks) | ~550 words

### Definition
- Hormonal cascade regulating **blood pressure, extracellular volume, Na⁺/K⁺ balance and GFR**; systemic and local (intrarenal) components. Campbell: Renal physiology; Renovascular hypertension chapters.

### Cascade
```
Angiotensinogen (liver, α2-globulin)
      | Renin (JG cells) – rate-limiting step
Angiotensin I (decapeptide, inactive)
      | ACE (lung endothelium mainly; also kidney) – also degrades bradykinin
Angiotensin II (octapeptide) ──► AT1 receptor (most actions) / AT2 receptor
      | Aminopeptidase A                   | ACE2
Angiotensin III (aldosterone release)   Angiotensin (1-7) ──► Mas receptor
                                          (vasodilatation, anti-fibrotic)
```

### Juxtaglomerular apparatus
- **JG (granular) cells** – modified smooth muscle of **afferent arteriole**; store/secrete renin; act as intrarenal baroreceptors.
- **Macula densa** – specialised TAL/early DCT cells sensing tubular NaCl.
- **Extraglomerular mesangial (Lacis) cells**.

### Stimuli for renin release
| Stimulate | Inhibit |
|---|---|
| ↓Renal perfusion pressure (baroreceptor) | ↑Perfusion pressure |
| ↓NaCl delivery to macula densa | ↑NaCl at macula densa |
| **Sympathetic β1** stimulation | Ang II (negative feedback) |
| Prostaglandins (PGE2, PGI2) | ANP, ADH, NSAIDs, β-blockers |
| Loop diuretics, hypovolaemia, upright posture | High-salt diet |

### Receptors and actions
- **AT1 (Gq):** arteriolar vasoconstriction; **aldosterone** release (zona glomerulosa) → ENaC Na⁺ retention, K⁺/H⁺ excretion; ADH release and thirst; ↑PCT Na⁺/H⁺ exchange; sympathetic facilitation; cardiac/vascular hypertrophy; **fibrosis** (TGF-β, PAI-1).
- **AT2:** vasodilatation, antiproliferative, apoptosis; important in **fetal kidney/urinary tract development** (AT2 knockout → CAKUT incl. VUR, UPJO-like anomalies in animals).
- **ACE2/Ang(1-7)/Mas:** counter-regulatory arm; ACE2 is also the **SARS-CoV-2 entry receptor**.

### Intrarenal effects on GFR
- Ang II constricts **efferent > afferent** arteriole → maintains glomerular hydrostatic pressure and GFR when perfusion is low; ↑filtration fraction.
- Hence **ACEi/ARB → acute fall in GFR** in bilateral renal artery stenosis/stenosis in a solitary kidney, hypovolaemia, NSAID use ("triple whammy" with diuretics).
- Also contracts mesangium (↓Kf) and enhances TGF sensitivity.

### Urological relevance
- **Renovascular hypertension** (Goldblatt 1934, clip model): atherosclerotic (ostial, older men) or fibromuscular dysplasia (young women, "string of beads"). Diagnosis: Duplex, CTA/MRA, captopril renography; **renal vein renin ratio >1.5** (affected:contralateral) with suppression of contralateral predicts response to revascularisation/nephrectomy.
- **Page kidney** – perirenal compression (subcapsular haematoma post-trauma/biopsy/ESWL) → renal ischaemia → renin-mediated hypertension (Page 1939 cellophane model). Treat: drainage/decortication, ACEi, nephrectomy if refractory.
- **Juxtaglomerular cell tumour (reninoma)** (Robertson 1967): young adults, severe hypertension, **hypokalaemia, secondary hyperaldosteronism**, small cortical mass; CD34⁺/renin⁺; **partial nephrectomy curative**.
- **Obstruction:** Ang II mediates the late afferent vasoconstriction (phase 3 of UUO) and tubulo-interstitial fibrosis; ACEi/ARB attenuate fibrosis experimentally.
- Other: segmental hypoplasia (Ask-Upmark), reflux nephropathy, Wilms tumour (renin secretion), post-transplant renal artery stenosis, RCC paraneoplastic hypertension, adrenal (Conn's – aldosterone:renin ratio).
- **Drugs:** ACEi (cough, angio-oedema via bradykinin), ARBs, **direct renin inhibitor (aliskiren)**, mineralocorticoid antagonists; all contraindicated in pregnancy (fetal renal dysgenesis).

**Diagram to draw:** JG apparatus (afferent arteriole with JG cells, macula densa, efferent arteriole) + RAS cascade flowchart.

### Recent advances
- Non-steroidal MRA **finerenone** (renal protection in diabetic CKD); aldosterone synthase inhibitors (baxdrostat) in resistant hypertension trials; angiotensinogen-silencing RNA (zilebesiran) in trials; renal denervation (FDA approval 2023) for resistant hypertension.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Renin = rate-limiting; JG cells of afferent arteriole; stimuli: ↓pressure, ↓macula densa NaCl, β1.
> - Ang II: efferent constriction maintains GFR → ACEi danger in bilateral RAS.
> - AT1 = pressor/fibrotic; AT2/ACE2-Ang(1-7) = protective.
> - Renal vein renin ratio >1.5 lateralises; reninoma → HTN + hypokalaemia, partial nephrectomy cures.

## I-3d. Patho-physiology of Brain stem death
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks) | ~550 words

### Definition
- **Irreversible loss of capacity for consciousness combined with irreversible loss of capacity to breathe**, due to permanent cessation of brainstem function – legally equivalent to death (India: THOA). The heart continues only with ventilatory support. Main source of **deceased donor (DBD)** kidneys.

### Pathophysiology
```
Primary brain injury (TBI, SAH, ICH, hypoxic injury)
  → cerebral oedema → ↑ICP (Monro–Kellie) → ↓CPP (MAP – ICP) → ischaemia
  → rostro-caudal herniation ("coning" – uncal/tonsillar)
  → Pontine ischaemia: CUSHING REFLEX (hypertension, bradycardia, irregular breathing)
  → Medullary ischaemia: AUTONOMIC (catecholamine) STORM
       - severe hypertension, tachyarrhythmia, ↑SVR
       - myocardial contraction-band necrosis, neurogenic pulmonary oedema
       - organ ischaemia (incl. kidneys)
  → Loss of sympathetic outflow (spinal cord) → vasodilatation,
       HYPOTENSION, ↓cardiac output
```
**Systemic consequences after brainstem death**
| System | Change | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Cardiovascular | Hypotension, arrhythmias, LV dysfunction | Loss of vasomotor tone, catecholamine injury, hypovolaemia |
| Endocrine – posterior pituitary | **Diabetes insipidus** (majority) – polyuria, hypernatraemia | ↓ADH |
| Anterior pituitary/thyroid/adrenal | ↓T3 (sick euthyroid), relative adrenal insufficiency | Hypothalamic–pituitary failure |
| Pancreas | Hyperglycaemia | Insulin resistance, catecholamines, steroids |
| Thermoregulation | **Hypothermia** (poikilothermia) | Hypothalamic loss + vasodilatation |
| Haematology | **Coagulopathy/DIC** | Release of tissue thromboplastin from necrotic brain |
| Respiratory | Pulmonary oedema, ARDS | Neurogenic + inflammation |
| Immune | **Systemic inflammatory response** (cytokines, complement) | ↑Graft immunogenicity → higher DGF and acute rejection in DBD vs living donor kidneys |

### Diagnosis (clinical)
**1. Preconditions**
- Apnoeic coma on ventilator; **known, irreversible structural cause**.

**2. Exclusions**
- Depressant/sedative drugs, neuromuscular blockers (check train-of-four), **hypothermia** (core temp <35 °C), severe metabolic/endocrine disturbance (Na, glucose, uraemia, hepatic), circulatory shock.

**3. Brainstem reflexes (all absent)**
| Test | Cranial nerves |
|---|---|
| Pupils fixed, no response to light | II, III |
| Corneal reflex absent | V, VII |
| Oculovestibular (caloric – ~50 mL ice-cold water each ear, tympanic membrane visible; no eye movement) | VIII, III, VI |
| Oculocephalic (doll's eye) absent | VIII, III, VI |
| No motor response in cranial nerve distribution to central pain | V, VII |
| Gag and cough (tracheal suction) absent | IX, X |

**4. Apnoea test**
- Pre-oxygenate with 100% O₂; baseline PaCO₂ normal; disconnect ventilator with O₂ insufflation via tracheal catheter; observe for respiratory effort until **PaCO₂ ≥60 mmHg** (and pH <7.30) – no breathing = positive. Abort if hypotension/desaturation/arrhythmia.
- Ancillary tests (EEG, 4-vessel angiography, transcranial Doppler, nuclear perfusion) – not mandatory in India/UK; used when clinical tests cannot be completed.

### Indian legal framework
- **Transplantation of Human Organs Act (THOA), 1994** – recognised brainstem death; **amended 2011** (Transplantation of Human Organs and Tissues Act); **THOTA Rules 2014**.
- Certified by a **Board of Medical Experts (4 members)**: (i) registered medical practitioner in charge of the hospital, (ii) an independent registered medical practitioner/specialist nominated from the approved panel, (iii) a neurologist or neurosurgeon from the panel (if unavailable – anaesthetist/intensivist/physician/surgeon nominated), (iv) the doctor treating the patient.
- **Two sets of tests, at least 6 hours apart** (Form 10); transplant team members must not be on the board; **mandatory request** to families of brainstem-dead patients in ICU.

### Donor management (kidney-focussed)
- **"Rule of 100s":** SBP >100 mmHg, urine output >100 mL/h, PaO₂ >100 mmHg, Hb >100 g/L.
- Volume (avoid over-hydration), vasopressors – **vasopressin** preferred (treats DI, spares catecholamines), noradrenaline; **desmopressin** for DI; hormone resuscitation (methylprednisolone, thyroid hormone, insulin) in unstable donors; correct Na⁺ (hypernatraemia linked to graft dysfunction in liver), warm to normothermia, lung-protective ventilation.
- Trials: low-dose **dopamine** donor pretreatment reduced dialysis need after transplant (Schnuelle, JAMA 2009); **mild donor hypothermia** reduced DGF (Niemann, NEJM 2015).

**Diagram to draw:** Herniation sequence with ICP curve; flowchart: storm → sympathetic loss → DI/hypothermia/DIC.

### Recent advances
- Machine perfusion (hypothermic) of DBD/ECD kidneys reduces DGF; normothermic regional perfusion in DCD; Indian NOTTO national registry and "One Nation One Policy" allocation reforms.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Sequence: ↑ICP → Cushing reflex → autonomic storm → loss of sympathetic tone (hypotension), DI, hypothermia, DIC.
> - Exclude drugs, hypothermia (<35 °C), metabolic causes before testing.
> - Apnoea test: PaCO₂ ≥60 mmHg with no respiratory effort.
> - India: THOA 1994 (2011 amendment), Rules 2014 – 4-member board, two tests ≥6 h apart.
> - Rule of 100s; vasopressin for DI/hypotension.

## I-3e. Male reproductive immunology
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks) | ~550 words

### Introduction
- Spermatozoa appear at puberty – long after central immune tolerance is established – so meiotic/post-meiotic germ cell antigens are "**foreign**" ("autoantigenic"). Testis is an **immune-privileged site** (tolerates allografts). Campbell: Male reproductive physiology; Male infertility chapters.

### Mechanisms of immune privilege
**1. Blood–testis barrier (BTB)**
- Formed by **tight junctions between adjacent Sertoli cells** (claudin-11, occludin, ZO-1) plus adherens and gap junctions; established at **puberty**.
- Divides seminiferous epithelium into **basal compartment** (spermatogonia, preleptotene spermatocytes – exposed to blood) and **adluminal compartment** (later spermatocytes, spermatids – sequestered).
- Dynamically opens/closes to let preleptotene spermatocytes migrate (stage VIII).
- **Weak points:** rete testis and efferent ducts/epididymis – barrier less tight; common site of antigen leak.

**2. Active immunoregulation (barrier alone is insufficient)**
- **Testicular macrophages** – predominantly M2-like, produce IL-10, low pro-inflammatory output.
- **Regulatory T cells (Tregs)** and tolerogenic dendritic cells; peripheral tolerance to some meiotic antigens that are exposed.
- **Sertoli cell** products – TGF-β, activin, protease inhibitors, complement inhibitors; **Leydig cell androgens** are immunosuppressive.
- Indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase (IDO), galectin-1, PD-L1 expression.
- **Fas ligand** on Sertoli cells inducing apoptosis of activated T cells – proposed but **debated/controversial**.
- Seminal plasma immunosuppressive factors (prostaglandin E, TGF-β) protect sperm in female tract.

### Antisperm antibodies (ASA)
**Causes (breach of BTB/excurrent duct)**
- **Vasectomy** (most common – ASA in majority of men afterward; relevant to vasectomy reversal outcomes), obstruction (CBAVD, epididymal), **testicular trauma, torsion**, testicular biopsy/surgery, **infection** (epididymo-orchitis, prostatitis, STI), cryptorchidism, possibly varicocele.
- IgG (serum + seminal) and IgA (local, more clinically significant in semen); also in female partner.

**Effects**
- Sperm **agglutination** (head–head, tail–tail), impaired motility and **cervical mucus penetration ("shaking phenomenon")**, impaired capacitation/acrosome reaction, **zona binding and fusion** failure, complement-mediated immobilisation.
- **Head-directed** antibodies more significant than tail-tip binding.

**Testing (direct tests on sperm)**
| Test | Principle |
|---|---|
| **Mixed antiglobulin reaction (MAR)** | Fresh semen + IgG/IgA-coated latex beads/RBCs + antiglobulin → mixed agglutinates with motile sperm |
| **Immunobead test (IBT)** | Washed sperm + beads coated with anti-human IgG/IgA/IgM; reports % and location |
| Indirect | Detect ASA in serum/seminal plasma/cervical mucus using donor sperm |
- **WHO 2010 manual: ≥50% motile spermatozoa with bound particles** = clinically significant (immunological infertility); <50% = reference limit.
- Indications: isolated asthenozoospermia, agglutination on semen analysis, unexplained infertility, post-trauma/reversal.

**Treatment**
| Option | Comment |
|---|---|
| Corticosteroids (e.g. prednisolone) | Inconsistent benefit; risk of **avascular necrosis of femoral head**, GI bleed – generally not recommended |
| Sperm washing + **IUI** | Bypasses cervical mucus; modest benefit |
| **IVF/ICSI** | **ICSI preferred** – fertilisation largely unaffected by ASA (EAU male infertility) |
| Treat cause | Treat infection; microsurgical reconstruction |

### Inflammatory states
- **Leukocytospermia:** **>1 × 10⁶ peroxidase-positive leukocytes/mL** (WHO) – reactive oxygen species → DNA fragmentation, lipid peroxidation of sperm membrane; evaluate for male accessory gland infection (semen culture, STI PCR); antibiotics if infection; antioxidant evidence limited.
- **Orchitis:**
  - Viral – **mumps** (post-pubertal; unilateral mostly; atrophy, subfertility), others incl. SARS-CoV-2 (orchitis/ACE2 expression – evolving data).
  - Bacterial epididymo-orchitis – *C. trachomatis/N. gonorrhoeae* (<35 y), *E. coli* (older/urinary).
  - Granulomatous (TB, idiopathic).
- **Autoimmune orchitis:** **primary** (isolated, ASA + azoospermia, no systemic disease) vs **secondary** (systemic vasculitis – polyarteritis nodosa, Behçet, Henoch–Schönlein). Experimental autoimmune orchitis model (immunisation with testicular homogenate) shows lymphocytic infiltration and aspermatogenesis.
- Testicular cancer risk link: chronic inflammation in GCNIS-related lesions (research).

**Diagram to draw:** Cross-section of seminiferous tubule showing Sertoli tight junctions dividing basal/adluminal compartments, with Leydig cells/macrophages in interstitium.

### Recent advances
- Sperm DNA fragmentation testing (TUNEL, SCSA) in men with leukocytospermia/ASA; testicular sperm for ICSI in high DFI.
- Immune checkpoint inhibitor–associated orchitis and hypogonadism; post-COVID orchitis data.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - BTB = Sertoli–Sertoli tight junctions; basal vs adluminal compartments; formed at puberty.
> - Privilege also active: M2 macrophages, Tregs, TGF-β, androgens; FasL role debated.
> - ASA: vasectomy commonest; MAR/IBT ≥50% motile sperm bound = significant (WHO).
> - Treatment: ICSI preferred; steroids out of favour (avascular necrosis).
> - Leukocytospermia >1 million/mL.

## I-3f. Principles of antimicrobial therapy in UTI
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks) | ~600 words

### Classification (EAU Urological Infections 2024)
- **Uncomplicated** – acute, sporadic/recurrent cystitis or pyelonephritis in non-pregnant women without anatomical/functional abnormality or comorbidity.
- **Complicated** – men, pregnancy, anatomical/functional abnormality, catheter, renal disease, immunosuppression (diabetes, transplant).
- **Recurrent** – **≥2 in 6 months or ≥3 in 12 months**.
- **Catheter-associated UTI (CA-UTI)**; **urosepsis** (Sepsis-3: life-threatening organ dysfunction, qSOFA).

### Principles
- **1. Confirm diagnosis, culture** (except simple cystitis in women).
- **2. PK/PD:**

| Pattern | Drugs | Dosing goal |
|---|---|---|
| **Time-dependent** (T > MIC) | β-lactams, carbapenems | Frequent dosing/extended infusion |
| **Concentration-dependent** (Cmax/MIC, AUC/MIC) | **Aminoglycosides, fluoroquinolones** | High once-daily dose; post-antibiotic effect |

- **Urinary vs tissue levels:** nitrofurantoin/fosfomycin – high urinary, **low tissue levels → cystitis only**, never pyelonephritis/prostatitis/urosepsis. FQ, TMP-SMX penetrate prostate; β-lactams poorly.
- **Renal function:** adjust doses; avoid nitrofurantoin at eGFR <30 mL/min.
- **3. Local resistance data** – use empirical agent only if local resistance **<20%** for cystitis (TMP-SMX, cephalosporins); FQ for pyelonephritis only if resistance **<10%**.
- **4. Collateral damage** – avoid FQs/cephalosporins for uncomplicated cystitis (ESBL, *C. difficile*; EMA FQ restrictions – tendinopathy, aortic aneurysm, neuropathy).
- **5. Shortest effective duration**, de-escalate to culture, IV → oral switch, **source control** (stent/PCN for obstructed infected kidney).
- **6. Pregnancy:** avoid FQ, tetracyclines; TMP in 1st trimester; nitrofurantoin near term.
- **7. Stewardship** – right drug, dose, route, duration; "start smart then focus"; don't treat ASB; local antibiograms.

### Empirical choices (EAU 2024 / IDSA)
| Scenario | First-line | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| **Uncomplicated cystitis (women)** | **Fosfomycin trometamol 3 g single dose**; **nitrofurantoin** (macrocrystal 50–100 mg qid, or monohydrate/macrocrystal 100 mg bd); **pivmecillinam 400 mg tds** | Fosfomycin 1 day; nitrofurantoin **5 d**; pivmecillinam **3–5 d** |
| Alternatives (if local resistance <20%) | TMP-SMX 160/800 mg bd; trimethoprim 200 mg bd; cefadroxil 500 mg bd | TMP-SMX 3 d; TMP 5 d; cefadroxil 3 d |
| **Cystitis in men** | TMP-SMX or FQ per culture | **7 d** |
| **Uncomplicated pyelonephritis (oral)** | **Ciprofloxacin 500–750 mg bd** (7 d) or **levofloxacin 750 mg od (5 d)**; alternatives: cefpodoxime 200 mg bd/ceftibuten 400 mg od (10 d), TMP-SMX (14 d) | 5–14 d |
| **Pyelonephritis (IV)** | Ceftriaxone 1–2 g od, cefotaxime, ciprofloxacin/levofloxacin, gentamicin 5 mg/kg od, amikacin 15 mg/kg od; carbapenems/new BL-BLI only for proven/suspected MDR | Switch to oral when afebrile |
| **Complicated UTI** | Amoxicillin + aminoglycoside, or 2nd-gen cephalosporin + aminoglycoside, or IV 3rd-gen cephalosporin; FQ only if low resistance & not recently used | **7–14 d** (14 d in men when prostatitis not excluded) |
| **CA-UTI** | Change catheter if in >2 weeks before culture | **7 d** (prompt response), 10–14 d (delayed) |
| **Urosepsis** | Broad-spectrum IV within 1 h + source control | Per response |

### Asymptomatic bacteriuria (ASB)
- **Treat only in pregnancy** and **before urological procedures breaching the mucosa** (e.g. TURP/TURBT, ureteroscopy).
- **Do not treat:** healthy women, elderly, diabetics, catheterised, spinal cord injury, renal transplant (>2 months).

### Prophylaxis
- **Recurrent UTI** (after behavioural advice and non-antimicrobial measures): vaginal oestrogen (postmenopausal), **immunoactive OM-89 (Uro-Vaxom)**, D-mannose, cranberry, ↑fluid intake, **methenamine hippurate** (ALTAR RCT, BMJ 2022 – non-inferior to antibiotic prophylaxis).
- Continuous low-dose (6–12 months): nitrofurantoin 50–100 mg od, fosfomycin 3 g every 10 days, trimethoprim 100 mg od, cephalexin 125–250 mg od; or **post-coital**; self-start therapy for compliant patients.
- **Peri-operative:** single dose/≤24 h – TURP, TURBT (large/high-risk), URS, PCNL; prefer **transperineal prostate biopsy** to reduce infection; no prophylaxis for uncomplicated cystoscopy/urodynamics.

### Recent advances
- **Pivmecillinam** FDA-approved for uUTI (2024); **sulopenem etzadroxil/probenecid** (oral penem) FDA-approved for uUTI in women with limited options (2024); **gepotidacin** (first-in-class triazaacenaphthylene; EAGLE-2/3 trials) FDA-approved for uUTI (2025).
- cUTI agents: ceftazidime-avibactam, meropenem-vaborbactam, ceftolozane-tazobactam, **cefiderocol**, plazomicin, imipenem-relebactam; **cefepime-taniborbactam** (CERTAIN-1 trial in cUTI) – regulatory review ongoing.
- Vaccines (MV140 sublingual) and phage therapy under evaluation.

**Diagram to draw:** PK/PD curve showing Cmax/MIC, AUC/MIC and T>MIC.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Recurrent UTI = ≥2/6 months or ≥3/12 months.
> - First-line cystitis: fosfomycin 3 g single, nitrofurantoin 5 d, pivmecillinam 3–5 d; TMP-SMX 3 d only if resistance <20%.
> - Pyelonephritis: cipro 7 d / levo 750 mg 5 d (if FQ resistance <10%); nitrofurantoin/fosfomycin never for pyelonephritis.
> - ASB: treat only in pregnancy and before mucosa-breaching urological surgery.
> - Aminoglycosides/FQ concentration-dependent; β-lactams time-dependent.

#### I-1. Describe in details basic patho-physiology of urine production and transport (LAQ)

- RBF ~20–25% CO; GFR 125 mL/min; FF 20%; autoregulation 80–180 mmHg (myogenic + TGF).
- PCT 65%, TAL 25% (water-impermeable diluting segment), DCT 5–7%, CD fine-tunes via aldosterone/ADH (V2 → cAMP → AQP2).

- High flow → bolus coalescence → dilatation without obstruction.
- UUO triphasic: ↑/↑, ↓/↑, ↓/↓ (RBF/pressure).

#### I-2. Describe female pelvic anatomy in relation to prolapse and SUI (LAQ)

- DeLancey levels: I suspension (cardinal–uterosacral), II attachment (ATFP), III fusion (perineal membrane/body).
- Hammock (DeLancey 1994) vs Integral theory (Petros & Ulmsten 1990 → TVT 1996, mid-urethra).
- Central (rugae lost) vs paravaginal (rugae preserved) cystocele.
- POP-Q: hymen = 0; Stage II = ±1 cm of hymen; Stage IV ≥ tvl–2.
- FDA 2019 – transvaginal mesh for anterior POP withdrawn; MUS and sacrocolpopexy mesh unaffected.

#### I-3a. Newer generation CT scan (SAQ)

#### I-3c. Renin-angiotensin system (SAQ)

- Renin = rate-limiting; JG cells of afferent arteriole; stimuli: ↓pressure, ↓macula densa NaCl, β1.
- Ang II: efferent constriction maintains GFR → ACEi danger in bilateral RAS.
- AT1 = pressor/fibrotic; AT2/ACE2-Ang(1-7) = protective.
- Renal vein renin ratio >1.5 lateralises; reninoma → HTN + hypokalaemia, partial nephrectomy cures.

#### I-3d. Patho-physiology of Brain stem death (SAQ)

- Sequence: ↑ICP → Cushing reflex → autonomic storm → loss of sympathetic tone (hypotension), DI, hypothermia, DIC.
- Exclude drugs, hypothermia (<35 °C), metabolic causes before testing.
- Apnoea test: PaCO₂ ≥60 mmHg with no respiratory effort.
- India: THOA 1994 (2011 amendment), Rules 2014 – 4-member board, two tests ≥6 h apart.
- Rule of 100s; vasopressin for DI/hypotension.

#### I-3e. Male reproductive immunology (SAQ)

- BTB = Sertoli–Sertoli tight junctions; basal vs adluminal compartments; formed at puberty.
- Privilege also active: M2 macrophages, Tregs, TGF-β, androgens; FasL role debated.
- ASA: vasectomy commonest; MAR/IBT ≥50% motile sperm bound = significant (WHO).
- Treatment: ICSI preferred; steroids out of favour (avascular necrosis).
- Leukocytospermia >1 million/mL.

#### I-3f. Principles of antimicrobial therapy in UTI (SAQ)

- Recurrent UTI = ≥2/6 months or ≥3/12 months.
- First-line cystitis: fosfomycin 3 g single, nitrofurantoin 5 d, pivmecillinam 3–5 d; TMP-SMX 3 d only if resistance <20%.
- Pyelonephritis: cipro 7 d / levo 750 mg 5 d (if FQ resistance <10%); nitrofurantoin/fosfomycin never for pyelonephritis.
- ASB: treat only in pregnancy and before mucosa-breaching urological surgery.
- Aminoglycosides/FQ concentration-dependent; β-lactams time-dependent.
