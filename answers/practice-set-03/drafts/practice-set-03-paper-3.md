## Q1. Describe in brief urological complications of OBGY surgery. Describe in detail, management of urethrovaginal fistula
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)
(urethrovaginal fistula also answered in 2025-Winter Paper 3, Q3a; iatrogenic ureteric injury in Practice Set 2 Paper 3, Q1)

*Assumed meaning: OBGY = obstetric and gynaecological surgery.*

### Part A: Urological complications of OBGY surgery
- The bladder, distal ureter and urethra lie against the cervix, uterine vessels and anterior vaginal wall. Risk rises with distorted anatomy: fibroids, endometriosis, cancer, previous caesarean, radiation, heavy bleeding, prolapse or mesh surgery.

| Complication | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Bladder injury** (commonest organ injured) | Caesarean 0.27–0.3%; hysterectomy about 0.3–0.75% (laparoscopic highest); retropubic tape 3–9%. Repair: debride, 2-layer closure, fill test, catheter 7–14 days; check ureters if trigonal |
| **Ureteric injury** | Gynaecology causes over half of iatrogenic injuries; over 90% distal third. Hysterectomy: vaginal 0.02–0.5%, abdominal 0.03–2%, minimally invasive 0.13–6%; prolapse surgery 1.7–4.3%. **Ligation** is the commonest gynaecological mechanism |
| **Fistulae** | VVF (vesicovaginal fistula), about 0.8 per 1000 hysterectomies; UVF (ureterovaginal fistula); vesicouterine fistula after caesarean (Youssef syndrome); urethrovaginal fistula |
| **Voiding dysfunction** | Radical hysterectomy (pelvic plexus injury) 16–80% historically: retention, poor compliance; CIC (clean intermittent catheterisation) and wait. Post-partum or post-epidural overdistension |
| **Mesh and sling** | Exposure, erosion into bladder or urethra, outlet obstruction, pain, dyspareunia |
| **Radiation** | Radiation cystitis; VVF in 0.6–2% after cervical cancer radiotherapy; ureteric stricture |

- **Ureteric danger sites:** pelvic brim (infundibulopelvic ligament), under the uterine artery, beside the uterosacral ligament, anterolateral vaginal fornix.
- **Prevention and recognition:** haemostasis and anatomy; prophylactic stents only selectively (they aid recognition but do not prevent injury); cystoscopy with dye (IV indigo carmine or oral phenazopyridine) after minimally invasive hysterectomy and prolapse or continence surgery.
- Only about one-third of ureteric injuries are recognised at open surgery. Missed injury presents with fever, ileus, flank pain, urinoma or leakage; do CTU (computed tomography urography) with delayed films, then retrograde stenting. Distal repair: ureteroneocystostomy ± psoas hitch or Boari flap (see Q3d).

### Part B: Urethrovaginal fistula
**Aetiology**
- Developing world: **obstructed labour**, often with a VVF.
- Developed world: **urethral diverticulectomy** (fistula in 1–8%; causes 20–25% of urethrovaginal fistulae), slings, bulking agents, anterior repair or mesh, indwelling catheter pressure necrosis, pessaries, radiation, trauma.

**Clinical features**
- Depend on position relative to the sphincter. **Distal** fistula: often asymptomatic, split stream, vaginal voiding, post-void dribble. **Proximal** (bladder neck): continuous leakage or SUI (stress urinary incontinence).
- Dyspareunia, recurrent UTI (urinary tract infection), perineal irritation; partner discomfort with exposed mesh.

**Evaluation**
- **Speculum examination** usually suffices; methylene blue test if rugae hide it (distal stain on tampon).
- **VCUG (voiding cystourethrogram), lateral view;** VUDS (videourodynamics) for coexisting SUI or detrusor overactivity.
- **Cystourethroscopy** (flexible is better, outflow at the lens): size, site, bladder neck, mesh. **About 20% have a separate VVF.** Image the upper tract.
- Goh location: type 1 >3.5 cm, 2: 2.5–3.5, 3: 1.5–2.5, 4: <1.5 cm from meatus. Closer means worse continence.
- Optimise: treat UTI, vaginal oestrogen, nutrition; biopsy if previous cancer.

### Management
- Asymptomatic distal fistula: may need no repair. Timing: early if tissue healthy; otherwise after inflammation settles.
- **Standard: transvaginal rotational-flap repair:**

| Step | Details |
|---|---|
| 1. Exposure | Lithotomy (prone gives better anterior view); cystourethroscopy, cannulate tract; suprapubic catheter (Lowsley tractor), urethral catheter |
| 2. Incision | Hydrodissect; circumscribe tract with 1–2 mm cuff, minimal excision; inverted **J or U** incision |
| 3. Flaps | Raise vaginal flap; mobilise periurethral tissue to bladder neck; open retropubic space if sling planned |
| 4. Closure | Over a urethral sound: **4-0 absorbable**, transverse or longitudinal; **second layer perpendicular**; buccal graft or flap onlay for large defects; leak test |
| 5. Interposition | **Martius flap** (labial fat pad, external pudendal pedicle) most often; omentum, gracilis, rectus fascia occasionally |
| 6. Cover | Advance vaginal flap beyond closure (2-0) |

- **Autologous pubovaginal sling** for SUI or bladder-neck involvement; **never synthetic** (erodes ischaemic urethra). Remove all mesh in the field.
- **Near-total urethral loss:** tubularised anterior bladder flap neourethra. Irreparable cases: diversion.
- **After repair:** urethral catheter out at 2–3 days; suprapubic ≥14 days; lateral VCUG before removal; nothing per vagina for 6 weeks.

### Outcomes and complications
- Closure **90–95%**, but continence only **50–80%**; postoperative SUI up to half. Sling then or staged later.
- Urethral stricture, recurrence, dyspareunia.

**Diagram to draw:** inverted-U vaginal flap, two perpendicular urethral layers, Martius flap.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Bladder is the commonest organ injured; ureteric injury is mostly distal and missed.
> - Diverticulectomy is the commonest Western cause; 20% have a coexisting VVF.
> - Layered closure, Martius flap, autologous sling only.
> - Closure 90–95%; continence only 50–80%.

## Q2. Discuss management options of VVF
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)
(timing in 2021-Summer Paper 2, Q3b; recurrent VVF in 2025-Winter Paper 2, Q3f)

### Introduction
- VVF (vesicovaginal fistula): epithelialised bladder–vagina tract; commonest acquired urinary fistula (>75% of genitourinary fistulae).
- **Developing world:** obstetric (>90%); fetal head impacted for days causes pressure necrosis. **Developed world:** iatrogenic (>75% after hysterectomy), usually supratrigonal at the vaginal cuff. Also radiation, malignancy, mesh, foreign body.
- Mechanism: ischaemic necrosis; continuous urine flow keeps the tract open until it epithelialises.

### Classification
| Goh (distance from meatus) | Size | Tissue |
|---|---|---|
| 1: >3.5 cm; 2: 2.5–3.5; 3: 1.5–2.5; 4: <1.5 cm | A <1.5 cm; B 1.5–3 cm; C >3 cm | i mild fibrosis; ii moderate/severe; iii radiation, ureteric, circumferential, previous repair |
- **Also know:** Waaldijk (closing mechanism involvement); WHO simple vs complex.

### Evaluation (guides choice)
- Speculum; fluid creatinine. **Dye tests:** single (blue backfill, tampon); **double** (oral phenazopyridine plus blue): orange = UVF (ureterovaginal fistula), blue = VVF.
- **Cystoscopy and vaginoscopy:** number, size, ureteric orifices, bladder neck, mesh; **biopsy if previous cancer.**
- **Upper tract (CTU, computed tomography urography) is mandatory:** coexisting ureteric fistula (up to 12–25%).
- Urodynamics: capacity, compliance, SUI (stress urinary incontinence) in 10–15%.

### Principles
- Optimise nutrition, treat infection, drain continuously, relieve obstruction, exclude malignancy.
- Surgery: tension-free, multilayer, **non-overlapping**, watertight closure; remove foreign material; consider interposition; drain afterwards. **The first repair has the best chance.**

### Option 1: Conservative and endoscopic
- **Catheter drainage** cures about 15%. Favourable: <1 cm (best <3 mm), started within **3 weeks**, non-irradiated. Unlikely after 6 weeks (epithelialised). EAU (European Association of Urology): if tried, ≥12 weeks.
- **Fulguration** plus 2 weeks' drainage for tracts <3.5 mm.
- **Sealants** (fibrin glue, cyanoacrylate, collagen): tiny series; not recommended by EAU.

### Timing
- Clean iatrogenic: early repair (EAU: no minimum delay if no infection or recent radiation). Obstetric with necrosis: about 3 months. Radiation: **≥12 months.** Some avoid 4–12 weeks.

### Option 2: Surgical route
| Vaginal | Abdominal (open, laparoscopic, robotic) |
|---|---|
| Low, trigonal, accessible apex | Supratrigonal, narrow vagina, no descent |
| Ureters distant | Ureter involved, reimplant needed |
| Less pain, blood loss, stay | Augmentation, Tanagho flap, diversion possible |
| Risk: vaginal shortening | Large (≥4 cm primary, ≥2 cm recurrent), radiation |
- First-attempt closure about **90% either route**; choose the route the surgeon knows best.

**Vaginal techniques**
- **Flap-splitting (Raz):** ureteric stents if within 1 cm; Foley through tract for traction; inverted J/U incision; raise flaps; layer 1 3-0/4-0 including tract edge; layer 2 perpendicular; fill test 200–300 mL; Martius or peritoneal flap; advance opposite flap ≥2 cm beyond.
- **Latzko (high partial colpocleisis):** denude vagina 1–2 cm around tract and close vaginal walls over it; bladder not opened. ≥90% in post-hysterectomy and radiation VVF near ureters; vaginal shortening.
- **Also know:** vaginal cuff excision technique.

**Abdominal techniques**
- **O'Conor (1951):** extraperitoneal; bivalve bladder from dome down to fistula; excise tract; separate bladder from vagina 2–3 cm beyond; close vagina; **omental or peritoneal flap**; close bladder; suprapubic catheter.
- **Transvesical** (limited cystotomy) and **extravesical** (no cystotomy; fewer LUTS).
- **Laparoscopic (1994) and robotic (2005):** same closure (85–100%) with less blood loss and shorter stay; vaginal stent, backfill, ICG (indocyanine green) perfusion check.

### Interposition flaps (for radiation, recurrent, ischaemic or large VVF)
| Flap | Supply | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Martius** (labial fat pad) | Internal and external pudendal | Mid–distal VVF; not apex |
| Peritoneal (vaginal) | Pouch of Douglas fold | Apical post-hysterectomy |
| **Omentum (J-flap)** | Gastroepiploic arteries | Abdominal; radiation, large |
| Gracilis, Singapore, rectus | Myocutaneous | Refractory, tissue loss |
- RCT: Martius added nothing in simple VVF, with donor-site pain in nearly a third.

### Postoperative care
- Catheter ≥10–14 days (urethral and/or suprapubic); antimuscarinic or β3-agonist for spasm; cystogram before removal; stents out at 4–6 weeks.

### Option 3: Urinary diversion
- Multiple failures, severe radiation, massive tissue loss: conduit or continent reservoir. Unfit: ureteric occlusion with nephrostomies. Resource-poor: ureterosigmoidostomy as last resort.

### Outcomes
- Residual urgency or incontinence despite closure (Goh 3–4 predicts); retention (vertical bladder closure); dyspareunia. ICS (International Continence Society): pelvic floor physiotherapy.

**Diagram to draw:** flap-splitting repair: perpendicular bladder layers, Martius flap, advanced vaginal flap.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Image upper tracts; exclude ureteric fistula and cancer.
> - Catheter trial only if small, fresh (<3 weeks), non-irradiated.
> - Vaginal and abdominal routes both close about 90%.
> - Interposition for complex VVF; radiation repair after ≥12 months.

## Q3a. Discuss diagnostics and operative pediatric laparoscopy in urology
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Evolution
- Diagnostic (non-palpable testis, 1976) → nephrectomy (1994) → pyeloplasty (1995) → robotic (2002).

### Diagnostic laparoscopy
- **Non-palpable testis** (procedure of choice, after examination under anaesthesia): note vessels, vas, testis and internal ring.

| Finding | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Blind-ending vessels and vas, closed ring | Vanishing testis: stop |
| Vessels and vas entering ring | Explore groin; excise remnant |
| Intra-abdominal testis | Orchidopexy (primary or Fowler-Stephens) |
- **DSD (differences of sex development):** gonadal inspection and biopsy, Müllerian structures.

### Operative laparoscopy
- **Testis:** primary orchidopexy (>90% success); Fowler-Stephens one- or two-stage; gonadectomy in DSD.
- **Kidney:** nephrectomy, heminephrectomy, **pyeloplasty** (commonest MIS (minimally invasive surgery) procedure).
- **Ureter and bladder:** extravesical or vesicoscopic (cross-trigonal) reimplantation, Mitrofanoff, augmentation, bladder neck reconstruction, utricle and urogenital sinus.
- **Also know:** RPLND (retroperitoneal lymph node dissection) for paratesticular rhabdomyosarcoma.

### Technical principles
- **Access:** open Hasson or Veress at umbilicus (Palmer's point if scarred); equal safety.
- 3–5 mm ports; robots use 8 mm. Transperitoneal (most space), retroperitoneal (spares peritoneum, useful in peritoneal dialysis), vesicoscopic.
- **Pressures:** CO₂ absorption, reduced venous return, raised airway pressure; reversible oliguria (no fluid bolus).

| Age | 0–1 month | 2–12 months | 1–2 years | 2–8 years |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Max IAP (intra-abdominal pressure) | ≤6 mmHg | ≤8 | ≤10 | ≤12 |
- **Only absolute contraindication:** inability to tolerate pneumoperitoneum.

### Complications
- Right common iliac vessel injury (behind umbilicus; commonest major), inferior epigastric (commonest minor), bowel injury, subcutaneous emphysema, **port-site hernia 0.5–3%** (close fascia).

### Pros and cons
- Shorter stay, better cosmesis, deep pelvic access; pain benefit unproven. Robot: cost, learning curve; infant working space only about 1 L.

**Diagram to draw:** laparoscopic view of internal ring with blind-ending vessels.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Diagnostic laparoscopy is the procedure of choice for non-palpable testis.
> - Pressure ceiling 6–12 mmHg by age.
> - Right common iliac at Veress entry; close port fascia.

## Q3b. Tadalafil
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Pharmacology
- Selective **PDE5 (phosphodiesterase type 5) inhibitor**; FDA (Food and Drug Administration) approved 2003.
- **Mechanism:** sexual stimulation releases NO (nitric oxide), raising cGMP (cyclic guanosine monophosphate), which relaxes cavernosal smooth muscle. PDE5 breaks down cGMP; tadalafil blocks this. It **augments, not initiates,** erection.
- Structurally distinct from sildenafil; cross-reacts with **PDE11**, little with PDE6 (retina).

| Property | Tadalafil (vs sildenafil) |
|---|---|
| Time to peak | 2 h (0.8 h) |
| Half-life | **17.5 h** (3–5 h) |
| Fatty food | No effect (reduces absorption) |
| Window | Widest (up to 36 h) |

### Uses and doses
1. **ED (erectile dysfunction):** 10–20 mg on demand, or 2.5–5 mg once daily. Intercourse success about 70%; 40–50% in diabetes and after prostatectomy.
2. **BPH-LUTS (lower urinary tract symptoms), with or without ED:** **5 mg daily**, the only PDE5I licensed. IPSS (International Prostate Symptom Score) falls about 6 points (placebo 3.6) **without Qmax change**; mechanism is NO–cGMP relaxation, perfusion and reduced afferent activity, not de-obstruction. Equal to tamsulosin for IPSS. EAU 2022 and AUA 2021: option for moderate–severe LUTS.
3. **Also know:** fixed finasteride–tadalafil (FDA 2021); penile rehabilitation (benefit unproven); prevention of stuttering priapism (PDE5I paradox); pulmonary arterial hypertension.

### Adverse effects
- Headache, dyspepsia, **back pain and myalgia** (characteristic), flushing, nasal congestion. Visual effects rare.
- NAION (non-arteritic anterior ischaemic optic neuropathy): no proven link; stop if sudden visual loss.

### Contraindications and interactions
- **Nitrates in any form: absolute.** After tadalafil, no nitrates for **48 h** (24 h for sildenafil).
- Caution: α-blockers (start lowest dose of the added drug), aortic stenosis, hypotension. CYP3A4 inhibitors (ketoconazole, ritonavir) raise levels.
- Not studied: MI (myocardial infarction) or stroke within 6 months, unstable angina, BP <90/50 or >170/100 mmHg, retinitis pigmentosa.
- Hypotension with nitrates: Trendelenburg, fluids, phenylephrine.

**Diagram to draw:** NO–cGMP–PDE5 pathway in cavernosal smooth muscle.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Half-life 17.5 h; food-independent; back pain.
> - 5 mg daily for LUTS: IPSS improves, Qmax does not.
> - No nitrates for 48 h after a dose.

## Q3c. DDAVP
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in 2023-Winter Paper 3, Q3b)

### Pharmacology
- DDAVP (**1-deamino-8-D-arginine vasopressin**, desmopressin): synthetic analogue of AVP (arginine vasopressin).
- Deamination and D-arginine give longer action and **selective V2 agonism without pressor (V1) effect**.
- **Mechanism:** V2 receptor on collecting-duct cells raises cAMP (cyclic adenosine monophosphate), inserting **aquaporin-2**; water is reabsorbed, so urine is less and concentrated.
- Endothelial V2 action releases von Willebrand factor and factor VIII.

### Formulations

| Form | Dose |
|---|---|
| Tablet | 0.2–0.4 mg, about 1 h before bed (enuresis) |
| Oral lyophilisate (melt) | 120–240 µg |
| Low-dose nasal spray (nocturia) | 0.83 or 1.66 µg, 30 min before bed |
| Sublingual ODT (orally disintegrating tablet) | 27.7 µg women, 55.3 µg men |
| IV or subcutaneous | 0.3 µg/kg (bleeding) |

### Uses
1. **Monosymptomatic nocturnal enuresis:** first line with the alarm. **Rule of thirds** (full, partial, no response). Works at once but relapses on stopping; avoid evening fluid; add oxybutynin if partial.
2. **Nocturia from nocturnal polyuria** (NPi [nocturnal polyuria index] >20–33%, age-dependent). EAU (European Association of Urology): offer under 65; low dose over 65 with ≥2 voids. Add to α-blocker for refractory nocturia in BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia).
3. **Neurogenic nocturia:** multiple sclerosis.
4. **Bleeding:** uraemic platelet dysfunction, von Willebrand disease (including acquired, in Wilms tumour). Tachyphylaxis after repeated doses.
5. **Diagnosis:** central versus nephrogenic DI (diabetes insipidus).

### Adverse effects
- **Hyponatraemia** (most important): headache, nausea, confusion, seizures.
- Risk factors: **age ≥65**, sodium <135 mmol/L, CrCl (creatinine clearance) <60 mL/min, higher dose; women more sensitive. Most events in the first **2–3 weeks**.

### Monitoring and contraindications
- Sodium at baseline, within 1 week and at 1 month (EAU adds day 3), and after dose change.
- **Avoid:** hyponatraemia, polydipsia, heart failure, SIADH (syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone), significant renal impairment, **loop diuretics**.

**Diagram to draw:** V2 receptor–cAMP–aquaporin-2 pathway in the collecting duct.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Selective V2 agonist; no pressor effect.
> - Enuresis: 0.2–0.4 mg tablet or 120–240 µg melt; rule of thirds.
> - Hyponatraemia: age ≥65, low sodium; check sodium early.

## Q3d. Boari flap
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition
- A **tubularised flap of anterior bladder wall**, based posterolaterally on the side of the lesion, swung up to bridge a distal or mid-ureteric gap. Described in dogs 1894, humans 1947; laparoscopic 2001, robotic 2008.

### Reconstructive ladder (distal ureter)
| Option | Gap bridged |
|---|---|
| Ureteroneocystostomy | Distal 3–5 cm |
| + Psoas hitch | Adds up to 5 cm (total 6–10 cm) |
| **Boari flap (+ hitch)** | **10–15 cm**; spiral flap may reach pelvis (right) |
| Downward nephropexy | Adds up to 5–8 cm |
- Beyond: TUU (transureteroureterostomy), ileal ureter, autotransplantation.

### Indications
- Long distal or mid-ureteric loss: gynaecological injury (commonest), stricture, failed reimplantation, distal TCC (transitional cell carcinoma) ureter, tuberculosis.

### Contraindications
- **Small, contracted or poorly compliant bladder** (radiation, tuberculosis); uncorrected outlet obstruction or neurogenic bladder. Not usually for acute unstable trauma.
- Preoperative: urodynamics or cystogram for capacity; antegrade and retrograde studies for length.

### Technique
1. Mobilise bladder; divide contralateral superior vesical pedicle if needed.
2. Mark flap on the **non-distended** bladder: **base ≥4 cm, apex ≥3 cm, length:base ≤3:1**; length = gap + 3 cm if tunnelled.
3. Raise flap; **psoas hitch** its base (sutures superficial, parallel to fibres: spare genitofemoral and femoral nerves).
4. Anastomose spatulated ureter: **non-refluxing** 3 cm submucosal tunnel, or **refluxing** direct (apex about 3 times ureteric diameter).
5. Stent (double-J); tubularise in two layers 2-0 absorbable; drain, catheter 7–14 days.
- Laparoscopic and robotic: transperitoneal, refluxing anastomosis, results excellent.

### Complications
- **Recurrent stricture** (commonest; ischaemia or tension), reduced capacity with frequency and urgency, leak, nerve palsy, iliac vessel injury, pseudodiverticulum.
- eGFR (estimated glomerular filtration rate) fell in 44% within 2 years after reimplantation: counsel.
- **Alternative:** bladder advancement flap, similar reach and simpler.

**Diagram to draw:** bladder outline with oblique flap (4 cm base, 3 cm apex), hitched and tubularised to ureter.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Bridges 10–15 cm; combine with psoas hitch.
> - Base ≥4 cm, length:base ≤3:1, measure bladder empty.
> - Contraindicated in small contracted bladder.

## Q3e. Single stage repair of Extrophy of UB.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

*Assumed meaning: single-stage (complete primary) repair of exstrophy of the urinary bladder, mainly CPRE (complete primary repair of exstrophy, Mitchell).*

### Background
- Classic bladder exstrophy: about 2 per 100,000 births; M:F 2.3:1. Absent infraumbilical mesoderm: open bladder, epispadias, pubic diastasis (mean 4.8 cm).
- **MSRE (modern staged repair):** closure at birth, epispadias repair 6–12 months, bladder neck reconstruction 4–7 years. Single-stage repairs aim for fewer operations and continence without bladder neck repair.

### Single-stage options
| Repair | Features |
|---|---|
| **CPRE (Mitchell)** | Newborn closure + penile disassembly + bladder neck tailoring |
| Erlangen ("total") | All in one at about 8 weeks, no osteotomy |
| Kelly | Radical soft-tissue mobilisation, no osteotomy (actually staged) |
| Combined closure + epispadias repair | Delayed or failed closures, boys >6 months |

### Selection
- Template ≥3 × 3 cm, elastic, few polyps; experienced team. Not for penoscrotal duplication, hypoplastic bladder, bilateral hydronephrosis.

### CPRE technique
1. Combined general and epidural anaesthesia; **osteotomy if >72 hours** (3 days) old or diastasis >4 cm or stiff pelvis.
2. Incise around plate; free bladder; **divide urogenital diaphragm fibres** to levator hiatus so the unit sits deep.
3. **Penile disassembly:** right and left hemicorporoglanular bodies plus urethral wedge on spongiosum (now often left attached to glans).
4. Urethra placed ventrally between corpora; corpora joined; meatus usually **hypospadiac**.
5. Bladder and neck closed and tapered; ± bilateral ureteric reimplantation (BUR-CPRE).
6. Pubic No. 2 nylon mattress suture; suprapubic catheter 4 weeks, ureteric stents.
7. Immobilise: external fixation plus traction (failure 7.5% vs spica 47%).

### Complications
- Same frequency as MSRE but **more serious**: dehiscence, prolapse, vesicocutaneous fistula, **glans or corporal loss** (ischaemia), early reimplantation for VUR (vesicoureteric reflux) or hydronephrosis; retention in girls.

### Outcomes
- Most still need bladder neck surgery. Daytime dryness: about 56% after CPRE with YDL (Young-Dees-Leadbetter), versus 70–74% after MSRE.
- Continence predictors: **template size and successful first closure**.

**Diagram to draw:** penile disassembly into two hemicorpora and urethral wedge.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - CPRE: closure + penile disassembly in newborn.
> - Osteotomy if >72 h or diastasis >4 cm.
> - Risks: glans loss, hypospadias, VUR.
> - First closure success predicts continence.

## Q3f. Retrograde ejaculation
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition
- RE (retrograde ejaculation): semen passes backwards into the bladder at orgasm because the **bladder neck fails to close**; orgasm is preserved, ejaculate absent or low volume.

### Physiology
| Phase | Event | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Emission | Vas, seminal vesicles, prostate deliver semen to posterior urethra | Sympathetic T10–L2 |
| Ejection | **Bladder neck closes**, external sphincter relaxes, bulbospongiosus contracts | Somatic S2–S4 + sympathetic reflex |
| Orgasm | Cerebral perception | Cortical |

### Causes
- **Anatomical:** **TURP (transurethral resection of prostate)** 42–100%, TUIP (incision) 5–45%, HoLEP and photovaporisation, bladder neck surgery, Y-V plasty. Ejaculation-sparing techniques, PUL (prostatic urethral lift) and Aquablation preserve ejaculation.
- **Neurogenic:** **diabetic autonomic neuropathy** (commonest medical), RPLND (retroperitoneal lymph node dissection; less with nerve sparing), colorectal surgery, sympathectomy, aortic surgery, spinal cord injury, multiple sclerosis.
- **Drugs:** α-blockers (silodosin, tamsulosin), antipsychotics, antidepressants, thiazides.
- **Congenital:** posterior urethral valves, bladder exstrophy, ectopic ureter.

### Evaluation
- Low-volume or absent ejaculate with orgasm; infertility.
- **Post-ejaculatory urinalysis:** void, ejaculate, void again; centrifuge. **>5–10 sperm per HPF (high-power field)** confirms RE; significant if urinary sperm approach or exceed antegrade sperm.
- Sperm and fructose in urine separate RE from **failure of emission**; TRUS (transrectal ultrasound) if EDO (ejaculatory duct obstruction) suspected.

### Treatment
- Reassure if fertility not desired; stop offending drug.
- **Sympathomimetics** (close bladder neck): **pseudoephedrine** 60–120 mg (120 mg 6-hourly the day before and twice on the day); midodrine, ephedrine, phenylpropanolamine. Side effects: dry mouth, hypertension.
- **Imipramine** 25 mg twice daily; antihistamines (brompheniramine) add to sympathomimetics.
- **Bladder neck reconstruction:** poor results; collagen injection experimental.
- **Sperm retrieval:** alkalinise urine (sodium bicarbonate), void or catheterise after orgasm, wash sperm in buffer → IUI (intrauterine insemination), IVF (in vitro fertilisation) or ICSI (intracytoplasmic sperm injection).
- **Prevention:** nerve-sparing RPLND, ejaculation-preserving BPH procedures.

**Diagram to draw:** bladder neck open at ejaculation with semen entering the bladder.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - TURP and diabetic neuropathy are the commonest causes.
> - Post-ejaculatory urine: sperm and fructose confirm RE.
> - Pseudoephedrine first; else urinary sperm retrieval for ART.
