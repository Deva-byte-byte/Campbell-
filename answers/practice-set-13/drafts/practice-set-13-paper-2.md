## Q1. Describe role of nephron sparing surgery in management of RCC
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)
(also answered in 2022-Summer Paper 3, Q1 and 2025-Winter Paper 1, Q2)

### Definition and rationale
- NSS (nephron-sparing surgery) is complete removal of the tumour with **maximal preservation of functioning parenchyma**. PN (partial nephrectomy) is the standard form; thermal ablation is the non-excisional form.
- **Why it matters:** RN (radical nephrectomy) can cause CKD (chronic kidney disease), linked to cardiovascular events and death. PN keeps about **90%** of global function (RN 65–70%) with equal cancer control for small masses.
- **CKD from surgery alone** is stable, with near-normal survival if the new GFR (glomerular filtration rate) stays >45; **CKD from medical disease** (diabetes, hypertension) progresses. So NSS matters most when medical renal disease coexists.

### Role by clinical setting

| Setting | Role of NSS |
|---|---|
| **cT1a (≤4 cm)** | PN is the **standard of care** when intervention is needed; RN is overtreatment |
| cT1b–T2 | PN when feasible and function matters; otherwise debatable |
| Hereditary RCC (renal cell carcinoma) | Observe until the largest tumour is **3 cm**, then enucleate (except HLRCC [hereditary leiomyomatosis and RCC]: wide excision) |
| Bilateral tumours, solitary kidney | Imperative PN, staged if bilateral; avoids dialysis |
| Frail, comorbid, cT1a <3 cm | Thermal ablation or active surveillance |
| Locally advanced, thrombus, cN1, metastatic | Generally RN or cytoreductive surgery |

### Indications

| Category | Situations |
|---|---|
| **Imperative** | Bilateral RCC; solitary functioning kidney; RN would mean dialysis |
| **Relative** | Contralateral kidney threatened: stones, renal artery stenosis, diabetes, hypertension, proteinuria; hereditary RCC |
| **Elective** | Localised tumour with a normal contralateral kidney |

- **AUA (American Urological Association) 2021:** RN is preferred only if **all three** apply: high complexity; no CKD or proteinuria; normal contralateral kidney with new GFR likely **>45** after RN. Otherwise PN.
- **Relative contraindications:** **<20% of nephron mass retained**; pedicle encasement; venous tumour thrombus; cT4.

### Preoperative evaluation
- Multiphase CT (computed tomography) or MRI (magnetic resonance imaging); chest imaging; eGFR (estimated GFR) and urine protein.
- **R.E.N.A.L. nephrometry** (Radius, Exophytic/endophytic, Nearness to collecting system, Anterior/posterior, Location to polar lines): **4–6 low, 7–9 moderate, 10–12 high** complexity. PADUA is an alternative.
- **Biopsy** when it changes management (about **20%** of enhancing cT1 masses are benign). Genetic counselling if age ≤46, multifocal or bilateral.

### Techniques of PN
**Approach:** open (flank extraperitoneal), laparoscopic, or **RAPN (robot-assisted PN)**: transperitoneal for anterior and retroperitoneal for posterior tumours.

**Steps**
1. **Early hilar control** of artery, vein and accessory vessels, even if off-clamp is planned.
2. Defat the kidney; **intraoperative ultrasound** maps borders and depth.
3. Clamp the artery. Use **ice slush** if ischaemia is expected to exceed **25–30 minutes** (solitary kidney, CKD).
4. Excise with a thin rim. **A negative margin matters; its width does not.**
5. **Two-layer renorrhaphy:** inner layer closes vessels and collecting system; outer capsular layer over a haemostatic bolster.
6. Unclamp; check haemostasis; close Gerota fascia; drain.

| | Standard (wedge) resection | Enucleation |
|---|---|---|
| Plane | Through normal parenchyma | Along the pseudocapsule |
| Advantage | Fewer positive margins | Less bleeding, maximal parenchyma |
| Use | Default | Hereditary, multifocal, severe CKD |

- **Ischaemia:** warm ischaemia **<25–30 minutes**. Function depends mainly on the **parenchyma preserved**; off-clamp, selective clamping and mannitol show no long-term eGFR benefit.

### Thermal ablation
- **Cryoablation** (double freeze–thaw) or **RFA (radiofrequency ablation)**; percutaneous preferred.
- AUA: alternative for **cT1a <3 cm**; biopsy before (preferred) or at ablation. Local recurrence is higher than PN but treatable by repeat ablation.
- **Also know:** MWA (microwave ablation), SBRT (stereotactic body radiotherapy) for non-surgical candidates.

### Outcomes and evidence
- **Trifecta:** negative margin + no major complication + warm ischaemia ≤25 minutes. RAPN: shorter ischaemia than laparoscopy, less blood loss than open.
- Elective PN: local recurrence **1–2%**; imperative 3–5%.
- **EORTC (European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer) 30904 (2011):** RN vs PN for ≤5 cm tumours: PN had better function and similar oncology; 10-year overall survival 81% RN vs 76% PN. The trial is flawed.

### Complications
- **Urine leak** (drain, stent); **haemorrhage, pseudoaneurysm or AV (arteriovenous) fistula** (delayed haematuria: angioembolisation); acute kidney injury; temporary dialysis in a solitary kidney.
- **Positive margin 0–7%:** surveillance, not reoperation.
- **Hyperfiltration injury** when >50% of a solitary kidney is removed: proteinuria, FSGS (focal segmental glomerulosclerosis); give an ACE (angiotensin-converting enzyme) inhibitor.
- **Follow-up:** risk-based imaging; eGFR and urine protein.

### Recent advances
- 3D (three-dimensional) models; ICG (indocyanine green) for selective clamping; neoadjuvant TKI (tyrosine kinase inhibitor) downsizing in imperative cases.

**Diagram to draw:** clamped renal artery, tumour excised with a rim, two-layer renorrhaphy.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - PN is the standard for cT1a; ablation or surveillance for frail patients with tumours <3 cm.
> - AUA: RN only if complex + no CKD + new GFR likely >45.
> - Preserved parenchyma, not clamp time, governs function.
> - EORTC 30904: better function, similar cancer control.

## Q2. Desc. factors influencing outcome of ESWL. Discuss complic. & strategies to avoid them.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)
(also answered in 2021-Summer Paper 2, Q1; mechanism and indications in 2019-Summer Paper 2, Q2)

### Introduction
- ESWL (extracorporeal shock wave lithotripsy, now SWL) breaks stones with shock waves (peak 30–100 MPa, negative tail about −10 MPa) generated at F1 and focused on the stone at F2.
- Stones break by spallation, shear, squeezing and **cavitation**. Cavitation also causes most tissue injury, so steps that improve outcome usually reduce complications too.
- Outcome = SFR (stone-free rate) and need for retreatment.

### Factors influencing outcome
**1. Stone factors**

| Factor | Favourable | Unfavourable |
|---|---|---|
| Size (most important) | <10 mm | Renal >20 mm (PCNL [percutaneous nephrolithotomy] first); ureteric ≥10 mm favours URS (ureteroscopy) |
| Density on NCCT (non-contrast CT [computed tomography]) | <900 HU (Hounsfield units) | >1000 HU |
| Composition | Calcium oxalate dihydrate, uric acid | Cystine, brushite, calcium oxalate monohydrate |
| Impaction (ureter) | Recent, mobile | Chronic impaction: thick ureteral wall around the stone on CT |
| Burden and site | Single, renal pelvis or upper calyx | Multiple sites; lower pole >1 cm |

**2. Renal anatomical factors**
- **Lower pole:** fragments drain against gravity. **Lower Pole I (Albala, 2001):** stones >1 cm, SFR **21% SWL vs 91% PCNL**. A steep infundibulopelvic angle and a long, narrow infundibulum reduce clearance.
- Obstruction distal to the stone (UPJO [ureteropelvic junction obstruction], infundibular stenosis, calyceal diverticulum) traps fragments. Horseshoe and ectopic kidneys drain poorly.

**3. Patient factors**
- **SSD (skin-to-stone distance) >10 cm** (obesity) predicts failure. Skeletal deformity can prevent targeting.
- Respiratory movement of 10–15 mm moves the stone out of F2.
- **Triple D score:** stone density, stone volume and skin-to-stone distance together predict success.

**4. Machine and technique factors**
- **Energy source:** electrohydraulic (HM3) breaks best; piezoelectric has low energy and a tiny focal zone; modern machines are electromagnetic. A focal zone wider than the stone breaks better.
- **Coupling:** air pockets in the gel cut fragmentation by up to two-fifths.
- **Rate 60–90 shocks/min** beats 120/min, because the bubble cloud clears between shocks. **Ramping** energy up also helps.
- **Analgesia:** general anaesthesia or sedation keeps the stone in focus.
- Prone position for mid-ureteric stones over the sacrum; continuous imaging; experienced team. Little gain beyond two sessions.
- **Adjuncts:** α-blockers (faster clearance); potassium citrate; percussion, diuresis and inversion for lower pole fragments.

### Complications

| Group | Complication | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Fragment-related | Renal colic; **steinstrasse** ("stone street" of fragments in the ureter); residual fragments | Steinstrasse rises several-fold with burden >2 cm² |
| Infective | UTI (urinary tract infection), pyelonephritis, urosepsis | Infection stones, stents |
| Renal tissue injury | Transient haematuria (common); **subcapsular or perirenal haematoma** (minority) | Risk: age, hypertension, obesity, diabetes, coronary disease, anticoagulation, high energy, many shocks |
| Other organs | Liver, spleen, pancreas, colon, lung (children) | Very rare |
| Cardiac | Arrhythmia during treatment | ECG (electrocardiogram) gating |
| Skin | Petechiae, bruising | Minor |

- **Refuted long-term effects:** new hypertension, diabetes, loss of renal function, infertility.

### Strategies to avoid complications
1. **Select.** Absolute contraindications: pregnancy; uncorrected coagulopathy or ongoing antithrombotics; untreated UTI; obstruction distal to the stone; aneurysm near the stone (aortic >4 cm); severe untreated hypertension. Send large, dense, lower pole or impacted stones to URS or PCNL.
2. **Prepare.** Urine culture; treat bacteriuria. Prophylaxis only with risk factors (stent, nephrostomy, struvite, recent UTI). Stop antithrombotics before and for about 48 hours after. Control blood pressure; check pacemakers.
3. **Protect the kidney.** Slow rate; **slow ramping** (induces protective renal vasoconstriction and lowers haematoma risk); fewest shocks and lowest effective energy.
4. **Prevent steinstrasse.** PCNL or URS for large burdens. Routine stenting does not improve SFR, though it may reduce steinstrasse with large stones. α-blockers after SWL.
5. **Treat early.** Steinstrasse: MET (medical expulsive therapy), SWL to the lead fragment or URS; **nephrostomy if fever or obstruction**. Haematoma: bed rest, transfusion, embolisation if bleeding continues.
6. **Follow up** with KUB (kidney-ureter-bladder radiograph) and ultrasound.

### Recent advances
- **Burst wave lithotripsy** (ultrasound bursts: less cavitation, no anaesthesia, uniform fragments) and **ultrasonic propulsion** of fragments.

**Diagram to draw:** ellipsoid reflector with F1, F2, stone and gel coupling; box of failure predictors.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Size is the main predictor; HU >1000 and SSD >10 cm predict failure.
> - Lower Pole I: SFR 21% SWL vs 91% PCNL for lower pole stones >1 cm.
> - Slow rate (60–90/min), ramping and good coupling improve breakage and reduce injury.
> - Steinstrasse risk rises with burden >2 cm²; no routine stent.

## Q3a. Bicalutamide
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(combined androgen blockade is answered in 2018-Summer Paper 4, Q3f)

### Introduction and mechanism
- Non-steroidal **AR (androgen receptor) antagonist**, called first- or second-generation. It competes with testosterone and DHT (dihydrotestosterone) at the ligand-binding domain.
- Given alone, it blocks central feedback, so **LH (luteinising hormone) and testosterone rise** (about 1.5 times); aromatisation to oestradiol causes gynaecomastia; libido is partly preserved.
- **Half-life about 6 days** (once daily); unaffected by age or renal impairment. Most potent and best tolerated of its class.

### Uses

| Use | Dose | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **CAB (combined androgen blockade)** with LHRH (LH-releasing hormone) agonist, metastatic | 50 mg daily | Small survival gain (2–3%, PCTCG [Prostate Cancer Trialists' Collaborative Group]) |
| **Flare prevention** | 50 mg, with or before the agonist, 2–4 weeks | Main use today |
| Monotherapy, locally advanced | 150 mg daily | **EPC (Early Prostate Cancer) programme:** better progression-free survival; **harm in localised disease** (death HR [hazard ratio] 1.23) |
| With salvage radiotherapy | 150 mg for 2 years | **RTOG 9601:** 12-year overall survival 76.3% vs 71.3% |
| CRPC (castration-resistant prostate cancer) | 50 mg | Second-line step; largely replaced by newer agents |

- 150 mg monotherapy roughly equals castration in locally advanced disease, with better sexual interest; not FDA (US Food and Drug Administration) approved alone, but used in some countries.
- **Antiandrogen withdrawal:** stopping bicalutamide gives a PSA (prostate-specific antigen) fall >50% in **15–30%** for 3.5–5 months. Mutant AR turns the drug into an agonist.
- **Also know:** stuttering priapism (sickle cell disease, spinal injury); feminising hormone therapy; studied in BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia).

### Adverse effects
- **Gynaecomastia and breast pain** (two-thirds at 150 mg): prevent with breast radiotherapy (10–15 Gy single fraction) or tamoxifen 10 mg daily.
- **Hepatotoxicity** (monitor liver tests); rare **interstitial pneumonitis**; erectile dysfunction; fewer hot flushes than castration.

### Comparison
- Enzalutamide beat bicalutamide in CRPC (TERRAIN, STRIVE) and in **ENZAMET** (hormone-sensitive metastatic disease).

**Diagram to draw:** hypothalamus–pituitary–testis axis with bicalutamide blocking the AR.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Half-life about 6 days; 50 mg with LHRH agonist, 150 mg monotherapy.
> - Monotherapy raises testosterone; gynaecomastia two-thirds.
> - EPC: harm in localised disease; RTOG 9601: survival gain with salvage RT.
> - Withdrawal response 15–30%.

## Q3b. Mullerionosis of UB
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

*"Mullerionosis of UB" is read as müllerianosis of the urinary bladder.*

### Definition
- Benign lesions of **müllerian-type epithelium** in the bladder wall, in three forms:

| Lesion | Tissue |
|---|---|
| **Endometriosis** (commonest) | Endometrial glands **and stroma** |
| **Endocervicosis** | Endocervical mucinous glands |
| **Endosalpingiosis** | Ciliated tubal epithelium |

- **Müllerianosis** strictly means two or more of these together.
- Women of reproductive age, often after **caesarean section**. Usually **posterior wall or dome** (vesicouterine pouch).
- Bladder is the commonest urinary tract site of endometriosis, ahead of the ureter.

### Pathogenesis
- **Implantation:** retrograde menstruation (Sampson) into the vesicouterine pouch.
- **Iatrogenic implantation** at caesarean or pelvic surgery (primary vs iatrogenic forms).
- **Metaplasia** of coelomic or subperitoneal müllerian remnants (the "secondary müllerian system").
- Lymphatic or vascular spread; direct extension of anterior uterine adenomyosis.

### Clinical features
- **Cyclical haematuria** or irritative LUTS (lower urinary tract symptoms) with menses; suprapubic pain, dysmenorrhoea, dyspareunia.
- Endocervicosis: non-cyclical mass or LUTS.
- **Also know:** menouria differential includes vesicouterine fistula (Youssef syndrome).

### Evaluation
- **Ultrasound** (transabdominal plus transvaginal): size and depth of a hypoechoic nodule.
- **MRI (magnetic resonance imaging):** extent; haemorrhagic foci **bright on T1**, fibrotic thickening dark on T2.
- **Cystoscopy:** bluish submucosal nodule with neovascularity, best seen during menses. Of **limited value**, because disease is mainly **extravesical** (from serosa inwards).
- **Biopsy:** excludes adenocarcinoma, especially with endocervicosis. Immunohistochemistry: ER/PR (oestrogen/progesterone receptor) positive; CD10 marks endometrial stroma.
- Ultrasound of upper tracts for ureteric involvement.
- **Differential:** adenocarcinoma, cystitis glandularis, nephrogenic adenoma, urachal remnant.

### Management
- **Hormonal suppression** (combined pill, progestins, GnRH [gonadotropin-releasing hormone] agonists): symptom relief; recurrence after stopping.
- **TUR (transurethral resection):** incomplete for transmural disease; perforation risk; recurs.
- **Partial cystectomy** (laparoscopic or robotic) with a margin: **primary treatment**. Ureteric stent or reimplantation if the orifice is involved.
- Excise other pelvic endometriosis with the gynaecologist.
- Malignant change (endometrioid or clear cell adenocarcinoma) is rare.

**Diagram to draw:** sagittal pelvis with a dome nodule growing from the vesicouterine pouch into the detrusor.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Endometriosis, endocervicosis, endosalpingiosis; ≥2 = müllerianosis.
> - Cyclical haematuria; MRI bright on T1.
> - Disease is extravesical: cystoscopy limited, partial cystectomy preferred.

## Q3c. Brainstem death
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in 2022-Summer Paper 4, Q3b and 2024-Winter Paper 2, Q3d)

### Definition and law
- **Irreversible loss of the capacity for consciousness and breathing**; legally death in India: THOA (Transplantation of Human Organs Act) 1994, amended 2011, Rules 2014.

### Pathophysiology
- Raised ICP (intracranial pressure) → coning → Cushing reflex → **catecholamine storm** → loss of sympathetic tone → **hypotension**.
- Consequences: **diabetes insipidus** (polyuria, hypernatraemia), hypothermia, DIC (disseminated intravascular coagulation), neurogenic pulmonary oedema.

### Preconditions
- Apnoeic coma on a ventilator with a **known irreversible cause**.
- Exclude sedatives, relaxants, hypothermia, metabolic or endocrine causes, shock.

### Tests: all brainstem reflexes absent

| Reflex | Method | Cranial nerves |
|---|---|---|
| Pupillary | Bright light: pupils fixed | II, III |
| Corneal | Touch cornea: no blink | V, VII |
| Oculocephalic | Turn head: eyes move with head | VIII, III, VI |
| Oculovestibular | Ice-cold water in each ear: no eye movement | VIII, III, VI |
| Motor in cranial nerve area | Supraorbital pressure: no grimace | V, VII |
| Gag and cough | Pharynx touch, tracheal suction: no response | IX, X |

- **Apnoea test (last):** pre-oxygenate with 100% oxygen; disconnect with oxygen insufflation; **no breathing at PaCO₂ (arterial carbon dioxide tension) ≥60 mmHg**. Abort for hypotension, desaturation or arrhythmia.
- **Ancillary tests** if examination is impossible: angiography, perfusion scan, EEG (electroencephalogram).

### Indian Board of Medical Experts
- Four members: hospital in-charge; independent specialist nominated from an approved panel; neurologist or neurosurgeon; treating doctor. **No transplant-team member.**
- **Two sets of tests at least 6 hours apart**; recorded on **Form 10**.

### Clinical implications
- **Mandatory request** for donation; next-of-kin consent; NOTTO (National Organ and Tissue Transplant Organisation) allocates.
- **Donor management, rule of 100s:** systolic pressure >100 mmHg, urine >100 mL/h, PaO₂ (arterial oxygen tension) >100 mmHg, haemoglobin >100 g/L. Vasopressin, thyroid hormone, steroids, warming.

**Diagram to draw:** flowchart: coma → preconditions → test 1 → 6 h → test 2 → Form 10 → consent → NOTTO.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Six brainstem reflexes absent; apnoea at PaCO₂ ≥60 mmHg.
> - Four-member Board; no transplant-team member.
> - Two tests ≥6 h apart; Form 10.

## Q3d. Screening of CA prostate.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Concept
- Screening tests healthy, asymptomatic men to cut deaths through earlier cure. The price is **overdiagnosis** (cancers that would never cause harm) and **overtreatment**.
- Since PSA (prostate-specific antigen) testing, US mortality has fallen over 40%.

### Tools
- **PSA:** biopsy threshold 4 ng/mL (many use 2.5–3; ERSPC used 3). Repeat a raised PSA before biopsy.
- **DRE (digital rectal examination):** little value alone; abnormal DRE raises risk.
- **Refinements:** PSA density ≥0.15; free PSA ≤25%; PHI (Prostate Health Index), 4Kscore, Stockholm3; urine tests (PCA3 [prostate cancer antigen 3], ExoDx, SelectMDx).
- **MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) before biopsy:** fewer biopsies and less low-grade overdiagnosis.

### Evidence

| Trial | Result |
|---|---|
| **ERSPC (European Randomised Study of Screening for Prostate Cancer)** | About **20% fall in prostate cancer death** at 16 years; 570 invited and 18 extra diagnoses per death prevented |
| **PLCO (Prostate, Lung, Colorectal and Ovarian)** | No difference: control arm heavily PSA-tested |
| Göteborg-1 | Larger mortality reduction with biennial screening |
| **Göteborg-2 (2022)** | MRI-targeted biopsy only: halved overdiagnosis |

- Corrected for non-attendance and contamination, benefit is 27–31%.

### Guidelines

| Body | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| USPSTF (US Preventive Services Task Force) 2018 | **Grade C**: shared decision at 55–69; not ≥70 (was Grade D in 2012) |
| AUA (American Urological Association) | Shared decision 55–69; 2-yearly interval |
| NCCN (National Comprehensive Cancer Network) | Baseline PSA and discussion at 45 |
| EAU (European Association of Urology) | Risk-adapted: informed men from 50, from 45 with family history or African descent, from 40 with BRCA2 |

- **No body advises unselected population screening.**
- **High risk:** Black men, father or brother diagnosed <65, BRCA2 and other DNA-repair mutations: start earlier.
- Stop when life expectancy is under 10 years.

### Reducing harm
- **Active surveillance** for Grade Group 1; MRI and biomarkers before biopsy; intervals by baseline PSA.

**Diagram to draw:** pathway: PSA → repeat → risk calculator or MRI → targeted biopsy → active surveillance or treatment.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - ERSPC: about 20% fall in prostate cancer death; PLCO null from contamination.
> - USPSTF Grade C at 55–69.
> - MRI-first and active surveillance reduce overdiagnosis and overtreatment.

## Q3e. Imaging measures in CA prostate
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(mpMRI detail in 2024-Winter Paper 1, Q3d; PSMA PET in 2019-Summer Paper 4, Q3a)

### 1. Ultrasound
- **TRUS (transrectal ultrasound):** guides biopsy, measures volume (PSA [prostate-specific antigen] density). Cancer is a hypoechoic PZ (peripheral zone) lesion, but **39% are isoechoic**.
- **Also know:** Doppler, contrast TRUS and elastography (adjuncts only); **micro-ultrasound** (29 MHz, PRI-MUS score, close to MRI accuracy).

### 2. MRI: detection and local staging
- **mpMRI (multiparametric magnetic resonance imaging)**, 3 T without endorectal coil:

| Sequence | Shows | Cancer |
|---|---|---|
| T2-weighted | Anatomy; EPE (extraprostatic extension), SVI (seminal vesicle invasion) | Low signal |
| DWI (diffusion-weighted imaging) with ADC (apparent diffusion coefficient) | Cellularity | Bright on DWI, dark on ADC |
| DCE (dynamic contrast-enhanced) | Vascularity | Early enhancement, washout |

- **PI-RADS v2.1 (Prostate Imaging Reporting and Data System)** 1–5: DWI dominant in PZ, T2 in TZ (transition zone); DCE only upgrades PZ 3 to 4.
- **PROMIS** (sensitivity 93% vs 48% for TRUS biopsy) and **PRECISION** (more significant, less insignificant cancer): **MRI before biopsy**. Cochrane: sensitivity 0.91, specificity 0.37.
- **Also know:** biparametric MRI; **PI-QUAL** (image quality); **PRECISE** (change on surveillance); fusion biopsy.

### 3. Staging by risk group
- **Low or intermediate risk:** no routine CT (computed tomography) or bone scan.
- **High risk:** CT plus **⁹⁹ᵐTc-MDP (methylene diphosphonate) bone scan**, or **PSMA (prostate-specific membrane antigen) PET (positron emission tomography)**.
- **proPSMA:** accuracy 92% vs 65% for conventional imaging. Nodal sensitivity only 30–40%, specificity >90%: a negative scan does not exclude micrometastases.

### 4. Biochemical recurrence
- **PSMA PET** detects disease at low PSA. **Calais trial:** 56% vs 26% for fluciclovine. **CONDOR:** management changed in 63.9%.
- Choline and fluciclovine PET are older; FDG (fluorodeoxyglucose) PET is poor except in neuroendocrine change.

### 5. Advanced disease
- PSMA PET selects men for **¹⁷⁷Lu-PSMA-617** (VISION); whole-body MRI. No proof yet that PSMA-guided treatment improves survival.

**Diagram to draw:** prostate zones with a PZ lesion dark on ADC; PSMA PET nodal uptake.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - MRI before biopsy (PROMIS, PRECISION); PI-RADS v2.1.
> - High risk: bone scan + CT or PSMA PET (proPSMA 92% vs 65%).
> - PSMA nodal sensitivity 30–40%.

## Q3f. Prostatic stents
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(urethral stents also answered in Practice Set 4 Paper 4, Q3f and 2015-Summer Paper 4, Q3d)

### Definition
- A device placed in the **prostatic urethra**, between bladder neck and verumontanum, to relieve BOO (bladder outlet obstruction) without resection. Mainly for men **unfit for surgery**.

### Classification

| Type | Examples | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Temporary, first generation** | Urospiral, Prostakath (spirals) | Steel or gold-plated coil; not epithelialised |
| **Temporary, second generation** | **Memokath** (thermo-expandable nitinol); Allium (covered nitinol) | Expands with warm water, softens with cold water for removal |
| Temporary polyurethane | Intraurethral catheter, Spanner | Bulbar anchor; sphincter left free |
| Biodegradable | Polylactic acid stents | Dissolve; trial use |
| **Permanent, epithelialising** | **UroLume** (woven mesh) | Urothelium covers the mesh; withdrawn |

### Indications
- Retention or severe BOO from BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia) in men unfit for surgery: avoids a long-term catheter.
- Drainage after thermal treatments; trial of de-obstruction.
- **Also know:** DSD (detrusor–sphincter dyssynergia), now abandoned; bulbar stricture.

### Contraindications
- Poor detrusor contractility (do urodynamics if in doubt); untreated UTI (urinary tract infection); large median lobe; bladder stones; stricture.

### Technique
- Measure prostatic urethral length cystoscopically. Place under local anaesthesia with endoscopic or fluoroscopic guidance. The stent must **end above the external sphincter**, or incontinence follows.

### Complications
- **Migration** (commonest with temporary stents); **encrustation**; UTI; irritative symptoms, perineal pain, haematuria.
- **Hyperplastic ingrowth** and restenosis with permanent stents (difficult removal); incontinence if the sphincter is crossed.

### Current status
- Most unfit men void after placement, but many stents are later removed.
- **EAU (European Association of Urology):** alternative to catheterisation in men unfit for surgery (weak). Not in the **AUA (American Urological Association) 2023** BPH list.
- **Recent:** **iTind**: temporary nitinol device left 5–7 days; it remodels the urethra by ischaemic pressure necrosis. AUA 2023 option for 25–75 mL without median lobe. **Optilume BPH** (paclitaxel balloon).

**Diagram to draw:** sagittal prostate with a Memokath from bladder neck to verumontanum, above the external sphincter.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Temporary (Memokath) vs permanent (UroLume, withdrawn).
> - Need detrusor contractility; never cross the sphincter.
> - Migration, encrustation, ingrowth; iTind is the modern temporary device.
