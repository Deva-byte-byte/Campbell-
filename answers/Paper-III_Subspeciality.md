## III-1. Discuss various ureteric stents. Discuss ureteroscopy with or without stenting.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

### Introduction
- Ureteric stent = hollow tube keeping the upper tract draining past obstruction, injury or anastomosis.
- **Zimskind 1967**: first indwelling silicone splint (straight → migrated). **Finney 1978**: **double-J**; self-retaining coils solved migration.

### Stent design
| Parameter | Details |
|---|---|
| Diameter | 3–8.5 Fr (adults 4.7–7 Fr; 6 Fr default); bigger → better extraluminal flow in extrinsic obstruction |
| Length | Adults 20–30 cm (usual 24–26 cm); estimate from height or measure on ureteric catheter. Children ≈ age + 10 cm |
| Coils | Double-pigtail, J, multi-length, loop-tail (fewer symptoms), open/closed tip |
| Flow | Mostly **extraluminal** + side holes; poor in malignant extrinsic compression → tandem/metallic stents or PCN (percutaneous nephrostomy) |
| Features | Radio-opaque, hydrophilic coating, extraction string |

**Diagram to draw:** double-J in situ (renal coil, side holes, bladder coil; length/Fr labelled).

### Materials
| Material | Features |
|---|---|
| Polyethylene | Rigid, brittle; abandoned |
| Silicone | Most biocompatible, least encrustation; soft, high friction |
| Polyurethane | Stiff, cheap; more encrustation/irritation |
| Copolymers (Percuflex, C-Flex) | Strength + softness; soften at body temperature |
| **Metallic – Resonance** | Resists compression in malignant obstruction; up to 12 months; still encrusts |
| **Thermo-expandable nitinol – Memokath 051** | Expands with warm saline, cooling softens for removal; long-term strictures |
| Coated | **Heparin** (anti-encrustation), **triclosan** (no convincing benefit), hydrogel, silver, drug-eluting (investigational) |
| Biodegradable | No removal/forgotten stent; experimental (fragmentation, uneven degradation) |

### Special stents
- Endopyelotomy: tapered 14/7 or 8.2/6 Fr across incised PUJ (pelviureteric junction).
- Magnetic-tip: removed by magnetic catheter, no cystoscopy. Stent on string: avoids cystoscopy, dislodgement risk.
- Others: mono-J (diversion), anti-reflux valve, tail stents.

### Indications
- **Obstruction**: stone with sepsis (stent = PCN), malignant extrinsic, stricture, pregnancy.
- **After intervention**: URS (ureteroscopy, selected), endopyelotomy, pyeloplasty, reimplant, transplant.
- **Healing**: injury, leak, fistula.
- **Prophylactic**: complex pelvic surgery, pre-ESWL (extracorporeal shock wave lithotripsy, selected), passive dilatation.

### Stent-related symptoms
- Up to ~80%: frequency, urgency, suprapubic/flank pain (on voiding = reflux), haematuria, sexual dysfunction.
- **USSQ (Ureteral Stent Symptom Questionnaire; Joshi 2003)**: validated tool.
- Mechanism: trigonal irritation (bladder coil crossing midline), reflux, spasm.
- Rx: **alpha-blocker + antimuscarinic** (combination > either); correct length, soft/tail stents, shortest dwell.

### Complications
| Complication | Notes |
|---|---|
| Migration | Proximal (too short) → URS retrieval; distal → symptoms/expulsion |
| Infection | Colonisation near-universal with longer dwell; biofilm |
| Haematuria | Common, mild; rare uretero-arterial fistula |
| Encrustation | ↑ with time (>6 weeks–3 months); stone formers, urease UTI (urinary tract infection; Proteus), pregnancy, CKD (chronic kidney disease) |
| **Forgotten stent** | Encrustation, obstruction, sepsis, renal loss → registries, SMS reminders |
| Others | Fracture, knotting, erosion, VUR (vesicoureteral reflux) |

**FECal (Forgotten, Encrusted, Calcified) grading** (Acosta-Miranda 2009): I minimal linear; II circular; III partial, one/both coils; IV complete, one coil; V complete, both coils.

### Encrusted stent
- Never pull forcefully (avulsion). NCCT (non-contrast computed tomography) KUB (kidney-ureter-bladder) for burden; DMSA (dimercaptosuccinic acid scan) if function doubtful; treat UTI first.
- **Bladder coil** (cystolithotripsy) → **ureter** (URS laser) → **renal coil** (fURS, or PCNL (percutaneous nephrolithotomy) for large burden); ESWL adjunct.
- Single session where possible; staged if heavy; nephrectomy if non-functioning. Metabolic evaluation.

### Ureteroscopy
| | Semirigid | Flexible (fURS) |
|---|---|---|
| Size | ~6–8.5 Fr tip | ~7.5–9.5 Fr shaft |
| Best for | Distal/mid ureter | Upper ureter, renal (RIRS: retrograde intrarenal surgery) |
| Deflection | None | Up to ~270° both directions |
| Optics | Fibre-optic/rod lens | **Digital** chip-on-tip |

- **UAS (ureteral access sheath)** 9.5/11.5–12/14 Fr: ↓intrarenal pressure, better vision, repeated passage; but **ureteral wall injury** (Traxer 2013; ~1 in 8 significant). Pre-stenting ↓injury.
- **Ho:YAG (holmium:YAG) 2120 nm**: gold standard (Moses pulse-modulation). **TFL (thulium fibre laser) ~1940 nm**: thinner fibres, fine dust, less retropulsion.
- Dusting: faster, no UAS, residual fines. Fragmentation + basketing: confirmed stone-free, stone analysis.

**Steps**: antibiotics, sterile urine → cystoscopy + safety guidewire → semirigid inspection ± dilatation → UAS under fluoroscopy → low-pressure irrigation, laser, basket → inspect ureter on withdrawal (PULS: Post-Ureteroscopic Lesion Scale) → stent decision.

### Stenting after URS
- **AUA (American Urological Association)/Endourological Society 2016**: may omit stent after **uncomplicated** URS.
- **EAU (European Association of Urology) 2024**: do not routinely stent after uncomplicated URS; pre-stenting not routine but ↑SFR (stone-free rate).
- RCTs (randomised controlled trials)/meta-analyses: no stent → less LUTS (lower urinary tract symptoms)/pain, cost; unplanned visits not significantly ↑.

**Stent if**: injury/perforation or significant UAS injury; significant residual fragments; solitary/transplant kidney; impacted stone/oedema; pregnancy; prolonged procedure, UTI/sepsis; renal insufficiency, bilateral URS; stricture; planned second-look.

**Duration**: uncomplicated 3–7 days (on string); injury 2–6 weeks; stricture/endopyelotomy 4–6 weeks. Overnight ureteric catheter = alternative.

```
After URS -> uncomplicated? (no injury, stone-free, normal anatomy, no infection)
  Yes -> no stent (or string stent 3-7 d / overnight catheter)
  No  -> double-J: injury 2-6 wk; residual/impacted/oedema 1-2 wk;
         solitary/pregnancy/UTI/CKD individualise
```

### Recent advances
- **Suction UAS incl. FANS (flexible and navigable suction access sheath)**: ↓intrarenal pressure/temperature, better SFR, fewer infections.
- TFL and pulse-modulated high-power Ho:YAG.
- **Single-use digital flexible ureteroscopes**.

## III-2. Describe medical management of VUR. Describe treatment of an 8-yr male child with b/l grade IV VUR.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

### Definition
- **VUR (vesicoureteral reflux)**: backflow of urine from bladder into ureter/upper tract.
- **Primary**: congenital UVJ (ureterovesical junction) flap-valve deficiency; short tunnel (normal length : diameter ~5:1, Paquin).
- **Secondary**: PUV (posterior urethral valves), neurogenic bladder, BBD (bladder and bowel dysfunction), iatrogenic.
- Prevalence ~1% normal children, ~30–40% with febrile UTI (urinary tract infection), ~30% siblings.

### Pathophysiology
- Reflux + infection → pyelonephritis; **compound (polar) papillae** → intrarenal reflux → **scarring** → hypertension, proteinuria, CKD (chronic kidney disease) = **reflux nephropathy**.
- Congenital dysplasia esp. **boys with high-grade antenatal VUR**.

### IRS (International Reflux Study) grading (VCUG: voiding cystourethrogram)
| Grade | Findings |
|---|---|
| I | Ureter only (non-dilated) |
| II | Ureter, pelvis, calyces; no dilatation |
| III | Mild–moderate dilatation; minimal fornix blunting |
| IV | Moderate dilatation/tortuosity; fornices obliterated, **papillary impressions maintained** |
| V | Gross dilatation/tortuosity; **papillary impressions lost**; intraparenchymal reflux |

**Diagram to draw:** grades I–V sketches; Cohen cross-trigonal reimplant.

### Natural history
- Grade, laterality, age, BBD matter.
- I–II ~80%+ resolve over ~5 years; III ~50%; IV unilateral ~50–60% at 5 years, **bilateral ~10%**; V rarely.
- Mostly resolves before puberty.

### Evaluation
- History: febrile UTIs, BBD (diary, constipation). Exam: BP, spine, genitalia.
- Urine culture, creatinine, urine protein/creatinine.
- **RBUS (renal-bladder ultrasound)**: hydronephrosis, size, cortex, bladder wall, PVR (post-void residual).
- **VCUG**: grade, urethra (exclude PUV), diverticulum, duplex. Radionuclide cystogram/ceVUS (contrast-enhanced voiding urosonography) for follow-up.
- **DMSA (dimercaptosuccinic acid scan)**: scars, DRF (differential renal function).
- Uroflow + PVR ± urodynamics if BBD/secondary VUR suspected.

### Medical management
**1. CAP (continuous antibiotic prophylaxis)** – single nightly dose, ~¼ therapeutic
| Drug | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| TMP-SMX (trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole) | **2 mg/kg TMP** (10 mg/kg SMX) | Avoid <2 months (kernicterus) |
| Nitrofurantoin | **1–2 mg/kg** | Avoid CKD/G6PD deficiency |
| Cephalexin/amoxicillin | Cephalexin ~10 mg/kg | Infants <2 months |

**2. Evidence**
- **RIVUR (NEJM 2014)**: 607 children, 2–71 months, grade I–IV; TMP-SMX vs placebo → **recurrent UTI halved** (HR (hazard ratio) ~0.50), most benefit with **BBD**; **no difference in new scarring**; more resistant breakthrough organisms.
- **Swedish Reflux Trial (Brandström 2010)**: 1–2 yr, grade III–IV; CAP vs endoscopic vs surveillance → in **girls** CAP/endoscopy ↓febrile UTI, more scars on surveillance; **boys** few recurrences in any arm.

**3. BBD treatment** (first, in toilet-trained): timed voiding (2–3 h), double voiding, treat constipation, urotherapy/biofeedback, anticholinergics/alpha-blockers, CIC (clean intermittent catheterisation) if needed. BBD ↓resolution, ↑breakthrough UTI, ↓endoscopic success.

**4. Circumcision**: ↓UTI in infant boys (EAU/ESPU (European Association of Urology/European Society for Paediatric Urology) for VUR in first year).

**5. Surveillance without CAP**: low-grade, no BBD, older child.

**6. Follow-up**: RBUS 6–12 months; VCUG/radionuclide 12–24 months; DMSA if breakthrough; BP, proteinuria.

### Guidelines
- **AUA (American Urological Association) 2010 (>1 year)**: CAP recommended with **BBD + VUR**, may be considered without; treat BBD first; breakthrough febrile UTI → change therapy/consider intervention.
- **EAU/ESPU**: **high-risk** (grade IV–V, scars, BBD, febrile UTI) → CAP, **early intervention** if breakthrough/persistent high grade; low-risk → surveillance or CAP. Reimplant for high grade, endoscopic for low–moderate.

**Indications for intervention**: breakthrough febrile UTI; new scarring/↓DRF; persistent IV–V unlikely to resolve; CAP non-compliance; paraureteric diverticulum/duplex; parental preference; persistent VUR at puberty in girls.

### Case: 8-yr boy, bilateral grade IV VUR
**Assessment**
- Exclude secondary: **PUV** (VCUG urethra), neurogenic bladder (spine), **BBD** (diary, uroflow, PVR).
- UTI/breakthrough history; DMSA, creatinine/eGFR (estimated glomerular filtration rate), BP, proteinuria.
- Bilateral IV at 8 → **resolution ~10%**, few years to puberty.

**Options**
1. **CAP + BBD management**: if no febrile UTI, no scars, good bladder; boys past infancy have lower UTI risk. Cons: long-term CAP, resistance, compliance.
2. **Endoscopic Deflux (dextranomer/hyaluronic acid)**: STING (subureteric) or **HIT/double-HIT** (hydrodistension-implantation). **Elder 2006** one-injection success: I–II ~79%, III ~72%, **IV ~63%**, V ~51%; ↑with repeat. Day-care; lower durability, late recurrence; ↓in BBD/duplex.
3. **Ureteral reimplantation** (gold standard high grade, **~95–98%**):
   - **Cohen cross-trigonal**: commonest, reliable; later URS access difficult. Politano-Leadbetter, Glenn-Anderson.
   - **Extravesical Lich-Gregoir**: less haematuria/spasm; **bilateral → transient retention risk** (pelvic plexus), ↓with nerve-sparing.
   - **RALUR (robotic-assisted laparoscopic ureteral reimplantation)**: less pain, shorter stay; ~85–95%.
   - **Bilateral Cohen** ideal: one bladder opening corrects both.

**Plan**
- Shared decision; treat BBD before surgery.
- **Febrile UTI/breakthrough, scars, DRF loss** → **bilateral reimplant** (open Cohen or robotic extravesical); Deflux acceptable alternative (~60–70% per session).
- **Asymptomatic, no scars, normal bladder** → CAP + BBD therapy, yearly imaging; intervene if UTI/new scar.

**Postop**: catheter 1–2 days, anticholinergics for spasm; CAP ~3 months; RBUS 4–6 weeks and ~1 year; VCUG optional after open (AUA), usually after Deflux; long-term BP, proteinuria, renal growth. Complications: persistent/contralateral VUR, obstruction.

```
Bilateral grade IV VUR, 8 yr
 -> exclude PUV/neurogenic, treat BBD; DMSA, creatinine, BP
 -> breakthrough UTI / scars / DRF loss / parental choice
      -> open Cohen (~95-98%) | RALUR | Deflux (~63%, repeat)
 -> no UTI, no scars -> CAP + BBD rx, RBUS 6-12 mo, VCUG 12-24 mo
      -> UTI / new scar / persistence -> intervention
```

### Recent advances
- ceVUS avoids radiation; "top-down" (DMSA first) approach.
- Vantris (polyacrylate-polyalcohol) bulking agent.
- Selective CAP after RIVUR; improving RALUR outcomes.

## III-3a. Sacral neuromodulation
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition and mechanism
- **SNM (sacral neuromodulation)**: continuous low-amplitude stimulation of **S3 root** via tined lead + IPG (implantable pulse generator).
- **Afferent modulation** (not motor): inhibits abnormal bladder reflexes at spinal/supraspinal levels → works in **both overactivity and retention**; suppresses sphincter "guarding reflex" in retention.

### Indications (FDA: Food and Drug Administration)
| Indication | FDA |
|---|---|
| Refractory UUI (urgency urinary incontinence) | 1997 |
| Urgency-frequency | 1999 |
| **Non-obstructive retention** (incl. **Fowler's syndrome**) | 1999 |
| Faecal incontinence | 2011 |
- **Fowler's**: young women, retention, abnormal sphincter EMG (electromyography); SNM best treatment.

### Devices
- Medtronic InterStim (II, Micro rechargeable, X recharge-free); Axonics. Newer = full-body MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) conditional.

### Technique
1. **Test phase**: PNE (percutaneous nerve evaluation) 3–7 days (false negatives from migration) vs **staged tined lead** ~1–2 weeks (more reliable).
2. Prone; needle into **S3 foramen** (≈9 cm above coccyx tip, ~2 cm lateral).
3. **S3 response: bellows + big-toe plantar flexion**. S2: anal clamp, whole-foot. S4: bellows only.
4. Motor response on ≥3 of 4 electrodes.
5. **≥50% improvement** in diary → stage 2 IPG in upper buttock.

### Outcomes
- **InSite (Siegel 2015)**: SNM > standard medical therapy at 6 months; durable at 5 years.
- **ROSETTA (JAMA 2016)**: Botox 200 U vs SNM, refractory UUI → Botox slightly greater ↓UUI at 6 months, but **more UTIs (urinary tract infections) and CISC (clean intermittent self-catheterisation)**; SNM had revisions/removals.
- Retention: ~70% of responders void.

### Complications
- IPG-site pain, lead migration, loss of efficacy, infection (explant), battery depletion. Revision historically ~30%+, lower now.

### Contraindications
- Failed test, cannot use programmer/keep diary, sacral anomaly, repeated MRI with old devices; relative: pregnancy, bleeding disorder.

### Recent advances
- Rechargeable/recharge-free MRI-compatible IPGs; tibial nerve stimulation (PTNS: percutaneous tibial nerve stimulation; implantable).

**Diagram to draw:** sacrum with S3 lead + buttock IPG.

## III-3b. Uses of buccal mucosa in urology
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### History
- **BMG (buccal mucosal graft)**: workhorse free graft for urethroplasty.
- **Humby 1941** (hypospadias); **Bürger 1992/El-Kasaby 1993** (stricture); Morey & McAninch 1996 (ventral onlay); Barbagli 1996 (dorsal onlay).

### Advantages
- Thick **non-keratinised** epithelium, infection-resistant.
- **Thin, vascular lamina propria + panlaminar plexus** → excellent take.
- Hairless, wet-adapted, easy harvest, hidden scar; usable in lichen sclerosus.

### Harvest
- Nasotracheal intubation; ~4–6 × 2–2.5 cm per cheek.
- Stay **1 cm from commissure**; **avoid Stensen's duct opposite upper 2nd molar**.
- Infiltrate saline ± adrenaline, excise mucosa without muscle, haemostasis; close or leave open (evidence mixed); **defat** on back table.
- Alternative: **lingual graft** (Simonato 2006).
- Donor morbidity (mostly transient): pain, numbness, trismus, duct injury, bleeding.

### Uses
| Area | Use |
|---|---|
| **Anterior urethroplasty** | Bulbar: dorsal onlay (Barbagli), ventral onlay (Morey), **dorsal inlay (Asopa 2001)**, **Kulkarni one-sided dorsal** (2009; preserves ventral blood supply, bulbospongiosus), augmented anastomotic (excise obliterative segment + graft rest). Penile: dorsal inlay/onlay |
| **Staged** (Johanson, Bracka) | Lay open + graft → tubularise ~6 months; **lichen sclerosus**, failed hypospadias, panurethral |
| Hypospadias | Redo/"cripples" |
| Bladder neck/vesico-urethral stenosis | Robotic BMG onlay (emerging) |
| **Ureter** | **Robotic BMG ureteroplasty (Zhao)** + omental wrap for long proximal/mid strictures; avoids ileal ureter |
| Others | Vaginoplasty, female urethral stricture, Peyronie's (rarely), neourethra in exstrophy (selected) |

### Outcomes
- Anterior urethroplasty **~85–90%** (dorsal = ventral in bulbar); lower in penile, lichen sclerosus, redo.
- Robotic ureteroplasty ~90% short-term.

### Recent advances
- Tissue-engineered oral mucosa (investigational); robotic BMG for ureter/bladder neck; single-cheek harvest without closure.

**Diagram to draw:** bulbar cross-section: dorsal onlay, ventral onlay, Asopa inlay.

## III-3c. HSG in neonates
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

*HSG (hysterosalpingography) is not done in neonates → taken as "USG (ultrasonography) in neonates".*

### Introduction and timing
- First-line, radiation-free; mainly for **ANH (antenatal hydronephrosis)**, ~1–2% of pregnancies.
- Indications: ANH, febrile UTI (urinary tract infection), mass, oligohydramnios/syndromes, poor stream, renal failure.
- **Avoid first 48–72 h** (oliguria underestimates dilatation); **within 24–48 h** if suspected **PUV (posterior urethral valves)**, bilateral severe HDN (hydronephrosis), solitary kidney, oligohydramnios. Normal/mild → **repeat 4–6 weeks**.
- **APD (anteroposterior diameter)** on transverse view at hilum.

### Normal neonatal kidney
- ~4–5 cm; **prominent hypoechoic pyramids** (not hydronephrosis); **cortex iso/hyperechoic to liver**; fetal lobulation.

### Grading
- **SFU (Society for Fetal Urology) 0–4**: 0 none; 1 pelvis; 2 + some calyces; 3 all calyces; 4 + parenchymal thinning.

**UTD (urinary tract dilation) 2014 – postnatal (≥48 h)**
| Grade | Findings |
|---|---|
| Normal | APD <10 mm |
| **P1** (low) | APD **10–<15 mm** or central calyces |
| **P2** (intermediate) | APD **≥15 mm**, peripheral calyces or **ureteral dilatation** |
| **P3** (high) | P2 + **abnormal parenchyma** or **bladder** |

### Specific conditions
| Condition | USG findings |
|---|---|
| PUJO (pelviureteric junction obstruction) | Pelvicalyceal dilatation, normal ureter/bladder |
| VUR (vesicoureteral reflux) | Fluctuating dilatation; **normal USG does not exclude** |
| **PUV** | Bilateral HUN (hydroureteronephrosis), **thick bladder**, dilated posterior urethra; **keyhole sign antenatal**; confirm by VCUG (voiding cystourethrogram) |
| MCDK (multicystic dysplastic kidney) | **Non-communicating** cysts |
| Ureterocele | Thin-walled intravesical cyst, upper-pole hydro |
| ARPKD (autosomal recessive polycystic kidney disease) | Bilateral **enlarged echogenic** kidneys |
| Adrenal haemorrhage | **Avascular** suprarenal mass (neuroblastoma vascular) |

### Follow-up
```
Postnatal USG (day 3-7; 24-48 h if PUV/bilateral)
Normal -> repeat 4-6 wk; P1 -> USG 1-6 mo
P2 -> USG 1-3 mo +/- VCUG, MAG3 renogram, antibiotic prophylaxis
P3 -> USG 1 mo, VCUG, MAG3/DMSA scans, prophylaxis; urgent if PUV
```

## III-3d. Types of penile prosthesis. What are biofilms in penile prosthesis?
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Introduction
- Third-line for ED (erectile dysfunction) failing PDE5i (phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors)/intracavernosal therapy; satisfaction **>85–90%**.

### Types
| Type | Examples | Pros / cons |
|---|---|---|
| **Malleable** | Genesis, Spectra, Tactra | Cheap, simple, no mechanical failure / constant rigidity, erosion |
| **2-piece** | **Ambicor** (pump-reservoir in scrotum) | No abdominal reservoir (prior pelvic surgery, transplant) / less flaccidity, girth |
| **3-piece IPP (inflatable penile prosthesis)** | **AMS 700 CX/LGX/CXR (narrow, fibrosis); Titan/Titan Touch** | Most natural / cost, mechanical failure |

- IPP: cylinders, scrotal pump (lock-out valve), reservoir in **Retzius** or **ectopic submuscular** (after pelvic surgery). Penoscrotal approach commonest. Mechanical survival ~80–90% at 5 years, 60–70% at 10 years.
- Coatings: **InhibiZone = rifampin + minocycline**; **Coloplast hydrophilic** dipped in antibiotic → infection ~halved.

### Biofilm
- **Definition**: structured microbial community in a self-produced **EPS (extracellular polymeric substance)** matrix adherent to the implant; slow-growing persister cells.
- **Stages**: conditioning film → reversible/irreversible adhesion → microcolonies → maturation (quorum sensing) → dispersal (planktonic seeding) → clinical infection.
- **Organisms**: **CoNS (coagulase-negative staphylococci), S. epidermidis** commonest; S. aureus, Gram-negatives, anaerobes, Candida (diabetics); often polymicrobial.
- **Antibiotic failure**: poor penetration, dormant cells; **MIC (minimum inhibitory concentration) 100–1000×** higher; subclinical biofilm on many uninfected devices.

### Infection
- **~1–3% virgin**; higher in revision, poorly controlled diabetes, SCI (spinal cord injury), immunosuppression.
- **Prevention**: preop UTI (urinary tract infection) control, chlorhexidine-alcohol, IV antibiotics, **"no-touch" (Eid)**, coated device, irrigation, short surgery.
- **Mulcahy salvage (1996)**: remove all components → **7-solution washout** → immediate replacement; **~80–90%**. Not with sepsis, necrosis, erosion, DKA (diabetic ketoacidosis).
- Otherwise explant + delayed reimplant or malleable placeholder.

### Recent advances
- Tactra malleable (2019), Rigicon Infla10 IPP; ectopic reservoir standard after pelvic surgery.

## III-3e. Uretero-vaginal fistula
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition and aetiology
- **UVF (uretero-vaginal fistula)**: ureter–vagina communication → continuous leak per vaginum.
- **Hysterectomy** (abdominal > laparoscopic/vaginal): commonest in developed countries; injury at uterine artery crossing, cardinal ligament, vaginal cuff (clamp, ligature, thermal, devascularisation).
- **Obstetric** (caesarean, obstructed labour): common in developing countries incl. India.
- Others: malignancy, **radiation**, colorectal/vascular surgery, URS (ureteroscopy).

### Presentation
- **Continuous leak + normal voiding**, **1–4 weeks** post-surgery; flank pain, fever, urinoma; hydronephrosis often.

### Investigations
- **Three-swab test** (bladder methylene blue): blue = VVF (vesicovaginal fistula); **wet, clear = UVF**; oral phenazopyridine confirms ureteric source.
- **CTU (CT urography)**: extravasation, hydroureteronephrosis, urinoma.
- **Cystoscopy + RGP (retrograde pyelogram)**: site, wire passage, stent same sitting. Antegrade nephrostogram if fails.
- **Exclude coexisting VVF (~10–15%)**, bilateral injury. Creatinine, culture.

### Management
**Principles**: drain kidney, preserve function, treat sepsis, exclude malignancy.

**1. Endourological (first line if continuity preserved)**
- **Retrograde double-J** if wire crosses; else **PCN (percutaneous nephrostomy) + antegrade** or **rendezvous**.
- Stent ~6–8 weeks → RGP/CT; many heal; stricture → dilatation.

**2. Surgical** (stent failure, transection, ligation, stricture)
- Distal (juxtavesical) → **ureteroneocystostomy**: **psoas hitch** (~5 cm), **Boari flap** (~10–15 cm).
- Long defects: TUU (transureteroureterostomy), ileal ureter, autotransplant.
- **Early repair** (first weeks, stable, no sepsis) now preferred over 3–6 month wait; delay if infection, radiation, poor condition.
- Open, laparoscopic or robotic; radiation → omental interposition.

```
Leak + normal voiding -> 3-swab: clear wet = UVF
 -> CTU + cysto/RGP (exclude VVF, bilateral)
 -> wire passes? Yes -> double-J 6-8 wk
                 No  -> PCN +/- antegrade/rendezvous
 -> fails -> reimplant + psoas hitch/Boari, early if fit
```

### Prevention / advances
- Identify ureter at hysterectomy; selective prophylactic stents; intraoperative cystoscopy; ICG (indocyanine green) fluorescence; robotic reimplant.

**Diagram to draw:** three-swab test; psoas hitch.

## III-3f. Prune belly syndrome
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition
- **Eagle–Barrett/triad syndrome**: (1) deficient abdominal musculature; (2) urinary tract dilatation; (3) bilateral intra-abdominal undescended testes.
- ~1 in 30,000–50,000 live births; **~95% male**.

### Pathogenesis
- **Mesodermal arrest** (~6–10 weeks) vs **urethral obstruction theory** (fetal outlet obstruction → bladder distension).
- Yolk-sac theory (less accepted).
- Mostly sporadic; **CHRM3** in familial cases; HNF1B, FLNA reported; ↑in twins.

### Features
- **Kidneys**: dysplasia (determines prognosis). **Ureters**: dilated, tortuous; VUR (vesicoureteral reflux) ~75%.
- **Bladder**: large, smooth, poor contractility, large PVR (post-void residual), urachal pseudodiverticulum.
- **Prostatic hypoplasia**: dilated triangular prostatic urethra; ± megalourethra.
- **Testes**: intra-abdominal; infertility, malignancy risk.
- Extra-GU (genitourinary): pulmonary hypoplasia; cardiac ~10%; orthopaedic ~45%; GI (gastrointestinal) malrotation.

### Woodard classification
| Category | Features | Outlook |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Oligohydramnios, **pulmonary hypoplasia**, renal dysplasia | Fatal |
| **II** | Full triad, moderate–severe uropathy, **no pulmonary hypoplasia** | Urosepsis/CKD (chronic kidney disease) risk |
| **III** | Mild/incomplete, **stable renal function** | Good |

### Management
- **Neonatal**: respiratory support; creatinine (nadir prognostic); USG (ultrasonography), **VCUG (voiding cystourethrogram) with caution**, DMSA (dimercaptosuccinic acid)/MAG3 (mercaptoacetyltriglycine) scans; CAP (continuous antibiotic prophylaxis); avoid instrumentation.
- **Conservative** for most: CAP, timed/double voiding, CIC (clean intermittent catheterisation).
- **Drainage** (infection, rising creatinine, obstruction): **cutaneous vesicostomy** preferred; pyelostomy/ureterostomy for upper tract.
- **Reconstruction** (selected): reduction cystoplasty, ureteral tailoring + reimplant, Mitrofanoff.
- **Orchiopexy**: **transabdominal**, **by 6–12 months**; Fowler–Stephens for short vessels; combine with abdominoplasty.
- **Abdominoplasty**: Randolph, Ehrlich, **Monfort** (most popular).
- **Prognosis**: ~one-third CKD; transplant results similar to other causes.

### Variants
- Females ~5% (no testicular component); "pseudo-prune belly" = incomplete triad, milder.
- Recent: fetal vesicoamniotic shunting (benefit unproven); laparoscopic Fowler–Stephens with abdominoplasty.

#### III-1. Discuss various ureteric stents. Discuss ureteroscopy with or without stenting. (LAQ)

#### III-2. Describe medical management of VUR. Describe treatment of an 8-yr male child with b/l grade IV VUR. (LAQ)

#### III-3a. Sacral neuromodulation (SAQ)

#### III-3b. Uses of buccal mucosa in urology (SAQ)

#### III-3d. Types of penile prosthesis. What are biofilms in penile prosthesis? (SAQ)

#### III-3e. Uretero-vaginal fistula (SAQ)

- Hysterectomy commonest; obstetric in developing countries.
- Continuous leak + normal voiding; clear wet swab = UVF.
- Exclude coexisting VVF (~10–15%).
- Stent if wire passes; else psoas hitch/Boari reimplant, early if fit.

#### III-3f. Prune belly syndrome (SAQ)

- Triad: deficient abdominal wall, urinary dilatation, intra-abdominal testes; ~95% male.
- Woodard I fatal (pulmonary hypoplasia); II uropathy; III mild.
- Mostly conservative; vesicostomy if drainage needed.
- Transabdominal orchiopexy by 6–12 months + Monfort abdominoplasty; ~one-third CKD.
