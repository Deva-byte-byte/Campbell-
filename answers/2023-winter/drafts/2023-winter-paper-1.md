## Q1. Principles of tissue engineering and its use in urology
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)
(also answered in 2022-Summer Paper 4, Q2; this answer stresses the principles)

### Definition and rationale
- **Tissue engineering** applies engineering and life sciences to build biological substitutes that restore, maintain or improve tissue function (Langer and Vacanti, 1993).
- **Why in urology:** bowel in the urinary tract causes mucus, stones, metabolic acidosis, infection and cancer; grafts for long urethral defects are scarce. The aim is to replace "wrong tissue" with "right tissue".

### Principles: the triad plus bioreactor and blood supply
```
Biopsy -> cells expanded in culture -> seeded on scaffold (+ signals)
       -> conditioned in bioreactor -> implanted -> host vascularises and remodels
```

**1. Cells**
| Source | Comment |
|---|---|
| Autologous differentiated cells (urothelial and smooth muscle cells from a bladder biopsy) | No rejection; expand well; not usable if the organ is malignant |
| Adult stem cells: bone marrow or adipose mesenchymal, muscle-derived, urine-derived | Easy harvest; act largely by paracrine (growth factor) effects |
| Embryonic stem cells | Pluripotent; ethical issues, teratoma risk |
| iPSCs (induced pluripotent stem cells) | Adult cells reprogrammed; patient-specific; tumour risk |

**2. Scaffolds (biomaterials)**
- **Requirements:** biocompatible; degrade at the rate new tissue forms; porous for cell entry and vessel ingrowth; mechanically suited (a compliant bladder, a non-collapsing tube).

| Type | Examples | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| Natural polymers | Collagen, alginate, silk | Good cell recognition; weak |
| Acellular tissue matrices | Bladder acellular matrix, SIS (small intestinal submucosa) | Native architecture and stored growth factors |
| Synthetic polymers | PGA (polyglycolic acid), PLA (polylactic acid), PLGA (their copolymer) | Controlled shape and degradation; no bioactivity |
| Newer | Electrospun nanofibres, hydrogels, bio-ink | Mimic extracellular matrix |

- **Seeding:** urothelial cells on the luminal side and smooth muscle cells on the outer side, copying native layers.
- **Unseeded** matrices rely on host cells migrating in, so they work only for short onlay defects (about 0.5 cm for tubes in animal work). Longer or tubular gaps need **seeded** scaffolds, or they fibrose and contract.

**3. Signals**
- Growth factors (for example VEGF, vascular endothelial growth factor, for vessels) released from the scaffold, extracellular-matrix cues and mechanical stimuli direct cell growth and differentiation.

**4. Bioreactors**
- Culture chambers that perfuse nutrients and oxygen through thick constructs and apply **physiological conditioning**, such as cyclic stretch and filling for bladder smooth muscle, so the tissue is functional before implantation.
- Types: static culture, spinner flask, rotating wall, perfusion and mechanical-stretch bioreactors. They also allow monitoring and sterile, standardised production.

**5. Vascularisation and innervation**
- Oxygen diffuses only about 100–200 µm, so thick constructs die centrally without vessels. This is the main barrier.
- Strategies: **omental wrap** (used by Atala), angiogenic factors, co-seeding endothelial cells, built-in channels by bioprinting.
- Innervation and coordinated contraction cannot yet be recreated.

### Uses in urology
| Organ | Status |
|---|---|
| **Bladder** | **Atala et al. (2006):** seeded collagen–PGA constructs with omental wrap in 7 myelomeningocele patients; compliance improved. Industry phase II trial: **no significant gain in compliance or capacity**. Experimental (Chapter 48). |
| **Urinary conduit** | Neo-Urinary Conduit trial (NCT01087687): survived about 250 days on average; **half developed stenosis**; stiff material, no blood supply (Chapter 135) |
| **Urethra** | Acellular matrix onlay for short strictures and hypospadias; **Raya-Rivera et al. (2011):** seeded tubular PGA–PLGA urethras in **5 boys**, functional up to 6 years; tissue-engineered oral mucosa |
| **Sphincter** | Autologous muscle-derived cells for SUI (stress urinary incontinence); biodegradable scaffold slings |
| **Vagina** | Engineered vaginas in MRKH (Mayer–Rokitansky–Küster–Hauser) syndrome (Raya-Rivera, 2014) |
| **Penis** | Corporal tissue and tunica grafts (stem cell-seeded SIS in Peyronie disease): animal or experimental; stem cells for ED (erectile dysfunction) investigational |
| **Kidney** | Decellularised–recellularised scaffolds (animal), kidney organoids, renal assist devices |
| **Testis** | Spermatogonial stem cell banking in prepubertal cancer patients |

### Recent advances
- **3D bioprinting:** cells in hydrogel "bio-ink" laid layer by layer from CT (computed tomography) or MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) data; vascular channels built in. Printed (non-living) models already aid planning.
- **Organoids:** kidney organoids from iPSCs; bladder and prostate tumour organoids for drug testing.
- Robotic vascularised composite bladder allograft (Chapter 135): a transplant route that avoids bowel but needs immunosuppression.

### Challenges
- Vascularity, innervation, fibrosis and contracture; scale-up, cost, culture time, regulation; stem cell tumour risk; promising pilots followed by disappointing controlled trials.
- **Also know:** gene-modified cells; growth factor-releasing "smart" scaffolds; cell sheet engineering.

**Diagram to draw:** biopsy → culture → seeding on scaffold → bioreactor → implant with omental wrap.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Triad: cells, scaffold, signals; plus bioreactor and blood supply.
> - Diffusion limit about 100–200 µm: vascularity is the main barrier.
> - Atala 2006 promising; phase II trial showed no gain; conduit half stenosed.
> - Raya-Rivera 2011: seeded urethras in 5 boys.

## Q2. Enlist various grafts used in urology and role of BMG in urology
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)
(BMG uses also in 2015-Summer Paper 3, Q3b; vascular grafts in 2021-Summer Paper 3, Q2)

### Graft versus flap
| | Graft | Flap |
|---|---|---|
| Blood supply | Separated; gets a new supply from the bed | Moved with its own pedicle |
| Depends on | A well-vascularised host bed | Its pedicle |
| Example | BMG (buccal mucosal graft) | Penile fasciocutaneous island flap |

### Graft take (about 96 hours)
| Phase | Duration | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Imbibition** | about 48 h | Graft "drinks" nutrients from the bed; graft is cooler than core |
| **Inosculation** | about 48 h | Bed and graft vessels connect; true microcirculation returns |
- Take fails with poor beds (scar, radiation), haematoma, seroma, infection or shear. Hence **quilting or pie-crusting** and **immobilisation for 5–10 days** (bolster or vacuum dressing).

### Grafts used in urology
| Group | Graft | Main uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Skin** | **STSG** (split-thickness skin graft), meshed | Staged urethroplasty only; penile and scrotal cover (Fournier gangrene, buried penis) |
| | **FTSG** (full-thickness skin graft) | Fossa navicularis, penile urethra (avoid in lichen sclerosus) |
| | Posterior auricular (Wolfe) FTSG | Staged hypospadias repair |
| **Mucosa** | **Buccal, lingual, labial (oral mucosa)** | Urethra, hypospadias, ureter |
| | Bladder epithelium | Urethra, hypospadias |
| | Rectal (colonic) mucosa | Panurethral stricture when oral mucosa is unavailable |
| **Other autologous** | Dermis | Tunica albuginea in Peyronie disease |
| | Tunica vaginalis | Peyronie disease, urethral cover; easy harvest |
| | Saphenous vein | Peyronie defects; vascular repair |
| | Rectus fascia, fascia lata | Pubovaginal sling |
| **Allograft / xenograft** | Pericardium, porcine SIS, porcine dermis, cadaveric fascia | Peyronie grafting ("off the shelf"); slings |
| **Synthetic** | Polypropylene (macroporous type I mesh) | Mid-urethral sling, prolapse |
| | Dacron, PTFE (polytetrafluoroethylene) | Vascular grafts; not advised for Peyronie (infection, fibrosis) |
| **Newer** | Collagen fleece (TachoSil); acellular matrix, tissue-engineered oral mucosa | Peyronie; experimental urethra |

- Vein is not a true graft: it survives by direct endothelial perfusion and its vasa vasorum.
- **Peyronie grafts:** pericardium (thin, strong, does not contract) and SIS (cut about 25% oversize; contracts) are commonest; AUA gives no preference.
- **STSG versus FTSG:** STSG carries little dermis, takes easily, can be meshed (up to sixfold) but contracts. FTSG carries full dermis, contracts less, looks better but is more "fastidious" in take.

### Role of BMG
**Why it works**
- Thick, non-keratinised, elastin-rich epithelium used to a **wet environment**; resists infection.
- Thin, **highly vascular lamina propria** with a panlaminar plexus, so imbibition and inosculation are rapid.
- Hairless; little contraction; handles and holds sutures well; low donor morbidity.

**Harvest**
1. Nasotracheal intubation; mouth gag; mark the graft (often about 4–5 × 2 cm per cheek).
2. Stay 1 cm from the commissure and avoid **Stensen's (parotid) duct** opposite the upper second molar.
3. Infiltrate, incise, raise the mucosa off buccinator; haemostasis.
4. Donor site: leave open or close; both acceptable.
5. Back table: **defat** the graft, cut about 10% oversize, fenestrate with a needle.
- **Lingual** (under the tongue) and **labial** grafts are alternatives. Buccal and lingual give equal urethroplasty success; lip mucosa is thinner, suits the glans.

**Uses in urethroplasty**
| Technique | Principle | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Dorsal onlay** (Barbagli, 1995) | Dorsal urethrotomy; graft spread-fixed on corpora | Bulbar; reliable bed |
| **Ventral onlay** | Ventral urethrotomy; graft covered by spongioplasty | Bulbar with healthy spongiosum |
| **Dorsal inlay** (Asopa) | Ventral approach; dorsal plate incised; graft inlaid | Penile and bulbar |
| **Augmented anastomosis** | Excise narrowest part, floor anastomosis, graft roof | Long, dense bulbar stricture |
| **Kulkarni** (2012) | One-sided dissection; penile invagination; dorsal graft | Panurethral, one stage |
| **Staged** (Johanson, Bracka) | Stage 1 graft; tubularise after 6 months or more | Lichen sclerosus, failed hypospadias |
| **BMG perineal urethrostomy** | Graft completes dorsal wall to skin | Panurethral or failed repairs |

- Dorsal and ventral results are similar; onlay beats tubes. **Skin is avoided in lichen sclerosus** because the disease recurs in skin.

**Other uses**
- **Ureter:** BMG onlay ureteroplasty (first human report 1999), now robotic with an **omental wrap**; for long proximal or mid strictures in dense scar. CORRUS (Collaborative of Reconstructive Robotic Ureteral Surgery) failure about 15–25% by site.
- Redo and staged hypospadias; urethral closure in rectourethral fistula; bladder neck contracture; female urethral stricture; Peyronie grafting (small defects only).

**Complications**
- **Donor:** pain, swelling (first month), numbness (up to 6 months), mouth tightness, salivary duct injury.
- **Recipient:** graft failure, restricture, sacculation, fistula, ED.

**Outcomes**
- Bulbar BMG urethroplasty about 85–90% success; most failures within 12 months.

**Diagram to draw:** bulbar cross-section showing dorsal onlay, ventral onlay and dorsal inlay positions.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Take: imbibition 48 h, then inosculation 48 h; graft lives on its bed.
> - BMG: non-keratinised, vascular lamina propria, wet-adapted.
> - Avoid Stensen's duct; buccal equals lingual in success.
> - Staged BMG, not skin, in lichen sclerosus.

## Q3a. Management of perforated mid urethral sling
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Terminology (IUGA/ICS, International Urogynecological Association / International Continence Society)
- **Perforation:** mesh in a hollow organ (bladder, urethra). **Exposure:** mesh seen through unhealed vaginal epithelium. **Extrusion:** passage out of a tissue. "Erosion" is no longer used.
- Two settings: **intraoperative trocar injury** and **late mesh perforation**.

### A. Intraoperative trocar injury
- Bladder injury: 4.5% retropubic versus **0.6% transobturator**; registries 2.7–3.8%.
- **Detection:** cystoscopy with a **70° lens and full bladder distension** after every trocar pass.

| Injury | Management |
|---|---|
| **Bladder** | Withdraw trocar, **repass more laterally**, confirm on cystoscopy, complete the sling; catheter overnight to 72 h. Benign; no later mesh perforation. |
| **Urethra** | **Do not place mesh** (AUA/SUFU, American Urological Association / Society of Urodynamics, Female Pelvic Medicine and Urogenital Reconstruction). Repair, catheter drainage; **staged** anti-incontinence surgery after healing. |

### B. Late perforation of bladder or urethra
- **Presentation:** recurrent UTI (urinary tract infection), haematuria, urgency, pain, stones on mesh, voiding dysfunction, fistula. Often months later.
- **Evaluation:** examination, **cystourethroscopy**, translabial ultrasound (best imaging for mesh); exclude fistula.

**Management**
- **Bladder:** endoscopic excision (scissors, resection or holmium laser) flush with the wall; or transvesical laparoscopic or robotic excision; open cystotomy if large.
- **Urethra:** transvaginal excision of mesh in the field and layered urethral repair; **Martius flap** if complex or fistula.
- **Continence after excision:** SUI recurs in many (44–100% after synthetic sling revision). Options: concomitant **autologous pubovaginal sling** (Shah 2013: good results) or staged. A damaged urethra predicts poor continence.
- Avoid urethral dilation after mesh slings; it can cause perforation.

### Prevention
- Empty bladder; trocar hugging the pubis; bottom-up retropubic route; transobturator route when suitable.

**Also know:** Amid type I macroporous polypropylene integrates best; chronic pain or infection may need excision from both sides of the bone.

**Diagram to draw:** retropubic and transobturator trocar paths with bladder at risk.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Bladder trocar injury: repass laterally, continue.
> - Urethral injury: abandon mesh; stage the repair.
> - Late perforation: excise mesh, repair, consider autologous sling.

## Q3b. Robotic radical prostatectomy
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Introduction
- RARP (robot-assisted radical prostatectomy): Walsh's anatomical nerve-sparing principles done with **3D, 10× magnified vision and wristed instruments**. The robot removed the laparoscopic suturing barrier; da Vinci from 2000 (S, Si, Xi, SP).

### Indications and contraindications
- Localised cancer (cT1–T2, selected cT3); life expectancy over 10 years.
- **Absolute:** uncorrectable bleeding diathesis; unfit for general anaesthesia.
- **Relative:** morbid obesity, prostate >80 g, median lobe, prior TURP (transurethral resection of the prostate), salvage surgery.

### Set-up
- Low lithotomy, arms tucked, **steep Trendelenburg (22–25°)**, padding; pressure 12–15 mmHg; camera port 15–17 cm above pubis, three robotic and two assistant ports.

### Steps (transperitoneal anterior approach)
1. Drop bladder; enter space of Retzius.
2. Incise endopelvic fascia; ligate DVC (dorsal venous complex).
3. Divide bladder neck (fat line, balloon traction); check for median lobe.
4. Seminal vesicles and vasa; incise Denonvilliers fascia.
5. Clip pedicles athermally; **interfascial nerve sparing** (extrafascial if high risk).
6. Apical dissection preserving urethral length.
7. Extended PLND (pelvic lymph node dissection) if indicated.
8. Posterior (Rocco) reconstruction; **Van Velthoven running anastomosis**.

### Postoperative care
- ERAS (enhanced recovery after surgery); discharge within 24 h; catheter about 1 week (not before day 5: retention); pelvic floor training; penile rehabilitation with PDE5 (phosphodiesterase type 5) inhibitors.

### Outcomes
- **Yaxley 2016** randomised trial: equal function and early margins versus open; less blood loss (300 versus 1400 mL) and shorter stay.
- Margins about 15% (9% if pT2); 1-year continence mid-90s% in expert hands.

### Complications
- Transfusion 1–2%; bladder neck stricture <2% (open up to 16%); rectal injury; ureteric injury; lymphocele; positioning neuropathy, corneal abrasion; robot failure <0.5%.

### Recent advances
- **Retzius-sparing** (earlier continence, more positive margins); Hood technique; single-port robot; same-day discharge.

**Diagram to draw:** port layout and fascial planes (intra-, inter-, extrafascial).

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Surgeon, not platform, decides outcome (Yaxley 2016).
> - Interfascial plane is the standard nerve-sparing plane.
> - Bladder neck stricture <2%; transfusion 1–2%.

## Q3c. Kidney retrieval techniques for organ donation
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in 2021-Summer Paper 2, Q2, with donor selection)

### Types of donor
- **Deceased:** DBD (donation after brain death; circulation maintained) or DCD (donation after circulatory death; warm ischaemia).
- **Living:** related or unrelated (THOA, Transplantation of Human Organs Act, in India).

### A. Deceased multi-organ retrieval (DBD)
1. Midline laparotomy with sternotomy.
2. Mobilise bowel off retroperitoneum (Cattell–Braasch); expose aorta and IVC (inferior vena cava); **look for and preserve accessory renal arteries**.
3. Heparin; cannulate distal aorta; clamp supracoeliac aorta; **vent IVC**; flush cold UW (University of Wisconsin) or HTK (histidine–tryptophan–ketoglutarate) solution; surface ice; open Gerota's fascia laterally.
4. Heart, lungs, liver and pancreas removed first.
5. Kidneys removed **en bloc** with aorta, IVC, perinephric fat and ureters, keeping periureteric tissue.
- **DCD:** super-rapid laparotomy and direct aortic cannulation to cut warm ischaemia; usually machine perfusion.
- **Back table:** separate kidneys; aortic **Carrel patch**; IVC lengthens the short right renal vein; ligate lymphatics; biopsy marginal kidneys; package at 4 °C.
- Paediatric donors <15 kg: en bloc kidneys.

### B. Living donor nephrectomy
- **Principles:** donor safety first; maximal vessel length; keep periureteric tissue and the lower pole–ureter triangle; minimal handling; short warm ischaemia.
- **Left kidney preferred** (longer vein).

| Technique | Features |
|---|---|
| Open (flank or mini-incision) | Complex anatomy, more often right side |
| **Laparoscopic** (Ratner, 1995) | Preferred; less pain, faster recovery |
| **Hand-assisted** | About two-thirds of US cases; tactile control |
| Retroperitoneoscopic, LESS (laparoendoscopic single-site), robotic | Selected centres |
- Sequence: hilar dissection → ureter divided → artery then vein stapled or clipped → rapid extraction → cold flush.
- **Risks:** complications about 16%; mortality 3 per 10,000; conversion 0.26–3.34%.

### Preservation
- Cooling slows metabolism; hypothermic machine perfusion reduces DGF (delayed graft function), not long-term loss.

**Diagram to draw:** aortic cannula, supracoeliac clamp, IVC vent, en bloc kidneys.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Cannulate, clamp, vent, cool; kidneys removed last, en bloc.
> - Left kidney preferred for living donation.
> - Machine perfusion reduces DGF.

## Q3d. Diagnosis and management of hemorrhagic cystitis
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in 2022-Summer Paper 3, Q3b)

### Definition
- Diffuse inflammation and bleeding of the bladder mucosa, after other sources are excluded.

### Causes
| Cause | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Cyclophosphamide, ifosfamide** | Liver forms **acrolein**, which sloughs urothelium; onset within 48 h |
| **Pelvic radiation** (about 5%; 6 months to 10 years) | **Obliterative endarteritis**, ischaemia, fibrosis |
| **Viral** | BK polyomavirus; adenovirus 11, 35; after transplant |
| Others | Bacteria; busulfan, thiotepa; penicillins; amyloidosis |

### Diagnosis
- History: drugs, radiotherapy, transplant, anticoagulants. Severity: microscopic, gross, clots, clot retention.
- Blood count, coagulation, renal function, urine culture, viral PCR (polymerase chain reaction) if transplanted.
- **Exclude cancer:** cytology, cystoscopy with biopsy, upper tract imaging (CT urography).

### Prevention
- **Mesna** inactivates acrolein; hyperhydration, forced diuresis.

### Management (escalate by severity)
```
Resuscitate, transfuse, correct coagulopathy, treat infection
→ 3-way catheter + CBI (continuous bladder irrigation)
→ clot evacuation, fulguration, biopsy under anaesthesia
→ intravesical alum 1%
→ bleeding after 48 h: hyperbaric oxygen (+ nephrostomies)
→ formalin 1%, then 4% (cystogram first)
→ cystectomy + conduit diversion
(Unstable at any stage: internal iliac embolisation)
```

| Agent | Key point |
|---|---|
| **Alum 1%** | First line; no anaesthesia; avoid in renal failure (aluminium toxicity) |
| Aminocaproic acid | Antifibrinolytic CBI; clot-free bladder only |
| Prostaglandins, silver nitrate, hyaluronate | Second-line options |
| **Formalin 1–4%** | 80–90% success; under anaesthesia, instilled by gravity for 10–15 min; **cystogram to exclude reflux** (occlude ureters if present); risks: contracted bladder, ureteric stricture |

- **HBOT** (hyperbaric oxygen therapy): 100% oxygen at 2–3 atmospheres, 30–40 sessions; raises tissue oxygen and drives neovascularisation; 80–90% response, but many relapse; **RICH-ART** trial improved urinary scores in radiation cystitis. Nephrostomies divert urokinase-rich urine if bleeding continues.
- **Embolisation:** selective anterior division of internal iliac (posterior division causes gluteal pain); initial control about 90%.
- **Cystectomy** last; remove bladder (pyocystis).
- **Viral:** supportive (hydration, irrigation); cidofovir anecdotal.
- **Also know:** tranexamic acid and laser ablation are unproven.

**Diagram to draw:** escalation ladder.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Acrolein (mesna); radiation endarteritis; BK virus.
> - Exclude bladder cancer.
> - Alum first; formalin after cystogram; HBOT for radiation.

## Q3e. Surgical management of primary scrotal cancer
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in 2021-Summer Paper 4, Q3c)

### Introduction
- Rare; **SCC (squamous cell carcinoma)** commonest. **Percivall Pott (1775)**: chimney sweeps, the first occupational cancer.
- **Risks:** soot, tar, mineral oils, arsenic, PUVA (psoralen + ultraviolet A), HPV (human papillomavirus).
- **Others:** EMPD (extramammary Paget disease), BCC (basal cell carcinoma), melanoma, sarcoma.
- Drains to **superficial inguinal nodes, crossing the midline**.

### Evaluation
- Painless nodule or ulcer on the anterolateral scrotum, often presenting late; **biopsy** confirms.
- Examine both groins (lymphatics cross the midline); FNAC (fine-needle aspiration cytology) of nodes; CT (computed tomography) of abdomen and pelvis for staging.

### Staging (Lowe)
| Stage | Extent |
|---|---|
| A1 | Confined to scrotum |
| A2 | Invades testis, cord, penis, pubis or perineum |
| B | Inguinal nodes |
| C | Pelvic nodes |
| D | Distant metastases |

### Primary tumour
1. **Wide local excision, 2 cm margin**, full thickness with dartos; frozen sections.
2. Testes spared unless invaded; hemi- or total scrotectomy for large lesions.
3. A2: en bloc removal of involved testis, penis, perineum or pubis.
4. Positive margin: re-excise.

### Reconstruction (Chapter 87)
| Defect | Management |
|---|---|
| <50%, no tension | Primary closure |
| <50%, tension | Scrotal advancement flap or secondary intention |
| >50% | Testes in thigh pockets; later STSG (split-thickness skin graft) or flap |

### Nodes (as in penile cancer)
- **Palpable, cytology positive:** ILND (inguinal lymph node dissection) ± pelvic dissection. Nodal status is the main prognostic factor.
- **Impalpable:** surveillance; sentinel node biopsy in high-grade tumours.
- **Bulky or fixed:** neoadjuvant cisplatin-based chemotherapy, then surgery; RT (radiotherapy) for palliation.
- Routine prophylactic dissection is not advised.

### Other types
- **EMPD:** wide excision guided by mapping biopsies; screen for other cancers.
- **Also know:** BCC, excision or Mohs surgery; melanoma, excision by Breslow depth ± sentinel node; sarcoma, wide excision.

**Diagram to draw:** elliptical excision with 2 cm margin and bilateral inguinal drainage.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Pott 1775: chimney sweeps.
> - 2 cm margin; testes spared.
> - Nodes decide prognosis.

## Q3f. Female circumcision
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in 2019-Summer Paper 2, Q3f)

*Taken as female genital mutilation/cutting (Chapter 58 calls it "female circumcision (infibulation)").*

### Definition and epidemiology
- **FGM (female genital mutilation):** partial or total removal of, or other injury to, the external female genitalia for non-medical reasons (WHO, World Health Organization).
- About **200 million** females affected; about 3 million African girls a year. Africa, Middle East, Indonesia and Malaysia; some Indian communities.
- Usually at 4–10 years, without anaesthesia, as a rite of passage; predates Islam. Pharaonic type = infibulation.

### WHO classification
| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| I (clitoridectomy) | Partial or total removal of the clitoris and/or prepuce |
| II (excision) | Clitoris and labia minora, ± labia majora |
| III (infibulation) | Narrowing of the introitus by cutting and joining the labia, ± clitoral excision |
| IV (other) | Pricking, piercing, incising, scraping, cauterising |
- Older Toubia scheme numbers differ: infibulation is Type III in WHO.

### Complications
- **Immediate:** pain, bleeding, shock, infection, **urinary retention**, urethral or rectal injury.
- **Urological:** recurrent **UTI** (urinary tract infection), slow stream, meatal obstruction, vaginal stone from urine stasis, **fistula** after obstructed labour.
- **Gynaecological:** retained menses, dysmenorrhoea, cysts, keloid, dyspareunia, apareunia.
- **Obstetric:** obstructed labour, tears, haemorrhage.
- **Psychological:** anxiety, depression, PTSD (post-traumatic stress disorder).

### Management
- Sensitive, culturally respectful history; examine and classify; drawings or photographs for consent.
- **Deinfibulation:** simple **midline division of the scar** with subcuticular closure of each edge; exposes meatus and introitus. High success. Best before pregnancy or antenatally.
- **Never reinfibulate.**
- Excise cysts, treat stenosis, repair fistula; counselling.
- Clitoral reconstruction: less predictable, limited follow-up.

### Legal aspects
- WHO and UN (United Nations) condemn FGM, including by health workers. Illegal in many countries (UK FGM Act 2003). India has no specific law.

**Diagram to draw:** vulva with types I–III and the deinfibulation line.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - WHO types I–IV; infibulation is type III.
> - Retention, UTI, stones, fistula.
> - Midline deinfibulation; never reinfibulate.
