## III-1. Embryology and management of ectopic ureter.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

### Definition
- An **ectopic ureter** is any ureter, single or duplex, that does **not open on the trigone**. True ectopia means an orifice **at or distal to the bladder neck**.
- Commoner in girls. In girls most ectopic ureters drain the **upper pole of a duplex kidney**; in boys most are **single-system**.

### Embryology
- **Week 4–6:** the **ureteric bud** arises from the **mesonephric (wolffian) duct** and induces the metanephric blastema to form the kidney.
- The duct segment distal to the bud is absorbed into the **urogenital sinus**; the ureteric orifice then moves cranially and laterally, the wolffian orifice caudally to the verumontanum.
- **Mackie–Stephens bud theory:**

| Bud position on wolffian duct | Absorption | Orifice | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Too caudal | Early | High and lateral, short tunnel | VUR (vesicoureteral reflux) |
| Too cranial | Late or never | Low and medial (bladder neck, urethra) or stays on wolffian duct | **Ectopic ureter**, often obstructed |

- A bud from the wrong place meets poor blastema, so **renal dysplasia increases with the degree of ectopia**.
- **Duplex system:** the upper-pole bud is more cranial, absorbed later, and ends **lower and more medial** (**Weigert–Meyer law**): upper pole obstructs; lower pole refluxes.

### Sites of ectopia (the key sex difference)
| | Female | Male |
|---|---|---|
| Sites (commonest first) | Urethra, vestibule, vagina; rarely cervix, uterus, rectum (via **Gartner duct**) | Posterior urethra, seminal vesicle; also vas, ejaculatory duct |
| Relation to sphincter | Can open **below** the external sphincter | Always **above** the external sphincter |
| Continence | **Continuous dribbling with normal voiding** | **Never incontinent** |
| Typical presentation | Constant dampness, perineal pus, UTI (urinary tract infection) | **Epididymo-orchitis**, UTI, flank pain, hydronephrosis |

- **Zinner syndrome:** ectopic ureter into the seminal vesicle with ipsilateral renal agenesis or dysplasia and a seminal vesicle cyst.

### Clinical features
- **Prenatal hydronephrosis**: most are detected this way.
- **Girl:** wet day and night despite normal voids; she is never dry for 30–60 minutes. Often misdiagnosed as voiding dysfunction.
- **Infection:** low-grade fever with spikes; urine culture may be negative because the infected moiety does not drain into the bladder.
- **Boy:** recurrent epididymitis in a young boy demands an upper-tract ultrasound.
- **Also know:** pain then perineal discharge; bilateral single-system ectopia with small bladder and wide bladder neck.

### Evaluation
| Test | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **Ultrasound** (first) | Dilated upper pole and ureter behind the bladder; parenchyma. Bladder view separates it from ureterocele |
| **VCUG** (voiding cystourethrogram) | Lower-pole or contralateral VUR ("drooping lily"), reflux into the ectopic ureter |
| **DMSA** (dimercaptosuccinic acid) | Gold standard for moiety function ("some" versus "none"); MAG3 (mercaptoacetyltriglycine) if drainage needed |
| **MR (magnetic resonance) urography** | Non-dilated upper pole, small dysplastic kidney, complex anatomy |
| **Examination / cystovaginoscopy** | Perineal orifice between urethra and vagina, cannulated for contrast |

- **Also know:** clear urine leaking despite methylene blue in the bladder suggests an ectopic ureter; CT for a small unseen kidney.

### Management
**Goals:** preserve renal function; remove infection, obstruction and reflux; achieve continence; with least morbidity. Start antibiotic prophylaxis in the neonate with significant dilation.

```
Neonate with sepsis or massive dilation
   → end cutaneous ureterostomy (or nephrostomy); DMSA later
DUPLEX system
   Upper pole non-functioning → upper-pole heminephroureterectomy
   Upper pole worth keeping   → ureteroureterostomy (UU) / pyeloureterostomy
   + lower-pole VUR           → common sheath reimplant
                                (or lower-pole reimplant + distal UU)
SINGLE system
   Functioning     → ureteral reimplant (± tapering)
   Non-functioning → nephroureterectomy (laparoscopic)
   Bilateral       → bladder neck reconstruction ± augmentation
```

- **Upper-pole heminephroureterectomy** (open, laparoscopic or robotic): upper-pole ureter as a handle, passed behind the hilum; ligate polar vessels; excise the pole; close over fat. Dissect on the ureter wall to protect the **lower-pole blood supply**. The stump is rarely a problem.
- **Ureteroureterostomy (UU):** end-to-side upper into lower pole ureter, distal (inguinal incision) or proximal (preferred with big size mismatch). **Stent the recipient lower-pole ureter** cystoscopically for identification. Now used even for non-functioning poles; feared hypertension has not been shown.
- **Common sheath reimplantation:** do not separate the two ureters (they share blood supply).

### Complications
- Loss of lower-pole function (most serious); urinoma (up to 20% after minimally invasive partial nephrectomy, mostly harmless); stump infection; new reflux; persistent incontinence.

### Recent advances
- Robotic UU and heminephrectomy; single-site surgery; non-dismembered side-to-side ureterovesicostomy in infants.

**Diagram to draw:** duplex kidney showing Weigert–Meyer orifices, and female versus male ectopic sites relative to the external sphincter.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Ectopia = orifice at or below the bladder neck; bud too cranial on the wolffian duct.
> - Girls: continuous dribbling with normal voiding; boys: never incontinent, epididymitis.
> - DMSA decides keep (UU/reimplant) versus remove (heminephroureterectomy).
> - Protect the lower-pole blood supply; stent the recipient ureter for UU.

## III-2. Discuss the role of tissue typing and cross match in live and cadaveric
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

*The question is incomplete; it is taken to mean "... live and cadaveric (deceased-donor) renal transplantation".*

### Introduction
- Two immune barriers decide compatibility: **preformed antibodies** (anti-A/B isoagglutinins and anti-HLA (human leukocyte antigen) antibodies) and **T-cell responses** to mismatched HLA.
- **Tissue typing** measures how well donor and recipient HLA match. **Antibody screening and crossmatch** detect preformed antibodies before the kidney meets them. Together they prevent **hyperacute rejection**, now practically eliminated.

### The HLA system
- HLA is the human MHC (major histocompatibility complex), a gene cluster on **chromosome 6 (short arm)**. Genes are inherited as two **haplotypes** (one from each parent) and expressed codominantly.

| | Class I | Class II |
|---|---|---|
| Loci | HLA-A, -B, -C | HLA-DR, -DQ, -DP |
| Expression | All nucleated cells | Antigen-presenting cells, endothelium |
| Presents to | CD8 (cytotoxic) T cells | CD4 (helper) T cells |

- **Sibling** chance: 25% two-haplotype identical, 50% one-haplotype, 25% none. A parent or child always shares one haplotype.

### Tissue typing methods
| Method | Principle | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Serological** (microlymphocytotoxicity) | Lymphocytes + typed antisera + complement; dead cells take up dye | Low resolution; largely replaced |
| **Molecular** (DNA-based) | PCR (polymerase chain reaction) with SSP (sequence-specific primers), PCR-SSO (sequence-specific oligonucleotide probes), sequence-based typing, **NGS** (next-generation sequencing) | High (allele-level) resolution; now standard |

- **Matching:** usually reported as mismatches at **A, B and DR** (0–6). **DR matters most**, then B, then A; DQ increasingly typed. Fewer mismatches mean better graft survival and less sensitisation for a future transplant (Collaborative Transplant Study, Opelz). The effect is larger in deceased-donor grafts.

### ABO compatibility
- Same rules as transfusion: **O universal donor, AB universal recipient**.
- **A2 (non-A1) donors** can go to O or B recipients with low anti-A titre.
- **ABO-incompatible** living transplant is possible: lower titres (plasmapheresis or immunoadsorption, rituximab, IVIG (intravenous immunoglobulin)) to a centre target before surgery.

### Sensitisation and antibody screening
- Anti-HLA antibodies come from **pregnancy, blood transfusion, previous transplant** (and infection).
- **PRA (panel reactive antibody):** recipient serum tested against a panel of lymphocytes; result as % of panel killed.
- **Solid-phase (Luminex) single-antigen beads:** identify each antibody and its strength (MFI, mean fluorescence intensity). Antibodies against a donor are **DSA (donor-specific antibodies)**.
- **cPRA (calculated PRA):** % of donors the patient would be incompatible with, calculated from listed "unacceptable antigens". It replaced cell-panel PRA for allocation; very high cPRA gets allocation priority.

### Crossmatch
| Type | Method | Meaning if positive |
|---|---|---|
| **CDC** (complement-dependent cytotoxicity) | Donor T and B lymphocytes + recipient serum + complement; cell death scored. **DTT** (dithiothreitol) removes IgM autoantibody; AHG (antihuman globulin) increases sensitivity | **Positive T-cell CDC with IgG DSA = absolute contraindication** (hyperacute rejection; Patel and Terasaki, 1969) |
| **Flow cytometry crossmatch** | Antibody binding on donor cells detected by fluorescent anti-IgG | More sensitive; CDC-negative/flow-positive = higher risk of AMR (antibody-mediated rejection); desensitise or exchange |
| **Virtual crossmatch** | Compare recipient's bead-defined antibodies with donor HLA type | Speeds deceased-donor allocation; confirm by cell-based crossmatch |

- **B-cell positive** alone: class II antibody or non-specific binding; interpret with bead results.
- Crossmatch uses a **recent serum** plus historical peak sera.

### Living versus deceased donor
| | Living donor | Deceased (cadaveric) donor |
|---|---|---|
| Typing | ABO and HLA early in donor work-up; choose best of several donors | ABO and HLA at donor referral |
| Crossmatch | At work-up and a **final crossmatch shortly before surgery** | At the time of offer, often virtual first; done against the list rapidly (cold ischaemia) |
| Role of matching | Less critical: even unrelated living donors do better than well-matched deceased donors (Terasaki, 1995) | Allocation uses HLA match, sensitisation (cPRA), waiting time and donor quality (KDPI, Kidney Donor Profile Index) |
| If incompatible | **Paired (swap) exchange**, desensitisation, ABO-incompatible protocol | Patient waits; acceptable-mismatch programmes |

- **India:** swap (paired) donation is legal under the Transplantation of Human Organs (THOA) amendment, 2011.

### Managing the incompatible or sensitised recipient
- **Kidney paired donation**, including chains with altruistic donors.
- **Desensitisation:** plasmapheresis + low-dose IVIG, or high-dose IVIG, ± rituximab; **imlifidase** (cleaves IgG) for highly sensitised deceased-donor recipients (conditional EU approval). Desensitised HLA-incompatible living transplant gave better survival than waiting on dialysis (Montgomery, 2011).

### After transplant
- Monitor for **de novo DSA** (especially class II) with dd-cfDNA (donor-derived cell-free DNA) and biopsy; de novo DSA predicts graft loss.

### Recent advances
- **Eplet (epitope) matching**, high-resolution NGS typing, virtual crossmatch, imlifidase.

**Diagram to draw:** CDC crossmatch: donor lymphocytes + recipient serum + complement, dead cells take up dye (positive).

> **Key points to remember:**
> - HLA on chromosome 6; match A, B, DR; DR matters most.
> - Positive T-cell CDC crossmatch (IgG DSA) = no transplant: hyperacute rejection.
> - Flow and bead assays are more sensitive; cPRA guides allocation.
> - Incompatible pair: paired exchange (legal in India since 2011) or desensitisation.

## III-3a. Treatment algorithm of undescended testis.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Principles (AUA (American Urological Association) 2014; EAU (European Association of Urology) similar)
- **Observe for 6 months** (corrected age) for spontaneous descent; refer if still undescended.
- **Orchidopexy after 6 months, preferably by 12 months, and by 18 months at the latest.** Reason: germ-cell and Leydig-cell loss starts in infancy; early surgery improves testicular growth and lowers cancer risk.
- **No ultrasound or MRI (magnetic resonance imaging)**: inaccurate (ultrasound sensitivity about 45% for non-palpable testes); never changes the operation; delays surgery.
- **No hormonal therapy** (hCG (human chorionic gonadotrophin) or LHRH (luteinising hormone-releasing hormone)): descent in about 20% only; hCG may increase germ-cell apoptosis.
- **Bilateral non-palpable testes** (especially with hypospadias or small phallus): urgent **karyotype and hormones** to exclude **CAH (congenital adrenal hyperplasia)** and other DSD (differences of sex development).

### Algorithm
```
Boy with empty scrotum
  ├─ Bilateral non-palpable ± abnormal phallus → DSD work-up (karyotype, hormones, electrolytes)
  └─ Otherwise → re-examine at 6 months corrected age
        Still undescended → refer → examination under anaesthesia (EUA)
          ├─ Retractile → yearly review (risk of ascent)
          ├─ Palpable → inguinal or scrotal (Bianchi) orchidopexy
          └─ Non-palpable → diagnostic laparoscopy
                ├─ Testis seen → laparoscopic orchidopexy
                │       short vessels → Fowler–Stephens (1 or 2 stage) / Shehata
                ├─ Vessels + vas enter ring → inguinal exploration (excise nubbin)
                └─ Blind-ending vessels → stop (vanished testis)
```
- **Scrotal nubbin with contralateral hypertrophy** may start with scrotal exploration.

### Key operative points
- **Inguinal orchidopexy:** free the cord, ligate the processus, retroperitoneal mobilisation ± **Prentiss manoeuvre**, subdartos pouch.
- **Fowler–Stephens:** divides testicular vessels; testis survives on vasal collaterals; for testes not within 2–4 cm of the ring. Primary orchidopexy, when possible, is more successful.
- **Postpubertal intra-abdominal testis with normal contralateral testis: orchidectomy** (poor function, cancer risk); offer prosthesis.

### Follow-up
- Check position and size at 6 months; teach **self-examination** after puberty (cancer risk 2–5 times).

**Diagram to draw:** laparoscopic findings at the internal ring (testis, vessels entering ring, blind-ending vessels).

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Operate from 6 months, preferably by 12 months and by 18 months at the latest.
> - No imaging, no hormones.
> - Non-palpable: EUA then diagnostic laparoscopy.
> - Bilateral non-palpable: exclude CAH/DSD first.

## III-3b. Collin's knife.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

*Taken to mean the **Collings knife**, the knife-shaped cutting electrode of the resectoscope (the reference chapters spell it both "Collins" and "Collings").*

### Description
- A **hot (electrosurgical) knife electrode** fitted to the **resectoscope working element** in place of the loop.
- Thin **straight or angled (L- or hook-shaped) blade**; cuts a precise line with **cutting current** under irrigation and direct vision.
- **Monopolar** versions need **non-conducting irrigant** (glycine, sorbitol or mannitol); bipolar versions allow normal saline.
- Adult and **infant (9.5 Fr) resectoscope** sizes.
- **Principle:** it **incises rather than resects**; a deep, controlled cut releases a fibrous ring with little tissue loss.

### Uses (from the chapters)
| Use | Technique point |
|---|---|
| **TUIP** (transurethral incision of prostate) | Gland under 30 mL, no median lobe; incise at 5 and 7 o'clock from below the ureteric orifice to just proximal to the verumontanum, down to the capsule |
| **Bladder neck contracture** (after TURP (transurethral resection of prostate) or simple prostatectomy) | Incise until the scar springs open, to about 22 Fr; mitomycin C for recurrence |
| **Ureterocele incision** (older children) | Low transverse full-thickness incision near the bladder floor to reduce reflux |
| **PUV (posterior urethral valve) ablation** | Infant resectoscope only if urethral calibre is normal; incise at 5, 7 (± 12) o'clock; avoid hot-loop resection (stricture) |
| **Bladder diverticulum neck** | Incise the narrow neck so the diverticulum drains |
| **PCNL (percutaneous nephrolithotomy) tract** | Incise scarred fascia when rigid dilators fail |

### Alternatives
- **Cold knife (Sachse)** urethrotome: preferred for urethral stricture; hot knife unnecessary there.
- **Holmium or thulium laser**, Bugbee electrode, hook electrode, **Mohan's valvotome** (for valves).

### Precautions and complications
- Stay away from the **ureteric orifices** and external sphincter; control depth (perforation, extravasation).
- TUR (transurethral resection) syndrome with monopolar irrigant if veins open.
- **Retrograde ejaculation** after bladder neck incision; stricture if the urethra is too small for the sheath.

**Diagram to draw:** resectoscope tip with L-shaped knife electrode, and the 5 and 7 o'clock TUIP incisions.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Knife-shaped resectoscope electrode for incision, not resection.
> - Uses: TUIP, bladder neck contracture, ureterocele, PUV, diverticulum neck.
> - Monopolar needs glycine or sorbitol; bipolar uses saline.

## III-3c. Autosomal recessive polycystic kidney disease.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition and genetics
- **ARPKD (autosomal recessive polycystic kidney disease)**: bilateral, symmetrical renal enlargement from **dilated collecting ducts**, **always with congenital hepatic fibrosis**.
- Gene **PKHD1 on chromosome 6p**; protein **fibrocystin (polyductin)** on the **primary cilium** of tubular and bile-duct cells, so it is a **ciliopathy**.
- Incidence about 1 in 10,000–50,000 live births. Siblings have a 25% risk.
- "Infantile" is a misnomer: a **spectrum**; earlier onset means more severe disease.

### Pathology
- Radially arranged dilated collecting ducts (**microcysts**).
- **Liver:** periportal fibrosis, biliary ectasia; **Caroli syndrome** if intrahepatic ducts dilate.

### Clinical features
| Age | Features |
|---|---|
| **Fetus** | Large echogenic kidneys, **oligohydramnios** → Potter facies, limb deformity, **pulmonary hypoplasia** |
| **Neonate** | Huge, smooth, hard flank masses; respiratory distress; rising creatinine; **30–50% die early** |
| **Child** | Hypertension, CKD (chronic kidney disease), growth failure, anaemia, osteodystrophy |
| **Older child** | **Portal hypertension**: varices, splenomegaly, cholangitis; liver function stays normal |

- No association with renal tumours.

### Investigations
- **Ultrasound:** bilateral very large, **homogeneously hyperechoic** kidneys (brighter than liver) from countless microcyst interfaces; discrete cysts appear with age.
- **Parents' ultrasound** normal (excludes ADPKD); **PKHD1 genetic testing**; liver ultrasound.
- **Differential:** neonatal ADPKD (autosomal dominant PKD), bilateral hydronephrosis, MCDK (multicystic dysplastic kidney), glomerulocystic disease, bilateral tumours, renal vein thrombosis.

### Management (no cure)
- **Neonatal:** ventilation, fluid and electrolyte care, antihypertensives (ACE (angiotensin-converting enzyme) inhibitors).
- **CKD care:** nutrition, growth hormone, anaemia and bone treatment; dialysis and **kidney transplantation**.
- **Nephrectomy** (breathing, feeding, blood pressure, room for peritoneal dialysis): cautiously; **very early bilateral nephrectomy** (first 3 months) had the worst neurological outcomes (ARegPKD registry).
- **Liver:** variceal sclerotherapy, portosystemic shunt; **combined liver–kidney transplant** for severe liver disease.
- **Genetic counselling** and prenatal diagnosis.

### ARPKD versus ADPKD
| | ARPKD | ADPKD |
|---|---|---|
| Gene | PKHD1 (chromosome 6) | PKD1 (16), PKD2 (4) |
| Age | Perinatal | 30–50 years |
| Cysts from | Collecting duct | Whole nephron |
| Ultrasound | Symmetric, homogeneous, echogenic | Large discrete cysts, asymmetric |
| Liver | Fibrosis (always) | Cysts |
| Other | None | Berry aneurysm, mitral prolapse |

**Diagram to draw:** cut kidney with radial dilated collecting ducts.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - PKHD1, chromosome 6, fibrocystin; always congenital hepatic fibrosis.
> - Huge homogeneous echogenic kidneys with oligohydramnios.
> - Neonates die of pulmonary hypoplasia; survivors develop portal hypertension.

## III-3d. Embryology and Management of P.U. valve patient.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Embryology
- **Weeks 5–6:** the **urorectal septum** divides the cloaca into rectum and **urogenital sinus**; wolffian ducts migrate to the verumontanum, leaving small folds.

| Young type | Anatomy | Origin |
|---|---|---|
| **I (about 95%)** | Leaflets from the verumontanum, fused anteriorly | Abnormal anterior insertion of the wolffian duct ends |
| II | Folds from verumontanum to bladder neck | Not real (secondary hypertrophy) |
| **III** | Ring diaphragm with central hole | **Persistent urogenital membrane** |

- **COPUM (congenital obstructing posterior urethral membrane):** one membrane, torn by the first catheter into type I leaflets.
- Fetal urine from 10–14 weeks: obstruction causes **renal dysplasia**, oligohydramnios, pulmonary hypoplasia.

### Antenatal
- Bilateral hydroureteronephrosis, thick bladder, **keyhole sign**, oligohydramnios. **Vesicoamniotic shunt** helps survival (lungs), not kidneys (PLUTO trial, 2013).

### Neonatal management
1. **Drain the bladder:** 5–7 Fr feeding tube (coudé if it curls); minimal balloon inflation.
2. **Stabilise:** ventilation; correct **post-obstructive diuresis**, hyponatraemia, acidosis, hyperkalaemia; antibiotics.
3. **Confirm:** VCUG (voiding cystourethrogram), renal ultrasound, MAG3 (mercaptoacetyltriglycine) at 2–3 months.
4. **Primary endoscopic valve ablation** (preferred): laser, Bugbee or hook at 5 and 7 o'clock ± 12; VCUG within 1 month.
5. **Vesicostomy** if the urethra is too small or drainage fails. **Upper-tract diversion** rarely: no proven renal benefit.
6. **Circumcision:** cuts febrile UTI (urinary tract infection) sharply.
- **VUR (vesicoureteral reflux)** (50–80%) is secondary; treat the bladder, not the ureter.

### Prognosis
| Nadir creatinine (first year) | ESRD (end-stage renal disease) risk |
|---|---|
| < 0.4 mg/dL | Nil |
| 0.4–0.69 | 2% |
| 0.7–0.99 | 27% |
| > 1.0 | Up to 100% |

- Lifetime ESRD risk 20–50%.

### Long-term care
- **Valve bladder** (Mitchell): polyuria, poor sensation and residual urine keep the upper tracts dilated. Timed and double voiding, anticholinergic or α-blocker, **CIC (clean intermittent catheterisation)**, **overnight drainage**, Mitrofanoff, rarely augmentation.
- **CKD (chronic kidney disease) care; transplantation** after urodynamics and bladder optimisation; graft survival similar.

**Diagram to draw:** type I leaflets versus type III membrane, with keyhole VCUG.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Type I (95%): wolffian duct insertion; type III: persistent urogenital membrane.
> - Catheter, stabilise, then primary valve ablation; vesicostomy if not possible.
> - Nadir creatinine below 0.7 mg/dL predicts good renal outcome.
> - Valve bladder: overnight drainage and CIC.

## III-3e. CSIC.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

*Taken to mean **CISC – clean intermittent self-catheterisation**.*

### Definition and principle
- Regular, **clean (not sterile)** passage of a catheter by the patient (or carer) to empty the bladder, then removal.
- **Lapides (1971–72)**: infection comes from **stasis and overdistension** (poor bladder-wall blood flow), not from bacteria alone. Regular complete emptying protects the bladder wall and upper tracts, even though bacteriuria develops.
- Gold standard for chronic emptying failure.

### Indications
- **Neurogenic bladder:** spina bifida, spinal cord injury, multiple sclerosis, cauda equina, diabetic cystopathy.
- **Underactive or acontractile detrusor**; chronic retention; post-operative retention.
- **Detrusor–sphincter dyssynergia**: CISC bypasses the obstructed sphincter.
- After **augmentation or continent diversion**; **valve bladder**; high residual after botulinum toxin.
- **Contraindications:** poor hand function or cognition without a carer, urethral false passage or stricture, unwillingness.

### Technique
- Wash hands → clean meatus with water → lubricated catheter (or pre-lubricated **hydrophilic** catheter) → pass until urine flows → drain fully → withdraw slowly.
- Sizes: adults about 12–14 Fr; children by age (8–10 Fr).
- **Frequency:** 4–6 times a day, keeping each volume **below about 400–500 mL** (child: below expected bladder capacity).
- Combine with **antimuscarinics** for overactivity; bladder diary.

### Catheters
| Type | Note |
|---|---|
| Plain PVC (polyvinyl chloride), reusable after washing | Cheap; common in India |
| **Hydrophilic-coated** single-use | Less urethral trauma; longer time to first UTI (urinary tract infection) |
| Compact or closed-system | Convenience, outdoor use |

### Children
- Start **early (infancy)** in spina bifida with hostile bladder; families adapt best; child involved from 3–5 years.
- **Mitrofanoff appendicovesicostomy** (1980) if urethral catheterisation is difficult (wheelchair, sensate urethra, girls).

### Complications
- **Asymptomatic bacteriuria** (common): **do not treat or give prophylaxis**; treat only symptomatic UTI.
- Urethral trauma, bleeding, **false passage**, stricture, epididymitis, bladder stones (hair, mucus), rarely lost catheter.

### Outcome
- Less pyelonephritis, epididymo-orchitis and urosepsis than indwelling catheters; preserves the upper tracts; with drugs, continence in many.

**Diagram to draw:** neurogenic bladder management ladder: CISC + antimuscarinic → botulinum toxin → augmentation + channel.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Lapides: overdistension, not bacteria, causes infection; clean is enough.
> - 4–6 times daily, under about 500 mL each.
> - Do not treat asymptomatic bacteriuria.
> - Mitrofanoff when urethral access is hard.

## III-3f. Controversies in management of varicocele.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Background
- Dilated pampiniform veins: about 15% of men, 35% with primary and up to 80% with secondary infertility; mostly left-sided (taller venous column to the left renal vein, valve failure).
- Harm: **heat**, oxidative stress, **sperm DNA fragmentation**; progressive atrophy, lower testosterone.
- **Grades:** subclinical (Doppler only), I (palpable with Valsalva), II (palpable), III (visible).

### The controversies
| Question | Evidence | Current position |
|---|---|---|
| **Does repair improve fertility?** | Madgar RCT (randomised controlled trial, 1995): pregnancy 44% versus 10% at 1 year; meta-analyses mixed | Repair **clinical** varicocele with **abnormal semen** in an infertile couple with normal female partner (AUA/ASRM (American Urological Association / American Society for Reproductive Medicine)) |
| **Subclinical varicocele?** | No benefit for semen or pregnancy | **Do not treat** |
| **Normal semen?** | No proven benefit | Not for fertility |
| **Adolescent varicocele** | Testis volume is a surrogate; link to adult fertility unproven | Observe; repair for **testis asymmetry above 20%**, pain, or abnormal semen (Tanner 5 or 18 years) |
| **NOA (non-obstructive azoospermia)** | Small series: sperm returns to ejaculate in some | No definitive evidence; may be considered before micro-TESE (microsurgical testicular sperm extraction) |
| **Before IVF/ICSI** (in vitro fertilisation / intracytoplasmic sperm injection) | Repair may improve live-birth rate or allow less invasive ART (assisted reproduction) | Discuss, especially with oligozoospermia |
| **High DNA fragmentation** | Repair lowers fragmentation | Reasonable indication |
| **Hypogonadism** | Repair raises testosterone in hypogonadal men | Emerging indication |
| **Pain** | Dull ache on standing; relief in most after microsurgery | Repair after other causes excluded |

### Choice of technique
| Technique | Recurrence | Hydrocele | Comment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Microsurgical subinguinal/inguinal** | Lowest (about 1%) | Near 0 | **Best**: artery and lymphatics preserved |
| Retroperitoneal (Palomo) | High with artery spared | High with mass ligation | Children (artery ligation acceptable) |
| Laparoscopic | 3–15% | Up to 12% | Bilateral; serious morbidity possible |
| Embolisation | 4–25% | Nil | Outpatient; technical failure |

- **Also know:** isolated right or non-decompressing varicocele → scan for renal tumour; recurrence diagnosed by upright colour Doppler.

**Diagram to draw:** cord cross-section at microsurgery: artery, lymphatics and vas preserved, veins ligated.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Treat only palpable varicocele with abnormal semen; never subclinical.
> - Madgar 1995: 44% versus 10% pregnancy.
> - Adolescents: observe; repair for asymmetry above 20%.
> - Microsurgical subinguinal: lowest recurrence and hydrocele.
