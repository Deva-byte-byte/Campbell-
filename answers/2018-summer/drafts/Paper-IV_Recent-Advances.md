## IV-1. Radiology for solid renal masses.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

### Introduction
- Most renal masses are now found incidentally. Imaging is the **strongest predictor of malignancy**; size and **enhancement** are the two key variables.
- About 20% of solid enhancing cT1 masses are benign (oncocytoma, fat-poor AML (angiomyolipoma)).
- **Goals:** solid or cystic? enhancing? fat? subtype? stage and surgical anatomy.

### A. CT (computed tomography): the standard test
**Renal mass protocol** (dedicated, thin slices):

| Phase | Timing | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Non-contrast | – | Baseline HU (Hounsfield units), fat, calcification, haemorrhage |
| Corticomedullary | 30–70 s | Hypervascular masses; arteries; clear cell vs other subtypes |
| Nephrographic | 90–120 s | **Most sensitive** for detection (tumour enhances less than parenchyma); renal vein and IVC (inferior vena cava) thrombus |
| Excretory | 7–10 min | Collecting system relation |

**Enhancement thresholds** (rise from unenhanced value):

| Rise | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ≥20 HU | Enhancing: solid tumour |
| 10–20 HU | Equivocal (MRI, magnetic resonance imaging, or CEUS) |
| <10 HU | Non-enhancing: cyst |

- **Pitfall:** pseudoenhancement of small (<10–15 mm) cysts; dual-energy CT iodine maps help.
- **Macroscopic fat = AML:** fat ≤ −10 to −20 HU on unenhanced CT is diagnostic. Fat **with calcification** suggests RCC (renal cell carcinoma).
- **Fat-poor AML:** hyperdense on unenhanced CT, homogeneous, avid early enhancement and washout.
- **Subtype clues:** clear cell RCC enhances early, avidly and heterogeneously (necrosis); papillary RCC is homogeneous and **hypoenhancing**; chromophobe and oncocytoma intermediate, with a central scar. Overlap is large.

### B. MRI
- **Indications:** contrast allergy, CKD (chronic kidney disease), equivocal CT enhancement, young patients, thrombus. Enhancement = signal rise ≥15–20%. Macrocyclic gadolinium carries very low NSF (nephrogenic systemic fibrosis) risk.

| Feature | Clear cell | Papillary | Chromophobe / oncocytoma | AML |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T2 signal | High, heterogeneous | **Low**, homogeneous (haemosiderin) | Central T2-bright scar | Fat signal |
| Enhancement | Avid (>200%) | Weak | Intermediate / avid | Avid |
| Diffusion (ADC, apparent diffusion coefficient) | High | Lowest | Intermediate | – |
| Chemical shift | **Signal drop** (intracellular lipid, ~59%) | No drop | – | **India-ink** artefact; loss on fat saturation |

- **ccLS (clear cell likelihood score, 1–5):** structured MRI algorithm for solid cT1a masses using T2 signal, corticomedullary enhancement and microscopic fat, plus diffusion and **segmental enhancement inversion** (favours oncocytoma). Score 4–5 suggests clear cell RCC; 1–2 a non-clear-cell tumour. It guides biopsy versus surveillance.

### C. Ultrasound and CEUS (contrast-enhanced ultrasound)
- Grey-scale: cyst versus solid; poor below 2 cm. AML is hyperechoic, but so are some small RCCs.
- **CEUS** (microbubbles, not nephrotoxic): shows enhancement; used in CKD or allergy, indeterminate lesions, ablation and surveillance.

### D. Cystic masses: Bosniak 2019 (CT and MRI)
| Class | Key feature | Malignancy / action |
|---|---|---|
| I | Simple, thin wall ≤2 mm, no septa | Benign; none |
| II | 1–3 thin septa; homogeneous hyperdense (≥70 HU unenhanced) | Benign; none |
| IIF | Wall or septa 3 mm, or ≥4 thin septa | <10%; follow 6, 12 months, then yearly for 5 years |
| III | Wall or septa ≥4 mm, or irregular | ~50%; surgery or surveillance |
| IV | **Enhancing nodule** | ~90%; treat |

### E. Functional and molecular imaging
- **99mTc-sestamibi SPECT/CT** (single-photon emission CT): mitochondria-rich **oncocytoma** and hybrid oncocytic or chromophobe tumours take it up; clear cell RCC does not.
- **Girentuximab PET (positron emission tomography)** targets CA-IX (carbonic anhydrase IX) on clear cell RCC. **ZIRCON** (⁸⁹Zr-girentuximab, indeterminate cT1 masses): about 85% sensitivity and 87% specificity for clear cell RCC.
- **FDG-PET** (fluorodeoxyglucose PET): not recommended (low sensitivity). PSMA (prostate-specific membrane antigen) PET: experimental.

### F. Renal mass biopsy
- **Indications:** result may change management; suspected lymphoma, metastasis or infection; before ablation or surveillance; metastatic disease. Not needed if surgery is planned regardless.
- Cores: PPV (positive predictive value) 98.8%; subtype accuracy ≥90%, grade 60–80%; non-diagnostic ~14%. Seeding almost nil. Oncocytoma cannot be confirmed on cores.

### G. Staging and surgical planning
- **Venous thrombus:** CT sensitivity 78% (renal vein), 96% (IVC). MRI or multiplanar CT shows the cephalad tip; enhancing thrombus is tumour, non-enhancing is bland.

| Mayo level | Extent | T stage |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Renal vein | T3a |
| I | IVC ≤2 cm above renal vein | T3b |
| II | IVC below hepatic veins | T3b |
| III | Intrahepatic IVC, below diaphragm | T3b |
| IV | Above diaphragm or atrium | T3c |

- Nodes ≥2 cm usually malignant. Chest CT; bone scan only if symptomatic.
- **Nephrometry:** R.E.N.A.L. (Radius, Exophytic/endophytic, Nearness to collecting system, Anterior/posterior, Location), PADUA, C-index: predict complexity and complications; guide partial versus radical nephrectomy.
- **After ablation:** persistent or new enhancement = residual or recurrence.
- **Recent:** radiomics and AI (artificial intelligence).

**Diagram to draw:** algorithm: incidental mass → renal-protocol CT/MRI → fat (AML) / cyst (Bosniak) / enhancing solid → biopsy or treat.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Enhancement ≥20 HU = solid tumour; macroscopic fat = AML.
> - MRI: clear cell T2-bright with chemical-shift drop; papillary T2-dark and hypoenhancing; ccLS 1–5.
> - Sestamibi for oncocytoma; ⁸⁹Zr-girentuximab PET (ZIRCON) for clear cell.
> - Biopsy PPV ~99%, non-diagnostic ~14%.

## IV-2. Diagnosis and management for Adrenal Carcinoma.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

### Introduction and epidemiology
- ACC (adrenocortical carcinoma): rare (0.7–2 per million per year), aggressive cortical tumour. **Bimodal** (children <10 years; adults 40–50 years); slight female excess.
- **Syndromes:** Li–Fraumeni (TP53), Beckwith–Wiedemann (IGF-2, insulin-like growth factor 2), Lynch, MEN-1 (multiple endocrine neoplasia 1), Carney complex, FAP (familial adenomatous polyposis). Surgery is the only cure.

### Clinical features
- **Functional in 50–79% of adults** (90% of children):
  - Cortisol (commonest): rapidly progressive Cushing syndrome, often with virilisation.
  - Androgens: virilisation in women. **Oestrogen in a man (feminisation) suggests carcinoma.**
  - Aldosterone: rare.
- Non-functional: pain, fullness, weight loss, or incidental.

### Hormonal work-up (ESE–ENSAT, European Society of Endocrinology–European Network for the Study of Adrenal Tumours)
| Axis | Tests |
|---|---|
| Glucocorticoid | 1 mg overnight dexamethasone suppression test (or 24-h urine or late-night salivary cortisol); ACTH (adrenocorticotropic hormone) |
| Sex steroids and precursors | DHEA-S (dehydroepiandrosterone sulphate), androstenedione, testosterone, 17-OH-progesterone, 11-deoxycortisol; oestradiol in men and postmenopausal women |
| Mineralocorticoid | Aldosterone:renin ratio if hypertension or hypokalaemia |
| Catecholamine | Plasma or urine **metanephrines**: must exclude phaeochromocytoma |

- Why: confirms cortical origin, plans steroid cover, gives **tumour markers** for follow-up.

### Imaging
- **Size:** mean 10–12 cm; >90% exceed 5 cm. Carcinoma in ~2% <4 cm, ~6% at 4–6 cm, ~25% >6 cm.
- **CT (computed tomography):** irregular, heterogeneous, necrosis, calcification; **unenhanced >10 HU (Hounsfield units)** (mean ~39 HU vs ~8 HU in adenoma); **slow washout** (absolute <60%, relative <40% at 15 min). Growth ~1.9 cm/year.
- **MRI (magnetic resonance imaging):** no chemical-shift signal loss; best for IVC (inferior vena cava) thrombus and invasion.
- **FDG-PET/CT** (fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography): avid, but cannot separate ACC from metastasis.
- **Staging:** CT chest, abdomen, pelvis; bone or brain only if symptomatic.
- **No biopsy** if ACC is suspected (seeding, poor accuracy); only for unresectable or metastatic disease, after excluding phaeochromocytoma.

### Pathology: Weiss score (≥3 of 9 = malignant)
| Domain | Criteria |
|---|---|
| Nuclear / cytology | High nuclear grade; >5 mitoses per 50 high-power fields; atypical mitoses |
| Architecture | ≤25% clear cells; diffuse architecture; necrosis |
| Invasion | Venous; sinusoidal; capsular |

- SF-1 (steroidogenic factor 1) confirms cortical origin. **Ki-67** and mitotic rate grade the tumour (low vs high grade).

### ENSAT staging
| Stage | Definition | 5-year disease-specific survival (ENSAT 2009) |
|---|---|---|
| I | T1 (≤5 cm), N0 M0 | ~80% |
| II | T2 (>5 cm), N0 M0 | ~60% |
| III | T3 (periadrenal fat) or T4 (adjacent organ, venous thrombus) or N1 | ~50% |
| IV | Distant metastasis only | ~13% |

- ENSAT differs from older UICC (Union for International Cancer Control) staging by moving T4 and N1 without metastasis into stage III.

### Management
**1. Surgery (stages I–III)**
- **Open transperitoneal adrenalectomy** for suspected ACC, especially >6 cm or invasive. Laparoscopy risks capsule rupture and peritoneal carcinomatosis; only for small organ-confined tumours by experts.
- **R0, intact capsule:** rupture gives dismal results. **En bloc** removal of involved kidney, liver, spleen, pancreas or IVC; IVC thrombectomy (bypass if needed).
- **Locoregional lymphadenectomy** (periadrenal fat, renal hilum, para-aortic or paracaval) improves staging and may reduce recurrence.
- High-volume surgeon (ESE–ENSAT: >6, preferably ≥20 adrenalectomies a year). Hydrocortisone cover if cortisol-secreting.

**2. Adjuvant therapy**
- **Mitotane** (adrenolytic DDT (dichlorodiphenyltrichloroethane) derivative) for **high risk**: stage III, R1/Rx, or Ki-67 >10% (ESE–ENSAT 2018), for at least 2 years.
  - Target blood level **14–20 mg/L**; needs **glucocorticoid replacement** (it destroys the cortex and raises cortisol-binding globulin).
  - Toxicity: gastrointestinal, neurological, hepatic, hypothyroidism, gynaecomastia.
- **ADIUVO** (RCT, randomised controlled trial; R0, stage I–III, Ki-67 ≤10%): no recurrence-free survival benefit. So observe low-risk patients.
- **Adjuvant radiotherapy** (tumour bed): R1 or Rx, or stage III. It cuts local recurrence but not overall survival.

**3. Advanced or metastatic (stage IV)**
- **EDP-M** (etoposide, doxorubicin, cisplatin + mitotane) is first line. **FIRM-ACT** (2012): versus streptozotocin–mitotane, response 23% vs 9% and PFS (progression-free survival) 5.0 vs 2.1 months; overall survival not significantly better.
- Mitotane alone for low-volume, slow disease.
- **Surgery:** resect oligometastases or late recurrence; debulk only if >90% removable.
- **Hormone control:** metyrapone, ketoconazole, mifepristone, etomidate. Radiotherapy for bone and brain metastases.

### Follow-up
- CT chest, abdomen, pelvis and baseline-raised hormones **every 3 months for 2 years**, then 6-monthly to 5 years.

### Prognosis
- Overall 5-year survival **20–47%**. Bad factors: stage, R1, high Ki-67 or mitoses, cortisol excess, age, size >12 cm.

### Recent advances
- Molecular classes (CIMP, CpG island methylator phenotype) predict prognosis. Checkpoint inhibitors give modest responses; IGF-1R (IGF-1 receptor) inhibitor linsitinib failed (GALACCTIC). ADIUVO-2 tests mitotane ± platinum–etoposide in high risk.

**Diagram to draw:** algorithm: adrenal mass → hormones + CT (HU, washout) → suspected ACC → open R0 en bloc resection + LND (lymph node dissection) → risk-based adjuvant mitotane or radiotherapy.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Feminisation in a man, >6 cm, >10 HU, poor washout: think ACC. Never biopsy before resection.
> - Weiss ≥3/9; ENSAT stage IV = distant metastasis only.
> - Open, R0, intact capsule, en bloc + lymphadenectomy.
> - Mitotane level 14–20 mg/L; ADIUVO (low risk: no benefit); FIRM-ACT: EDP-M first line.

## IV-3a. Struvite stones.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition and epidemiology
- **Infection stones:** magnesium ammonium phosphate hexahydrate (MgNH₄PO₄·6H₂O), usually mixed with **carbonate apatite**.
- 5–15% of stones (falling); female:male ~2:1; highest in India and Pakistan. Risk: neurogenic bladder, spinal injury, diversion, catheters, obstruction, diabetes, elderly.

### Pathogenesis
```
Urease-producing bacteria
  Urea + H2O -> 2NH3 + CO2
  NH3 -> NH4+ (urine pH > 7.2)
  CO2 -> carbonate; phosphate -> PO4(3-)
  + Mg -> struvite;  + Ca -> carbonate apatite
```
- Sterile urine has no urease, so infection is essential. Ammonia damages the GAG (glycosaminoglycan) layer; bacteria live inside the stone in a **biofilm**, so antibiotics cannot sterilise a retained stone.
- **Organisms:** **Proteus mirabilis** (commonest), Klebsiella, Pseudomonas, Providencia, Staphylococcus, **Ureaplasma** (easily missed). E. coli is rarely urease-positive.

### Clinical features
- Recurrent UTI (urinary tract infection), fever, flank pain, haematuria; often few symptoms. Grow fast into **staghorn** calculi. Untreated: pyonephrosis, XGP (xanthogranulomatous pyelonephritis), sepsis, renal loss.

### Evaluation
- Urine pH >7; culture (urine and stone cultures often differ). NCCT (non-contrast computed tomography): burden and anatomy; DMSA (dimercaptosuccinic acid) or renogram for split function. Stone analysis; 24-h urine (many have hypercalciuria or hypocitraturia).

### Management
- **Complete stone clearance** is the goal: each fragment is an infected nidus.
- Culture-specific antibiotics before surgery; drain an obstructed infected kidney first.
- **PCNL (percutaneous nephrolithotomy): first line** for staghorns (AUA, American Urological Association). Add flexible nephroscopy, ECIRS (endoscopic combined intrarenal surgery) or second look. SWL (shock wave lithotripsy) or RIRS (retrograde intrarenal surgery) for small stones only.
- Anatrophic nephrolithotomy rarely; nephrectomy for a poorly functioning kidney.
- **Prevention:** surveillance cultures, suppressive antibiotics, fluids, correct stasis or metabolic defects.
- **AHA (acetohydroxamic acid)** 250 mg three times daily (urease inhibitor), only when surgery is exhausted. Side effects in up to 30% (haemolytic anaemia, DVT (deep vein thrombosis)); avoid if creatinine >2.5 mg/dL or pregnancy.
- **Also know:** hemiacidrin irrigation (historical); matrix stones (radiolucent, Proteus).

**Diagram to draw:** urease cascade; staghorn with PCNL tract.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Urease → ammonia → pH >7.2 → struvite + carbonate apatite.
> - Proteus mirabilis commonest; Ureaplasma easily missed.
> - Complete clearance with PCNL; AHA only when surgery is not possible.

## IV-3b. Klinefelter's syndrome.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition and genetics
- **47,XXY**: commonest sex chromosome aneuploidy and **commonest genetic cause of male infertility**; 1 in 500–1000 male births.
- Non-mosaic in **80–90%**; the rest mosaic **46,XY/47,XXY** (milder, more sperm) or 48,XXXY variants. Extra X from non-disjunction (maternal age).
- Underdiagnosed: most never diagnosed; increasingly detected on NIPT (non-invasive prenatal testing).

### Pathophysiology
- Seminiferous tubules **hyalinise** progressively from puberty; germ cells are lost. Leydig cells fail, so testosterone falls. Puberty starts on time, then stalls.

### Clinical features
| Age | Features |
|---|---|
| Infant | Usually normal; micropenis, cryptorchidism, hypospadias |
| Adolescent/adult | **Small firm testes (<3.5 cm, 1–4 mL)**, gynaecomastia, tall stature with long legs (only ~30%), sparse hair, female fat distribution |
| Neurodevelopment | Language, learning and attention difficulties |

### Hormones and tests
- **High FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone) and LH (luteinising hormone), low or low-normal testosterone, high oestradiol** (raised oestradiol:testosterone ratio). Inhibin B low.
- Semen: **azoospermia** in most (~8% have ejaculated sperm).
- **Karyotype** confirms (AUA/ASRM, American Urological Association/American Society for Reproductive Medicine, guideline: karyotype all men with non-obstructive azoospermia or severe oligozoospermia).

### Management
- **Fertility:** **micro-TESE** (microdissection testicular sperm extraction) + ICSI (intracytoplasmic sperm injection). Sperm found in **~40–50%** (68% in one series); offspring are usually chromosomally normal.
- Spermatogenesis declines with age, so retrieve early (late adolescence or young adulthood). Some give clomiphene, anastrozole or hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin) beforehand.
- **TRT (testosterone replacement therapy):** for hypogonadism (bone, muscle, mood, libido). It **suppresses spermatogenesis**, so offer sperm retrieval or banking **before** starting.
- Gynaecomastia: reduction mammoplasty if persistent. Speech, educational and psychological support.

### Associated risks
- Osteoporosis, metabolic syndrome, diabetes, VTE (venous thromboembolism), autoimmune disease.
- **Mediastinal (extragonadal) germ cell tumours**; male breast cancer (~1% lifetime).

**Diagram to draw:** hypothalamic–pituitary–testis axis showing high FSH and LH, low testosterone.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - 47,XXY; non-mosaic 80–90%; 1 in 500–1000.
> - Small firm testes, high FSH and LH, azoospermia.
> - Micro-TESE ~50% success; retrieve sperm before testosterone.
> - Risks: mediastinal germ cell tumour, breast cancer, osteoporosis.

## IV-3c. Brain dead committee.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

*Assumed meaning: the Board of Medical Experts that certifies brain-stem death under Indian law.*

### Legal basis
- **THOA (Transplantation of Human Organs Act) 1994** legalised brain-stem death in India; **amended 2011** (Transplantation of Human Organs and Tissues Act); **Rules 2014**.
- Brain-stem death: irreversible loss of consciousness and capacity to breathe.

### Composition of the Board (Rules 2014)
| Member | Role |
|---|---|
| 1. Registered medical practitioner **in charge of the hospital** | Hospital authority |
| 2. **Independent specialist**, nominated by the in-charge from a panel approved by the Appropriate Authority | Independent check |
| 3. **Neurologist or neurosurgeon** from the approved panel (if unavailable: surgeon, physician, anaesthetist or intensivist) | Expert |
| 4. Medical practitioner **treating the patient** | Clinical history |

- **No member may belong to the transplant team.**

### Certification procedure
- **Preconditions:** known irreversible cause, apnoeic coma on ventilator; exclude sedatives, relaxants, hypothermia, metabolic or endocrine causes, shock.
- **Two sets of tests, at least 6 hours apart**, recorded on **Form 10**.

| Brainstem reflex (all absent) | Method | Cranial nerves |
|---|---|---|
| Pupillary | Fixed pupils, no light reaction | II, III |
| Corneal | Touch cornea: no blink | V, VII |
| Oculocephalic | Turn head: eyes move with head | VIII, III, VI |
| Oculovestibular | Ice-cold water in each ear: no eye movement | VIII, III, VI |
| Motor in cranial nerve area | Supraorbital pressure: no grimace | V, VII |
| Gag and cough | Pharynx and tracheal suction: no response | IX, X |

- **Apnoea test:** pre-oxygenate with 100% oxygen, disconnect ventilator with oxygen insufflation; **no breathing effort at PaCO₂ (arterial CO₂) ≥60 mmHg**. Abort if hypotension, desaturation or arrhythmia.

### Linked duties
- **Mandatory request:** ICU (intensive care unit) staff must inform relatives of the option to donate; a **transplant coordinator** counsels; next of kin give consent.
- **NOTTO** (National Organ and Tissue Transplant Organisation), with regional and state bodies (ROTTO, SOTTO: regional and state organ and tissue transplant organisations), allocates organs and keeps the registry.

**Diagram to draw:** flowchart: coma → preconditions → test 1 → 6 h → test 2 → Form 10 → consent → NOTTO.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - 4-member Board; no transplant-team member.
> - Two tests ≥6 h apart; Form 10.
> - Apnoea test: no breathing at PaCO₂ ≥60 mmHg.

## IV-3d. Chronic non bacterial prostatitis.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition and classification
- Now called **CP/CPPS (chronic prostatitis/chronic pelvic pain syndrome)**, NIH (National Institutes of Health) category III: pelvic pain for **≥3 of the last 6 months** without uropathogenic infection. 90–95% of prostatitis.

| NIH category | Description |
|---|---|
| I | Acute bacterial prostatitis |
| II | Chronic bacterial prostatitis (recurrent same-organism UTI) |
| **IIIA** | CP/CPPS, **inflammatory** (leucocytes in EPS (expressed prostatic secretion), VB3 (post-massage urine) or semen) |
| **IIIB** | CP/CPPS, **non-inflammatory** |
| IV | Asymptomatic inflammatory |

- IIIA vs IIIB has no treatment value.

### Aetiology
- Interplay of past infection, neurogenic inflammation, **pelvic floor muscle dysfunction**, autoimmunity, psychological stress and **central sensitisation**. The prostate may not be the pain source.

### Clinical features
- Perineal (commonest), testicular, suprapubic or penile pain; ejaculatory pain; LUTS (lower urinary tract symptoms); sexual dysfunction.

### Evaluation (diagnosis of exclusion)
- **NIH-CPSI (Chronic Prostatitis Symptom Index):** pain, urinary and quality-of-life domains (0–43).
- DRE (digital rectal examination): prostate tender in fewer than half; palpate the pelvic floor for **trigger points** (tender in ~half).
- Urinalysis and culture; **two-glass test** (pre- and post-massage). Cytology if irritative symptoms or haematuria. PVR (post-void residual), flow.
- Selected: cystoscopy, urodynamics.
- **UPOINT phenotyping:** **U**rinary, **P**sychosocial, **O**rgan-specific, **I**nfection, **N**eurological/systemic, **T**enderness of muscles.

### Management: multimodal, phenotype-directed (monotherapy trials fail)
| Domain | Therapy |
|---|---|
| Urinary | **α-blockers** in recent-onset, α-blocker-naive men (<1 year); antimuscarinics |
| Psychosocial | CBT (cognitive behavioural therapy), stress reduction |
| Organ (prostate) | **Quercetin**, **pollen extract** (small RCTs, randomised controlled trials), 5-ARI (5α-reductase inhibitor) if BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia) |
| Infection | Quinolone or tetracycline for ≥6 weeks if treatment-naive and <1 year (EAU, European Association of Urology), or positive VB3; no repeated courses |
| Neurological | Pregabalin, amitriptyline, duloxetine |
| Tenderness | **Pelvic floor physiotherapy**, myofascial trigger-point release, biofeedback |

- **Others:** **acupuncture** (beat sham in a large RCT), PTNS (percutaneous tibial nerve stimulation), shockwave therapy, aerobic exercise; NSAIDs (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs) only in combination.
- **Not recommended:** TURP (transurethral resection of the prostate), radical prostatectomy.

**Diagram to draw:** UPOINT snowflake with a therapy for each domain.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - NIH III: pain ≥3 of 6 months, no infection; IIIA inflammatory, IIIB not.
> - NIH-CPSI to score; UPOINT to phenotype.
> - Multimodal therapy; α-blocker plus antibiotic only early.

## IV-3e. Semen analysis nomenclature.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Collection (WHO, World Health Organization, 2021, 6th edition)
- Masturbation into a clean wide-mouthed non-toxic container (special condom if needed); complete sample.
- Abstinence **2–7 days** (evidence: 1–2 days best). Keep at 20–37 °C; analyse within **1 hour**, after liquefaction (~30 min).
- At least **two samples, 2–3 weeks apart**, because of variability.

### Lower reference limits (5th centile, fertile men)
| Parameter | Lower limit |
|---|---|
| Volume | **1.4 mL** |
| Concentration | **16 million/mL** |
| Total count | **39 million/ejaculate** |
| Total motility | **42%** |
| Progressive motility | **30%** |
| Normal forms (strict) | **4%** |
| Vitality | **54%** |
| Peroxidase-positive leucocytes | <1 million/mL |

- These are reference values, not fertility cut-offs. The 6th edition grades motility as progressive, non-progressive or immotile.

### Nomenclature
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Normozoospermia | All parameters at or above reference |
| Oligozoospermia | Total count (or concentration) below reference |
| Asthenozoospermia | Progressive motility below reference |
| Teratozoospermia | Normal forms below reference |
| OAT (oligoasthenoteratozoospermia) | All three abnormal |
| Azoospermia | No sperm, **including centrifuged pellet** |
| Cryptozoospermia | Sperm absent in fresh smear, present only after centrifugation |
| Aspermia | No ejaculate (dry orgasm) |
| Hypospermia | Volume below reference |
| Necrozoospermia | Low vitality (many dead sperm) |
| Leucocytospermia | Peroxidase-positive white cells above 1 million/mL |
| Haemospermia | Blood in ejaculate |
| Globozoospermia | Round-headed sperm without acrosome |

### Notes
- **Low volume or aspermia:** post-ejaculatory urine for retrograde ejaculation; TRUS (transrectal ultrasound) for EDO (ejaculatory duct obstruction).
- **Vitality:** eosin–nigrosin, or **HOS (hypo-osmotic swelling)** test, which keeps sperm usable for ICSI (intracytoplasmic sperm injection).
- Only azoospermia is definitive; values overlap between fertile and infertile men.
- **Also know:** Kruger strict morphology; DNA fragmentation; antisperm antibodies (MAR, mixed antiglobulin reaction); semen pH no longer recommended.

**Diagram to draw:** normal sperm (head, midpiece, tail) beside a WHO reference box.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - WHO 2021: 1.4 mL, 16 million/mL, 39 million, 42%/30%, 4%, 54%.
> - Cryptozoospermia = sperm only in pellet; aspermia = no ejaculate.
> - Two samples 2–3 weeks apart; abstinence 2–7 days.

## IV-3f. Combined Androgen Blockade.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition and rationale
- **CAB (combined androgen blockade)**, also called maximal or total androgen blockade: castration (orchidectomy or LHRH (luteinising hormone-releasing hormone) agonist) **plus a non-steroidal antiandrogen**.
- **Rationale (Labrie):** castration removes ~90–95% of testosterone, but **adrenal androgens** (DHEA, dehydroepiandrosterone; androstenedione) are converted to DHT (dihydrotestosterone) inside the prostate. An antiandrogen blocks these at the AR (androgen receptor).

### Antiandrogens
| Drug | Dose | Signature toxicity |
|---|---|---|
| Flutamide | 250 mg three times daily | Diarrhoea, hepatotoxicity |
| Nilutamide | 300 mg then 150 mg daily | Delayed dark adaptation, pneumonitis |
| **Bicalutamide** | **50 mg daily** (with LHRH agent) | Best tolerated; gynaecomastia |
| Cyproterone (steroidal) | 100 mg two to three times daily | Cardiovascular, hepatic; not used for CAB now |

- Class effects: gynaecomastia, hepatotoxicity, pneumonitis.

### Evidence
- **PCTCG (Prostate Cancer Trialists' Collaborative Group) meta-analysis, 2000** (27 trials, ~8,000 men): with non-steroidal antiandrogens, 5-year survival improved by only **~2–3%** (borderline significance). Cyproterone gave no benefit (slightly worse).
- So the benefit is small, with more cost and toxicity. **AUA (American Urological Association): long-term CAB not recommended**.

### Current uses
1. **Flare prevention:** an LHRH agonist raises LH (luteinising hormone) and testosterone for 10–20 days, risking cord compression, pain or obstruction. Start antiandrogen **with or before** the agonist for **21–28 days**. (Antagonists such as degarelix or relugolix cause no flare.)
2. **Antiandrogen withdrawal:** on progression, stopping bicalutamide gives PSA (prostate-specific antigen) fall >50% in 15–30% (mutant AR made the drug an agonist).

### Modern intensification (replaces CAB in mHSPC, metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer)
| Trial | Addition to ADT (androgen deprivation therapy) | Result |
|---|---|---|
| LATITUDE, STAMPEDE (arm G) | Abiraterone + prednisone | OS (overall survival) HR (hazard ratio) 0.62 / 0.63 |
| ENZAMET | Enzalutamide (vs ADT + older antiandrogen) | OS HR 0.67 |
| ARCHES | Enzalutamide | Radiographic PFS (progression-free survival) HR 0.39 |
| TITAN | Apalutamide | OS HR 0.67 |
| PEACE-1, ARASENS | **Triplet**: + docetaxel + abiraterone or darolutamide | OS benefit (ARASENS HR 0.68) |

> **Key points to remember:**
> - CAB = castration + non-steroidal antiandrogen (adrenal androgens).
> - PCTCG: ~2–3% 5-year survival gain only.
> - Main use now: flare cover for 21–28 days.
> - ARPIs (androgen receptor pathway inhibitors) or triplets now standard in mHSPC.
