## Q1. Describe metabolic evaluation in stone disease
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)
(also answered in 2015-Summer Paper 2, Q2 and 2025-Winter Paper 1, Q3b)

### Introduction and rationale
- Stone disease is a chronic metabolic disorder: first-time formers have about a **50% risk of recurrence within 10 years**.
- Metabolic evaluation finds the treatable abnormality behind the stone. Targeted therapy matched to 24-hour urine results reduces symptomatic stone events.
- The **ROKS (Recurrence of Kidney Stone) nomogram** estimates an individual's recurrence risk.

### Physicochemical basis (why we measure these)
- Stones form when urine is **supersaturated**: nucleation, growth, aggregation and retention, often on **Randall plaque**.
- In the **metastable zone** (between solubility and formation products) crystals grow on existing nuclei but do not form spontaneously.
- **Promoters** (calcium, oxalate, uric acid, low volume, abnormal pH) raise supersaturation; **inhibitors** (citrate, magnesium, Tamm-Horsfall protein) oppose crystallisation. The 24-hour urine measures both.

### Step 1: Screening evaluation (every first-time former; AUA [American Urological Association] and EAU [European Association of Urology])
| Component | What is assessed |
|---|---|
| History | GI (gastrointestinal) disease or bariatric surgery, gout, sarcoidosis, recurrent UTI (urinary tract infection), family history; diet (fluid, salt, protein, oxalate); lithogenic drugs: calcium, vitamins C and D, acetazolamide, topiramate, steroids, triamterene, indinavir |
| Blood | Creatinine, sodium, potassium, calcium, uric acid, bicarbonate; **PTH (parathyroid hormone)** if calcium is high or high-normal |
| Urine | pH, crystals (hexagonal = cystine), culture; cyanide–nitroprusside cystine screen |
| **Stone analysis** | Infrared spectroscopy or X-ray diffraction: uric acid points to metabolic syndrome, cystine to a genetic cause, struvite to infection |
| Imaging | NCCT (non-contrast computed tomography): burden predicts future events; uric acid usually <500 HU (Hounsfield units) |

- Screening is a **triage**: low risk gets general advice; high risk goes on to 24-hour urine.

### Who is high risk
- Recurrent formers, **children**, solitary kidney, bilateral or large burden, GI disease, obesity, metabolic syndrome, diabetes, primary hyperparathyroidism, RTA (renal tubular acidosis), sarcoidosis, gout, family history; uric acid, cystine or infection stones.

### Step 2: Extended metabolic evaluation
- **One or two 24-hour urines** on the usual diet, at least 4 weeks after the episode, stone-free and infection-free. Two collections are often advised because results vary. Creatinine checks completeness (men about 20–25, women about 15–20 mg/kg/day).

| Parameter | Desirable per day | Abnormality and meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Volume | **>2.5 L** | Low volume: commonest, easiest to fix |
| Calcium | <250 mg women, <300 mg men (or <4 mg/kg) | Hypercalciuria |
| Oxalate | **<40 mg** | Dietary, **enteric** (fat binds calcium, freeing oxalate), **primary** (often >100 mg) |
| Citrate | **>320 mg** | Hypocitraturia: distal RTA, diarrhoea, hypokalaemia, animal protein |
| Uric acid | <600 mg | Hyperuricosuria promotes calcium oxalate stones |
| pH | 5.8–6.2 | <5.5 uric acid; >6.5 calcium phosphate, RTA, infection |
| Sodium | <100 mmol | High sodium raises calcium excretion |
| Cystine | Negative screen | Cystinuria; keep <250 mg/L |
- **Also know:** magnesium (hypomagnesiuria), potassium, phosphate, sulphate (protein marker); RSS (relative supersaturation) is calculated from these.

### Interpreting hypercalciuria (Pak classification)
| Type | Defect | Serum calcium | PTH | Fasting urine calcium |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Absorptive (20–40%) | Increased gut absorption | Normal | Normal or suppressed | Normal |
| Renal leak (5–8%) | Tubular calcium loss | Normal | High (secondary) | High |
| Resorptive (3–5%) | Primary hyperparathyroidism | High | High | High |
- Fast and calcium-load subtyping is no longer advised, because it does not change treatment.

### Special tests
- **Distal RTA:** urine pH >6, low citrate, calcium phosphate stones. Incomplete form: **ammonium chloride load** (0.1 g/kg) or furosemide–fludrocortisone test fails to lower urine pH below 5.5.
- **Cystinuria:** quantitative 24-hour cystine; solubility about 300 mg/L at pH 5 and 400 mg/L at pH 7.
- **Primary hyperoxaluria:** genetic testing (type 1, AGXT gene, about 70%).
- **Also know:** medullary sponge kidney and calyceal diverticulum combine stasis with metabolic defects; 2,8-dihydroxyadenine stones (APRT [adenine phosphoribosyltransferase] deficiency).

### Linking results to treatment
| Abnormality | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Low volume | Fluid for urine >2.5 L/day |
| Hypercalciuria | Thiazide (hydrochlorothiazide 25 mg twice daily) plus sodium restriction; parathyroidectomy if primary hyperparathyroidism |
| Hypocitraturia, distal RTA, uric acid stones | Potassium citrate (urine pH 6.0–6.5 for uric acid) |
| Enteric hyperoxaluria | Low fat and oxalate; calcium with meals |
| Hyperuricosuria | Allopurinol 300 mg/day |
| Cystinuria | Urine ≥3 L/day, pH 7.0–7.5, tiopronin |
- **Borghi 1996:** urine >2 L/day cut 5-year recurrence (12% vs 27%). **Borghi 2002:** normal-calcium, low-salt, low-protein diet beat a low-calcium diet.

### Special groups and follow-up
- **Children:** all evaluated (spot ratios if not toilet-trained). **Pregnancy:** defer 24-hour urine (physiological hypercalciuria).
- **Follow-up (AUA):** repeat 24-hour urine **within 6 months** of starting therapy, then yearly; blood tests for drug effects (potassium on thiazide or citrate); analyse every new stone.

### Recent advances
- **Lumasiran** (RNAi [ribonucleic acid interference]) for primary hyperoxaluria type 1; **NOSTONE (2023):** hydrochlorothiazide did not reduce recurrence; smart water bottles and apps for adherence.

```
Stone episode → screening evaluation
  Low risk → general advice
  High risk → 24-h urine ×1–2 → targeted therapy → repeat ≤6 months → yearly
```

**Diagram to draw:** supersaturation zones (undersaturated, metastable, unstable) and the evaluation algorithm.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Screening for every first-time former; 24-hour urine for high-risk and recurrent formers.
> - Targets: volume >2.5 L, oxalate <40 mg, citrate >320 mg, calcium <250/300 mg.
> - Distal RTA: ammonium chloride fails to lower pH below 5.5.
> - Borghi 1996: fluid halves recurrence; repeat 24-hour urine within 6 months.

## Q2. Describe principles of vascular anastomosis. Enumerate and describe options for vascular access surgery for HD
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)
(vascular access also answered in 2020-Summer Paper 4, Q3b and 2025-Winter Paper 2, Q3c)

*Assumed meaning: HD = haemodialysis.*

### Introduction
- A vascular anastomosis joins two vessels so that flow is smooth, leak-free and lasting. Urologists use it in renal transplantation, autotransplantation, caval reconstruction and dialysis access.
- **Types:** end-to-end, **end-to-side** (commonest: transplant, fistula), side-to-side, and patch (Carrel aortic patch).

### Principles of vascular anastomosis
| Principle | How and why |
|---|---|
| Exposure and control | Proximal and distal control with vessel loops, Rummel tourniquets, bulldog or Satinsky clamps, so the field is bloodless |
| Anticoagulation | Systemic heparin before clamping; heparinised saline flush of lumens; prevents thrombosis in the static column |
| Atraumatic handling | Hold the adventitia, never the intima; clamp clear of atheroma, because clamp injury causes intimal dissection and stenosis |
| Healthy ends | Trim damaged or diseased ends; resect venous valve cusps at the suture line (they obstruct outflow) |
| No tension, twist or kink | Adequate length; trial the final position before suturing |
| **Intima-to-intima eversion** | Smooth endothelium faces the lumen; adventitia and rough surfaces stay outside, as they are thrombogenic |
| Wide opening | Spatulate or bevel small vessels, or use end-to-side with an elliptical arteriotomy (aortic punch), to avoid stenosis |
| Suture | **Non-absorbable monofilament polypropylene**, double-armed: smooth, low drag, low thrombogenicity. IVC (inferior vena cava) or aorta 3-0/4-0; transplant vessels 5-0/6-0; wrist fistula about 6-0/7-0 |
| Needle direction | Equal, small, close bites; pass **inside-out on the artery** so plaque and intima are tacked down, not lifted into a flap |
| Continuous or interrupted | Continuous is quick and haemostatic; **interrupted** for small vessels and children, allowing growth |
| Technique | **Carrel triangulation** (three stay sutures keep the back wall clear); **parachute** (sew loosely, then draw up; one knot) or tack both apices and run to the midpoint |
| Flush and release | Before the last knot, release clamps briefly to flush air, clot and debris; leave a little slack ("growth factor") so the suture does not purse-string; release the venous/distal clamp first, then arterial |
| Confirm | Haemostasis, pulse, thrill, Doppler; in transplant the Hume test (vein compression makes the kidney swell) |
- **Failure:** early from technical error (thrombosis, leak, stenosis); late from **intimal hyperplasia**; also pseudoaneurysm and infection.
- **Also know:** vascular staplers and couplers; graft choice (autologous vein for small or contaminated fields; PTFE [polytetrafluoroethylene] for large vessels).

### Options for vascular access surgery for HD
- Haemodialysis needs a blood flow of about **250–350 mL/min**.

| Option | Construction | Ready | Comment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Non-tunnelled CVC** (central venous catheter) | Dual-lumen 10–12 Fr in internal jugular or femoral vein | At once | AKI (acute kidney injury) and emergencies; days only |
| **2. Tunnelled cuffed CVC** | Right internal jugular; Dacron cuff in tunnel blocks bacterial migration | At once | Bridge or last resort; highest infection and central stenosis |
| **3. Native AVF** (arteriovenous fistula) | Artery joined to a superficial vein, usually vein end to artery side | 6–8 weeks | **First choice:** best patency, fewest infections |
| a. Snuffbox or **radiocephalic (Brescia–Cimino)** | Radial artery to cephalic vein at wrist | | Most distal; preserves proximal sites; higher failure to mature |
| b. Brachiocephalic | Brachial artery to cephalic vein at elbow | | Higher flow; more steal |
| c. Brachiobasilic transposition | Deep basilic vein mobilised and moved superficially | Longer, often two stages | When the cephalic vein is unusable |
| d. Lower limb | Femoral vein transposition | | Upper limbs exhausted |
| **4. AVG** (arteriovenous graft) | PTFE forearm loop, upper-arm straight or loop, thigh loop | 2–3 weeks (early-cannulation grafts: days) | Poor veins; more stenosis (venous anastomosis), thrombosis, infection |
| **5. Complex access** | HeRO (Haemodialysis Reliable Outflow) graft bypasses central venous occlusion into the right atrium | | Central vein occlusion |
| **6. Endovascular AVF** | Percutaneous radiofrequency or thermal anastomosis between adjacent forearm vessels | | No incision; selected anatomy |
- **Also know:** peritoneal dialysis needs no vascular access.

### Planning
- **Hierarchy:** AVF, then AVG, then CVC; **distal before proximal; non-dominant arm first**. KDOQI (Kidney Disease Outcomes Quality Initiative) 2019 asks for an individual "ESKD (end-stage kidney disease) Life-Plan".
- **Timing:** refer at eGFR (estimated glomerular filtration rate) <30; create the AVF about **6 months** before dialysis.
- **Vein preservation:** no venepuncture or PICC (peripherally inserted central catheter) in that arm; avoid subclavian lines.
- **Assessment:** pulses, **Allen test** (ulnar collateral flow), **Doppler vein mapping** (vessels about ≥2 mm); venography if central stenosis is suspected.
- **Maturity "rule of 6s":** flow >600 mL/min, diameter >6 mm, depth <6 mm, at 6 weeks.

### Complications of access
| Complication | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Failure to mature (juxta-anastomotic stenosis) | Angioplasty; ligate side branches |
| Stenosis (neointimal hyperplasia) | PTA (percutaneous transluminal angioplasty), stent graft |
| **Thrombosis** (usually on a stenosis) | Thrombectomy, then treat the stenosis |
| **Steal syndrome** (hand ischaemia) | Banding, DRIL (distal revascularisation–interval ligation) |
| Aneurysm; infection (CVC > AVG > AVF) | Repair; antibiotics, excise graft, remove catheter |
- **Also know:** venous hypertension; high-output cardiac failure; monitoring by thrill, bruit and arm-elevation test.

**Diagram to draw:** radiocephalic end-to-side fistula with Carrel triangulation stay sutures.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Intima-to-intima eversion, monofilament polypropylene, inside-out on the artery, flush before tying.
> - AVF first, distal, non-dominant arm, about 6 months ahead.
> - Rule of 6s: >600 mL/min, >6 mm, <6 mm deep, 6 weeks.
> - Thrombosis sits on a stenosis; steal → banding or DRIL.

## Q3a. Bladder preserving approach in invasive TCC of bladder
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in 2019-Summer Paper 4, Q2)

### Introduction
- Standard for MIBC (muscle-invasive bladder cancer) is cisplatin NAC (neoadjuvant chemotherapy) plus RC (radical cystectomy). Bladder preservation suits **unfit** patients and **fit, motivated patients with limited disease**.
- Single modalities fail because they leave the urothelium or occult nodes untreated.

### Options
| Option | Use |
|---|---|
| Radical TURBT (transurethral resection of bladder tumour) alone | Not a primary treatment |
| **Partial cystectomy + PLND (pelvic lymph node dissection)** | Solitary tumour **<3 cm** in mobile bladder (dome), **no CIS (carcinoma in situ)**, 1–2 cm margin, frozen sections; **urachal adenocarcinoma** |
| RT (radiotherapy) or chemotherapy alone | Inferior |
| **TMT (trimodality therapy)** | Best studied, guideline-accepted |

### Trimodality therapy
- **Components:** (1) maximal **visibly complete TURBT**; (2) RT to bladder ± nodes; (3) concurrent **radiosensitising chemotherapy**.
- **Selection:** cT2, small solitary tumour, **no hydronephrosis, no CIS**, good bladder function, compliant patient. Only 6–19% of MIBC qualify.
- **Radiosensitisers:** **mitomycin C + 5-FU (fluorouracil)** (**BC2001:** 2-year locoregional DFS [disease-free survival] 67% vs 48% vs RT alone); cisplatin; gemcitabine.
- **Dose:** 64–66 Gy in 32 fractions or **55 Gy in 20** (non-inferior).
```
TURBT → chemoradiation → cystoscopy + biopsy at 3–4 months
  Complete response → surveillance
  Persistent or recurrent invasive → salvage cystectomy
  NMIBC recurrence → TURBT + BCG
```
- **Salvage cystectomy** in **10–30%**: the designed safety net.

### Outcomes
- 5-year CSS (cancer-specific survival) 50–84%. Propensity-matched RC vs TMT: equal 5-year metastasis-free survival (about 75%) and CSS (81% vs 84%). No completed randomised trial (SPARE closed).

### Follow-up
- Cystoscopy every 3–4 months for 2 years, then less often, for 10 years; CT (computed tomography) urogram and chest imaging; cytology.

### Recent advances
- Checkpoint inhibitors with chemoradiation under trial (S1806, KEYNOTE-992); bladder sparing after complete response to NAC (RETAIN: about half recurred).

**Diagram to draw:** TMT flowchart (as above).

> **Key points to remember:**
> - TMT = complete TURBT + RT + radiosensitiser; T2, no hydronephrosis, no CIS.
> - BC2001: MMC/5-FU + RT, DFS 67% vs 48%.
> - Salvage cystectomy 10–30%; partial cystectomy for solitary <3 cm dome tumour.

## Q3b. mRCC.
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in 2024-Winter Paper 3, Q3b; immunotherapy in detail in 2024-Winter Paper 2, Q3e)

*Assumed meaning: management of metastatic renal cell carcinoma (mRCC).*

### Assessment
- CT (computed tomography) chest, abdomen and pelvis; bone scan or brain MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) if symptomatic; **biopsy** for histology.
- **IMDC (International Metastatic RCC Database Consortium) risk factors:** Karnofsky <80%; haemoglobin below normal; calcium above ULN (upper limit of normal); neutrophils above ULN; platelets above ULN; <1 year from diagnosis to systemic therapy.
- Favourable 0, intermediate 1–2, poor ≥3; median survival 43.2, 22.5 and 7.8 months. (MSKCC [Memorial Sloan Kettering] model: 5 factors including LDH [lactate dehydrogenase].)

### First-line systemic therapy (clear cell)
| IMDC risk | Preferred | If IO (immuno-oncology) not possible |
|---|---|---|
| Favourable | Pembrolizumab + axitinib; nivolumab + cabozantinib; pembrolizumab + lenvatinib | Sunitinib, pazopanib; surveillance for indolent low-volume disease |
| Intermediate/poor | Same three, **or nivolumab + ipilimumab** | Cabozantinib, sunitinib, pazopanib |
- **Rationale:** VHL (von Hippel–Lindau) loss → HIF (hypoxia-inducible factor) → VEGF (vascular endothelial growth factor), the target of TKIs (tyrosine kinase inhibitors); checkpoint inhibitors release T-cell brakes.

### Later lines
- After IO: a VEGF TKI not used before (cabozantinib, axitinib, tivozanib) or **belzutifan** (HIF-2α inhibitor). IO rechallenge is not advised (CONTACT-03 negative).

### Cytoreductive nephrectomy (CN)
- **CARMENA (2018):** sunitinib alone non-inferior to CN + sunitinib in intermediate/poor risk. **SURTIME:** deferred CN safe.
- Upfront CN only for fit, low-burden patients; **deferred CN** in responders; avoid in poor risk.

### Metastasectomy and radiotherapy
- Complete resection of limited metastases: 5-year survival 35–50%.
- **SBRT (stereotactic body radiotherapy)** for oligometastases; radiosurgery for brain; palliative radiotherapy for bone pain.

### Supportive and special groups
- Zoledronic acid or denosumab for bone. Non-clear cell: trials, cabozantinib or sunitinib. Adjuvant pembrolizumab after complete metastasectomy (KEYNOTE-564).

**Diagram to draw:** flowchart: biopsy + IMDC → IO combination → deferred CN in responders → switch class on progression.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - IMDC 6 factors; poor = ≥3.
> - IO combinations for all risks; nivolumab + ipilimumab for intermediate/poor.
> - CARMENA and SURTIME: no routine upfront CN.
> - Belzutifan after IO and TKI.

## Q3c. Dexamethasone suppression test
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Principle
- **Dexamethasone** is a potent synthetic glucocorticoid that is **not detected by the cortisol assay**. In a normal axis it suppresses pituitary ACTH (adrenocorticotropic hormone) by negative feedback, so morning cortisol falls.
- **Autonomous** cortisol (adrenal adenoma) or excess ACTH drive (pituitary, ectopic) fails to suppress.
- "Low-dose" 1 mg is still 3–4 times physiological glucocorticoid.

### Indications
- Suspected Cushing syndrome; **every adrenal incidentaloma** (5–8% secrete cortisol); before adrenalectomy; functional work-up of suspected ACC (adrenocortical carcinoma).

### Types
| Test | Method | Abnormal |
|---|---|---|
| **Overnight 1-mg LD-DST** (low-dose DST; preferred screen) | Dexamethasone 1 mg at 11 pm–midnight; serum cortisol 8–9 am | See below |
| 48-hour 2-mg LD-DST | 0.5 mg 6-hourly for 48 hours; cortisol at 48 hours | >1.8 µg/dL; improves accuracy |
| High-dose DST | 8 mg overnight or 2 mg 6-hourly for 48 hours | Over 50% suppression suggests pituitary source; **no longer routine** (some ectopic tumours also suppress) |

### Interpreting the 1-mg test
| Morning cortisol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **≤1.8 µg/dL (50 nmol/L)** | Normal; >90% sensitive, rules out |
| 1.8–5.0 µg/dL | Possible autonomous cortisol secretion |
| **>5.0 µg/dL (140 nmol/L)** | Autonomous secretion; >95% specific |
- ESE (European Society of Endocrinology) 2023 incidentaloma guideline: cortisol >1.8 µg/dL without overt signs = **MACS (mild autonomous cortisol secretion)**.
- If abnormal: repeat testing and measure **morning ACTH** (low = adrenal; high = pituitary or ectopic, IPSS [inferior petrosal sinus sampling]).

### Pitfalls
- **False positive:** oral contraceptives and oestrogens (raise cortisol-binding globulin; up to 50%); CYP3A4 inducers (phenytoin, carbamazepine, rifampicin); missed dose; pseudo-Cushing states (depression, alcohol, obesity, poorly controlled diabetes, stress).
- **False negative:** CYP3A4 inhibitors (itraconazole, ritonavir, fluoxetine, diltiazem, cimetidine).
- **Choice of test:** pregnancy → UFC (urinary free cortisol); epilepsy → LNSC (late-night salivary cortisol); renal failure → DST (not affected by GFR [glomerular filtration rate]).
- Exclude exogenous steroids (including creams and sprays) first.

### Urological relevance
- Autonomous cortisol suppresses the opposite adrenal: give **perioperative steroid cover** and taper after adrenalectomy.

**Diagram to draw:** HPA (hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal) axis with dexamethasone feedback.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - 1 mg at 11 pm; 8 am cortisol ≤1.8 µg/dL excludes; >5.0 µg/dL confirms autonomy.
> - Oral contraceptives and enzyme inducers cause false positives.
> - High-dose DST no longer routine; ACTH separates adrenal from pituitary/ectopic.

## Q3d. HIFU
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition and principle
- HIFU (high-intensity focused ultrasound) focuses ultrasound from a bowl-shaped transducer or lens onto a point. Energy is absorbed only where the beams converge, so tissue there is heated while the path is spared.
- **Intensity 1500–10,000 W/cm²** (diagnostic ultrasound causes negligible heating).
- **Mechanisms:** **thermal coagulative necrosis** (≥55 °C; prostate exceeds 100 °C within seconds) plus **cavitation** (bubbles collapse and damage cells).
- Each lesion is about the size of a **grain of rice**; the target is "painted" with overlapping lesions. Ablated and viable tissue look alike on ultrasound, so gaps can be missed.

### Devices and routes
- **Transrectal** probe that both plans/monitors and treats (posterior zone).
- **TULSA (MRI [magnetic resonance imaging]-guided transurethral ultrasound ablation)** and MRgFUS (MRI-guided focused ultrasound): **MR thermometry** confirms lethal temperature; TULSA reaches the anterior gland.

### Indications
| Setting | Use |
|---|---|
| **Focal therapy, prostate cancer** | MRI-visible, unilateral, grade group 2–3 index lesion; hemiablation; based on the index-lesion hypothesis |
| Whole-gland ablation | Localised cancer; retreatment 29–38% |
| **Salvage after EBRT** (external beam radiotherapy) | Biopsy-proven local recurrence, **≥2 years after EBRT** |
| BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia) | Approved |
| Kidney | Investigational (incomplete ablation; respiratory motion) |

### Limitations
- **Anterior lesions** (energy lost, oedema displaces target), large gland, gland height and **calcification** (some advise TURP [transurethral resection of the prostate] first).

### Outcomes
- **Focal HIFU:** continence 90–95%, potency 50–78%; infield positive biopsy 16.7–54% (csPCa [clinically significant prostate cancer] 0–21.8%).
- **HEAT registry** (1379 men): 7-year failure-free survival 69%.
- **Salvage HIFU:** biochemical progression-free survival about 48% at 3 years; continence 64–87.5%.

### Complications
- Retention (about 8%), UTI (urinary tract infection) 6–18%, stricture (4%), ED (erectile dysfunction), incontinence; rectourethral fistula rare (about 2% after salvage); osteitis pubis.

### Follow-up
- PSA (prostate-specific antigen) falls only 50–80%; **mpMRI (multiparametric MRI) + targeted biopsy at 3–6 months**, then MRI and biopsy at 1–2 and 5 years. EAU (European Association of Urology): focal therapy within trials or registries.

**Also know:** **histotripsy** (>10,000 W/cm², mechanical liquefaction, visible bubble cloud).

**Diagram to draw:** transrectal transducer focusing beams on a rice-grain lesion in the posterior prostate.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Focused ultrasound → ≥55 °C coagulative necrosis plus cavitation.
> - Transrectal HIFU suits posterior lesions; TULSA for anterior.
> - Focal HIFU: continence 90–95%; HEAT 7-year failure-free 69%.

## Q3e. Discuss techniques of dialysis
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(HD principles also answered in 2017-Winter Paper 2, Q1; CAPD in 2018-Summer Paper 2, Q3e)

*Assumed meaning: principles and techniques of HD (haemodialysis) and PD (peritoneal dialysis), with continuous therapies.*

### Physical principles
| Process | Driving force | Removes |
|---|---|---|
| **Diffusion** | Concentration gradient | Small solutes (urea, potassium); bicarbonate enters blood |
| **Convection** | Transmembrane pressure; solute dragged with water | Middle molecules (β2-microglobulin) |
| **Ultrafiltration** | Hydrostatic (HD) or osmotic (PD) gradient | Water |

### Haemodialysis
- Blood (250–350 mL/min) passes through a hollow-fibre **synthetic membrane**; dialysate runs **countercurrent** to keep a gradient along the dialyser.
- Ultrapure dialysate; heparin (citrate if bleeding risk); about 4 hours, 3 times a week.
- **Access:** AVF (arteriovenous fistula), AVG (arteriovenous graft) or CVC (central venous catheter).
- **Adequacy:** Kt/V ≥1.2 (target 1.4); URR (urea reduction ratio) ≥65%.
- **Complications:** hypotension (commonest), cramps, **disequilibrium syndrome**, access infection, thrombosis, steal.

### Peritoneal dialysis
- The **peritoneum** is the membrane: diffusion for solutes; **glucose or icodextrin** draws water osmotically.
- **Tenckhoff catheter** (two Dacron cuffs), placed open, laparoscopically (omentopexy) or percutaneously.
- **CAPD (continuous ambulatory PD):** about 4 exchanges of 2 L daily. **APD (automated PD):** night cycler.
- **Adequacy:** weekly Kt/V ≥1.7; PET (peritoneal equilibration test) classifies transport.
- **Complications:** peritonitis, exit-site infection, catheter malfunction, hernia, hyperglycaemia, peritoneal fibrosis.

### Continuous and hybrid therapies
- **CRRT (continuous renal replacement therapy):** CVVH (continuous veno-venous haemofiltration; convection), CVVHD (haemodialysis; diffusion), CVVHDF (both). For haemodynamically unstable AKI (acute kidney injury).
- **Also know:** SLED (sustained low-efficiency dialysis); HDF (haemodiafiltration: CONVINCE 2023 lowered mortality versus high-flux HD); home and nocturnal HD.

### HD versus PD
| | HD | PD |
|---|---|---|
| Setting | Centre or home | Home, continuous |
| Haemodynamics | Rapid shifts | Gentle; preserves residual function |
| Favoured in | Abdominal surgery, failed PD | Children, cardiac instability, no vascular access |

### Indications ("AEIOU", refractory to medical treatment)
- Acidosis, Electrolytes (hyperkalaemia), Ingestions (lithium, salicylates), Overload, Uraemia.

**Diagram to draw:** dialyser with countercurrent flow beside a PD catheter in the pelvis.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Diffusion for small solutes, convection for middle molecules, ultrafiltration for water.
> - HD Kt/V ≥1.2; PD weekly Kt/V ≥1.7.
> - CRRT for unstable AKI; PD preferred in children.

## Q3f. RIRS
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in 2017-Winter Paper 2, Q3e)

### Introduction
- RIRS (retrograde intrarenal surgery) is flexible ureterorenoscopy inside the kidney through the natural route. Active deflection, miniaturised digital scopes and the laser made it practical.

### Instrument
| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Tip | About 7.5–9 Fr |
| Working channel | **3.6 Fr** (laser fibre, basket) |
| Deflection | ≥270° both ways; lower pole needs a steep angle |
| Types | **Fibreoptic** (image degrades); **digital** (CCD [charge-coupled device] chip on tip, better image); **single-use** |

### Indications
- **Stones:** renal stones **<2 cm**; lower pole <1 cm, or 1–2 cm as an alternative to PCNL (percutaneous nephrolithotomy); patients who must stay on anticoagulants; failed SWL (shock wave lithotripsy); obesity; staged treatment of larger stones.
- **Others:** low-grade UTUC (upper tract urothelial carcinoma); filling defects; calyceal diverticulum; infundibular stenosis; endopyelotomy.

### Technique
1. Sterile urine; single-dose antibiotic.
2. Cystoscopy, **safety guidewire**, retrograde pyelogram.
3. **Ureteral access sheath** (often 12/14 Fr): lowers intrarenal pressure and eases repeated passes, but causes ureteric injury in nearly half (severe in about 1 in 8).
4. Low-pressure irrigation. **Ho:YAG (holmium:yttrium-aluminium-garnet, 2120 nm)** or **thulium fibre laser**: dusting (low energy, high frequency) or fragment-and-basket.
5. Relocate lower pole stones to an upper calyx with a basket.
6. Inspect the ureter on withdrawal. Stent may be omitted if anatomy and function are normal, no injury and no second stage (AUA [American Urological Association]).
- **Unfavourable lower pole:** steep infundibulopelvic angle, long or narrow infundibulum.

### Complications
- Sepsis about 5%; stricture about 3%; perforation (a few per cent); bleeding or subcapsular haematoma <1%; avulsion <1%; stent symptoms.

### Recent advances
- Single-use digital scopes; thulium fibre laser and pulse-modulated holmium (less retropulsion); **suction and flexible navigable sheaths**; intrarenal pressure sensors; robotic flexible ureteroscopy.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - RIRS: stones <2 cm and patients on anticoagulants.
> - Access sheath lowers pressure but injures the ureter.
> - Sepsis about 5%, stricture about 3%.
