## Q1. Discuss anatomy of kidney. Describe role of nephron sparing nephrectomy in small renal mass.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)
(also answered in 2017-Summer Paper 1, Q1; NSS also in 2022-Summer Paper 3, Q1 and 2025-Winter Paper 1, Q2)

*"Nephron sparing nephrectomy" is read as nephron-sparing surgery (NSS): partial nephrectomy and ablation.*

### Surgical anatomy of the kidney
**Position:** T12–L3; right kidney 1–2 cm lower (liver). About 11 cm, 125–170 g. On the cone-shaped psoas, the upper pole is medial and posterior, and the kidney is angled 30–50° behind the coronal plane.

**Relations**

| Aspect | Right | Left |
|---|---|---|
| Superior | Liver, adrenal, 12th rib | Spleen, stomach, adrenal, 11th–12th ribs |
| Medial/anterior | Duodenum (2nd part), hepatic flexure | Pancreatic tail with splenic vessels, splenic flexure, jejunum |
| Posterior (both) | Diaphragm, psoas, quadratus lumborum, subcostal nerve | Same |

**Coverings:** true capsule, perirenal fat, **Gerota fascia**, pararenal fat. Gerota fascia is closed above and laterally but open below, so it contains tumour and collections, yet fluid tracks into the pelvis.

**Internal structure:** cortex, 8–18 pyramids; 0.4–1.2 million nephrons. Collecting system: see Q3d.

**Vascular anatomy (the basis of partial nephrectomy)**
- Hilum, anterior to posterior: vein, artery, pelvis, posterior segmental artery.
- Renal artery from the aorta at L1–L2; the right passes behind the IVC (inferior vena cava).
- **Five segmental end arteries**: posterior (first branch, behind the pelvis) and four from the anterior division (apical, upper, middle, lower). With no anastomoses, occlusion causes segmental infarction, but each segment can be clamped or resected separately.
- **Brödel line:** avascular plane between anterior (two-thirds) and posterior (one-third) territories, just behind the lateral border.
- **Accessory arteries** in 25–28%, end arteries too (see Practice Set 1 Paper 2, Q3c).
- **Veins anastomose freely**, so a segmental vein can be ligated safely. The long left renal vein receives adrenal, gonadal and lumbar veins.
- **Also know:** lymph to para-aortic (left) or interaortocaval and paracaval nodes (right).

**Diagram to draw:** transverse kidney section with segmental arteries and Brödel line.

### Small renal mass (SRM)
- An enhancing renal mass **≤4 cm (cT1a)**, usually incidental.
- 20–30% of masses ≤4 cm and about 40% ≤2 cm are benign (oncocytoma, fat-poor angiomyolipoma); under 2% of the cancers metastasise.
- **Work-up:** multiphase CT (computed tomography) or MRI (magnetic resonance imaging), chest imaging, eGFR (estimated glomerular filtration rate), proteinuria. **RMB (renal mass biopsy)** if it may change management (non-diagnostic about 14%).
- **R.E.N.A.L. nephrometry** (Radius, Exophytic, Nearness to collecting system, Anterior/posterior, Location): 4–6 low, 7–9 moderate, 10–12 high complexity.

### Why spare nephrons
- RN (radical nephrectomy) causes CKD (chronic kidney disease), linked to cardiovascular events and death. PN (partial nephrectomy) keeps about 90% of global function (RN 65–70%), with equal cancer control for cT1a.

### Indications

| Category | Situations |
|---|---|
| Imperative | Solitary kidney, bilateral tumours, RN would mean dialysis |
| Relative | Contralateral kidney threatened (stones, stenosis, diabetes); hereditary RCC (renal cell carcinoma) |
| Elective | cT1a with a normal contralateral kidney: **standard of care** |

- **AUA (American Urological Association) 2021:** prioritise PN for cT1a. RN only if all three: high complexity; no CKD or proteinuria; normal contralateral kidney with eGFR likely >45 after RN.

### Partial nephrectomy
1. Open, laparoscopic or robotic (equal oncology); early hilar control; intraoperative ultrasound.
2. Clamp the artery; ice slush if ischaemia ≥25–30 minutes is expected.
3. Excise with a thin rim; a negative margin matters, its width does not.
4. Close vessels and collecting system; renorrhaphy; drain.
- Function depends mainly on the **parenchyma preserved**; ischaemia is secondary (off-clamp gave no lasting gain in randomised trials).
- **Enucleation** along the pseudocapsule for familial, multifocal disease or severe CKD.
- **Trifecta:** negative margin, no major complication, warm ischaemia ≤25 minutes.

### Other nephron-sparing options
- **Thermal ablation** (cryoablation, radiofrequency): alternative for cT1a **<3 cm**, percutaneous, biopsy first. Local recurrence is higher than PN but salvaged by repeat ablation.
- **Active surveillance:** <2 cm, elderly or comorbid; intervene if >3 cm or growth >5 mm a year (registry data: almost no metastases).

### Complications of PN
- Urine leak (stent); **delayed haematuria from pseudoaneurysm or arteriovenous fistula** (angioembolisation); acute kidney injury; positive margin.
- **Hyperfiltration** if >50% of a solitary kidney is lost: proteinuria; ACE (angiotensin-converting enzyme) inhibitor.

### Outcomes and evidence
- Elective PN: local recurrence 1–2%; imperative 3–5%.
- **EORTC (European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer) 30904 (2011):** PN versus RN, ≤5 cm: better function, similar oncology; survival slightly favoured RN, but the trial is flawed.
- **Recent advances:** 3D (three-dimensional) models, segmental clamping.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Five segmental end arteries; veins anastomose; Brödel line avascular.
> - SRM ≤4 cm: 20–30% benign; PN is standard, ablation <3 cm, surveillance <2 cm.
> - AUA: RN only if complex + no CKD + eGFR likely >45.
> - Preserved parenchyma, not clamp time, governs function; EORTC 30904.

## Q2. Anatomy of prostate and its neurovascular bundles with its clinical details.
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)
(nerve-sparing techniques also in 2023-Winter Paper 4, Q3a)

### Introduction
- A fibromuscular, glandular organ around the proximal urethra, from bladder neck (**base**) to urogenital diaphragm (**apex**), in front of the rectum; about 20 g.
- Buds from the urogenital sinus at week 10, driven by androgen acting on the **mesenchymal** AR (androgen receptor).

### Relations
- **Anterior:** retropubic space (of Retzius), puboprostatic ligaments, DVC (dorsal venous complex).
- **Posterior:** Denonvilliers fascia, then rectal ampulla, hence DRE (digital rectal examination) and TRUS (transrectal ultrasound).
- **Lateral:** levator ani with its fascia; NVB (neurovascular bundle) posterolaterally.
- **Base:** bladder neck; seminal vesicles behind. **Inside:** the urethra bends about 35° at the **verumontanum**, where the ejaculatory ducts open beside the utricle.

### Zonal anatomy (McNeal 1981)

| Zone | Glandular share | Position | Clinical |
|---|---|---|---|
| Peripheral (PZ) | About 70% | Posterior and lateral; longest ducts | **70% of cancers**; felt on DRE; biopsy target |
| Central (CZ) | About 25% | Cone around the ejaculatory ducts at the base | Few cancers; mimics HGPIN (high-grade prostatic intraepithelial neoplasia) |
| Transition (TZ) | About 5% | Two lobes beside the preprostatic sphincter; shortest ducts | **BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia)**; anterior cancers (about 15%) |
| Periurethral glands | Small | Within the preprostatic sphincter | Middle-lobe BPH |
| Anterior fibromuscular stroma | No glands | Anterior | — |

- **Capsule:** no true histological capsule, so cancer shows "extraprostatic extension". In BPH the compressed PZ forms the **surgical capsule**, the plane of enucleation. The capsule transmits growth pressure to the urethra, so TUIP (transurethral incision) relieves obstruction.

### Fascial investments

| Layer | Position | Surgical rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Prostatic fascia** | On the gland; carries DVC tributaries anteriorly | Stays on the prostate in nerve sparing |
| **Levator fascia** | Covers levator; fuses with prostatic fascia as the lateral pelvic fascia | Incised to reach the NVB |
| **Denonvilliers fascia** | Between prostate and rectum; thickest at base and vesicles | Excised completely for the posterior margin |


### Blood supply and lymphatics
- **Inferior vesical artery** ends as: **urethral group** (enters at the vesicoprostatic junction, 5 and 7 o'clock; supplies the adenoma) and **capsular group** (posterolateral, in the lateral pelvic fascia, marking the cavernous nerves).
- **Accessory pudendal arteries** run over the anterolateral prostate; dividing them reduces penile inflow.
- **Veins:** the deep dorsal vein passes under the pubis into a superficial branch and lateral (**Santorini**) plexuses, draining to the internal iliac vein. They anastomose freely, so tears bleed heavily.
- **Lymph:** obturator, internal and external iliac, presacral nodes.

### Neurovascular bundle
- **Pelvic plexus:** parasympathetic S2–S4 plus sympathetic (T10–L2) via the hypogastric nerves; beside the rectum, centred on the seminal vesicle tip.
- **NVB of Walsh (Walsh and Donker, 1982):** capsular vessels plus **cavernous nerves**. The nerves are invisible; the vessels mark them.
- It runs posterolaterally **between prostatic and levator fascia**, 3–5 mm from the capsule, outside Denonvilliers fascia.
- At the apex it lies at **5 and 7 o'clock**, posterolateral to the sphincter. The nerves then swing to about **3 and 9 o'clock** at the mid-urethra and enter the corpora behind the dorsal penile artery. The apex is where they are most often injured.
- Distribution varies: posterolateral bundle, posterior "hammock", anterolateral fibres (basis of high anterior release).
- **Striated sphincter:** a vertical tube over the apex (Oelrich), pudendal nerve; passive continence.

**Diagram to draw:** axial prostate showing the three fasciae and NVBs at 5 and 7 o'clock.

### Clinical details
- **BPH surgery:** TURP (transurethral resection of the prostate) stays proximal to the verumontanum (sphincter lies just distal); urethral-group vessels at 5 and 7 o'clock bleed.
- **Cancer:** PZ cancer exits posterolaterally along nerves; anterior TZ tumours are missed by DRE and biopsy, so MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) helps.
- **Radical prostatectomy (RP):**
  1. Incise endopelvic fascia; spare the pubourethral ligament.
  2. Ligate the DVC over the urethra before division.
  3. Preserve accessory pudendal arteries.
  4. Excise Denonvilliers fascia; the apex is the commonest positive-margin site.

| Nerve-sparing plane | Course | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Interfascial** | Between prostatic and levator fascia | **Standard plane** |
| Intrafascial | Under the prostatic fascia | Most nerves; more positive margins |
| Extrafascial | Outside levator fascia | Widest margin; ED (erectile dysfunction) likely |

- **NVB injury:** incision, entrapment, **heat**, **traction**, so dissect athermally without traction. Excise an NVB for palpable extracapsular disease; margins are equal with or without nerve sparing.
- **Continence:** Retzius-sparing (Bocciardi 2010) and hood techniques preserve anterior support; continence returns earlier.
- **Also know:** rectal injury at the ampulla; PDE5 (phosphodiesterase type 5) inhibitor rehabilitation.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - PZ 70% of cancers; TZ gives BPH; CZ around ejaculatory ducts.
> - NVB between prostatic and levator fascia, 5 and 7 o'clock; apex most vulnerable.
> - Interfascial plane is standard; avoid heat and traction.
> - Ligate DVC; excise Denonvilliers fascia; spare accessory pudendals.

## Q3a. MR urography
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition
- MRU (magnetic resonance urography): magnetic resonance imaging of kidneys, ureters and bladder giving anatomy and function **without ionising radiation**.

### Techniques

| | Static MRU | Excretory MRU |
|---|---|---|
| Sequence | Heavily T2-weighted; urine is bright | Gadolinium-enhanced T1 |
| Contrast | None | GBCA (gadolinium-based contrast agent) |
| Depends on function | No | Yes; low-dose furosemide improves it |
| Best for | Dilated or obstructed system | Non-dilated system, excretion |

- **Functional MRU (children):** hydration, bladder catheter, furosemide 1 mg/kg (maximum 20 mg), T2 images, then dynamic T1 after slow contrast injection. Patlak analysis gives differential function, GFR (glomerular filtration rate) and drainage.

### Appearances
- Stones and calcification give **signal voids**; tumour, clot, gas and sloughed papillae are low-signal defects in bright urine.

### Indications
- CT (computed tomography) urography contraindicated: contrast allergy, renal insufficiency, **pregnancy** (separates physiological from calculous dilatation; small stones missed).
- **Children:** complex anomalies, ectopic ureter in the incontinent girl, duplex systems, megaureter, crossing vessels in UPJ (ureteropelvic junction) obstruction, ectopic kidneys.
- Hydronephrosis: alternative to MAG3 (mercaptoacetyltriglycine) renography; equivalence not yet proven.
- Upper tract tumours and renal masses.

### Advantages and limitations

| Advantages | Limitations |
|---|---|
| No radiation; best anatomical detail | Cost, availability, long study (up to 1 hour) |
| Anatomy, function and drainage in one test | Sedation often needed under 6 years |
| As accurate as CTU (CT urography) for obstruction | Poor for stones; complex post-processing |
| Safe in iodinated contrast allergy | Pacemakers, metal implants, claustrophobia |

### Gadolinium safety
- **NSF (nephrogenic systemic fibrosis)** was the concern at GFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m². Group II agents (gadobenate, gadoteridol, gadoterate, gadobutrol) carry little or no risk even in end-stage disease.
- **Pregnancy:** gadolinium only if it clearly helps.
- **EAU (European Association of Urology):** CTU is preferred to MRU for upper tract urothelial cancer.

**Diagram to draw:** coronal static T2 MRU of a hydronephrotic kidney with a dark stone in the ureter.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Static T2 (no contrast, function-independent) versus excretory gadolinium T1.
> - Stones are signal voids; CTU preferred for urothelial cancer.
> - Functional MRU: furosemide 1 mg/kg, Patlak analysis for split function.
> - NSF risk minimal with group II agents.

## Q3b. Urine cytology
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition and principle
- Microscopy of shed urothelial cells (Papanicolaou, 1945).
- **Principle:** high-grade cells are poorly cohesive, shed readily and look atypical; low-grade cells look almost normal. So cytology detects **high-grade tumours and CIS (carcinoma in situ)** but misses low grade.

### Specimens
- Voided urine (not the first morning sample, whose cells have degenerated), at least 30 mL; bladder washing; upper tract washings.

### Performance
- Sensitivity 31–62%, specificity 94–100%; strongly grade-dependent.
- **False positives:** instrumentation, stones, infection, radiotherapy, intravesical BCG (bacille Calmette–Guérin), polyomavirus ("decoy" cells), ileal conduit.
- **False negatives:** low grade, low cellularity, degeneration.

### The Paris System
- Published 2016; **2nd edition 2022**. Aim: detect **HGUC (high-grade urothelial carcinoma)**. Key criterion: **N:C (nuclear-to-cytoplasmic) ratio**, plus hyperchromasia, coarse chromatin and irregular nuclear membrane.

| Category | Criteria |
|---|---|
| Non-diagnostic | Inadequate |
| Negative for HGUC | N:C <0.5, or atypia explained by a benign cause |
| AUC (atypical urothelial cells) | N:C ≥0.5 and one feature |
| Suspicious for HGUC | N:C ≥0.7, at least two features, few cells (<5–10) |
| HGUC | N:C ≥0.7, at least two features, many cells |
| LGUN (low-grade urothelial neoplasm) | Fibrovascular cores; rarely diagnosable |
| Other malignancy | Squamous, adenocarcinoma, secondary |

- It reduces equivocal "atypia" reports; cancer risk rises with each category.

### Clinical uses
- **Not** for screening or initial microhaematuria evaluation (AUA/SUFU [American Urological Association / Society of Urodynamics, Female Pelvic Medicine and Urogenital Reconstruction] 2020).
- Gross haematuria; NMIBC (non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer) surveillance (mandatory in high risk); suspected CIS; upper tract tumours.
- **Positive cytology, normal cystoscopy:** image upper tracts, selective washings ± ureteroscopy, random bladder and prostatic urethral biopsies.
- **Also know:** FISH (fluorescence in situ hybridisation; chromosomes 3, 7, 17, 9p21), NMP22 (nuclear matrix protein 22), BTA (bladder tumour antigen), CxBladder: more sensitive, less specific; none replaces cystoscopy.

**Diagram to draw:** normal urothelial cell beside an HGUC cell with high N:C ratio.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - High specificity, low sensitivity; good for high grade and CIS.
> - Paris System: N:C ≥0.7 for suspicious and HGUC; AUC N:C ≥0.5.
> - Positive cytology, normal cystoscopy: upper tracts, random biopsies.
> - Not for screening or initial microhaematuria.

## Q3c. Varicocele – Pathophysiology
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition and epidemiology
- Abnormal dilatation and tortuosity of the **pampiniform plexus** of the internal spermatic veins.
- About 15% of men, 35% with primary and 75–81% with secondary infertility. Appears after age 10, peaks at Tanner 3. About 90% left-sided.
- **Venous anatomy:** anterior (internal spermatic), middle (deferential) and posterior (cremasteric) groups; the last two give return after repair and cause recurrence.

### Why it develops
1. **Left-sided anatomy:** the left vein enters the left renal vein at a right angle, 8–10 cm higher than the right enters the IVC (inferior vena cava). The taller column overloads its valves. The right vein enters obliquely, which acts as a valve.
2. **Valve incompetence** or absence; continuous reflux predicts progression.
3. **Nutcracker:** left renal vein compressed between aorta and SMA (superior mesenteric artery).
4. **Collateral pathways**; **genetic** (4–8 times commoner in first-degree relatives); tall, thin habitus.
- **Secondary:** a **right-sided or non-decompressing** varicocele suggests a retroperitoneal or renal mass, so image the abdomen.

### How it damages the testis

| Mechanism | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Heat (primary)** | Lost countercurrent heat exchange; the testis normally runs 2–4 °C below core |
| Oxidative stress | Raised reactive oxygen species → sperm DNA fragmentation |
| Hypoperfusion and stasis | Hypoxia, toxin accumulation |
| Apoptosis | Germ cell loss |
| Leydig dysfunction | Lower testosterone than controls |
- The injury is **progressive and duration-dependent**, and may affect **both** testes.

### Consequences
- **Testicular hypotrophy** (asymmetry above about 20% guides adolescent repair); poor semen; hypogonadism; ache on standing (2–11%).
- Repair improves semen, DNA fragmentation, testosterone and pregnancy (**Madgar RCT [randomised controlled trial], 1995:** 44% versus 10% at 1 year).

### Grading
| Grade | Finding |
|---|---|
| Subclinical | Doppler only (not treated) |
| I | Palpable with Valsalva only |
| II | Palpable, not visible |
| III | Visible |

**Diagram to draw:** left and right testicular veins draining into renal vein and IVC.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Left vein: right-angled entry, 8–10 cm higher column; 90% left.
> - Heat (lost countercurrent exchange) is the main injury; also oxidative DNA damage.
> - Right or non-decompressing varicocele: image for a renal mass.
> - Injury is progressive; subclinical varicocele is not treated.

## Q3d. Anatomy of renal collecting system and its relevance to PCNL
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in 2022-Summer Paper 1, Q1 and 2017-Winter Paper 1, Q1)

### Anatomy of the collecting system
- **Minor calyx:** a cup around a papilla, with side projections (**fornices**); it drains by an infundibulum to a major calyx and pelvis. **Compound calyces** cluster at the poles.
- 7–9 calyces (upper 3, interpolar 3–4, lower 2–3); hilar ones in **anterior and posterior rows**.
- **Pelvis:** 3–10 mL, intrarenal or extrarenal; a **duplex pelvis** does not link upper and lower calyces.
- The kidney is rotated 30–50° posteriorly, so **posterior calyces point towards the prone surgeon**.

### Sampaio classification
| Type | Frequency | Midzone drainage |
|---|---|---|
| A-I | 45% | Via a polar group |
| A-II | 17.2% | **Crossed calyces** to both groups |
| B-I | 21.4% | Independent major calyx |
| B-II | 16.4% | Minor calyces straight into the pelvis |
- **Perpendicular minor calyx (11.4%):** hidden on pyelography.

### Vascular relations
- Interlobar arteries run in the **columns of Bertin**, hugging the infundibula; the posterior segmental artery passes behind the pelvis.
- **Brödel line:** avascular plane behind the lateral border, along the posterior calyces.

| Puncture site (Sampaio) | Vascular injury |
|---|---|
| Upper-pole infundibulum | 67% (arterial 26%) |
| Mid-pole infundibulum | Arterial 23% |
| Lower-pole infundibulum | Infundibular artery in 38% |
| **Calyceal fornix** | Venous <8%, **no arterial injury** |

### Relevance to PCNL (percutaneous nephrolithotomy)
- **Target:** a posterior calyx with a short, wide infundibulum, entered **through the papilla and fornix** along its axis, never through the infundibulum or pelvis.
- **Identify posterior calyces:** they shorten when the C-arm rotates towards the surgeon; air fills them when prone.
- **Upper pole:** best reach to pelvis, UPJ (ureteropelvic junction) and staghorns; chest risk above the 12th rib, highest above the 11th.
- **Lower pole:** chest-safe; retrorenal colon risk (left); poor UPJ reach.
- **Also know:** traps: perpendicular or crossed calyces, duplex pelvis.

**Diagram to draw:** transverse kidney section with a needle through a posterior fornix.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Sampaio A-I 45%, A-II (crossed) 17.2%, B-I 21.4%, B-II 16.4%.
> - Fornix puncture: no arterial injury; upper-pole infundibulum 67%.
> - Target a posterior calyx with a short, wide infundibulum; upper pole reaches most.

## Q3e. Compare and contrast various detrusor relaxants in management of OAB
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(mirabegron also answered in 2024-Winter Paper 3, Q3d)

### Introduction and mechanisms
- In OAB (overactive bladder), drugs follow behavioural therapy. Two classes: **antimuscarinics** and **β3-adrenoceptor agonists**.
- **Antimuscarinics:** acetylcholine acts on **M3** receptors (raising intracellular calcium) to contract the detrusor; the commoner M2 enhances it. Blockade reduces involuntary contractions and afferent signalling.
- **β3 agonists:** β3 is the commonest detrusor adrenoceptor. Activation raises **cAMP (cyclic adenosine monophosphate)** and relaxes the detrusor during storage, sparing the voiding contraction.

### Agents

| Drug | Distinguishing feature | Usual dose |
|---|---|---|
| Oxybutynin | Non-selective; direct relaxant; metabolite causes dry mouth | 5 mg two to three times daily; extended-release (ER), patch |
| Tolterodine | Bladder over salivary selective | ER 4 mg daily |
| Fesoterodine | Prodrug of tolterodine's active metabolite | 4–8 mg daily |
| Solifenacin | 10-fold M3 > M2; long half-life | 5–10 mg daily |
| Darifenacin | About 30-fold M3-selective | 7.5–15 mg daily |
| Trospium | Quaternary; does not cross the blood–brain barrier | 20 mg twice daily |
| β3: mirabegron | Inhibits CYP2D6 (a liver enzyme): metoprolol interaction | 25–50 mg daily |
| β3: vibegron | No CYP2D6 interaction | 75 mg daily |

### Comparison

| Feature | Antimuscarinics | β3 agonists |
|---|---|---|
| Efficacy | Similar | Similar (TAURUS: mirabegron ≈ tolterodine) |
| Dry mouth, constipation | Common; main reason for stopping | Near placebo |
| Cognition | Central M1 blockade; **dementia risk 10–65% higher with use >3 months** | No anticholinergic burden |
| Retention | Up to 3% in men | Low |
| Contraindications | Urinary or gastric retention, uncontrolled narrow-angle glaucoma | Uncontrolled hypertension (≥180/110 mmHg) |

- **Choice:** in the elderly, prefer a β3 agonist or ER trospium, darifenacin or fesoterodine; ER forms cause less dry mouth.

### Combination
- Solifenacin plus mirabegron beats each alone (**SYNERGY**); in non-responders it equals solifenacin 10 mg with less dry mouth (**BESIDE**). In men, add to an α-blocker; watch PVR (post-void residual).
- **Also know:** intravesical oxybutynin; imipramine; refractory OAB: onabotulinumtoxinA 100 U, neuromodulation.

**Diagram to draw:** detrusor cell: acetylcholine → M3 → contraction; noradrenaline → β3 → cAMP → relaxation.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - M3 mediates contraction; β3 raises cAMP to relax.
> - Equal efficacy; β3 avoids dry mouth and dementia risk (10–65% with antimuscarinics >3 months).
> - Solifenacin + mirabegron (SYNERGY) works.

## Q3f. B/l congenital VUR - Pathophysiology and natural history
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(VUR management also answered in 2015-Summer Paper 3, Q2 and 2020-Summer Paper 1, Q2)

*"B/l congenital VUR" is read as bilateral primary (congenital) VUR (vesicoureteral reflux), often detected antenatally.*

### Pathophysiology
- **Embryology:** a ureteric bud arising too early over-rotates, so the orifice lies **high and lateral** with a short tunnel. Its abnormal contact with the metanephros causes **renal dysplasia**, so high-grade VUR and dysplasia coexist.
- **Failed UVJ (ureterovesical junction) flap valve:** filling normally compresses the intramural ureter against detrusor backing. Tunnel length to diameter is **5:1** normally versus 1.4:1 in refluxing units (Paquin). High infant male voiding pressure and BBD (bladder and bowel dysfunction) add a secondary component.

| Renal damage | Reflux-associated dysplasia | Acquired scar |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | Abnormal bud | Infected urine plus VUR |
| Typical patient | Boy, grade IV–V, antenatal hydronephrosis | Girl, recurrent febrile UTI (urinary tract infection), BBD |
| Preventable | No | Yes |

- **Intrarenal reflux** through compound polar papillae needs only 2 mmHg in neonates, so scars are polar. **Sterile reflux rarely scars** unless pressure is high.
- **Also know:** massive bilateral VUR gives the megacystis–megaureter association.

**International grading:** I ureter only; II up to calyces, undilated; III mild–moderate dilatation; IV moderate dilatation and tortuosity; V gross dilatation, papillary impressions lost.

### Natural history
- Resolution comes from **elongation of the intravesical ureter** with growth. Grade II resolves in 63–85%, grade III in about 50%; **grade IV–V and bilateral grade III rarely** (International Reflux Study).
- **Bilaterality and older age delay resolution:** persistence is highest in bilateral grade IV, and in bilateral grade III at 6–10 years. Infants with grade IV–V may still resolve.
- **Consequences of bilateral damage:** adult hypertension in 34–38% of scarred children; CKD (chronic kidney disease), with reflux nephropathy a leading paediatric transplant diagnosis; poor growth; pre-eclampsia (worse with bilateral scars). Correction cannot reverse dysplasia.

**Diagram to draw:** long oblique tunnel versus short lateral one.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - One embryological fault: lateral orifice, short tunnel, dysplasia.
> - Dysplasia (boys, congenital) versus scar (girls, infection).
> - Grade IV–V and bilateral grade III rarely resolve.
> - Scarred children: 34–38% adult hypertension.
