## Q1. Classify testicular tumors and describe management of seminoma
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)
(stage I seminoma also answered in 2018-Summer Paper 2, Q2)

### Introduction
- Over 95% of testicular tumours are GCTs (germ cell tumours). Seminoma is 52–56% of GCNIS (germ cell neoplasia in situ)-derived GCTs, peaks in the fourth–fifth decade.
- **Risk factors:** cryptorchidism, previous contralateral GCT (twelvefold), family history, GCNIS, White race, infertility.
- All postpubertal GCTs except spermatocytic tumour carry **isochromosome 12p**.

### WHO (World Health Organization) classification (2016, retained 2022)
| Group | Tumours |
|---|---|
| **1. GCNIS-derived GCTs** | GCNIS; **seminoma**; **NSGCT (non-seminomatous GCT)**: embryonal carcinoma, postpubertal yolk sac tumour, choriocarcinoma and other trophoblastic tumours, postpubertal teratoma, teratoma with somatic-type malignancy; mixed GCT |
| **2. Non-GCNIS-derived GCTs** | **Spermatocytic tumour** (formerly spermatocytic seminoma); prepubertal teratoma, dermoid and epidermoid cyst; prepubertal yolk sac tumour |
| **3. Sex cord–stromal tumours** (<5%) | Leydig cell, Sertoli cell (including large cell calcifying), granulosa cell (adult, juvenile), fibroma–thecoma, mixed |
| **4. Mixed germ cell–sex cord stromal** | Gonadoblastoma (DSD [difference of sex development]) |
| **5. Others** | Lymphoma (commonest testicular tumour in older men), rete testis and collecting duct tumours, adnexal and paratesticular tumours, metastases |

- Any NSGCT element, however small, makes the tumour an NSGCT and is treated as such.
- **Seminoma pathology:** sheets of clear cells with lymphocyte-rich fibrovascular septa. Immunohistochemistry: CD117 and PLAP (placental alkaline phosphatase) positive, CD30 negative. Syncytiotrophoblasts (hCG [human chorionic gonadotropin]) in about 15%. "Anaplastic seminoma" is no longer recognised.
- **Spermatocytic tumour** (<1%): older men, no GCNIS, no i(12p), benign unless sarcomatous.

### Evaluation and staging
- Scrotal ultrasound; AFP (alpha-fetoprotein), β-hCG and LDH (lactate dehydrogenase) before and after orchidectomy. **A raised AFP means NSGCT**, whatever the histology.
- **Radical inguinal orchidectomy** with early cord control at the internal ring; never trans-scrotal. Offer sperm banking and prosthesis.
- CT (computed tomography) of chest, abdomen and pelvis; TNMS (tumour, node, metastasis, serum markers) staging.
- About 80% of seminomas are clinical stage (CS) I; 15–20% CS II (nodes IIA ≤2 cm, IIB 2–5 cm, IIC >5 cm).

### Management of seminoma by stage
**CS I** (cure by orchidectomy alone 80–85%; cancer-specific survival about 100% with any option)

| | Surveillance (**preferred**) | Carboplatin | Radiotherapy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Method | Examination, markers, CT for at least 5 years | 1 cycle **AUC (area under the curve) 7**, dose = 7 × (GFR [glomerular filtration rate] + 25) mg | **20 Gy** para-aortic strip |
| Relapse | 14–20% | About 5% (1 cycle) | 3–5% |
| Drawback | Late relapses (10–20% after 4 years) | Underdosing; late effects unknown | Second cancers, GI (gastrointestinal) toxicity |

- **Relapse risk factors:** tumour **>4 cm** and **rete testis invasion** (relapse 12%, 16%, 32% with 0, 1, 2 factors). LVI (lymphovascular invasion) is not a factor in seminoma.
- **Trials:** MRC TE19/EORTC (Medical Research Council/European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer) 30982: one cycle of carboplatin equals radiotherapy (5-year relapse-free 94.7% vs 96%). MRC TE18: 20 Gy equals 30 Gy.
- Surveillance relapses are 84–100% retroperitoneal and almost all salvaged.

**CS IIA–B**
- **Non-bulky (≤3 cm):** dog-leg radiotherapy with nodal boost (25–30 Gy in the chapter; EAU [European Association of Urology] uses 30 Gy for IIA and 36 Gy for IIB), or chemotherapy, or **primary RPLND (retroperitoneal lymph node dissection)** (SEMS trial).
- **Bulky (>3 cm) or multiple nodes:** BEP (bleomycin, etoposide, cisplatin) ×3 or EP (etoposide, cisplatin) ×4.

**CS IIC–III: chemotherapy by IGCCCG (International Germ Cell Cancer Collaborative Group)**
| Risk | Seminoma definition | 5-year survival | Regimen |
|---|---|---|---|
| Good (90%) | Any primary, **no NPVM (non-pulmonary visceral metastases)**, normal AFP, any hCG/LDH | 86% | BEP ×3 or EP ×4 |
| Intermediate (10%) | NPVM present | 72% | BEP ×4 |
| Poor | **None for seminoma** | – | – |
- Carboplatin alone is inferior in advanced disease.

### Post-chemotherapy residual mass
```
Residual mass after chemotherapy
 ├─ <3 cm ─────────────► observe (necrosis about 90%)
 └─ >3 cm ─► FDG-PET (fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography) at ≥6 weeks
               ├─ negative ► observe
               └─ positive ► resection (full template PC-RPLND) or salvage
```
- Surgery is difficult because of desmoplasia; complete resection only 58–74%.

### Relapse and follow-up
- After surveillance or radiotherapy: radiotherapy for small retroperitoneal relapse, otherwise first-line BEP. After chemotherapy: VeIP (vinblastine, ifosfamide, cisplatin) or high-dose chemotherapy.
- Surveillance schedule: review every 2–4 months (years 1–3), 6-monthly (years 4–7), then yearly. CT detects >85% of relapses.

### Recent advances
- Serum **miR-371a-3p** microRNA: sensitive marker of residual disease and relapse.
- MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) for surveillance to cut radiation; primary RPLND for low-volume stage II.

**Diagram to draw:** WHO classification tree (GCNIS-derived vs non-GCNIS-derived vs sex cord–stromal) and the residual-mass flowchart.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - WHO 2016: GCNIS-derived vs non-GCNIS-derived; spermatocytic tumour is separate and benign.
> - CS I risk factors: >4 cm and rete testis invasion; surveillance preferred.
> - Carboplatin AUC 7 ×1 equals radiotherapy (TE19); 20 Gy equals 30 Gy (TE18).
> - No poor-risk seminoma; residual >3 cm → PET at ≥6 weeks.

## Q2. Define algorithm for evaluation of antenatally diagnosed unilateral HN in male fetus. What are the current rate of prenatal fetal anomalies
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)
(antenatal LUTO management also answered in 2021-Summer Paper 3, Q3b)

*Assumed meaning: HN = hydronephrosis, now called UTD (urinary tract dilation).*

### Introduction and rates of prenatal anomalies
- Major congenital anomalies occur in about **2–3% of births**. Urinary tract anomalies are among the commonest detected prenatally (commonly quoted as 20–30% of all prenatal anomalies).
- **UTD is the second commonest prenatal anomaly after cardiac defects, in 1–3% of pregnancies.**
- Most is mild: mild 57–88%, moderate 10–30%, severe 1.5–13% of UTD.
- Screening US (ultrasound) at 18–20 weeks detects dilation well (sensitivity about 90%), but predicts diagnosis poorly. Fetal urine production begins at about 14 weeks; amniotic fluid is mainly fetal urine after 16 weeks.

### Causes (SFU [Society for Fetal Urology] consensus)
| Cause | Incidence |
|---|---|
| Transient/physiological dilation | 50–70% |
| UPJ (ureteropelvic junction) obstruction pattern | 10–30% |
| VUR (vesicoureteral reflux) | 10–40% |
| UVJ (ureterovesical junction) obstruction/megaureter | 5–15% |
| MCDK (multicystic dysplastic kidney) | 2–5% |
| Duplex system, ureterocele, ectopic ureter | 5–7% |
| LUTO (lower urinary tract obstruction: PUV [posterior urethral valves], prune-belly) | 0.1–0.5% |
- Chance of postnatal pathology rises with grade: about 12% (mild) to 88% (severe). UPJ obstruction and PUV track grade; **VUR does not**.

### Grading systems
**APD (anteroposterior renal pelvic diameter):** transverse image at the hilum.
| Grade | Second trimester | Third trimester |
|---|---|---|
| Mild | 4 to <7 mm | 4 to <9 mm |
| Moderate | 7–10 mm | 9–15 mm |
| Severe | >10 mm | >15 mm |

**SFU (postnatal only):** 1 pelvis barely split; 2 pelvis and major calyces; 3 minor calyces uniformly dilated; 4 plus parenchymal thinning.

**UTD consensus 2014:** six parameters: APD, central/peripheral calyces, parenchymal thickness, parenchymal appearance, ureter, bladder (plus amniotic fluid prenatally).
| Prenatal | A1 low risk | A2–3 increased risk (any one) |
|---|---|---|
| APD 16–27 weeks | 4 to <7 mm | ≥7 mm |
| APD ≥28 weeks | 7 to <10 mm | ≥10 mm |
| Others | Central calyces only; all normal | Peripheral calyces, abnormal parenchyma, ureter or bladder, unexplained oligohydramnios |

### Prenatal algorithm (unilateral UTD, male fetus)
```
Anomaly scan: unilateral UTD
 → Detailed scan: contralateral kidney, ureter, bladder wall and emptying,
   posterior urethra (keyhole), amniotic fluid, other anomalies
 ├─ Bilateral / thick bladder / keyhole / oligohydramnios ─► LUTO pathway
 │    (fetal medicine; karyotype; Ruano staging; possible shunt)
 ├─ A1 ─► repeat US after 32 weeks
 └─ A2–3 ─► US every 4 weeks to delivery
 → Normal delivery at local centre; no fetal intervention for unilateral UTD
 → Postnatal US after 48 hours (earlier if solitary kidney or suspected LUTO)
```
- Repeat scans because the lesion may progress and a second anomaly is more likely. **Fetal MRI** only for complex anatomy.
- Counsel the parents: most cases resolve; prognosis with a normal opposite kidney is excellent.

### Postnatal algorithm
- **First US after 48 hours**, because neonatal dehydration under-reads dilation. Postnatal normal: APD <10 mm, no calyceal or ureteral dilation.

| | P1 (SFU 1–2) | P2 (SFU 3) | P3 (SFU 4) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Findings | APD 10–15 mm, central calyces | APD ≥15 mm, peripheral calyces or dilated ureter | Abnormal parenchyma or bladder |
| Repeat US | 3–6 months | Individualised | 1 month |
| Antibiotic prophylaxis | No | Discretion; **yes if ureter >7 mm** | Yes |
| VCUG (voiding cystourethrogram) | No | Discretion | Yes |
| MAG3 (mercaptoacetyltriglycine) renogram | No | Discretion | If persistent |

- **Male-specific points:** VCUG confirms a normal posterior urethra. **Circumcision** protects (NNT [number needed to treat] 10). UTI (urinary tract infection) risk: female sex, uncircumcised boys, high grade, dilated ureter.
- Prophylaxis: amoxicillin or a first-generation cephalosporin from birth; nitrofurantoin and trimethoprim only after 8 weeks.
- MAG3: DRF (differential renal function) <40%, T½ >20 minutes, or delayed retention suggest obstruction. MRU (magnetic resonance urography) for anatomy; DMSA (dimercaptosuccinic acid) for MCDK or duplex function.
- **Surgery indications (UPJ):** rising APD, DRF <40% with poor drainage, falling DRF, symptoms (UTI, stone, pain). Megaureter surgery: DRF <40%, febrile UTI; defer reimplant beyond 1 year.
- Outcome: SFU 1 resolves in nearly all by 2.5 years; SFU 2 about 95% by 4 years.

### Bilateral dilation and PUV: how they differ
- Bilateral hydroureteronephrosis, thick bladder, keyhole sign, oligohydramnios = LUTO until proved otherwise.
- At birth: 5 Fr feeding tube, prophylaxis, creatinine, early VCUG; prenatal intervention improves survival but not renal function (PLUTO).

**Diagram to draw:** UTD A1/A2–3 prenatal and P1–P3 postnatal flowchart.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - UTD: 1–3% of pregnancies; second commonest anomaly after cardiac.
> - Transient dilation 50–70%; UPJ obstruction commonest surgical cause.
> - First postnatal US after 48 hours; A2–3 scan every 4 weeks.
> - P1 no prophylaxis or VCUG; P3 prophylaxis, VCUG, MAG3.

## Q3b. Role of UDS in SUI
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(SUI evaluation also answered in 2019-Summer Paper 3, Q2)

### Introduction
- UDS (urodynamic studies) assess storage and voiding. In SUI (stress urinary incontinence) the key question is: **will the result change the operation?** Pure, demonstrable SUI gains little.

### What UDS shows in SUI
| Parameter | Method and meaning |
|---|---|
| USI (urodynamic stress incontinence) | Leak with raised abdominal pressure, no detrusor contraction |
| ALPP (abdominal leak point pressure) | Standing, from 150 mL, Valsalva or cough; **<60 cmH2O = ISD (intrinsic sphincter deficiency)**, 60–90 equivocal, ≥90 little ISD (McGuire 1993) |
| MUCP (maximum urethral closure pressure) | Urethral pressure profile; ≤20 cmH2O suggests ISD; does not diagnose or grade SUI |
| Filling cystometry | DO (detrusor overactivity), compliance, capacity; cough-induced DO mimics SUI |
| Pressure–flow study | Voiding dysfunction, poor contractility (retention risk after sling) |
| Prolapse reduction | Occult SUI |
- If no leak with the catheter, repeat with it removed.

### Evidence
- **ValUE trial (2012), 630 women** with uncomplicated SUI: office evaluation alone was **non-inferior** to office evaluation plus UDS (success 77.2% vs 76.9%).
- **Dutch VUE study:** immediate midurethral sling was non-inferior to UDS-tailored treatment.
- **SISTEr:** UDS did not predict outcome.

### Guidelines
- **AUA/SUFU (American Urological Association/Society of Urodynamics, Female Pelvic Medicine and Urogenital Reconstruction) 2023:** UDS may be omitted in the index patient with demonstrable SUI.
- **UDS advised in non-index patients:** previous anti-incontinence or prolapse surgery; urgency-predominant mixed incontinence; raised PVR (post-void residual) or voiding dysfunction; SUI not demonstrated; mismatch between symptoms and findings; neurogenic bladder.
- **EAU (European Association of Urology):** no routine UDS for uncomplicated SUI; do not use leak point or urethral pressure to grade severity.

### Men
- Before AUS (artificial urinary sphincter) or sling for post-prostatectomy incontinence: confirms USI, finds DO, poor compliance or weak contractility. MASTER trial recruited men with USI.

**Diagram to draw:** cystometrogram trace with abdominal pressure spike and leak (ALPP).

> **Key points to remember:**
> - ValUE: UDS not needed in uncomplicated SUI.
> - ALPP <60 cmH2O = ISD; MUCP ≤20 cmH2O.
> - Use UDS when it changes treatment: mixed, failed surgery, voiding dysfunction.

## Q3c. Retrocecal ureter
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in 2017-Winter Paper 3, Q3d)

*Assumed meaning: retrocaval (circumcaval) ureter; "retrocecal" is taken as a typo.*

### Definition
- The **right ureter** passes **behind the IVC (inferior vena cava)**, then crosses in front of it from medial to lateral.
- Better called **"preureteral vena cava"**: the anomaly is venous; the ureter is trapped.

### Embryology
- Fetal veins form a collar around each ureter: supracardinal veins dorsally, subcardinal veins ventrally.
- Normally the right **supracardinal** vein (dorsal to the ureter) forms the infrarenal IVC, so the ureter lies in front.
- If the lumbar part of the ventral **subcardinal** vein persists as the IVC, the ureter is trapped behind it.
- Variant: double right IVC (ureter between limbs).

### Incidence and presentation
- About **1 in 1500** autopsies; **male 3:1**.
- Presents in the **third–fourth decade** with right flank pain, UTI (urinary tract infection), stones or incidental hydronephrosis. Associations: horseshoe kidney, left renal anomalies.

### Classification (Bateson and Atkinson)
| | **Type I (low loop), commoner** | **Type II (high loop)** |
|---|---|---|
| Crossing | Lower (L3–L4) | Higher, near the renal pelvis |
| Shape | **"Fishhook"/reverse-J** | Smooth horizontal sickle curve |
| Obstruction | Moderate–severe, at the iliopsoas edge | Mild or none |

### Investigations
- **CT urography (computed tomography urography): investigation of choice**; MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) alternative.
- IVU (intravenous urography): fishhook; ureter not seen beyond the J hook. Retrograde pyelography: **S-curve** at L3–L4.
- **Diuretic renogram:** obstruction and split function.
- Pitfall: mistaken for **PUJ (pelviureteric junction) obstruction**; pyeloplasty fails.

### Management
- **Non-obstructed (often type II): observe.**
- **Obstructed:** **dismembered ureteroureterostomy** or ureteropyelostomy. Divide the ureter, transpose it in front of the IVC, spatulated anastomosis over a **double-J stent**. Excise or bypass the **aperistaltic retrocaval segment**.
- **Laparoscopic or robotic** repair now preferred; nephrectomy if non-functioning.
- **Also know:** retroiliac ureter; mimics: gonadal or lumbar vein, iliopsoas tendon.

**Diagram to draw:** right ureter hooking behind the IVC with fishhook pelvis.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Venous anomaly (persistent subcardinal vein); right side; 1 in 1500; male 3:1.
> - Type I fishhook obstructs; type II sickle often does not.
> - CT urography; anterior transposition with ureteroureterostomy.

## Q3d. Spermatogenesis
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in 2017-Winter Paper 1, Q2)

### Definition
- Production of haploid spermatozoa from diploid spermatogonia in the seminiferous tubules: about **64 days** (42–76); about 123 million sperm a day.

### Hormonal control: HPG (hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal) axis
- Pulsatile **GnRH (gonadotropin-releasing hormone)** → **LH (luteinising hormone)** → Leydig cells → testosterone. **FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone)** → Sertoli cells.
- Feedback: testosterone on LH; **inhibin B** (Sertoli) on FSH.
- **Intratesticular testosterone** is about 50 times serum, held by ABP (androgen-binding protein). Exogenous testosterone suppresses LH and so stops spermatogenesis.

### Sertoli cells and blood–testis barrier
- Nurse cells: nourish germ cells; secrete ABP, inhibin, fluid; phagocytose residual bodies.
- **Sertoli–Sertoli tight junctions** form a **basal** compartment (spermatogonia) and an **adluminal** compartment (spermatocytes, spermatids). This protects immunologically "foreign" post-meiotic cells.

### Phases
| Phase | Events |
|---|---|
| 1. Proliferation | **Ad (dark type A)** stem cells self-renew (GDNF [glial cell line–derived neurotrophic factor] niche); **Ap (pale type A)** divide every 16 days into type B → primary spermatocytes. Daughters remain joined by cytoplasmic bridges |
| 2. Meiosis | Primary spermatocytes cross into the adluminal compartment. Meiosis I (reduction division; synapsis and crossing-over) → secondary spermatocytes → meiosis II → 4 haploid round spermatids |
| 3. Spermiogenesis | No division: acrosome from Golgi, flagellum from centriole, nucleus compacts to 10%, mitochondria form the midpiece, cytoplasm shed |
| 4. Spermiation | Sertoli cell releases sperm into the lumen |
- **Cycle, stage, wave:** six stages in humans; cycles arranged as a helical wave, so production is continuous.

### Post-testicular maturation
- Epididymal transit 2–12 days: sperm gain **progressive motility** and fertilising ability; stored in the cauda. **Capacitation** and acrosome reaction occur in the female tract.

### Clinical relevance
- A semen sample reflects events about 3 months earlier; reassess 3 months after treatment.
- Disruptors: heat (testis normally 2–4 °C below core; varicocele, cryptorchidism), exogenous testosterone, chemotherapy, radiation.
- Defective meiotic recombination causes maturation arrest.

**Diagram to draw:** seminiferous tubule cross-section with basal and adluminal compartments.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - About 64 days; Ap divide every 16 days; six stages.
> - Intratesticular testosterone 50 × serum; FSH → Sertoli, LH → Leydig.
> - Tight junctions between Sertoli cells form the blood–testis barrier.

## Q3e. Enuresis
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition (ICCS [International Children's Continence Society])
- Intermittent urinary incontinence **during sleep** in a child **aged 5 years or more**.
- **Primary:** never dry. **Secondary:** wetting after at least **6 months** dry (look for stressors, diabetes, UTI [urinary tract infection], neurological disease).
- **MNE (monosymptomatic):** no daytime LUTS (lower urinary tract symptoms). **NMNE (non-monosymptomatic):** with daytime LUTS.

### Epidemiology
- About **5–10% at 7 years**; spontaneous resolution about 15% a year; about 2% of adults; familial.

### Pathophysiology: three-way mismatch
1. **Nocturnal polyuria** (reduced night-time ADH [antidiuretic hormone] rise).
2. **Reduced nocturnal bladder capacity** or DO (detrusor overactivity).
3. **Failure to arouse** from sleep.
- Comorbidities: constipation, sleep-disordered breathing (atrial natriuretic peptide raises urine output), ADHD (attention deficit hyperactivity disorder).

### Evaluation
- History: day symptoms, bowels, snoring, fluids, family history.
- **Bladder diary**: daytime voided volumes against EBC (expected bladder capacity) = (age + 2) × 30 mL in the chapter (ICCS uses [age + 1] × 30); night urine output by weighing nappies.
- Examination: spine, genitalia, abdomen for faecal loading; urinalysis.
- Uroflowmetry, PVR (post-void residual) and ultrasound only for NMNE or before adding anticholinergics. No invasive tests in MNE.

### Management
- **Treat daytime LUTS and constipation first** in NMNE.
- Education removes blame; standard urotherapy. Waking, fluid restriction alone and punishment do not work.

| | **Alarm** | **Desmopressin** |
|---|---|---|
| Target | Arousal (conditioning) | Night urine output |
| Use | Nightly for months; parent wakes child; continue to 14 dry nights | 0.2–0.4 mg tablet or 120–240 µg melt, 1 hour before bed |
| Response | 50–70%; durable | Rule of thirds; immediate but relapses |
| Caution | Needs motivated family | Restrict evening fluids (hyponatraemia) |
- Overlearning (extra evening fluid once dry) reduces alarm relapse.
- **Failure:** switch or combine. **Second line:** add an anticholinergic (oxybutynin) after checking PVR and bowels. **Resistant:** imipramine (cardiotoxic; ECG [electrocardiogram]), off-label β3-agonists; treat sleep apnoea (adenotonsillectomy).

**Diagram to draw:** triad diagram: nocturnal polyuria, small bladder capacity, arousal failure.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - ≥5 years; secondary = after 6 months dry.
> - Alarm (durable) and desmopressin (rule of thirds) are first line.
> - Treat daytime symptoms and constipation first.

## Q3f. Female sexual dysfunction
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Definition and prevalence
- FSD (female sexual dysfunction): persistent desire, arousal, orgasm or pain problems causing **personal distress**.
- About **40%** of women in their lifetime; **HSDD (hypoactive sexual desire disorder) commonest**. Almost half of women with UI (urinary incontinence) have FSD.

### Physiology and response models
- Arousal: pudendal afferents, pelvic parasympathetic efferents → **NO (nitric oxide)–cGMP (cyclic guanosine monophosphate)** → engorgement and lubrication. Oestrogen maintains tissues; testosterone drives desire.
- **Masters and Johnson** (linear): excitement, plateau, orgasm, resolution; Kaplan added desire. **Basson** (circular): desire is often responsive.

### Classification
| DSM-5 (Diagnostic and Statistical Manual, 2013) | ICSM (International Consultation on Sexual Medicine)/ISSWSH (International Society for the Study of Women's Sexual Health) |
|---|---|
| Female sexual interest/arousal disorder (≥3 of 6 criteria) | HSDD and arousal disorder kept separate (cognitive vs genital arousal) |
| Female orgasmic disorder | Orgasm disorders |
| Genito-pelvic pain/penetration disorder | Genital-pelvic pain dysfunction |
| – | PGAD (persistent genital arousal disorder) |
- ≥6 months with distress; lifelong or acquired; generalised or situational.

### Causes
- Hormonal (menopause, GSM [genitourinary syndrome of menopause]), drugs (SSRIs [selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors], antihypertensives), diabetes, depression, relationship factors.
- Urological: incontinence, prolapse, sling dyspareunia, cystectomy nerve or clitoral injury.

### Evaluation
- Clinician must ask. **PLISSIT** (permission, limited information, specific suggestions, intensive therapy); BSSC-W (Brief Sexual Symptom Checklist for Women) screen; **FSFI (Female Sexual Function Index, 19 items, six domains)**.
- Examination: atrophy, lichen sclerosus, cotton-swab test, pelvic floor tone. Laboratory tests rarely help.

### Management
- Treat causes; education; sex therapy (sensate focus, mindfulness).
- **HSDD (premenopausal, FDA [US Food and Drug Administration] approved):** **flibanserin** 100 mg at bedtime (5-HT1A agonist/5-HT2A antagonist; alcohol caution); **bremelanotide** 1.75 mg subcutaneously on demand (melanocortin agonist). Postmenopausal: transdermal testosterone (off-label).
- **Arousal:** testosterone; PDE5 (phosphodiesterase type 5) inhibitors only with neurovascular disease.
- **Orgasm:** education, directed masturbation.
- **Pain/vestibulodynia:** pelvic floor physiotherapy, dilators, biofeedback, topical lidocaine, vaginal oestrogen; vestibulectomy if refractory.
- **Also know:** ospemifene, intravaginal prasterone (menopausal dyspareunia).

**Diagram to draw:** Basson circular sexual response model.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - About 40% prevalence; HSDD commonest; distress required.
> - DSM-5 merges desire and arousal, dyspareunia and vaginismus.
> - Flibanserin and bremelanotide: only FDA-approved HSDD drugs (premenopausal).

#### Q3d. Spermatogenesis (SAQ)

- About 64 days; Ap divide every 16 days; six stages.
- Intratesticular testosterone 50 × serum; FSH → Sertoli, LH → Leydig.
- Tight junctions between Sertoli cells form the blood–testis barrier.

#### Q3e. Enuresis (SAQ)

- ≥5 years; secondary = after 6 months dry.
- Alarm (durable) and desmopressin (rule of thirds) are first line.
- Treat daytime symptoms and constipation first.

#### Q3f. Female sexual dysfunction (SAQ)

- About 40% prevalence; HSDD commonest; distress required.
- DSM-5 merges desire and arousal, dyspareunia and vaginismus.
- Flibanserin and bremelanotide: only FDA-approved HSDD drugs (premenopausal).
