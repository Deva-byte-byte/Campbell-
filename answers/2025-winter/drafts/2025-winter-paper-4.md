## Q1. Role of neoadjuvant therapy and Lymphadenectomy for muscle invasive bladder cancer and upper tract urothelial cell cancer?
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

### Introduction
- After RC (radical cystectomy) alone, nearly half of MIBC (muscle-invasive bladder cancer) patients develop metastases from occult micrometastases.
- Cure improves with **systemic therapy before surgery** and a **meticulous lymph node dissection** (accurate staging, possible therapeutic value).
- UTUC (upper tract urothelial carcinoma) behaves alike, but RNU (radical nephroureterectomy) removes a kidney, changing chemotherapy timing.

### A. Neoadjuvant therapy in MIBC
**Rationale**
- Treats micrometastases early; better tolerated before surgery, so more complete it.
- Downstages; **pT0 at cystectomy** gives about 90% 5-year recurrence-free survival.
- Drawback: delays surgery in non-responders.

**Selection:** cT2–T4a N0 M0, cisplatin-eligible. **Cisplatin-ineligible** if any one: GFR (glomerular filtration rate) <60 mL/min, ECOG (Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group) performance status ≥2, hearing loss grade ≥2, neuropathy grade ≥2, NYHA (New York Heart Association) class III–IV failure.

| Trial | Regimen | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **SWOG 8710** (Southwest Oncology Group) | MVAC (methotrexate, vinblastine, doxorubicin, cisplatin) ×3 then RC vs RC | pT0 38% vs 15%; 5-year OS (overall survival) 57% vs 43%, **P = 0.06** |
| **BA06 30894** (EORTC/MRC, European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer/Medical Research Council) | CMV (cisplatin, methotrexate, vinblastine) ×3 | 16% relative reduction in mortality; 10-year OS 36% vs 30% |
| ABC (Advanced Bladder Cancer) meta-analysis | Cisplatin combinations | OS HR (hazard ratio) 0.86; **5% absolute benefit** at 5 years |
| **VESPER** | ddMVAC (dose-dense MVAC) ×6 vs GC (gemcitabine–cisplatin) ×4 | Organ-confined response 77% vs 63%; 3-year PFS (progression-free survival) 66% vs 56%; more toxicity |
| **NIAGARA** | Durvalumab + GC, then adjuvant durvalumab, vs GC | Better event-free survival (HR 0.68) and OS (HR 0.75) |

- Single-agent cisplatin, carboplatin and neoadjuvant radiotherapy give **no survival benefit**.
- **Guidelines (EAU, European Association of Urology; AUA, American Urological Association; NCCN, National Comprehensive Cancer Network):** cisplatin-based NAC (neoadjuvant chemotherapy) for all eligible patients; ddMVAC 3–6 cycles or GC 4 cycles. NIAGARA has since added perioperative durvalumab + GC (the chapter predates it and calls immunotherapy unproven).
- **Cisplatin-ineligible:** proceed to RC; neoadjuvant pembrolizumab (PURE-01) and atezolizumab (ABACUS) are investigational.
- **Adjuvant** (pT3–4 or N+): cisplatin chemotherapy if not given before; **nivolumab** (CheckMate 274: disease-free survival 20.8 vs 10.8 months).

### B. Lymphadenectomy in MIBC
- **Primary drainage:** obturator, internal and external iliac, presacral. **Secondary:** common iliac, para-aortic, paracaval.
- Node positivity: pT2 8–26%, pT3 33–50%, pT4 36–80%.

| Template | Extent |
|---|---|
| **Standard** | Bilateral external and internal iliac and obturator nodes; genitofemoral nerve laterally, node of Cloquet caudally, up to the common iliac bifurcation |
| **Extended** | Adds common iliac and presacral nodes, up to the aortic bifurcation |
| Super-extended | Up to the inferior mesenteric artery |

- **Evidence:** German phase III (limited vs extended): no survival benefit. **SWOG S1011** (most had NAC): median nodes 24 vs 39, **no difference** in disease-free survival (HR 1.10) or OS; more grade ≥3 events. So **standard bilateral PLND (pelvic lymph node dissection)** is the standard of care.
- **Node count:** fewer than 10 nodes doubled the risk of death (SWOG 8710 reanalysis). **Also know:** lymph node density, extranodal extension (prognostic).
- Unexpected nodes at RC: frozen section, proceed if resectable within the pelvis. Clinically node-positive: chemotherapy first, then RC in responders.

### C. Neoadjuvant and adjuvant therapy in UTUC
- **Why neoadjuvant:** CKD (chronic kidney disease) is common and GFR falls after RNU, losing cisplatin eligibility. Give platinum **while both kidneys work**.
- Biopsy under-stages, so select by high-risk features (high grade, invasion on imaging, hydronephrosis).
- **EA8141** (phase II): accelerated MVAC, or gemcitabine–carboplatin if CrCl (creatinine clearance) 30–49; complete response 14%, below ypT2N0 60%.
- **AUA/SUO (Society of Urologic Oncology):** neoadjuvant platinum is a standard option for high-risk UTUC, especially if postoperative CrCl will fall below 50.
- **Adjuvant: POUT** (phase III, pT2–T4 or N+ after RNU): gemcitabine–platinum vs surveillance; disease-free survival HR 0.45, 3-year 71% vs 46%. Weaker with carboplatin, favouring neoadjuvant cisplatin. Adjuvant nivolumab (CheckMate 274) also included UTUC.

### D. Lymphadenectomy in UTUC
- Node positivity 12–25%. Lymph drains **right to left and top to bottom**, so the template depends on side and level.

| Primary site | Right | Left |
|---|---|---|
| Renal pelvis, upper two-thirds ureter | Hilar, paracaval, retrocaval, **interaortocaval** | Hilar, para-aortic |
| Lower third ureter | Ipsilateral common, external, internal iliac, obturator, presacral | Same |

- Value: pN0 better than pNx; ≥8 nodes improved disease-specific survival above pT1. Indicated for high-grade or invasive disease; robotic yield equals open. Risk: chylous leak.

**Diagram to draw:** standard vs extended pelvic templates, and right vs left UTUC templates.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Cisplatin combinations only: ABC 5% absolute 5-year survival gain; SWOG 8710 P = 0.06.
> - VESPER: ddMVAC beat GC; NIAGARA: perioperative durvalumab improved event-free and overall survival.
> - SWOG S1011: extended PLND gives no survival benefit over standard.
> - UTUC: neoadjuvant before kidney loss; POUT disease-free survival HR 0.45; right-sided needs interaortocaval nodes.

## Q2. Describe Basic Energy Modalities in Urological surgery?
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)

### Introduction
- Every modality delivers energy that tissue absorbs and releases as **heat, mechanical stress or cavitation**. Frequency sets penetration; absorption sets the effect.
- Tissue effects of heat: about 60 °C coagulates protein; 100 °C vaporises cell water; higher temperatures carbonise.

### Classification
| Energy | Examples |
|---|---|
| Electrical | Monopolar, bipolar, vessel sealers, RFA (radiofrequency ablation), IRE (irreversible electroporation), EMDA (electromotive drug administration) |
| Electromagnetic | Lasers, MWA (microwave ablation) |
| Acoustic | Ultrasonic dissector, SWL (shock wave lithotripsy), HIFU (high-intensity focused ultrasound), histotripsy |
| Direct contact | Cryoablation, water vapour, waterjet (Aquablation) |

### 1. Electrosurgery
- **Physics:** alternating current at about **500 kHz** (radiofrequency). Mains current (50 Hz) depolarises nerve and muscle; at 500 kHz ions only oscillate and produce heat.
- **Ohm's law** (V = I × R): tissue is the resistance. **Current density** decides the effect: a small tip concentrates current and heats; a large pad disperses it.
- **Monopolar:** generator → active electrode → patient → dispersive pad → generator. Partial pad contact causes burns.
- **Bipolar:** current passes only between two jaws; less power, less spread, no pad.
- **Electrocautery** (heated wire) differs: no current enters the patient.

| Waveform | Character | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Cut | Continuous, low voltage; 50–80 W | Rapid boiling, clean incision, little char |
| Coagulation | Interrupted (about 6% duty cycle), high voltage; 30–50 W | Desiccation; spread up to 3 mm |
| Fulguration (spray) | High voltage arcs across air | Non-contact surface haemostasis |
| Blend | Cut with interruptions | Cutting plus haemostasis |

- **Vessel sealers:** bipolar current plus jaw compression fuses collagen and elastin; a feedback generator stops when impedance rises. Seal vessels up to 7 mm in one application, with little spread.
- **Endoscopic:** monopolar TURP (transurethral resection of prostate) needs non-conducting **1.5% glycine**, risking hyponatraemia (TUR syndrome); **bipolar** uses saline, with comparable efficacy and better safety.
- **Obturator reflex:** lateral-wall TURBT (transurethral resection of bladder tumour) stimulates the nerve → adduction → perforation. Prevent with paralysis, obturator block, partial filling, short bursts, bipolar.
- **Safety:** pad burns; insulation failure; **direct coupling** (touching metal); **capacitive coupling** (current induced through intact insulation); fire with oxygen; inadvertent activation. Use AEM (active electrode monitoring), lowest power, bipolar near pacemakers.

### 2. Ultrasonic energy
- **Ultrasonic dissector:** blade vibrates at about 55.5 kHz; friction and cavitation denature protein. No current through the patient; little lateral spread, but the blade stays hot.
- Intracorporeal ultrasonic lithotripter: vibrating probe, suction of fragments.

### 3. Lasers
- **Principle:** pumped gain medium in a mirrored cavity → stimulated emission → **monochromatic, coherent, collimated** beam.
- **Effect** depends on wavelength (chromophore: water, haemoglobin) and pulse duration.

| Laser | nm | Penetration (mm) | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| KTP/LBO (potassium-titanyl-phosphate/lithium triborate) | 532 | 0.8 | Prostate photovaporisation (haemoglobin) |
| Nd:YAG (neodymium:yttrium-aluminium-garnet) | 1064 | 10 | Deep coagulation |
| TFL (thulium fibre laser) | 1940 | 0.08 | Stones, enucleation |
| Tm:YAG (thulium:YAG) | 2000 | 0.2 | Vaporesection, enucleation |
| Ho:YAG (holmium:YAG) | 2120 | 0.31 | Stones, HoLEP (holmium laser enucleation of prostate), strictures |

- **Mechanisms:** photothermal (long pulses), photomechanical (short pulses, shock waves), photochemical (PDT, photodynamic therapy).
- **Penetration predicts behaviour:** deep (Nd:YAG) coagulates widely with delayed sloughing; shallow (thulium) vaporises precisely with char.
- Safety: eye protection for all; irrigant heating; fibre damage.

### 4. Ablative energies
| Modality | Mechanism | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| **RFA** | 375–900 kHz current; ionic agitation | Target 60 °C; char at 150 °C; heat sink |
| **MWA** | 915 MHz or 2.45 GHz; dielectric hysteresis | Hotter, larger zones; less heat sink |
| **Cryoablation** | Argon expansion (Joule–Thomson) | Below −40 °C; **two freeze–thaw cycles**; ice ball visible |
| **HIFU** | Focused ultrasound absorbed → necrosis | 1500–10,000 W/cm²; rice-grain lesions; transrectal prostate |
| **IRE** | High-voltage microsecond pulses → permanent membrane pores | Non-thermal; spares vessels and ducts; focal prostate |

- Cryo mechanism: extracellular ice → cell dehydration → intracellular ice → membrane rupture on thawing; ischaemia and apoptosis follow.
- IRE mechanism: electric pulses open pores in cell membranes that do not reseal, so cells die by apoptosis without heat; collagen scaffolds survive. It needs general anaesthesia with muscle paralysis and ECG (electrocardiogram)-synchronised pulses.
- **Also know:** histotripsy (cavitation liquefaction, investigational); water vapour (Rezum, 103 °C, convective, no char); Aquablation (non-thermal saline jet); chemohyperthermia (42 °C) and EMDA for mitomycin.

### 5. Lithotripsy energies
- **SWL:** electrohydraulic, electromagnetic or piezoelectric sources. Stones break by **spallation, shear and cavitation**. Slower rates (about 1 Hz) work better and injure less.
- **Intracorporeal:** pneumatic (ballistic), ultrasonic, combined, electrohydraulic, laser. **Also know:** burst wave lithotripsy, ultrasonic propulsion.

**Diagram to draw:** monopolar vs bipolar circuit, with cut and coagulation waveforms.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Electrosurgery at about 500 kHz; current density decides heating.
> - Cut continuous low voltage; coagulation interrupted high voltage.
> - Capacitive coupling and insulation failure cause unseen burns.
> - Cryo below −40 °C, two cycles; RFA 60 °C; MWA less heat sink.

## Q3a. Immuno modulation in Renal, cell carcinoma
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in 2024-Winter Paper 2, Q3e)

*Assumed meaning: immunomodulation (immunotherapy) in renal cell carcinoma.*

### Rationale
- RCC (renal cell carcinoma) is immunogenic: rare (<1%) spontaneous regression after nephrectomy, tumour-infiltrating T cells.
- **Checkpoints:** PD-1 (programmed death-1) on T cells binds PD-L1 on tumour and switches T cells off; CTLA-4 (cytotoxic T-lymphocyte antigen-4) damps priming. Blockade **releases the brake** on existing immunity.
- VEGF (vascular endothelial growth factor) suppresses immunity, so TKIs (tyrosine kinase inhibitors) synergise.

### Cytokines (non-specific stimulation)
| | IFN-α (interferon-alpha) | High-dose IL-2 (interleukin-2) |
|---|---|---|
| Response | 10–15% | 15–20% |
| Durable complete response | <2% | **7–9%** |
| Toxicity | Flu-like | **Capillary leak**; intensive care |
| Role | Obsolete | Selected fit patients |

- High-dose IL-2: 600,000–720,000 IU/kg intravenously every 8 hours, maximum 15 doses; over 60% of complete responders stay disease-free.

### Checkpoint inhibitors (clear cell)
| Trial | Regimen vs comparator | Result |
|---|---|---|
| CheckMate 025 | Nivolumab vs everolimus, second line | OS (overall survival) 25.0 vs 19.6 months |
| **CheckMate 214** | Nivolumab + ipilimumab vs sunitinib | Intermediate/poor risk: OS HR (hazard ratio) 0.63; complete response 9% |
| KEYNOTE-426 / CheckMate 9ER | Pembrolizumab + axitinib / nivolumab + cabozantinib | Better PFS (progression-free survival) and OS |
| **CLEAR** | Pembrolizumab + lenvatinib | PFS 23.9 vs 9.2 months; response 71% |

- **First line:** IO/TKI (immuno-oncology plus TKI) for all IMDC (International Metastatic RCC Database Consortium) risk groups; nivolumab + ipilimumab for intermediate/poor only.
- After IO, switch to a VEGF-targeted TKI; IO rechallenge is not recommended.

### Adjuvant
- **KEYNOTE-564:** pembrolizumab for 1 year after nephrectomy in high-risk clear cell RCC (including resected metastases): disease-free survival HR 0.63; later OS benefit.

### Toxicity: irAEs (immune-related adverse events)
- Colitis, hepatitis, pneumonitis, thyroiditis, hypophysitis. Grade 2: hold, prednisolone; grade 3–4: stop, high-dose steroids.

**Also know:** PD-L1 does not select; sarcomatoid responds well; vaccines and allogeneic transplant failed.

**Diagram to draw:** T cell–tumour synapse with PD-1/PD-L1 and CTLA-4/B7 blocked.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - High-dose IL-2: durable complete response 7–9%.
> - CheckMate 214: nivolumab + ipilimumab, OS HR 0.63.
> - KEYNOTE-564: adjuvant pembrolizumab.

## Q3b. New LASER for stone Treatment
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in 2024-Winter Paper 4, Q3c)

### Background
- **Ho:YAG (holmium:yttrium-aluminium-garnet), 2120 nm** (often quoted as 2100 nm) is the standard. It is water-absorbed and **photothermal**: long pulses heat the stone until it decomposes.
- Newer lasers aim at less retropulsion, finer dust and faster work.

### 1. High-power holmium with pulse modulation
- 100–120 W machines allow **dusting** (low energy, high frequency) and popcorning.
- **Moses effect:** the start of each pulse boils a **vapour channel**; vapour absorbs less light, so the rest reaches the stone.
- **Moses technology** splits each pulse in two: the first opens the channel, the second strikes the stone. Less retropulsion.

### 2. TFL (thulium fibre laser), 1940 nm
- Diode-pumped silica fibre laser near the **water absorption peak** (higher absorption than holmium); penetration 0.08 mm.
- **Low peak power, long pulses, very high frequency** → finer dust, minimal retropulsion.
- **Thin fibres** (about 150 µm): better irrigation and scope deflection. Compact, quiet.
- **RCTs (randomised controlled trials):** TFL beat **low-power (30 W) holmium** (stone-free rate, time); it **equalled 120 W pulse-modulated holmium** in stone-free rate.

### 3. Pulsed Tm:YAG (thulium:YAG), 2013 nm
- Solid-state pulsed version of the continuous-wave thulium laser; photothermal lithotripsy and enucleation.

### Comparison
| | Ho:YAG | TFL |
|---|---|---|
| Wavelength | 2120 nm | 1940 nm |
| Peak power | High | Low |
| Retropulsion | More | Less |
| Fragments | Small | Smaller |

### Principles of settings
- **Retropulsion rises with pulse energy**, not frequency; it falls with longer pulses.
- Keep the fibre close and perpendicular to the stone.

### Safety
- Heat follows total power: use low power, pauses, high flow and an **access sheath**; urothelial injury begins at about 43 °C. Eyewear for all.

**Also know:** suction sheaths; burst wave lithotripsy (investigational).

**Diagram to draw:** water absorption curve marking 1940 and 2120 nm.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - TFL 1940 nm: low peak power, minimal retropulsion.
> - Moses: vapour channel; two-part pulse.
> - TFL beats low-power, equals high-power holmium.

## Q3c. Combination Intravesical therapy
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

### Rationale
- Single agents fail: chemotherapy reduces recurrence only; BCG (bacille Calmette-Guérin) reduces progression but fails in about a third.
- Combinations add **different mechanisms**, overcome resistance, and help during **BCG shortage** and in **BCG-unresponsive** NMIBC (non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer).

### 1. Sequential chemotherapy (doublets)
| Regimen | Method | Results |
|---|---|---|
| **Gem/Doce** (gemcitabine–docetaxel) | Gemcitabine 1 g (60–90 min) → drain → docetaxel 37.5 mg (60–120 min); weekly ×6, then monthly for 2 years | High-grade recurrence-free survival about 65% (1 year), 52% (2 years); 84% avoid cystectomy |
| Gem/MMC (mitomycin C) | Gemcitabine 1 g → MMC 40 mg | Durable recurrence-free about 38% |

- **Why it works:** gemcitabine (antimetabolite) and docetaxel (microtubule stabiliser) act on different cell-cycle phases.
- Oral sodium bicarbonate and antiemetics aid tolerance. Phase III BRIDGE (versus BCG in BCG-naive) is testing it.

### 2. Chemotherapy with BCG
- Sequential MMC–BCG: **no benefit** over BCG alone.
- **Exception: BCG alternating with EMDA (electromotive drug administration)-MMC** in T1: lower recurrence and progression than BCG alone. A later trial was non-significant.

### 3. BCG with immunomodulators
- **BCG + interferon-α:** no gain over BCG; allows reduced BCG dose.
- **N-803 (nogapendekin alfa)** = IL-15 (interleukin-15) superagonist activating CD8 T and NK (natural killer) cells. With BCG in BCG-unresponsive CIS (carcinoma in situ): complete response 71%, 45% at 12 months. FDA (Food and Drug Administration)-approved.

### 4. BCG with checkpoint inhibitors (BCG-naive high risk)
- **CREST** (subcutaneous sasanlimab + BCG) and **POTOMAC** (durvalumab + BCG): improved event-free/disease-free survival over BCG alone.
- **ALBAN** (atezolizumab + BCG): negative. KEYNOTE-676 (pembrolizumab) ongoing.

### 5. Device-assisted and novel
- **Chemohyperthermia** (MMC at 42 °C: RITE, radiofrequency-induced thermochemotherapeutic effect; HIVEC, hyperthermic intravesical chemotherapy) increases uptake.
- **TAR-200:** gemcitabine-releasing intravesical device; high complete response in BCG-unresponsive CIS (SunRISe-1).
- Cretostimogene + pembrolizumab: about two-thirds complete response at 12 months.

### Toxicity
- Additive cystitis; immune-related events with checkpoint inhibitors.

**Diagram to draw:** Gem/Doce sequence timeline (instil, dwell, drain, instil).

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Gem/Doce: gemcitabine 1 g then docetaxel 37.5 mg.
> - Only EMDA-MMC improves BCG sequentially.
> - N-803 + BCG: CIS complete response 71%.
> - BCG + checkpoint: CREST, POTOMAC positive; ALBAN negative.

## Q3d. Penile Inversion Vaginoplasty Technique
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in 2022-Summer Paper 2, Q3b)

### Introduction
- Commonest gender-affirming genital operation for transgender women: a sensate, functional vulva and vagina.
- **Principle:** homologous penile and scrotal tissues on their own blood supply.

### Preparation
- **WPATH (World Professional Association for Transgender Health) SOC8 (Standards of Care 8):** one assessment, **6 months of hormones**.
- **Permanent hair removal** of scrotal graft skin; BMI (body mass index) usually ≤35 kg/m²; stop smoking; fertility preservation.
- **Choose depth:** full-depth vs **vulvoplasty (zero-depth)**: vulva without a canal, chosen for high surgical risk, prior prostatectomy, or no wish for penetrative intercourse.

### Tissue transfer
| Male structure | Becomes |
|---|---|
| Glans on dorsal neurovascular bundle | **Clitoris** |
| Preputial collar | Clitoral hood, labia minora |
| Spatulated urethra | Vestibule |
| Inverted penile skin | **Neovaginal lining** |
| Scrotal full-thickness graft | Proximal lining |
| Lateral scrotal skin | Labia majora |

### Steps
```
Lithotomy; tranexamic acid 1 g; catheter
→ harvest, defat, tubularise scrotal graft
→ bilateral orchidectomy
→ deglove penis, keep dartos with skin (blood supply)
→ dorsal neurovascular bundle + glans raised;
   excise ALL erectile tissue
→ clitoroplasty; shorten, spatulate urethra
→ canal dissection between prostate and rectum;
   keep Denonvilliers fascia intact; rectal checks
→ invert skin tube + graft; labiaplasty
→ pack canal
```
- **Why excise all erectile tissue:** residual tissue engorges and narrows the canal.
- **Peritoneal flaps (robotic)** add depth; **intestinal vaginoplasty** when skin is inadequate or for revision.

### Postoperative care
- Pack and catheter out about day 5; **lifelong dilation** (several times daily at first, tapering); pelvic floor physiotherapy; continue oestrogen; most achieve orgasm.

### Complications
- **Wound dehiscence** (commonest, 12–33%).
- **Rectovaginal fistula** (0.8% primary, 6.3% revision); urethrovaginal fistula (0.8–3.9%).
- Urethral stricture, meatal malposition (spraying); **vaginal stenosis** (poor dilation); prolapse 4–10%.

**Diagram to draw:** sagittal view of neovagina between prostate and rectum.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Glans → clitoris; penile skin → canal; scrotal skin → graft.
> - Excise all erectile tissue; preserve Denonvilliers fascia.
> - Lifelong dilation; rectovaginal fistula is worst.

## Q3f. Management of Defecation Disorders resulting into LUTS
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

*Assumed meaning: bladder and bowel dysfunction, where constipation or defecation disorders cause LUTS (lower urinary tract symptoms).*

### Why the bowel causes LUTS
- **Shared space:** a loaded rectum compresses the bladder, reducing capacity and obstructing outflow.
- **Shared nerves:** bladder and bowel afferents converge on the same spinal segments; cross-organ sensitisation causes urgency and detrusor overactivity.
- **Shared muscle:** a pelvic floor that contracts during defecation (**dyssynergic defecation**) also contracts during voiding (**dysfunctional voiding**) → high PVR (post-void residual), UTI (urinary tract infection), reflux.
- Result: urgency, daytime incontinence, enuresis, retention, recurrent UTI.

### Evaluation
- Stool frequency, pain, **Bristol chart**; 3-day bladder and bowel diary.
- **Rome IV (child):** at least 2, weekly for 1 month: ≤2 defecations/week; faecal incontinence weekly; retentive posturing; painful or hard stools; large rectal mass; large-diameter stools.
- **Rome IV (adult), for 3 months:** ≥2 of straining, hard stools, incomplete evacuation, blockage, manual manoeuvres (each in >25% of defecations), <3 stools/week.
- Examine abdomen, spine and perineum; exclude organic causes (Hirschsprung disease, hypothyroidism, neurogenic).
- **Also know:** rectal diameter ≥30 mm on ultrasound; no routine X-ray; manometry if refractory.

### Management
**Rule: treat the bowel first (EAU, European Association of Urology/ESPU, European Society for Paediatric Urology).**
1. **Disimpaction:** oral PEG (polyethylene glycol) 3350, 1–1.5 g/kg/day for 3–6 days; enemas if needed.
2. **Maintenance:** PEG 0.4 g/kg/day (range 0.2–0.8), at least 2 months; lactulose alternative; stimulants (senna) for expulsion.
3. **Urotherapy:** toileting after meals (gastrocolic reflex); footstool posture; normal fluid and fibre.
4. **Biofeedback, pelvic floor physiotherapy** for dyssynergia.
5. **Refractory:** **transanal irrigation** (water via rectal cone; a quarter to a third stop); **ACE (antegrade continence enema, Malone)**; botulinum toxin to the sphincter; sacral or tibial neuromodulation.
- **Adults:** laxatives, prucalopride or linaclotide, biofeedback; rectocele repair; irrigation in neurogenic bowel.
- **Caution:** anticholinergics worsen constipation; LUTS return.

**Diagram to draw:** pelvic cross-section showing loaded rectum compressing bladder.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Treat the bowel first.
> - Rome IV: ≥2 criteria for 1 month (child).
> - PEG disimpaction 1–1.5 g/kg/day for 3–6 days.
