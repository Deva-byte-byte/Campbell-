## Q1. Describe etiology, clinical presentation and recent advances in management of Urethral Structure Disease
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)
(also answered in 2016-Summer Paper 2, Q2)

*"Structure" is taken to mean urethral stricture disease.*

### Definition
- A fixed narrowing of the **anterior** urethra (inside the corpus spongiosum) that will not accept instruments without tearing the mucosa. It is an epithelial scar, often with **spongiofibrosis**.
- Terminology (WHO (World Health Organization) 2004, ICUD (International Consultation on Urological Diseases) 2010): after pelvic fracture, **PFUI (pelvic fracture urethral injury)** or distraction defect; after prostate surgery or radiation, **stenosis or contracture**.
- Halving calibre (30 to 15 Fr) loses about three-quarters of lumen area, so symptoms appear late.
- **Anatomy**: bulbar urethra is eccentric (dorsal) in the spongiosum. Dual blood supply (bulbar arteries plus retrograde flow from the glans) lets the bulb be divided and mobilised.

### Etiology
| Category | Examples and mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Idiopathic** (commonest, about equal to iatrogenic) | No cause found |
| **Iatrogenic** | Catheters (very common in LMICs (low- and middle-income countries)), TURP (transurethral resection of prostate), TURBT (transurethral resection of bladder tumour), cystoscopy, hypospadias repair, radiotherapy. Mechanism: mucosal injury, pressure ischaemia, infection |
| **Traumatic** | **Straddle injury**: bulb crushed against the pubis; pelvic fracture gives a posterior obliterative defect |
| **Inflammatory** | Gonorrhoea (historically commonest, now rare); **lichen sclerosus (LS)**. Chlamydia: no proven link |
| Congenital | Rarest; short anterior stricture in infants |
- **LS** starts at the glans and meatus. High-pressure voiding forces urine into Littre glands, causing inflammation and deep spongiofibrosis. It **recurs in any genital skin** used for repair.
- **Jordan spectrum**: mucosal fold → iris constriction → full-thickness → deep spongiofibrosis → fibrosis beyond spongiosum → fistula or abscess.

### Clinical presentation
- Poor stream, straining, spraying, incomplete emptying, post-void dribble; UTI (urinary tract infection), prostatitis, epididymitis.
- **Retention** after long-tolerated symptoms; periurethral abscess, fistula ("watering-can perineum"), stones, upper-tract damage.

### Evaluation: define location, length, depth, density
- **Uroflowmetry** (flat plateau curve) and PVR (post-void residual).
- **Dynamic RUG (retrograde urethrogram) + VCUG (voiding cystourethrogram)**, done by the surgeon, oblique views.
- **Flexible urethroscopy**; **sonourethrography** for spongiofibrosis; MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) for PFUI only.
- In retention: **suprapubic catheter**, not blind dilation. **Urethral rest** (6–8 weeks) shows the true length.

### Standard management (brief)
- First agree the aim: **palliation or cure**.
- **Dilation** (balloon over a guidewire) stretches, not tears; cures only epithelial strictures.
- **DVIU (direct vision internal urethrotomy)**: heals by secondary intention, a race between epithelialisation and wound contraction. Only for **bulbar, <1.5 cm, no dense spongiofibrosis, first attempt**. A second DVIU adds almost nothing. Laser equals cold knife.
- **Urethroplasty**:

| Technique | Use |
|---|---|
| **EPA (excision and primary anastomosis)** | **Gold standard**; short bulbar (1–2 cm, sometimes 3–5 cm proximally). Excise all scar, spatulate, tension-free |
| **BMG (buccal mucosal graft) onlay** | Longer bulbar: dorsal (Barbagli), ventral, dorsal inlay (Asopa). Graft equals flap |
| **Penile skin flaps** (Orandi, circular) | Penile strictures (not LS) |
| **Staged repair** (Johanson) | LS, failed hypospadias, infection |
| **Perineal urethrostomy** | Panurethral disease, elderly, multiple failures |
- Onlay beats tube repairs. **PFUI**: suprapubic catheter, then delayed **perineal anastomotic repair** with progressive steps (crural separation, inferior pubectomy, rerouting).

### Recent advances in management
1. **Ladder abandoned**: the "reconstructive ladder" is archaic. Repeated endoscopy lowers later urethroplasty success, so **early urethroplasty** is offered after one failed DVIU (AUA (American Urological Association) and EAU (European Association of Urology) guidelines).
2. **Optilume paclitaxel-coated balloon** (FDA (Food and Drug Administration) 2023): paclitaxel inhibits fibroblasts. **ROBUST III**: stricture-free 74.6% vs 26.8% at 1 year after failed endoscopy; IPSS (International Prostate Symptom Score) 9 vs 19.9.
3. **Anti-scarring adjuncts after DVIU**: mitomycin C injection reduces recurrence; intermittent self-dilation; removable stents. Permanent UroLume stents withdrawn.
4. **Vessel-sparing and non-transecting EPA**: preserve bulbar arteries, theoretically less ED (erectile dysfunction).
5. **Augmented anastomosis**: excise the narrowest segment, graft the rest.
6. **Kulkarni one-sided dorsal BMG** through a perineal incision for panurethral strictures.
7. **Staged oral mucosa repair for LS**; avoid skin. **BMG perineal urethrostomy** reduces neomeatal stenosis.
8. **Robotic reconstruction** of bladder neck contracture and posterior stenosis (Y-V plasty, BMG).
9. **Tissue-engineered oral mucosa** (investigational). **Prevention**: silicone and hydrophilic catheters, smaller endoscopes.
10. **PROMs (patient-reported outcome measures)**: USS-PROM (Urethral Stricture Surgery PROM) is increasingly used to judge success.
- **Also know:** dartos-island fossa repair; lingual graft; clobetasol for early LS.

### Complications and follow-up
- Recurrence (EPA: mostly in first 6 months; grafts decline with time), ED, chordee, dribble, graft-site pain. Follow up with flow, PVR and symptom score.

**Diagram to draw:** RUG of a bulbar stricture, and cross-section of dorsal versus ventral onlay.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Idiopathic and iatrogenic are commonest; LS recurs in skin.
> - Define location, length, depth, density (dynamic RUG + VCUG).
> - DVIU only once, bulbar <1.5 cm; EPA is gold standard.
> - Optilume (ROBUST III 74.6% vs 26.8%); early urethroplasty, ladder abandoned.

## Q2. Describe use and principles of immunology and Immunotherapy in Urological Oncology
**Type:** LAQ (25 marks)
(immunomodulation in RCC (renal cell carcinoma): see 2025-Winter Paper 4, Q3a)

### Introduction
- Immunotherapy works by either **supplying a danger signal** that starts a Th1 response (BCG (bacille Calmette-Guérin), vaccine adjuvant) or **removing a brake** on an existing response (PD-1 (programmed death-1), CTLA-4 (cytotoxic T-lymphocyte antigen-4)).

### Principles of tumour immunology
| Arm | Cells and role |
|---|---|
| **Innate** (rapid, pattern-based) | PAMPs (pathogen-associated molecular patterns) bind **TLRs (Toll-like receptors)**; macrophages, neutrophils; urothelium as sentinel (IFN (interferon), IL-8 (interleukin-8)) |
| **Bridge** | **Dendritic cell (DC)**: takes up antigen, matures on danger signal, migrates to lymph node |
| **Adaptive** (specific, memory) | **CD8 killer** (sees class I MHC (major histocompatibility complex), ~9 amino acids); **CD4 helper** (class II); B cells |
| **Brake** | **Treg** (regulatory T cell, FoxP3+ CD4); MDSCs (myeloid-derived suppressor cells); M2 macrophages |
- **Two signals**: TCR (T-cell receptor) meets MHC–peptide (signal 1) plus **CD28–B7** costimulation (signal 2).
- **Th1 vs Th2**: IL-12 gives Th1 (IL-2, IFN-γ, TNF-α (tumour necrosis factor-α)), which is antitumour. IL-4 gives Th2 (antibody, chronic inflammation).
- **Checkpoints**: CTLA-4 (on Tregs) competes for B7; PD-1 on CD8 cells binds **PD-L1** (programmed death ligand-1); LAG-3 binds class II.
- **Adaptive immune resistance**: CD8 cells secrete IFN-γ, which induces PD-L1 on the tumour, which switches the same T cells off. So PD-L1 marks a suppressed ongoing response.

### Immunoediting (three Es)
- **Elimination** (small tumours destroyed) → **Equilibrium** (growth held) → **Escape** (clinical cancer).
- Escape: loss of antigen or MHC, Treg expansion, checkpoint ligands.
- **GU (genitourinary) landscape**: bladder: inflammation-driven (schistosomiasis), CD8 infiltration good. Kidney: **CD8 paradox** (more CD8, worse outcome). Prostate: Treg-dominated, PD-L1 low, so checkpoint inhibitors fail.

### Immunotherapy in practice
**1. BCG in NMIBC (non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer)**
- Bacilli attach via **fibronectin**; TLR2/4 activation → IL-8 and TNF-α → neutrophil wave → DC priming → **Th1 CD4** response → memory.
- Th1 predicts success, Th2 failure. About half stay in remission for years. Used for intermediate/high-risk NMIBC and CIS (carcinoma in situ) with induction plus maintenance.

**2. Cytokines and gene therapy**
- **IFN-α** and **high-dose IL-2** (durable complete responses in a few mRCC (metastatic renal cell carcinoma) patients; capillary leak). Intravesical IFN-α: mixed.
- **Nadofaragene firadenovec** (adenovirus with IFN-α2b gene + Syn3): BCG-unresponsive CIS, CR (complete response) 53.4%; FDA (Food and Drug Administration) 2022.
- **N-803** (IL-15 superagonist) with BCG: FDA approved 2024 for BCG-unresponsive CIS.

**3. Cancer vaccines**
- Antigen plus adjuvant (often GM-CSF (granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor)) to activate DCs.
- **Sipuleucel-T**: autologous blood cells pulsed with **PAP (prostatic acid phosphatase)–GM-CSF**, three infusions. Asymptomatic mCRPC (metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer): **OS (overall survival) 25.8 vs 21.7 months**, **no delay in progression**. First FDA-approved solid-tumour vaccine.
- Failed: PROSTVAC (prostate), IMA901 and AGS-003 (RCC).

**4. Checkpoint inhibitors**
| Site | Use (trial) |
|---|---|
| **BCG-unresponsive CIS** | Pembrolizumab if cystectomy declined/unfit (KEYNOTE-057: CR 41%) |
| **Adjuvant MIBC (muscle-invasive bladder cancer) and UTUC** (upper tract urothelial carcinoma) | Nivolumab after radical surgery (**CheckMate 274**: DFS (disease-free survival) 20.8 vs 10.8 months; included UTUC, benefit uncertain in that small subgroup). Atezolizumab (IMvigor010) negative |
| **Metastatic UC (urothelial carcinoma)** | Avelumab **maintenance** after platinum (JAVELIN Bladder 100, OS gain); pembrolizumab second line (**KEYNOTE-045**: OS 10.3 vs 7.4 months); **enfortumab vedotin (EV) + pembrolizumab** |
| **RCC** | Nivolumab + ipilimumab (CheckMate 214); IO (immuno-oncology) + TKI (tyrosine kinase inhibitor) combinations; adjuvant pembrolizumab (KEYNOTE-564, DFS HR (hazard ratio) 0.68) |
| **Prostate** | Monotherapy poor; pembrolizumab only for **MSI-H/dMMR** (microsatellite instability-high/mismatch repair-deficient) tumours |
- VEGF (vascular endothelial growth factor) suppresses DCs and T-cell entry, so TKIs synergise with IO.
- **Biomarkers**: PD-L1 CPS (combined positive score) only for first-line single-agent pembrolizumab in cisplatin-ineligible UC; TMB (tumour mutational burden), MSI.

**5. Adoptive cell therapy (brief)**
- **CAR-T (chimeric antigen receptor T cells)**: T cells engineered against a surface antigen (e.g. PSMA (prostate-specific membrane antigen)). Investigational in GU; limited by poor trafficking, hostile microenvironment and cytokine release syndrome.

### Toxicity: irAEs (immune-related adverse events)
- Colitis, hepatitis, pneumonitis, thyroiditis, hypophysitis, nephritis, myocarditis, rash.

| Grade | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Continue, monitor |
| 2 | Hold; prednisolone 0.5–1 mg/kg |
| 3–4 | Stop; methylprednisolone 1–2 mg/kg; infliximab or mycophenolate if refractory |
- Endocrinopathies need hormone replacement. BCG toxicity: cystitis, BCG sepsis.

### Recent advances
- **EV-302**: enfortumab vedotin + pembrolizumab first line in metastatic UC, OS 31.5 vs 16.1 months versus platinum; now the preferred first line.
- **NIAGARA**: perioperative durvalumab with neoadjuvant cisplatin chemotherapy improved event-free and overall survival in MIBC.
- **Also know:** CheckMate 901 (nivolumab + gemcitabine–cisplatin); ADT (androgen deprivation) + IO trials; anti-CD38 and LAG-3 targets.

**Diagram to draw:** T cell–tumour synapse with TCR–MHC, CD28–B7, CTLA-4 and PD-1/PD-L1 blocked by antibodies.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Three Es: elimination, equilibrium, escape; Th1 is antitumour.
> - BCG: TLR2/4 → neutrophils → Th1 memory.
> - Sipuleucel-T: OS +4.1 months, no delay in progression.
> - CheckMate 274 adjuvant nivolumab; EV-302 EV + pembrolizumab first line.

## Q3a. Non obstructive azoospermia
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in 2019-Summer Paper 1, Q3d)

### Definition
- **NOA (non-obstructive azoospermia):** no sperm in the **centrifuged** ejaculate on two samples, due to **failure of spermatogenesis**. About 60% of azoospermic men.

### Causes
- **Genetic:** **Klinefelter (47,XXY)**, commonest; **Y microdeletion** (AZF (azoospermia factor) a, b, c).
- **Acquired:** cryptorchidism, mumps orchitis, torsion, **chemotherapy or radiation**, trauma, varicocele.
- **Idiopathic**: commonest overall.
- *Note:* hypogonadotropic hypogonadism is pre-testicular and treated medically (hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin) ± FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone)).

### Evaluation
- Small soft testes, palpable vasa, normal ejaculate volume.
- **FSH >7.6 IU/L and testis long axis ≤4.6 cm** suggest NOA.
- LH (luteinising hormone), testosterone, oestradiol, prolactin.
- **Karyotype and Y microdeletion in all** (AUA/ASRM (American Urological Association/American Society for Reproductive Medicine)), with counselling.

| Histology | Meaning | Sperm retrieval |
|---|---|---|
| Hypospermatogenesis | All stages, fewer cells | Best |
| Maturation arrest | Halts at one germ-cell stage | About 40% |
| SCO (Sertoli cell–only) | No germ cells | About 30% |

### Management
**1. Optimisation**: stop exogenous testosterone; repair clinical varicocele (evidence weak); off-label clomiphene, anastrozole or hCG for low testosterone.

**2. Micro-TESE (microdissection testicular sperm extraction)**
- **Principle:** spermatogenesis in NOA is **focal**. At ×20–25 magnification, **larger, opaque tubules** are taken; little tissue removed, blood supply spared.
- Sperm found in **about 50%**; better than conventional TESE.

| Predictor | Retrieval (%) |
|---|---|
| Post-orchidopexy | 74 |
| AZFc deletion | 70 |
| Klinefelter | 68 |
| Post-chemotherapy | 53 |
| **AZFa or AZFb complete deletion** | **0: do not operate** |
- FSH and testis size do not reliably predict retrieval.

**3. ICSI (intracytoplasmic sperm injection)** with fresh or frozen sperm. Sons of AZF deletion carriers inherit the deletion.

**4. No sperm:** donor sperm or adoption.

**Also know:** in-vitro spermatogenesis and stem-cell transplantation are experimental.

**Diagram to draw:** opened testis under microscope: plump tubules versus thin Sertoli cell–only tubules.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - FSH >7.6 and testis ≤4.6 cm suggest NOA.
> - Karyotype and Y microdeletion in all.
> - Micro-TESE: sperm in about half; SCO 30%.
> - AZFa/b complete deletion: no retrieval.

## Q3b. Retroileal Ureter
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)

*"Retroileal" is taken as **retroiliac ureter**, the nearest real entity; retrocaval ureter is compared below (full answer: 2017-Winter Paper 3, Q3d).*

### Definition
- The ureter passes **behind the common iliac artery** and is compressed there.
- Better called "**preureteral iliac artery**": the anomaly is presumed vascular; the ureter is trapped.

### Embryology
- Normally the **ventral root of the umbilical artery** is replaced by a more **dorsal** branch, which lies behind the ureter.
- If the **ventral root persists** and the dorsal root fails, the ureter is trapped behind the artery.

### Features
- **Very rare**; either side; **bilateral** cases reported.
- Obstruction at **L5–S1**.
- **Associated anomalies are common**, especially **vasal and mesonephric duct ectopia**: ureter and vas draining together into the posterior urethra; vasa ending in the ureters; ectopic vaginal ureter.

### Presentation and diagnosis
- Flank pain, UTI (urinary tract infection) or hydroureteronephrosis down to the iliac crossing.
- **CT urography (computed tomography urography) with arterial phase** or MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) shows ureter and artery together; **diuretic renogram** for function and drainage.
- Caution: a vessel indenting an already dilated ureter may not be the cause.

### Management
- Non-obstructed: observe.
- Obstructed: **divide the ureter, re-route it in front of the artery** and do a spatulated ureteroureterostomy (or reimplantation) over a double-J stent; open, laparoscopic or robotic.

### Comparison with retrocaval ureter
| | Retroiliac | Retrocaval (circumcaval) |
|---|---|---|
| Vessel | Common iliac artery | IVC (inferior vena cava) |
| Embryology | Persistent ventral umbilical artery root | Persistent subcardinal vein |
| Side | Either, may be bilateral | Right |
| Level | L5–S1 | L3–L4, "fishhook" |
| Associations | Vasal/mesonephric ectopia | Horseshoe kidney, left renal anomalies |
- **Also know:** distal ureter obstruction by uterine, umbilical, obturator or hypogastric vessels.

**Diagram to draw:** ureter behind the common iliac artery at L5–S1, dilated above.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Retroiliac = preureteral iliac artery; persistent ventral umbilical artery root.
> - Obstruction at L5–S1; either side or bilateral.
> - Look for vasal and ureteric ectopia.
> - Retrocaval: right, L3–L4, fishhook, 1 in 1500.

## Q3c. Vascular access for hemodialysis
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in 2020-Summer Paper 4, Q3b)

### Introduction
- Haemodialysis needs blood flow of **250–350 mL/min**. Access failure is a major cause of morbidity in ESKD (end-stage kidney disease).

### Types
| Access | Construction | Ready | Comment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Radiocephalic AVF** (arteriovenous fistula; Brescia–Cimino) | Radial artery to cephalic vein at wrist, end-to-side | 6–8 weeks | **First choice**: best patency, fewest infections |
| Brachiocephalic AVF | At elbow | 6–8 weeks | Higher flow, more steal |
| **AVG** (arteriovenous graft) | PTFE (polytetrafluoroethylene) loop or straight | 2–3 weeks | More stenosis, thrombosis, infection |
| **Tunnelled cuffed CVC** (central venous catheter) | Right internal jugular | At once | Bridge or last resort; infection, central stenosis |
- Non-tunnelled catheters: acute dialysis only.

### Planning
- **Hierarchy**: AVF, then AVG, then CVC; **distal before proximal; non-dominant arm first**. KDOQI (Kidney Disease Outcomes Quality Initiative) 2019 favours an individual "ESKD Life-Plan".
- **Timing**: refer at eGFR (estimated glomerular filtration rate) <30; create the AVF about **6 months** before dialysis.
- **Vein preservation**: no venepuncture or PICC (peripherally inserted central catheter) in that arm; avoid subclavian lines.
- **Assessment**: pulses, **Allen test** (ulnar collateral flow), **Doppler vein mapping** (vessels about ≥2 mm).
- **Maturity "rule of 6s"**: flow >600 mL/min, diameter >6 mm, depth <6 mm, at 6 weeks.

### Complications
| Complication | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Failure to mature (juxta-anastomotic stenosis) | Angioplasty, ligate side branches |
| Stenosis (neointimal hyperplasia) | PTA (percutaneous transluminal angioplasty), stent graft |
| **Thrombosis** (usually on a stenosis) | Thrombectomy, then fix the stenosis |
| **Steal syndrome** (hand ischaemia) | Banding, DRIL (distal revascularisation–interval ligation) |
| Aneurysm; infection (CVC > AVG > AVF) | Repair; antibiotics, excise graft |
- **Also know:** brachiobasilic transposition AVF; venous hypertension; high-output cardiac failure.

### Monitoring
- Thrill and bruit, arm-elevation test, prolonged bleeding after needling, rising venous pressure; duplex when abnormal.

### Recent advances
- **Endovascular AVF** (percutaneous creation); drug-coated balloons; bioengineered vessels.

**Diagram to draw:** radiocephalic end-to-side fistula at the wrist.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - AVF first, distal, non-dominant arm, 6 months ahead.
> - Rule of 6s: >600 mL/min, >6 mm, <6 mm deep, 6 weeks.
> - Thrombosis sits on a stenosis; steal → banding or DRIL.

## Q3d. Botulinum Toxin and its role in Urology
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in 2017-Winter Paper 3, Q3b)

### Introduction
- *Clostridium botulinum* neurotoxin, serotypes A–G. Urology uses **onabotulinumtoxinA (BoNT-A)**.
- 150 kDa: heavy chain (100 kDa) plus zinc-dependent light chain (50 kDa).

### Mechanism
1. Heavy chain binds the cholinergic nerve terminal; toxin is internalised.
2. Light chain enters cytosol and **cleaves SNAP-25** (synaptosomal-associated protein 25), a SNARE vesicle-docking protein. Vesicles cannot fuse, so **acetylcholine release stops**.
- **Afferent action**: less ATP (adenosine triphosphate), substance P and CGRP (calcitonin gene-related peptide); fewer TRPV1 (capsaicin) and P2X3 (purinergic) receptors on C fibres, so urgency falls.
- Effect wanes as terminals sprout (6–9 months).

### Technique
- Cystoscopic detrusor injection, **20 sites (100 U) or 30 sites (200 U)**, sparing trigone; check PVR (post-void residual) at 2 weeks.

### Urological uses
| Indication | Dose and evidence |
|---|---|
| **Idiopathic OAB (overactive bladder)**, third line | **100 U** (EMBARK). **ABC**: equal to antimuscarinic, more complete dryness. **ROSETTA**: 200 U comparable to SNM (sacral neuromodulation), more UTI (urinary tract infection) |
| **NDO (neurogenic detrusor overactivity)**: SCI (spinal cord injury), MS (multiple sclerosis) | **200 U** (DIGNITY): less incontinence, larger capacity, lower detrusor pressure |
| **Paediatric NDO** (spina bifida) | About **10 U/kg (maximum 360 U)** in Campbell series; FDA (Food and Drug Administration) label (age ≥5) 6 U/kg, maximum 200 U |
| **DSD (detrusor–sphincter dyssynergia)** | Intrasphincteric injection |
| **IC/BPS (interstitial cystitis/bladder pain syndrome)** | 100 U, often trigonal; AUA (American Urological Association) option, off-label |
| **BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia)** | Intraprostatic: placebo-controlled RCTs (randomised controlled trials) showed **no benefit**; not recommended |
- **Also know:** pelvic floor spasm.

### Complications
- **Retention needing CISC (clean intermittent self-catheterisation)**, more in NDO; each 10 mL of baseline PVR raises risk about 27%. **Patient must accept CISC beforehand.**
- **UTI**; haematuria; rarely generalised weakness. Contraindications: active UTI, myasthenia gravis, pregnancy.

**Diagram to draw:** cholinergic synapse showing SNAP-25 cleavage by the light chain.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Cleaves SNAP-25; efferent and afferent action.
> - 100 U idiopathic OAB; 200 U NDO.
> - Children: 10 U/kg (max 360 U) in series; FDA label 6 U/kg (max 200 U).
> - Main risks: retention needing CISC, UTI.

## Q3e. Tubeless PCNL
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(also answered in 2016-Summer Paper 4, Q3e)

### Definition
- PCNL (percutaneous nephrolithotomy) that ends **without a nephrostomy tube**.
- **Tubeless**: drainage by a ureteral stent (double-J, or externalised stent or string). **Totally tubeless**: no stent and no tube.
- **Wickham (1984)** did the first tubeless PCNL; **Bellman (1997)** popularised it.

### Rationale
- A large nephrostomy tube causes pain, more analgesia, longer stay and higher cost. Most tracts stop bleeding without tamponade.

### Selection
| Suitable | Keep a nephrostomy |
|---|---|
| Complete clearance, single tract | Pyonephrosis or infected stone |
| Minimal bleeding | Significant bleeding |
| Intact collecting system | Significant collecting-system perforation |
| Supracostal access, bilateral cases (shown safe) | Residual stone needing a second look |

### Technique (exit strategy)
1. Confirm clearance by rigid and **flexible nephroscopy** and fluoroscopy.
2. Antegrade double-J stent, or externalised stent or string.
3. Withdraw the sheath under vision; **safety wire is removed last**.
4. Watch the tract; apply pressure (rib as backstop in thin patients). Fibrin glue did not reduce bleeding in randomised trials; some use haemostatic sealants or tract cautery.
5. Urethral catheter overnight.

### Evidence
- Meta-analyses of randomised trials: shorter stay and operative time.
- Less pain than large tubes (**>16 Fr**), but **no benefit over small 8–9 Fr tubes**.
- No difference in fever, infection, transfusion or stone-free rate.
- Stent discomfort: worse WiSQoL (Wisconsin Stone Quality of Life) scores than nephrostomy in two trials. Externalised stents come out before discharge without cystoscopy.
- Children: insufficient evidence.

### Disadvantages and complications
- Loss of tract for a second look.
- Stent symptoms and a further procedure for removal.
- Urine leak or urinoma.
- Bleeding without tamponade: re-insert a balloon or Kaye catheter, then **angioembolisation**.

### Current position
- Choose tubeless, small tube or stent case by case (AUA (American Urological Association)). Pairs well with mini-PCNL.

**Diagram to draw:** kidney with sealed tract and double-J stent.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - Tubeless = stent only; totally tubeless = neither.
> - Benefit is mainly over large (>16 Fr) tubes.
> - Keep a tube for bleeding, perforation, infection or second look.

## Q3f. Surgery For Recurrant Vesico Vaginal Fistulae
**Type:** SAQ (10 marks)
(timing of VVF repair: see 2021-Summer Paper 2, Q3b)

### Introduction
- Recurrent VVF (vesicovaginal fistula): persists after a failed repair. **The first repair has the best chance**; redo tissue is scarred and poorly vascularised.
- Failure causes: tension, ischaemia, overlapping suture lines, infection, poor drainage, **malignancy, radiation**, missed second tract.

### Evaluation before redo
- **Biopsy the tract** if any history of cancer (fistula may signal recurrence).
- **Cystoscopy and vaginoscopy**: size, number, relation to ureters and bladder neck.
- **CT urography (computed tomography urography)** to exclude a **ureterovaginal fistula**; dye test.
- Urodynamics for capacity and compliance; treat UTI (urinary tract infection), optimise nutrition.

### Timing
- Wait until inflammation settles (commonly about 3 months). Radiation: **≥12 months** after radiotherapy; failed radiation repairs were redone after **≥6 months**.

### Choice of route
| Vaginal | Abdominal (open, laparoscopic, robotic) |
|---|---|
| Distal or trigonal, accessible | **Recurrent ≥2 cm**, supratrigonal, narrow vagina |
| Failed **Latzko** can be cured by a second Latzko | Ureter involved, needs reimplantation |
| Flap-splitting, offset flaps | Augmentation or **Tanagho bladder flap** needed |
| Less pain, shorter stay | **O'Conor** bivalving finds the scarred plane |
- Robotic repair of recurrences gives high closure.

### Operative principles
- Excise scar; wide mobilisation; tension-free, multilayer, **non-overlapping** closure.
- **Interposition flap is widely used in recurrent fistula** (accepted though not proven superior; an RCT showed no benefit in simple VVF):

| Flap | Route | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Martius** (labial fat pad, pudendal vessels) | Vaginal | Mid–distal, bladder neck; not apex |
| Peritoneal | Vaginal or abdominal | Apical post-hysterectomy |
| **Omentum (J-flap)** | Abdominal | Radiation, recurrent, large |
| Gracilis, Singapore flaps | Vaginal/perineal | Refractory, large tissue loss |
- Ureteric stents if within 1 cm; fill test 200–300 mL; catheter **≥10–14 days**, then cystogram.

### When repair is unwise
- Multiple failures, severe radiation damage, massive tissue loss: **conduit or continent diversion**; if unfit, ureteric occlusion with nephrostomies.
- **Also know:** rectus abdominis flap (last resort); more than one repair predicts residual incontinence.

**Diagram to draw:** Martius flap between bladder and vaginal closures.

> **Key points to remember:**
> - First repair has the highest success.
> - Biopsy; exclude ureteric fistula before redo.
> - Interposition flap (Martius, peritoneum, omentum) in recurrent repair.
> - Diversion after multiple failures or severe radiation.

#### Q1. Describe etiology, clinical presentation and recent advances in management of Urethral Structure Disease (LAQ)

- Idiopathic and iatrogenic are commonest; LS recurs in skin.
- Define location, length, depth, density (dynamic RUG + VCUG).
- DVIU only once, bulbar <1.5 cm; EPA is gold standard.
- Optilume (ROBUST III 74.6% vs 26.8%); early urethroplasty, ladder abandoned.

#### Q2. Describe use and principles of immunology and Immunotherapy in Urological Oncology (LAQ)

- Three Es: elimination, equilibrium, escape; Th1 is antitumour.
- BCG: TLR2/4 → neutrophils → Th1 memory.
- Sipuleucel-T: OS +4.1 months, no delay in progression.
- CheckMate 274 adjuvant nivolumab; EV-302 EV + pembrolizumab first line.

#### Q3a. Non obstructive azoospermia (SAQ)

- FSH >7.6 and testis ≤4.6 cm suggest NOA.
- Karyotype and Y microdeletion in all.
- Micro-TESE: sperm in about half; SCO 30%.
- AZFa/b complete deletion: no retrieval.

#### Q3b. Retroileal Ureter (SAQ)

- Retroiliac = preureteral iliac artery; persistent ventral umbilical artery root.
- Obstruction at L5–S1; either side or bilateral.
- Look for vasal and ureteric ectopia.
- Retrocaval: right, L3–L4, fishhook, 1 in 1500.

#### Q3c. Vascular access for hemodialysis (SAQ)

- AVF first, distal, non-dominant arm, 6 months ahead.
- Rule of 6s: >600 mL/min, >6 mm, <6 mm deep, 6 weeks.
- Thrombosis sits on a stenosis; steal → banding or DRIL.

#### Q3d. Botulinum Toxin and its role in Urology (SAQ)

- Cleaves SNAP-25; efferent and afferent action.
- 100 U idiopathic OAB; 200 U NDO.
- Children: 10 U/kg (max 360 U) in series; FDA label 6 U/kg (max 200 U).
- Main risks: retention needing CISC, UTI.

#### Q3e. Tubeless PCNL (SAQ)

- Tubeless = stent only; totally tubeless = neither.
- Benefit is mainly over large (>16 Fr) tubes.
- Keep a tube for bleeding, perforation, infection or second look.

#### Q3f. Surgery For Recurrant Vesico Vaginal Fistulae (SAQ)
