## Instalment 1 — Chapter Preamble, External Genitalia, Perineum, Bony Pelvis, Ligaments of the Bony Pelvis, Pelvic Floor Musculature and Fasciae (pp. 2067–2070; Figs 101.1–101.6)

### The numbers to lock in
- **Meatus 1 cm anterior** to vaginal opening. **Ischial spines ≈ S5 level.**
- **True pelvis = 4 bones**; innominate = ilium + ischium + pubis; sacrum = 5 fused vertebrae.
- **Gold standard anchors:** SSL (apical suspension), Cooper's ligament (Burch), ischial spine (landmark).
- **Eponyms:** Skene's gland — prostate homologue; Bartholin's gland — under posterior vestibular bulb, cyst or abscess; Colles fascia — female dartos, roof of superficial pouch; Cooper's ligament — Burch anchor; Burch urethropexy — Cooper's ligament suspension.
- **Female vs male pelvis:** oval vs heart-shaped inlet; obtuse vs acute subpubic angle; short wide sacrum.
- **Levator ani** = puborectalis + pubococcygeus + iliococcygeus; S3–S5 (mainly S4).
- **Traps:** coccygeus is not levator ani; obturator canal is superolateral (transobturator trocar); S1 foramina lie lateral to S4 (S3 localisation); the levator plate is horizontal and prolapse follows hiatal widening.

## Instalment 2 — Vasculature and Lymphatics of External Genitalia, Innervation (Somatic), Relevant Thigh Anatomy, Pelvic Viscera: Urologic, Reproductive, Gastrointestinal (pp. 2070–2074; Figs 101.7–101.8)

### The numbers to lock in
- **Urethra 4.7–5.1 cm; cervix 2.5 cm; tubes 10–12 cm; rectum 12–15 cm; vagina anterior 7.5 cm vs posterior 9 cm; pelvic ureter ≈ half of the ureter's length.**
- **Roots:** sacral plexus L4–S4; pudendal S2–S4; pelvic floor S3–S5; ilioinguinal L1; genitofemoral L1–L2; posterior femoral cutaneous S1–S3.
- **Eponyms:** Martius flap (dual supply: external + internal pudendal); Alcock's canal (pudendal canal).
- **Operate if:** compartment syndrome after lithotomy is a surgical emergency. Intraperitoneal (dome) bladder injury → repair.
- **Sling position:** pubovaginal at bladder neck vs mid-urethral at mid-urethra.
- **Traps:** foot drop is common peroneal at fibular head; ureter under uterine artery; ureter over anterior vaginal fornix; left ureter at base of sigmoid mesocolon (intersigmoid fossa); diverticulum = ruptured periurethral gland through periurethral fascia; closure pressure is mucosal/submucosal and oestrogen-dependent.

## Instalment 3 — Fascia and Peritoneum, Pelvic Ligaments, Anterior Longitudinal Ligament and Sacrocolpopexy, Arterial Supply, Venous Drainage, Lymphatic Drainage, Leissner Levels (pp. 2074–2079; Figs 101.9–101.16)

### The numbers to lock in
- **ALL 1.3–2.5 mm thick; sutures over S1** (not L5/S1 disc); promontory → right ureter and left common iliac vein ≈ 2.7 cm each.
- **Aorta T12 → bifurcation L4; common iliac artery R ≈ 5 cm, L ≈ 4 cm; iliac veins unite ≈ L5.**
- **1–2 mm presacral vein → > 1 L/min.**
- **Internal iliac:** anterior division parietal + visceral; posterior division parietal only.
- **Lymph:** distal ⅓ urethra → inguinal; proximal ⅔ → iliac/obturator; lower ⅓ vagina and vulva → inguinal; ovary → gonadal/para-aortic.
- **Leissner levels 1–3.**
- **When not to operate / avoid:** unnecessary presacral dissection.
- **Traps:** infundibulopelvic (ovary → side wall) vs ovarian ligament (ovary → uterus); left ovarian vein → left renal vein; external iliac ligation threatens limb, internal iliac ligation is tolerated.

## Instalment 4 — Endoscopic Anatomy, Ultrasound (Translabial, Endoanal), MRI, Benign Vaginal Cysts, Uterine Zonal Anatomy, MR Defecography (pp. 2080–2083; Figs 101.17–101.25)

### The numbers to lock in
- **30-degree lens** at bladder neck for orifices.
- **Gold standard:** T2-weighted MRI for urethral diverticulum; MRI is the most comprehensive radiation-free pelvic modality.
- **HASTE / SSFSE** for dynamic pelvic floor MRI.
- **T2 zonal signal:** endometrium high, junctional zone low, myometrium intermediate–high.
- **Pubococcygeal line:** inferior symphysis → last coccygeal joint; grading mild/moderate/severe per compartment.
- **Traps:** internal anal sphincter defect is echogenic, external is hypoechoic; patulous orifice → reflux, ectopic → duplication; benign-looking trigonal lesions are biopsied if in doubt.

### Errors, inconsistencies and corrupted text (correct version carried in the documents)
1. **Levator ani innervation.** The source says "innervated by the fourth sacral nerve" and elsewhere "direct innervation from S3–S5". Both are carried as **S3–S5, mainly S4**.
2. **"The iliococcygeus muscle (also called the coccygeus)… inserts on the last two segments of the sacrum… anterior to the sacrospinous ligament."** This conflates two muscles. The description fits **coccygeus (ischiococcygeus)**, which is separate from levator ani. The iliococcygeus arises from the arcus tendineus levator ani and ischial spine and inserts into the anococcygeal raphe and coccyx.
3. **Key Points, vasculature of external genitalia.** The text is garbled: "…from the posterior labial vessels inferiorly. branches of the internal pudendal artery…". Corrected to **posterior labial branches of the internal pudendal artery**.
4. **Posterior femoral cutaneous nerve.** It is given as S2–S3 and later as S1–S3. Carried as **S1–S3**. The typo "form the sacral plexus" means "from".
5. **Ilioinguinal nerve.** It is given as L1 and later as "arises from T12". Carried as **L1**.
6. **"Perineal branch of the cutaneous nerve of the thigh"** has a dropped word. It is the **posterior femoral cutaneous nerve of the thigh**.
7. **"Pelvic somatic afferent nerves (L4–S4)… innervating these muscles".** Nerves described as motor supply cannot be solely afferent. Carried as **pelvic somatic nerves**.
8. **"Urethrovaginalis is another name for the female external sphincter".** This is imprecise. The urethrovaginal sphincter is one component of the striated urogenital sphincter complex, with the sphincter urethrae and the compressor urethrae. It is carried that way.
9. **Urethral wall.** The source says an "outer circular muscular layer and inner longitudinal mucosal layer", and then that the urethra "is composed of three anatomic layers: (1) epithelium, (2) submucosa, (3) mucosa". The mucosa includes the epithelium, so the second list is internally inconsistent. Carried as **inner longitudinal and outer circular smooth muscle**, and layers **epithelium → submucosa → muscle** within the periurethral fascia.
10. **"Rectus fascia… covers the anterior and lateral rectal wall"** should read **rectal fascia**.
11. **Infundibulopelvic ligament "runs from the ovary to the lateral surface of the uterus"** (body text and Key Points). This is wrong, and it contradicts the source's own statements that the ovary is suspended by the infundibulopelvic ligament and attached to the uterus by the ovarian ligament. Corrected to **ovary → pelvic side wall**.
12. **"The round ligament… contains the ovarian ligaments".** This is wrong. The round ligament and the ovarian ligament are separate gubernacular remnants, so the claim is dropped.
13. **Cardinal ligaments "originate from S2 to S4"; uterosacral ligaments "originate from the greater sciatic foramen".** The attachments are transposed. Corrected: **cardinal ligament from the pelvic side wall; uterosacral ligament from the sacrum (S2–S4)**. Typo: "blood vessels form the internal iliac" should read "from".
14. **Venous tributaries list "the lateral sacral arteries".** This should be the **lateral sacral veins**.
15. **"!50%"** (variant internal iliac veins) is a corrupted symbol, probably "~" or ">". Under the study-data rule no percentage is carried. It is taught as "duplication is common".
16. **Endoscopy.** The text says the uterine impression lies along "the proximal floor of the bladder and posterior vaginal wall", but the Fig. 101.19 legend says the **posterior bladder wall**. The legend is correct, because the vaginal wall is not visible cystoscopically.
17. **Pubococcygeal line** is defined as running to "the most distal aspect of the coccyx". The standard definition is the **inferior border of the pubic symphysis to the last coccygeal joint**.
18. **Minor:** "perimetrium… thin connective tissue (parametrium)". The parametrium is the lateral connective tissue, not a layer of the perimetrium. The vaginal muscle is said to be attached to "rectovesical fascia", a male term; carried as the surrounding endopelvic fascia. Myometrium: "the blood vessels and nerves are in this layer" is ambiguous and is carried as the middle circular layer. The ureter "lies medial to the uterine artery" is ambiguous; carried as passing under the uterine artery.
