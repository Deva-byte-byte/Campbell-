# Chapter 86: Renal Physiology and Pathophysiology Including Renovascular Hypertension

## Book answers

- **c086-a01** 1. b. Erythropoiesis is inhibited by low circulating oxygen tension. Erythropoiesis is increased by low circulating oxygen tension.
- **c086-a02** 2. b. Normal compensation for hyponatremia is decreased antidiuretic hormone (ADH) secretion and thirst suppression.
- **c086-a03** 3. d. Intranasal desmopressin is a useful adjuvant therapy. Desmopressin is useful to treat hypernatremia caused by diabetes insipidus.
- **c086-a04** 4. a. Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors may be a cause of hypokalemia.
- **c086-a05** 5. c. All patients with a serum potassium value greater than 5.5 mEq/L require immediate therapy. Patients with mild elevation of potassium, especially when chronic and not associated with ECG changes, do not require emergent therapy.
- **c086-a06** 6. a. Most bicarbonate is reabsorbed in the distal collecting tubule. Most bicarbonate reabsorption in the kidney occurs in the proximal tubule.
- **c086-a07** 7. d. The hallmark of type IV RTA is hypokalemia. RTA type IV is most commonly associated with hyperkalemia. Aldosterone deficiency or resistance leads to decreased secretion of potassium in the distal tubule.
- **c086-a08** 8. c. Dermally synthesized cholecalciferol must be hydroxylated by both the liver and kidney for maximal potency. Cholecalciferol is minimally active, but potency increases 100 times after it is hydroxylated at the 1- and 25-position to form calcitriol.
- **c086-a09** 9. d. PTH increases calcium and phosphorus reabsorption in the distal tubule. PTH increases phosphorus excretion in the kidney.
- **c086-a10** 10. e. Serum osmolality. GFR is not affected significantly by serum osmolality.
- **c086-a11** 11. a. Plasma creatinine is an accurate marker of early reductions in GFR.
- **c086-a12** 12. c. It has a minor role in sodium reabsorption. The PCT accounts for 65% of sodium reabsorption, the most of any tubular segment.
- **c086-a13** 13. d. The thin ascending limb actively reabsorbs sodium, chloride, and urea. Reabsorption of sodium, chloride, and urea occurs passively in the thin ascending limb.
- **c086-a14** 14. b. Add an angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor. According to the Coral trial, medical therapy consisted of an ACE inhibitor, a statin, and a diuretic. A calcium channel blocker was not necessarily part of the therapy unless blood pressure could not be controlled on an ACE inhibitor and a diuretic alone. The patient has significant renal failure, and gadolinium is relatively contraindicated because this may predispose to nephrogenic systemic fibrosis. In fact, further investigation at this point is not indicated because the patient’s renal function is stable. Similarly, an angiogram is not indicated. Increasing the diuretic is not indicated because the diuretic is already on board and the presumption is that the dose is adequate. The final choice is not indicated unless there is rapid deterioration of renal function, and even then, there are no data to support that intervening with angiography and percutaneous angioplasty is helpful.
- **c086-a15** 15. a. The two-kidney, one-clip Goldblatt model. The other choices do not allow for natriuresis/diuresis from the opposite unclipped kidney. Thus, those two models are volume-dependent causes of hypertension rather than angiotensin dependent.
- **c086-a16** 16. d. Medical intervention is equal to percutaneous angioplasty with stenting. The results of the trial are clearly outlined in the text and require no specific explanation.
- **c086-a17** 17. b. Retrograde filling of the distal renal artery by collateral circulation on radiographic or scintigraphic imaging studies is more likely to result in a successful surgical outcome. Most patients with renal vascular disease have significant vascular disease elsewhere. Renal artery correction and aortic aneurysm correction need not be done simultaneously. A renal ostial lesion may be corrected by percutaneous angioplasty and stenting depending on its radiologic appearance.
- **c086-a18** 18. b. Proinflammatory cytokines and/or angiotensin II. Renal blood flow and perfusion may sometimes be maintained at baseline in patients with ischemic nephropathy. Ischemic nephropathy can reverse, and it is not secondary to a failure of autoregulation alone, as explained in the text. Many patients with progressive ischemic nephropathy have collateral circulation.

## Chapter Review points

- **c086-r01** 1. GFR reflects total renal function.
- **c086-r02** 2. GFR can be approximated by creatinine clearance.
- **c086-r03** 3. Formulas based on patient’s age, weight, and serum creatinine can best estimate GFR.
- **c086-r04** 4. The nephron has different functional segments that control homeostasis.
- **c086-r05** 5. Most resorption of bicarbonate and ions occurs in the proximal tubule.
- **c086-r06** 6. The architecture of the loop of Henle allows a highly hypertonic interstitium to develop, which is crucial to maximal urinary concentration.
- **c086-r07** 7. Sodium imbalances should be evaluated in the context of fluid status.
- **c086-r08** 8. The best tools to determine the cause of a sodium disorder are the history, volume status, and urinary sodium.
- **c086-r09** 9. Severe sodium deficit or excess must be corrected slowly.
- **c086-r10** 10. Potassium is primarily intracellular.
- **c086-r11** 11. Serum potassium levels reflect total body potassium, as well as the equilibrium between intra- and extracellular potassium.
- **c086-r12** 12. Symptomatic hyperkalemia requires urgent intervention to prevent cardiac dysfunction.
- **c086-r13** 13. Water movement dictates solute concentrations, especially important for sodium balance.
- **c086-r14** 14. Regulation of water movement is highly dependent on antidiuretic hormone.
- **c086-r15** 15. Insertion of aquaporin channels facilitates water reabsorption.
- **c086-r16** 16. Physiologic chemical reactions require a narrow range of serum pH.
- **c086-r17** 17. Acid is excreted through the lungs and the kidney.
- **c086-r18** 18. Type 1 RTA (distal) is the only type associated with renal stones.
- **c086-r19** 19. In acid-base disorders, first determine whether the kidney (HCO– 3) or lungs (pCO2) are responsible for the primary disorder, then determine whether the compensatory response is appropriate.
- **c086-r20** 20. The renin-angiotensin system is one of multiple chemical mediators that act on renal vascular tone to control renal blood flow.
- **c086-r21** 21. Endothelin is the most potent vasoconstrictor.
- **c086-r22** 22. Nitric oxide and carbon monoxide are potent vasodilators.
- **c086-r23** 23. Renal physiology influences bone metabolism through several hormonal actions.
- **c086-r24** 24. In the kidney, vitamin D is hydroxylated into calcitriol which helps maintain systemic calcium and phosphate levels.
- **c086-r25** 25. PTH influences the kidney to increase calcium reabsorption.
- **c086-r26** 26. Renal artery stenosis is the most common cause of renovascular hypertension.
- **c086-r27** 27. Atherosclerotic vascular disease accounts for 2/3 of cases of renal artery stenosis.
- **c086-r28** 28. Four types of fibromuscular dysplasia can also cause renal artery stenosis. Of these types, medial fibroplasia is the most common and least likely to progress over time.
- **c086-r29** 29. Suspect renovascular hypertension in patients with severe or abrupt- onset hypertension who otherwise have no risk factors for high blood pressure.
- **c086-r30** 30. Two models of renovascular hypertension were established with classic dog model experiments—the two-kidney, one-clip and one- kidney, one-clip models.
- **c086-r31** 31. Noninvasive diagnostic imaging, including MR angiography, CT angiography, and duplex ultrasonography, should be first-line screening to diagnosis renal artery stenosis.
- **c086-r32** 32. Medical management for renovascular hypertension is the mainstay of therapy.
- **c086-r33** 33. Percutaneous intervention to treat renal artery stenosis may be effective, but growing evidence calls into question its efficacy to control blood pressure or prevent renal failure long term.
- **c086-r34** 34. While surgical treatment is no longer first-line therapy for renal artery stenosis, its role still exists for the appropriately selected patient.
