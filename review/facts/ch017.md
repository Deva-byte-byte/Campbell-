# Chapter 17: Complications of Urologic Surgery

## Book answers

- **c017-a01** 1. b. Open conversion, colostomy, and delayed repair. A good management strategy in this case includes open conversion, colostomy, and delayed repair.
- **c017-a02** 2. b. Patients with higher nephrometry score are at increased risk. Patients with higher nephrometry score are at increased risk for pseudoaneurysm development following partial nephrectomy.
- **c017-a03** 3. d. The most commonly injured vessel is the left common iliac vein. When obtaining pneumoperitoneum with the Veress needle, the most commonly injured vessel is the left common iliac vein.
- **c017-a04** 4. d. Longer warm ischemia times are directly related to the development of urine leaks. Longer warm ischemia times are directly related to the development of urine leaks after partial nephrectomy.
- **c017-a05** 5. a. Waiting 6 weeks for surgical repair allowing inflammation to subside is reasonable. Once all attempts for conservative management have failed for ureteral complications, it is agreed that waiting 6 weeks for surgical repair allowing inflammation to subside is reasonable.
- **c017-a06** 6. e. Genito-femoral nerve. The most commonly injured structure during Psoas hitch procedure is the genito-femoral nerve.
- **c017-a07** 7. c. Avoiding excess arm abduction of greater than 90 degrees. Avoiding excess arm abduction of greater than 90 degrees can prevent brachial plexus injuries.

## Chapter Review points

- **c017-r01** 1. A wide array of physiologic, urinary, vascular, bowel, and visceral complications can occur with all forms of urologic operations.
- **c017-r02** 2. With the wide adoption of laparoscopic and robotic surgery in urology, unique sets of complications are encountered.
- **c017-r03** 3. As more complex operations are performed in a minimally invasive fashion, the rate of complications can increase.
- **c017-r04** 4. Some complications after laparoscopic and robotic procedures can present in an atypical and delayed fashion.
- **c017-r05** 5. A high index of suspicion is necessary for prompt and timely intraoperative and postoperative diagnosis and management.
- **c017-r06** 6. The most important aspect of complication management is complication prevention with adequate planning, attention to detail, and meticulous surgical technique.
