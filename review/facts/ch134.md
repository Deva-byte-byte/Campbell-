# Chapter 134: Special Urologic Considerations in Transgender Individuals

## Book answers

- **c134-a01** 1. a. Urethral fistula.Urethral fistula is the most common urologic complication after phallic reconstruction.
- **c134-a02** 2. a. The anastomotic region between the fixed and phallic parts of the urethra.Multiple retrospective studies in large cohorts of patients show that the anastomotic region between the fixed and phallic parts of the urethra is the most frequent site of urethral stricture in transmen.
- **c134-a03** 3. b. 0.9%.Multiple retrospective studies in large cohorts of patients show that rectal injury as a consequence of vaginectomy occurs in approximately 0.9% of patients.
- **c134-a04** 4. a. Labia majora.The labia majora are the embryologic counterpart of the scrotum, so using them for scrotal reconstruction is logical and gives the best aesthetic outcomes.
- **c134-a05** 5. d. All of the above.In order to exclude any major urologic problem that could interfere with the reconstructive surgery, all three assessments are necessary.
- **c134-a06** 6. a. Tthe sharp curve between the female native urethra and the pars fixa of the neourethra.Transmen are usually difficult to catheterize transurethrally after surgery because of the sharp curve between the female native urethra and the pars fixa of the neourethra.
- **c134-a07** 7. c. 40%.Based on studies published in the field, the main complication rate including all parts of the surgery is 40%.
- **c134-a08** 8. b. 1 year after phalloplasty.The implantation is done when sensitivity is observed in the phallus. As the sensitivity is based on a nerve anastomosis, it takes about a year before this anastomosis becomes functional.
- **c134-a09** 9. c. Gender dysphoria.This term is based on the International Classification of Disease and DSM classifications and definitions.
- **c134-a10** 10. c. Transvaginally.The neovagina is reconstructed between the rectum and the prostate; thus this new structure allows easy access of the ultrasound probe to examine the prostate.
- **c134-a11** 11. a. It can be diagnosed at pediatric age.Gender identity disorder (GID), which is mentioned in DSM-5, can be diagnosed in childhood. GID is more frequent in males and is not based on hormonal imbalance. Psychotherapy as treatment has bad results and is no longer recommended in the standards of care.

## Chapter Review points

- **c134-r01** 1. The urologist has an important role in the treatment and follow-up of transgender individuals.
- **c134-r02** 2. Urinary incontinence is the most frequent complication in transwomen.
- **c134-r03** 3. Be aware of prostate pathology in transwomen.
- **c134-r04** 4. Discuss whether or not to perform vaginectomy in transmen.
- **c134-r05** 5. Urethral fistulas and strictures are frequent complications in transmen and most often occur at the anastomosis between the fixed and phallic part of the urethra.
- **c134-r06** 6. Metoidioplasty is an alternative to phalloplasty in transmen who refuse to accept the complications of phalloplasty.
- **c134-r07** 7. Implantation of an erectile prosthesis in transmen is associated with a higher risk of complications compared to native males.
