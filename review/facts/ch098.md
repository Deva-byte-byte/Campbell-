# Chapter 98: Urothelial Tumors of the Upper Urinary Tract and Ureter

## Book answers

- **c098-a01** 1. d. In Balkan countries and Taiwan. The highest incidence of UTUC is observed in the Balkan countries and is related to the “Balkan nephropathy.” The southeast coast of Taiwan also has a high prevalence of UTUC, which is related to the “Chinese herb nephropathy.” Both nephropathies are associated with the presence of aristolochic acid in plants endemic in these countries.
- **c098-a02** 2. c. Reduction of 60% to 70% with interruption for more than 10 years. McLaughlin et al. (1992) showed a 60% to 70% reduction in the risk for UTUC with interruption of smoking for more than 10 years.
- **c098-a03** 3. d. Is twice as common in females and related to younger onset of disease. UTUC is twice as common in the female population in comparison to the worldwide incidence and is related to younger onset of disease in the areas endemic to blackfoot disease, such as the southwest coast of Taiwan. Blackfoot disease is related to chronic exposure to arsenic.
- **c098-a04** 4. c. Are benign but may harbor malignancy in 18% of the cases. Inverted papillomas are considered benign. Nevertheless, studies have shown that 18% of these tumors of the ureter are malignant.
- **c098-a05** 5. b. Is an independent predictor of progression-free survival. The micropapillary variant of the urothelial carcinoma was related to an independent predictor of progression-free survival (HR 3.85, P = 0.003) and to poorer cancer-specific survival than the nonmicropapillary UTUC ( P < .001),
- **c098-a06** 6. e. Both a and b. Squamous cell carcinoma has an incidence 5 times higher in the renal pelvis than in the ureter and has been associated with the abuse of analgesics and chronic inflammation.
- **c098-a07** 7. c. Increases the diagnostic accuracy with excretory or retrograde urography. Ureteroscopy increases the diagnostic accuracy of excretory or retrograde urography alone from 75% to 85%–90%.
- **c098-a08** 8. e. b and c. Biopsy during ureteroscopy has been reported to have a correlation with the pathohistological result of 92% for high-grade tumors and successfully detects 67% of the invasive tumors.
- **c098-a09** 9. e. Loop biopsies have the highest yield of lamina propria layer. Loop biopsies may include the lamina propria layer in 100% of the specimens. The respective figure for cup biopsy is 62%.
- **c098-a10** 10. a. Measuring Hounsfield units (HU) in the range of 10 to 70 HU with an average 46 HU. Urothelial cancers have an average density of 46 HU and range between 10 and 70 HU.
- **c098-a11** 11. a. Has an incidence of 2% to 6% and a median time to recurrence >3 years. Upper tract recurrence after bladder cancer treatment has an incidence of 2% to 6% and is diagnosed later with a median time to recurrence >3 years. These rates have significant fluctuation when traditional series are considered. In most of the traditional series, the recurrence rate ranges between 2% and 4% with an interval to recurrence ranging from 17 to 170 months, but there are also series with rates up to 25%.
- **c098-a12** 12. e. a, b, and c. The presence of CIS increases the incidence of bilateral and multifocal disease. Bilateral disease has an overall incidence of 3% to 5% of UTUC cases. The presence of CIS increases this incidence to 25%. Patients with CIS have higher risk for subsequent panurothelial disease since there is a high probability for multifocal disease.
- **c098-a13** 13. e. a, b, and c. Age is one of the most important demographic predictors of survival in UTUC. Decreased cancer-specific survival has been reported in patients of older age at the time of radical nephroureterectomy. Older age represents an independent factor for decreased survival. Being a smoker at diagnosis increases the risk for disease recurrence and mortality after radical nephroureterectomy and recurrence of the urothelial cancer in the bladder. Patients with ureteral and/or multifocal tumors seem to have a worse prognosis than those with renal pelvic tumors when the stage is adjusted.
- **c098-a14** 14. d. a and b. Positive regional lymph nodes (LNs) is an independent factor for poor survival prognosis. Tumor stage remains the single most important factor for the oncological outcome of the UTUC patients.

## Chapter Review points

- **c098-r01** 1. Upper urinary tract carcinomas comprise only 5% of the urothelial cancers.
- **c098-r02** 2. The highest incidence is observed in individuals age 70 to 90 years in the Balkan countries, where the UTUC represents the 40% of all renal neoplasms.
- **c098-r03** 3. Multifocal presence of UTUC is diagnosed in 10% to 20% of the cases.
- **c098-r04** 4. Concurrent bladder cancer is diagnosed in 17% of the cases.
- **c098-r05** 5. UTUCs are twice as frequent in men as in women.
- **c098-r06** 6. Familial/hereditary UTUCs are linked to hereditary nonpolyposis colorectal carcinoma syndrome (Lynch syndrome).
- **c098-r07** 7. Tobacco increases the relative risk for UTUC from 2.5 to 7.
- **c098-r08** 8. UTUC “amino tumors” were related to occupational exposure to carcinogenic aromatic amines.
- **c098-r09** 9. Balkan nephropathy is observed as a familial not inherited condition related to the dietary exposure to aristocholic acid.
- **c098-r10** 10. Patients with pelvic and ureteral tumors have been reported to have a history of analgesic abuse in 22% and 11% of the cases, respectively.
- **c098-r11** 11. The excess of inorganic arsenic in drinking water from artesian wells creates a significant risk for UTUC.
- **c098-r12** 12. Urothelial carcinomas represent >90% of the upper urinary tract tumors. Pure nonurothelial upper urinary tract cancers are rare conditions.
- **c098-r13** 13. Variants of urothelial cancer are encountered in approximately 25% of all urothelial cancers.
- **c098-r14** 14. Papillomas, inverted papillomas, and Brunn’s nests are usually benign lesions.
- **c098-r15** 15. UTUC develops through a gradual progression of hyperplasia to dysplasia and eventually CIS in a significant proportion of the UTUC cases.
- **c098-r16** 16. CIS is difficult to diagnose with significant morphological variations.
- **c098-r17** 17. The muscle invasion or invasion to the renal parenchyma or the surrounding adventitia is more likely to take place on the upper tract.
- **c098-r18** 18. Localized disease: hematuria, dysuria, and flank pain.
- **c098-r19** 19. Advanced disease: flank or abdominal mass, weight loss, anorexia, bone pain.
- **c098-r20** 20. Cystoscopy should always be performed.
- **c098-r21** 21. Flexible ureteroscopy with biopsy is a key approach for diagnosis.
- **c098-r22** 22. Computed tomography urography has the highest diagnostic accuracy.
- **c098-r23** 23. Urine collection or washing of the ureter with a ureteral catheter could provide the most accurate cytologic results.
- **c098-r24** 24. Ureteral tumors are in the distal ureter in 70% of cases, midureter in 25% of cases, and upper ureter in 5% of cases. Bilateral disease occurs in 3% to 5% of cases.
- **c098-r25** 25. Prognosis for recurrence after radical nephroureterectomy is related to the location of the tumor and stage.
- **c098-r26** 26. The recurrence rate is 2% to 4%, with an interval of 17 to 170 months.
- **c098-r27** 27. Bladder recurrence occurs in 15% to 75 % of cases within 5 years.
- **c098-r28** 28. CIS: higher incidence of bilateral and multifocal disease.
- **c098-r29** 29. Disease spread: direct expansion of the tumor, lymphatics, and blood stream.
- **c098-r30** 30. The TNM classification and staging system. The 2004/2016 WHO grading classification.
- **c098-r31** 31. Age alone should not prevent a curable approach.
- **c098-r32** 32. Ureteral and/or multifocal tumors have a worse prognosis than renal pelvic tumors.
- **c098-r33** 33. Muscle-invasive tumors have poor prognosis.
- **c098-r34** 34. Positive regional LNs have poor survival prognosis.
- **c098-r35** 35. Lymphovascular invasion is a predictor of disease recurrence and survival.
