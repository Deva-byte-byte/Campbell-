# Chapter 143: Development, Molecular Biology, and Physiology of the Prostate

## Book answers

- **c143-a01** 1. c. Tunica albuginea. Sex accessory tissues include the prostate gland, seminal vesicles, ampullae, and bulbourethral glands. They are believed to play a major, but unknown, role in the reproductive process.
- **c143-a02** 2. d. Testosterone. The wolffian ducts develop into the seminal vesicles, epididymis, vas deferens, ampulla, and ejaculatory duct; the developmental growth of this group of glands is stimulated by fetal testosterone and not DHT .
- **c143-a03** 3. c. The seminal vesicles derive from the posterior portion of the wolffian ducts, whereas the prostate derives from the anterior portion. The prostate develops from the urogenital sinus, not the wolffian duct.
- **c143-a04** 4. b. DHT. The prostate first appears and starts its development from the urogenital sinus during the third month of fetal growth, and development is directed primarily by DHT, not testosterone.
- **c143-a05** 5. c. Both males and females will develop prostates in the presence of sufficiently high levels of androgens. If androgen (testosterone or DHT) levels are sufficiently high at the right time in fetal development, prostate development will proceed in the urogenital sinus regardless of whether the embryo is male or female. For prostate development to proceed, androgen receptor is required to be functional in the mesenchyme.
- **c143-a06** 6. b. α1A. Research work has demonstrated three subtypes of the α1-adrenergic receptor (α1A, α1B, and α1D), of which the α1A receptor appears to be linked to contraction .
- **c143-a07** 7. b. Testosterone is synthesized by the Leydig cells of the testes. Foremost among the hormones and growth factors that stimulate the prostate is the prohormone testosterone, which must be converted within the prostate into the active androgen DHT. Testosterone is synthesized in the Leydig cells of the testes from pregnenolone by a series of reversible reactions; however, once testosterone is reduced by 5α-reductase into DHT or to estrogens by aromatase, the process is irreversible. In other words, whereas testosterone can be converted into DHT and into estrogens, estrogens and DHT cannot be converted into testosterone.
- **c143-a08** 8. a. 1%. Less than 1% of the total testosterone in the plasma is derived from DHEA.
- **c143-a09** 9. e. Sex hormone-binding globulin (SHBG). The majority of testosterone bound to plasma protein is associated with SHBG.
- **c143-a10** 10. b. Type 2. The type 2 isoform is mutated in 5α-reductase deficiency and is the dominant isoform present in the prostate gland.
- **c143-a11** 11. d. Seminal vesicles. The source of fructose in human seminal plasma is the seminal vesicles. Patients with congenital absence of the seminal vesicles also have an associated absence of fructose in their ejaculates.
- **c143-a12** 12. e. All of the above. After binding ligand, the androgen receptor dissociates from chaperonins, dimerizes, and then is transported to the nucleus via a nuclear localization motif, which activates Ran-dependent active transport, a process that is ATP dependent.
- **c143-a13** 13. b. Poly CAG repeats. The shorter the length, the more actively the androgen receptor is thought to function.
- **c143-a14** 14. a. Estrogens are not required for the development of a prostate because ER-α and ER-β knockout mice have phenotypically normal prostates.
- **c143-a15** 15. a. The dimerization of the receptor is always head to head, regardless of whether the sequence is a direct repeat or an inverted repeat. Although most models predicted that inverted repeat and direct repeat androgen response elements would have dimers that bind with opposite polarity, this prediction was not observed in x-ray crystallography studies.

## Chapter Review points

- **c143-r01** 1. The seminal vesicles are extremely resistant to disease.
- **c143-r02** 2. There are two major cellular components in the prostate: epithelial and stromal.
- **c143-r03** 3. The stromal component consists of connective tissue, smooth muscle cells, and fibroblasts.
- **c143-r04** 4. The epithelial component consists of basal cells, intermediate cells, and neuroendocrine cells.
- **c143-r05** 5. The prostate contains a rich plexus of autonomic nerves.
- **c143-r06** 6. Because of the diurnal variation of serum testosterone, to avoid inconsistency it should be measured in the morning.
- **c143-r07** 7. The role of circulating adrenal androgens on prostate growth is minor.
- **c143-r08** 8. Because the plasma half-life of testosterone is 10 to 20 minutes, patients who undergo bilateral orchiectomy are functionally castrate within 1 to 2 hours after surgery.
- **c143-r09** 9. The androgen receptor is transported to the nucleus and then back to the cytoplasm; nuclear matrix is the major target for androgen and estrogen receptor binding in the nucleus.
- **c143-r10** 10. The longer the CAG repeat in the androgen receptor, the lower its activity in activating target genes.
- **c143-r11** 11. The source of prostaglandins, fructose, and semenogelin is the seminal vesicle.
- **c143-r12** 12. The seminal vesicle contributes the most volume to the seminal fluid.
- **c143-r13** 13. The source of citrate, zinc, spermine, and choline is the prostate.
- **c143-r14** 14. Prostate-specific antigen is a serine protease and degrades semenogelin. Semenogelin gives rise to the coagulation of semen.
- **c143-r15** 15. Complexed PSA is irreversibly bound to α1-antichymotrypsin and α2-macroglobulin.
- **c143-r16** 16. Increases in human kallikrein 2, pro-PSA, and bound PSA are associated with prostate cancer.
- **c143-r17** 17. Few drugs reach concentrations in the prostatic secretions that approach or surpass their concentrations in the serum. The exceptions include erythromycin, oleandomycin, sulfonamides, tetracycline, clindamycin, trimethoprim, and the fluoroquinolones.
- **c143-r18** 18. Prostatic fluid is more acidic than is serum.
- **c143-r19** 19. Acid phosphatase produced in the prostate may be elevated in prostate cancer; it is also produced in the bone and may be elevated in diseases that affect the bone such as Paget disease, osteoporosis, and bone metastases.
- **c143-r20** 20. The wolffian ducts develop into the seminal vesicles, epididymis, vas deferens, ampulla, and ejaculatory duct; the developmental growth of this group of glands is stimulated by fetal testosterone and not DHT.
- **c143-r21** 21. Testosterone is reduced by 5α-reductase into DHT or to estrogens by aromatase; the process is irreversible.
