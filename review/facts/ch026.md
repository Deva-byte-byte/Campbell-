## Chapter Review points

- **c026-r01** 1. Families should be informed of current FDA recommendations including what is known and what studies are currently underway to allow thoroughly informed consent.
- **c026-r02** 2. Anesthesia risks include nausea, vomiting, bronchospasm, laryngospasm, stridor, emergence delirium, and postanesthesia maladaptive behaviors.
- **c026-r03** 3. NPO violation significantly increases risk of gastric aspiration and pneumonitis.
- **c026-r04** 4. Premature infants have a significantly increased risk of perioperative complications.
- **c026-r05** 5. Spina bifida patients have the potential for multiple comorbidities that should be considered perioperatively.
- **c026-r06** 6. Autonomic dysreflexia is life threatening, and the pediatric urologist should know signs and symptoms of onset.
- **c026-r07** 7. Antibiotics are the mainstay of sepsis treatment and should be administered as soon as possible. Cultures should be obtained if possible.
- **c026-r08** 8. Urine cultures should be obtained preoperatively when indicated and positive cultures treated with antibiotics based on sensitivities.
- **c026-r09** 9. Antibiotics are the mainstay to prevent surgical site infections. Selection of antimicrobial should consider the site and potential source of infection.
- **c026-r10** 10. Pain management is important to discuss with anesthesia for intraoperative and postoperative management.
- **c026-r11** 11. Perioperative complications most often occur in the immediate postoperative period.
- **c026-r12** 12. Postoperative pain control is important for optimal surgical outcomes. A multimodal drug approach is recommended.
- **c026-r13** 13. Proper administration and disposal of unused narcotics are necessary to prevent overdoses with these medications.
