Chapter 13 — Incisions, Exposure, and Drains — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else

  • Fascial bites 1 cm from edge, 1 cm apart
  • Continuous + rapidly absorbable suture → ↑ hernia
  • Gibson = standard for renal transplant and transplant nephrectomy
  • Thoracoabdominal → level III caval thrombus, T4, large upper-pole/adrenal mass
  • Flank → pleural risk; anterior → bowel risk, early vascular control
  • Chest radiograph only if pleural tear repaired or suspected
  • Scrotal incision: only contraindication = suspected testicular malignancy → inguinal
  • Radical orchiectomy: 5–7 cm, from 2 cm cephalad/lateral to pubic tubercle
  • Subinguinal ILND (inguinal lymph node dissection) 2 cm below ligament; commonest; flap necrosis, lymphocele
  • Drains: no evidence for routine use; closed suction, Penrose only scrotum

Abdominal incisions

  • Midline: most versatile; incise linea alba → avoids rectus vessels
  • Lower midline = commonest urological incision (bladder, prostate)
  • Complete midline: trauma nephrectomy, RPLND (retroperitoneal lymph node dissection), polycystic bilateral nephrectomy
  • Closure: one layer; running = interrupted for hernia
  • Smead-Jones: near-far figure-of-eight, 2 cm apart
  • Pfannenstiel: 2 fingerbreadths above pubis; ↓ hernia vs midline; extraction site
  • Gibson: medial to ASIS (anterior superior iliac spine) → just lateral to rectus; extraperitoneal
  • Gibson: ureter found crossing iliac artery

Anterior approaches to kidney

  • Transverse → paediatric Wilms, pyeloplasty
  • Paramedian → rectus nerve injury, ventral hernia; avoid unless complex midline
  • Midline transperitoneal → trauma, IVC (inferior vena cava) thrombus, horseshoe kidney
  • Chevron → bilateral tumours, left-sided caval thrombus; liver/spleen injury
  • Subcostal: 2 fingerbreadths below costal margin → tip of 11th rib
  • Supine subcostal → better hilar control for large anterior tumours
  • Subcostal + xiphoid → suprahepatic IVC; + sternotomy → atrial thrombus
  • Thoracoabdominal: 9th–10th rib bed; modified via 8th rib bed
  • Chest tube omissible if parietal pleura intact
  • Diaphragm: two layers, running absorbable

Flank incisions

  • Flank simple nephrectomy → XGP (xanthogranulomatous pyelonephritis)
  • Layers: external oblique → internal oblique → transversus abdominis
  • Position: lower leg flexed, upper extended, over table break
  • Supracostal → spares rib, pleural risk
  • 11th transcostal → pleural injury + flank bulge
  • Doyen rib stripper frees rib posteriorly; bone wax to edges
  • Neurovascular bundle between layers 2 and 3
  • Pleural tear: red rubber catheter under water, big breaths, remove when no bubbles
  • Large pleural tear → formal chest tube
  • Reoperation: extend into unviolated tissue, enter peritoneum there
  • General surgeon → prior cystectomy/diversion, trauma, bowel surgery

Inguinal incisions

  • Benign inguinal (varicocele, orchidopexy): 2–3 cm, 2 fingerbreadths above symphysis
  • Aim: just below external ring
  • Subinguinal → obese or prior inguinal surgery
  • Protect ilioinguinal nerve; new external ring not too tight
  • ILND flaps: below Scarpa fascia; ± myocutaneous flap
  • Nodal extent chooses ILND/PLND (pelvic lymph node dissection) incision

Specific surgeries

  • Bilateral scrotal → vertical median raphe
  • Scrotum: close dead space or Penrose → prevent haematoma
  • Adult circumcision: two-incision sleeve; commonest
  • Inner incision 5–10 mm from glans; outer at coronal sulcus
  • Frenular vessels ventrally — highest bleeding risk
  • Closure: 3-0/4-0 chromic interrupted; continuous → strangulation risk
  • Penile fracture: degloving standard; direct incision → better sexual function, needs imaging
  • Perineal urethral: 8–10 cm vertical from perineoscrotal junction
  • Lambda (inverted-Y) → ↑ 90-day wound complications
  • Colles fascia closed before skin
  • Radical perineal prostatectomy: inverted horseshoe, apex ~2 cm from anal verge
  • Balfour 1912 → retropubic, transvesical
  • Finochietto 1941 → rib spreader, chest approach
  • Bookwalter/Omni-Tract/Thompson → table-fixed; extra articulation no benefit
  • Lone Star (F. Brantley Scott) → perineal, stepwise layers
  • Retractor safety: moist pads first, minimum force
  • Drain: separate stab incision; avoid inferior epigastrics; nonabsorbable stitch
  • Drain if: partial nephrectomy, XGP nephrectomy

Classic traps

  • Trap: thoracoabdominal more painful than flank — Actually: no difference
  • Trap: routine chest radiograph after nephrectomy — Actually: only if tear repaired
  • Trap: scrotal approach to testicular mass — Actually: inguinal
  • Trap: hockey-stick Gibson safer — Actually: higher hernia rates
  • Trap: wound protectors cut infection in cystectomy — Actually: general surgery only
  • Trap: transcostal spares rib — Actually: supracostal spares rib
  • Trap: Lambda perineal is superior — Actually: more wound complications
  • Trap: routine drains after RARP (robotic-assisted radical prostatectomy) or partial nephrectomy — Actually: little evidence; discretion
  • Trap: Penrose default drain — Actually: closed suction; Penrose for scrotum
  • Trap: paramedian as safe midline alternative — Actually: nerve injury, ventral hernia
