Chapter 65 — Adolescent and Transitional Urology — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else
  1. Start transition ~age 12 (AAP); transfer 18–21; complete 18–23; latest 26
  2. Paediatric team initiates; Three Ps: Preparation, Plan, named Provider
  3. Transfer ≠ transition; outcome metric = stability + retention in follow-up
  4. No transition during crisis; exception pregnancy → co-management
  5. Ileal augment: annual B12 from 5–10 y; bicarbonate/chloride
  6. After cystoplasty: urine hCG 57–75 % false + → serum hCG
  7. Cancer: ileal ~1.2 %, gastric 2.8–10.3 %; cystoscopy only if symptoms/filling defect
  8. Perforation 0.8–13 %; mortality ≤ 25 % if delayed
  9. SBA (Spina Bifida Association): annual urinalysis, creatinine, renal and bladder ultrasound (RBUS); cystatin C if low muscle mass
  10. Bladder exstrophy–epispadias complex (BEEC) / cloaca / urinary reconstruction → planned caesarean
  11. Bladder re-evaluation at transplant listing is mandatory; first graft left pelvis
  12. SB: normal IQ; ↓ working memory + executive function

What constitutes adolescent urology?

  • Adult reconstructive urologist: operatively capable; underequipped for whole patient
  • Aim: seamless lifelong care; adolescent urology = bridge

Definition of transition

  • Got Transition: child/family-centred → adult/patient-centred care
  • Three parts: planning → transfer → integration

Why is transition needed?

  • AHRQ (Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality): "no significant difference", but metrics inadequate
  • Avoid "normal": patient = own baseline

Who needs transition?

  • 1. Complex congenital (exstrophy, posterior urethral valves, complex hypospadias, SB) → lifelong
  • 2. Quiescent (vesicoureteric reflux, VUR) → may be discharged, open return
  • 3. Cured, treatment sequelae (pelvic rhabdomyosarcoma) → late-effects surveillance
  • 4. Beyond paediatric scope → fertility, pregnancy

What happens when transition fails?

  • Failure → non-adherence, ↑ emergency department visits, admissions, morbidity, mortality

Transition as a process

  • Six core elements: policy 12–14 → tracking/readiness/planning 14–18 → transfer 18–21 → complete 18–23
  • Readiness tool: TRAQ (Transition Readiness Assessment Questionnaire)
  • Top preparation element: how/where to find valid information
  • Private time alone → sex, sexual function, sexual debut
  • Programmes: Good 2 Go (Toronto), Ready Steady Go (UK), Got Transition (US)
  • Best programme = the one that fits the unit
  • Age 26 = US limit on parent's insurance
  • No independent living → plan medical custodianship

Systems and models of care

  • Routes: transition (1) + new adolescent problem (3) ideal; no-transition referral (2) suboptimal; emergency after discharge (4) worst
  • Lifelong paediatric urologist → suits limited life expectancy; fails when patient outlives provider
  • Integrated paediatric + adult dept → joint clinics; barriers: cost, geography, privileging
  • Direct transfer, no plan → invisible until emergency
  • Multidisciplinary team (MDT) backbone: nursing
  • Most complex MDT needs: neurological disease (SB)

Developmental considerations

  • Genital surgery windows: < 6 months (both); 2–3 y (theory) vs 4th–5th y (AAP 1975)
  • Stages: bonding year 1; individuation 18–30 mo; phallic–oedipal 3–6 y
  • Augmentation cystoplasty: child helps catheterise + wants to be dry
  • Exstrophy closure (non-elective) → as soon as safe
  • Self-management components (4): adherence, responsibility, self-care skills, knowledge
  • Medical post-traumatic stress disorder: no adult congenital urology data; cognitive-behavioural therapy mitigates (cardiac data)

The practicalities: setting up a service

  • Training pathway: none defined; usually paediatric + reconstructive
  • Core MDT: paediatric + transitional urologist, nurses, coordinator, nephrologist, psychologist
  • Biggest adult-provider barrier: unfamiliarity with the condition
  • Paediatric provider: "Am I the best person?"

Service logistics (cont.)

  • Barriers 3–4: institution (apathy, "not profitable") → infrastructure, philanthropy; health system (cost, inertia) → patients + marketing
  • Hub-and-spoke ideal; telehealth limited by dual state licensing, no examination
  • Success metric proposed: one attended post-transfer visit (AHRQ: no definition)

Male genital reconstruction

  • Adult hypospadias repair complications: 10.1–37.5 % first; 27.5–63.6 % redo
  • After paediatric repair: cosmesis dissatisfied 17–36 %; curvature ~12 %; ED ~19 %
  • Proximal: ~half revised by puberty (median 16.5 y) → follow into adulthood
  • Recurrent chordee: plication alone ~25 % vs ventral lengthening 5 %
  • BEEC: dorsal chordee near-universal; full correction rare (diastasis, corporal disproportion)
  • Phalloplasty + prosthesis: ~50 % complications
  • Hypospadias paternity HR 0.79; semen impaired mainly with undescended testis (UDT) (↑ FSH)
  • BEEC male: retrograde ejaculation ~half; > half azoospermic; 70.6 % of triers succeed, half with assisted reproduction (ART)
  • Regret drivers: number of operations + final appearance, not meatal position; 54.7 % mild, 12 % moderate–strong
  • BEEC HRQoL = general population; tracks continence

Female BEEC / cloaca / anorectal malformation (ARM)

  • Cloaca: void 46 %; incontinent 23 %; haematometra 38 %; primary amenorrhoea 25 %
  • Cloaca: chronic kidney disease (CKD) ~half; end-stage (ESRD) 17 %; worst = solitary kidney + reflux
  • BEEC sex: dyspareunia ½; FSFI dysfunction ⅓; 78 % satisfied; cloaca 42 % sexually active
  • BEEC fertility: 68 % conceive; 21 % without ART in year 1
  • ARM: Müllerian anomaly > ½; commonest uterus didelphys; ≥ ½ need ART
  • BEEC pregnancy: loss ~20 %; fetal loss 5–7 %; UTI ⅓–⅔; breech (wide pelvis)
  • Reflux nephropathy pregnancy: pregnancy-induced hypertension OR 5.55; pre-eclampsia OR 6.04; miscarriage 11–23 %
  • Pelvic organ prolapse 18–52 %; acute postpartum in ½; pregnancy → pessary/bed rest

Reconstruction and diversion

  • Appendicovesicostomy ~50 % fewer revisions than Monti; Mitrofanoff revision 30–50 %
  • Stenosis commonest at skin; fascia/valve → perforation
  • Can't catheterise: overdistension kink → urethral/floppy-tip → needle via old SP site
  • Conduit stomal complications 18–35 % (≤ 34.4 %); parastomal recurrence ≤ 56 % → keyhole/Sugarbaker mesh
  • Ureteroileal stricture ~10 % → endoscopic first
  • BEEC after bladder neck reconstruction: 59 % void; 64 % of those dry
  • Stones up to 40 % (2 % with dependent drainage); < 1.5 cm via channel; > 2 cm/multiple open
  • 10–12 Fr catheters too small to irrigate; irrigation ≥ 240 mL
  • Gastric segment → alkalosis; ileum/colon → hyperchloraemic acidosis
  • Multiply operated: 91.5 % complication risk

Surveillance and UTI

  • SB CKD ¼–½; only 62 % surveyed within 2 y
  • Highest CKD risk: congenital obstructive nephropathy; PUV decline late adolescence
  • SBA: no routine urodynamics after childhood; creatinine overestimates GFR if non-ambulant
  • Refer to nephrology: eGFR ≤ 30; loss ≥ 5/y; ACR ≥ 300 mg/g; only after urological optimisation
  • "Tempered" MAG3 = catheter in; not routine in adults
  • ISC bacteriuria 50–100 %; treat symptoms only; > 4 UTIs/y → investigate
  • Same organism → source; different → reinoculation

ESRD and transplant

  • Congenital anomalies of kidney and urinary tract (CAKUT) = 34–43 % paediatric ESRD; posterior urethral valves (PUV) 23 % ESRD by median 31
  • PUV: ≤ ¼ decline post-puberty; ≤ 75 % bladder dysfunction
  • Polyuria mainstay: overnight catheter; native nephrectomy discouraged
  • Native nephrectomy: pyelonephritis, stones, malignant HTN, flank pain, nephrotic
  • Peritoneal dialysis relative contraindications: adhesions, ventriculoperitoneal shunt
  • Transplant work-up: cystoscopy + video urodynamics; willingness to self-catheterise
  • Post-transplant pyelonephritis 0.49 vs 0.31–0.44/patient-year; graft 92.3 % at 5 y
  • BEEC: avoid hypogastric artery → hemi-penile ED; VACTERL → MRA/CTA
Classic traps
  • Trap: AHRQ shows transition futile — Actually: poor metrics; stability = success
  • Trap: transfer = transition — Actually: transfer one step; complete 18–23
  • Trap: all need lifelong follow-up — Actually: quiescent (VUR) may be discharged
  • Trap: MDT need = lifelong need — Actually: exstrophy lifelong, urologist-led
  • Trap: new adolescent problem suboptimal — Actually: route 3 ideal
  • Trap: lifelong paediatric urologist best — Actually: fails as patient outlives provider
  • Trap: elective timing follows theory — Actually: school, parental anxiety, surgeon
  • Trap: weak processing → parent consents — Actually: capacity present; support consent
  • Trap: confidentiality reviewed once — Actually: with family, then alone
  • Trap: adult team starts transition — Actually: paediatric team initiates
  • Trap: annual screening cystoscopy after cystoplasty — Actually: symptoms or filling defect only
  • Trap: positive urine hCG after augment = pregnant — Actually: check serum hCG
  • Trap: new continence is good news — Actually: hostile bladder; investigate
  • Trap: treat all bacteriuria on ISC — Actually: symptomatic only
  • Trap: creatinine reliable in wheelchair users — Actually: add cystatin C
  • Trap: native nephrectomy for polyuria — Actually: overnight catheter first
  • Trap: regret tracks severity — Actually: more with distal; driven by number of operations
