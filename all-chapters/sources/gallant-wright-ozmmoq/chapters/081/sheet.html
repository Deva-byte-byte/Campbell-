Chapter 81 — Minimally Invasive RPLND — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else

  • MI-RPLND must duplicate open RPLND — approach never alters extent.
  • Postchemotherapy standard: bilateral full template + nerve-sparing.
  • Stage I / small-volume II: extended unilateral nerve-sparing template acceptable.
  • Boundaries: renal hila ↑, ureters laterally, ureter–common iliac crossing ↓.
  • Lumbar vessels must be divided → retroaortic/retrocaval clearance.
  • Conversion ≈5%; bleeding = unique MI complication.
  • Antegrade ejaculation ≥90% after primary MI-RPLND.
  • No absolute contraindications to MI-RPLND.
  • RFS ≈85% limited pN+ without adjuvant chemo; ≈80% any pN1–3.
  • Always therapeutic intent — never a staging biopsy.

Indications and context

  • RPLND (retroperitoneal lymph node dissection), chemo-naive: marker-negative stage I NSGCT (nonseminomatous germ cell tumour); small-volume stage II seminoma/NSGCT.
  • Postchemotherapy: NSGCT residual mass >1 cm; select seminoma.
  • 20–30% with enlarged nodes are pN0 → chemo avoided.
  • First laparoscopic RPLND 1992; first robotic 2006.
  • Laparoscopic vs open: ↓ stay, ↓ blood loss, ↑ operative time.

Guidelines

  • NCCN (National Comprehensive Cancer Network): silent.
  • AUA (American Urological Association): may offer — GCT + MIS expertise; no long-term data.
  • EAU (European Association of Urology): specialist centres only, expert in open + MIS.

Controversy

  • Most controversial operation in urological oncology; no level 1 data.
  • pN0 rates up to 83% in MI series → selection concern.
  • Stage IA → observe, except teratoma with somatic transformation.
  • No large series >5 years; atypical relapse incl. peritoneal carcinomatosis.

Surgical technique

  • Imaging ≤≈4 weeks, markers ≤1–2 weeks preoperatively.
  • Enlarged nodes intraoperatively → consider bilateral full template.
  • GETUG T93BP (BEP ×3 vs EP ×4): relapses 70%, deaths 50% in unoperated/incomplete RPLND.
  • Nerve-sparing: postganglionic fibres L1–L4 → hypogastric plexus below IMA (inferior mesenteric artery).
  • ≈3 efferent fibres per sympathetic chain.

Perioperative preparation

  • Safety suture: 4-0/5-0 Prolene, RB needle, LAPRA-TY ≈4 inches.
  • Prior bleomycin → review fluids and oxygenation.
  • Open set in theatre; second time-out.

Patient and tumour factors

  • Relative contraindications: extensive prior abdominal surgery; morbid obesity.
  • MI candidate stage II: 1–2 nodes, primary landing zone, none >3 cm.
  • Ideal post-chemo MI: solitary landing-zone mass, small response (teratoma).
  • Open if: post-chemo, large residual mass, big size change (desmoplasia), vessel resection.
  • MI less suitable: somatic malignancy; retrocrural disease.

Counselling and preparation

  • High-fat diet day before → lymphatics visible.
  • No full bowel prep; ERAS (Enhanced Recovery After Surgery) + carbohydrate loading.
  • Lumbar control: Weck clip vessel side, sealer specimen side.

Positioning

  • Modified lateral decubitus 45° — unilateral; ports as renal, more caudal.
  • Laparoscopic supine: 4 × 12-mm trocars, first 1 cm below xiphoid.
  • Steep Trendelenburg: camera 4–6 cm below umbilicus; bilateral without repositioning.
  • Docking: Si over left shoulder; Xi from side.

Template dissection

  • Right: Toldt → foramen of Winslow; Kocherise duodenum.
  • Right hazard: right renal artery in interaortocaval space; accessory lower-pole arteries.
  • Left: medialise spleen + pancreatic tail; gonadal vein divided at left renal vein.
  • Left renal vein = lymphatic hotspot → clip liberally.
  • Fascial closure: extraction site + ports ≥10 mm; no routine drain.
  • Bag packets (Endo Catch) immediately.

Adverse events

  • Bleed: pressure + ↑ pneumoperitoneum → prepare → undock, convert.
  • Blood on lens → single-eye setting or move camera.
  • No blind clipping or suturing.
  • Lymph leaks may hide even at low pressure.

Postoperative and outcomes

  • Discharge 1–2 days.
  • Chylous ascites ≈ open rates.
  • Open node-yield benchmark ≈30.
  • Post-chemo MI data: small series — no conclusions.
  • miR-371a-3p → future selection for RPLND and adjuvant chemo.

Classic traps

  • Trap: conversion = complication — Actually: may be elective, for safety or oncology.
  • Trap: low MI recurrence proves equivalence — Actually: confounded by pN0 + adjuvant chemo.
  • Trap: pIIA after RPLND → adjuvant chemo — Actually: observation preferred.
  • Trap: stage IA → RPLND — Actually: observe (except somatic transformation).
  • Trap: MI allows smaller template — Actually: extent identical to open.
  • Trap: sealers secure lymphatics — Actually: unreliable; clip.
  • Trap: MI contraindicated post-chemo — Actually: no absolute contraindication; open favoured.
  • Trap: guidelines endorse MI — Actually: NCCN silent; AUA/EAU conditional.
  • Trap: lowering pressure excludes leak — Actually: low-pressure lymphatics may still hide.
  • Trap: laparoscopic staging RPLND adequate — Actually: no lumbar division → dual therapy.
