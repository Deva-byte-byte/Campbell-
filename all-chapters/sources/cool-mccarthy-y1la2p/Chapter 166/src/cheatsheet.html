Chapter 166 — Management of Castration-Resistant Prostate Cancer

If you read nothing else
  • Castrate testosterone <50 ng/dL; continue LHRH agent indefinitely in CRPC.
  • nmCRPC treat if PSADT ≤10 mo → apalutamide, enzalutamide or darolutamide.
  • Abiraterone 1000 mg fasting + prednisone; enzalutamide needs no steroid.
  • Docetaxel 75 mg/m² 3-weekly = first-line chemotherapy (TAX 327).
  • Cabazitaxel 20 mg/m² post-docetaxel; beats second ARPI (CARD).
  • ARPI → ARPI works in only 15–30%: switch mechanism.
  • Radium-223: symptomatic bone-only; never with abiraterone.
  • 177Lu-PSMA-617 after ARPI + taxane; PSMA PET-positive (VISION).
  • PARPI benefit greatest in BRCA2; rucaparib not for ATM.
  • Cord compression → IV dexamethasone → MRI → surgery + RT.

Clinical considerations

  • Framework: PCWG3 clinical states model, not TNM.
  • First sign of progression: usually rising PSA — but not after early ARPI (ARCHES).
  • Antiandrogen withdrawal: stop first-generation antiandrogen, watch PSA 4–8 weeks.
  • Check testosterone: suspected non-compliance; antiandrogen, oestrogen or 5α-reductase-inhibitor monotherapy.
  • PSADT <3 mo → aggressive course → treat aggressively.
  • Bone pain or visceral-predominant → upfront chemotherapy.
  • Somatic NGS: tumour tissue (usually primary); liquid biopsy false-positives from CHIP.
  • Germline testing: all metastatic; BRCA/Lynch/HOXB13 family history; Ashkenazi.

Nonmetastatic CRPC

  • Predictors: PSA kinetics; baseline PSA >10 ng/mL + PSA velocity.
  • Natural history: ~⅓ bone metastasis at 2 yr; median ~30 mo.
  • Approved: apalutamide (first), enzalutamide, darolutamide. Abiraterone not approved.

Metastatic CRPC

  • Visceral <20%; nodal 30–40%; bone overwhelmingly commonest.
  • Preferred end point: PFS (radiographic), not response rate.
  • Commonest haematological problem: anaemia; avoid ESAs (↑ mortality).
  • Thrombocytosis: clotting rare; no treatment.
  • Obstructed kidneys: stent/nephrostomy before renally cleared chemotherapy.

Evaluation of treatment efficacy

  • PCWG3 bone scan: new baseline at 12–16 wk; progression = ≥2 new lesions.
  • Flare: PSA (radium-223, Lu-PSMA), bone scan, PSMA PET (~13%, resolves by 3 mo).
  • Soft tissue: RECIST.
  • No PSA fall expected with sipuleucel-T or radium-223.

Neuroendocrine / aggressive variant

  • Suspect: low/undetectable PSA + rapid visceral, lytic, brain or bulky pelvic disease.
  • Hypercalcaemia via PTHrP; low PSMA; chromogranin A not required.
  • Molecular: ≥2 of PTEN loss, TP53 mutation, RB1 loss.
  • Treat: cisplatin–etoposide or taxane–carboplatin ± RT; biopsy first.
  • Radiotherapy: brain metastases, bulky or critical-site disease.
  • Survival <12 mo.

Androgen signaling inhibitors

  • Adrenal androgens: up to 10% of total; untouched by LHRH agents.
  • Only approved CYP17 inhibitors: abiraterone (and Yonsa); orteronel failed.
  • Abiraterone toxicity: hypokalaemia, hypertension, oedema, LFTs; CV mortality. Correct K⁺ first.
  • Abiraterone with food: up to 10× exposure; alternative 250 mg with low-fat meal.
  • COU-AA-301 (post-docetaxel): OS 14.8 vs 10.9 mo, HR 0.65.
  • COU-AA-302 (pre-chemo): rPFS ↑; chemo delayed 26.5 vs 16.8 mo.
  • AFFIRM (post-docetaxel): OS 18.4 vs 13.6 mo, HR 0.63.
  • PREVAIL (pre-chemo): death HR 0.71; radiographic progression HR 0.19.
  • SPARTAN: MFS HR 0.28; OS 73.9 vs 59.9 mo.
  • ARAMIS: MFS 40.4 vs 18.4 mo; OS HR 0.69.
  • PROSPER: death risk ↓27%.
  • Doses: enzalutamide 160 mg; apalutamide 240 mg; darolutamide 300 mg twice daily.
  • Enzalutamide: seizures ~1%; worse with prednisone.
  • Enzalutamide interactions: avoid CYP2C8 inhibitors; lowers CYP3A4/2C9/2C19 substrates.
  • Apalutamide: rash, hypothyroidism. Apalutamide/darolutamide: no BBB penetration.
  • Falls/fractures: caution >75 yr.
  • After docetaxel → ARPI; after ARPI → chemo/radium/sipuleucel-T; after both → radioligand or cabazitaxel.

Novel AR-targeting approaches

  • PROTAC degraders: ARV-110 (PSA50 in T878A/S, H875Y); ARV-766 (L702H).

Cytotoxic chemotherapy

  • TAX 327: OS 18.9 (3-weekly) vs 17.3 (weekly) vs 16.4 mo (mitoxantrone); HR 0.76 — first survival gain.
  • Docetaxel schedules: 3-weekly → neutropenia; weekly → nausea, nails, lacrimation, diarrhoea.
  • SWOG 9916: + estramustine → thrombosis, no added benefit.
  • TROPIC: cabazitaxel vs mitoxantrone OS 15.1 vs 12.7 mo, HR 0.70.
  • TROPIC subgroup: benefit even if progression <3 mo from docetaxel start → little cross-resistance.
  • Cabazitaxel: poor P-gp affinity; neutropenia 82%; G-CSF; little neuropathy.
  • PROSELICA: 20 mg/m² non-inferior to 25.
  • FIRSTANA: cabazitaxel not better than docetaxel first line.
  • CARD: OS 13.6 vs 11.0 mo, HR 0.64.
  • Mitoxantrone (1997): palliation only, no survival gain.
  • Platinum: neuroendocrine; germline BRCA2 (docetaxel–carboplatin).
  • mCSPC triplets: ADT + docetaxel + abiraterone (PEACE-1) or darolutamide (ARASENS).
  • Failed with docetaxel: anti-angiogenics, atrasentan, zibotentan, dasatinib, calcitriol.

Radiopharmaceuticals

  • Radium-223: alpha, path <100 µm; 50 kBq/kg 4-weekly × 6.
  • ALSYMPCA: OS HR 0.70, +3.6 mo; SSE HR 0.66; fewer AEs than placebo.
  • Radium eligibility: no visceral disease, no bulky nodes.
  • Radium + any ARPI → add zoledronic acid or denosumab.
  • TheraP: Lu-PSMA vs cabazitaxel PSA response 65% vs 37%; no OS difference.
  • VISION: rPFS 8.7 vs 3.4 mo (HR 0.40); OS 15.3 vs 11.3 mo (HR 0.62).
  • Lu-PSMA toxicity: fatigue, dry mouth, nausea, anaemia.
  • Lu-PSMA FDA 2022: post-ARPI and taxane; PSMAfore tests pre-chemotherapy.

PARP inhibitors

  • Somatic DNA-repair mutations: 20–25% of mCRPC.
  • PROfound (olaparib, BRCA/ATM): rPFS 7.4 vs 3.6 mo, HR 0.34; FDA May 2020, broad HRR panel.
  • Rationale for ARPI + PARPI: ADT upregulates PARP-dependent repair.
  • TRITON3 (rucaparib, BRCA): rPFS 11.2 vs 6.4 mo; ATM HR 0.95.
  • PROpel (olaparib + abiraterone): OS 42.1 vs 34.7 mo — longest first-line median.
  • MAGNITUDE (niraparib + abiraterone): BRCA rPFS 19.5 vs 10.9 mo; no benefit HRR-negative.
  • TALAPRO-2 (talazoparib + enzalutamide): all-comers rPFS HR 0.63; approved HRR-mutated.
  • GALAHAD (niraparib): single-arm; BRCA rPFS 8.2 mo.
  • Class toxicity: anaemia, nausea, fatigue; rarely MDS, PE.
  • ORR (RECIST CR + PR): commonest basis for FDA solid-tumour approval.

Novel molecular approaches

  • ATM-deficient: ATR inhibitors (synthetic lethality) rather than PARPI.

Immunotherapy

  • IMPACT (sipuleucel-T): OS 25.8 vs 21.7 mo, HR 0.78; no PFS/PSA change.
  • Sipuleucel-T: autologous dendritic cells + PAP–GM-CSF; FDA April 2010; 64% placebo crossover.
  • Sipuleucel-T eligibility: asymptomatic/minimally symptomatic; no visceral mets; no opioids.
  • Ipilimumab phase III ×2: no OS gain (HR 0.85; HR 1.11).
  • Pembrolizumab: MSI-H/dMMR or TMB ≥10/Mb; 200 mg 3-weekly; dMMR 2–5%.
  • dMMR testing: NGS or IHC for MSH2, MSH6, MLH1, PMS2.
  • dMMR found → germline testing for Lynch syndrome.
  • Ipilimumab irAE: colitis 15–20% commonest.

Emerging approaches

  • BAT: testosterone cypionate 400 mg IM 4-weekly; not if opioid pain or bulky visceral disease.
  • TRANSFORMER: BAT = enzalutamide for PFS; PFS2 28.2 vs 19.6 mo BAT-first.
  • PSMA BiTEs: dose-limiting CRS → tocilizumab.
  • PTEN loss ~40%; IPATential150 benefit only PTEN-deficient.

Palliative management

  • Dexamethasone: 16–100 mg/day; load 10 mg IV (20–40 mg PO), then 4–10 mg 6–12-hourly; taper 2–3 wk.
  • Imaging: spinal MRI (replaces myelography).
  • Operate if: progression on RT, unstable fracture, post-RT recurrence; surgery + RT > RT alone (Patchell).
  • Zoledronate: 4 mg IV 4-weekly, renally adjusted; Ca 1000 mg + vitamin D 800 IU.
  • Denosumab: 120 mg SC 4-weekly; no renal adjustment; ONJ 2–4%.
  • Denosumab vs zoledronate: first SRE 20.7 vs 17.1 mo; no OS difference.
  • ONJ risk: dental work, poor dentition — avoid zoledronate.
  • Hypercalcaemia rare → think neuroendocrine (PTHrP).
  • Bone agents: reduce SREs; no antitumour or survival effect.
  • Hydronephrosis: drainage not needed if contralateral kidney adequate.
  • Circumoral numbness → skull base → radiotherapy.
Classic traps
  • Trap: CRPC is androgen independent — Actually: AR-driven; continue ADT.
  • Trap: falling PSA = response — Actually: may be neuroendocrine transformation.
  • Trap: new bone-scan lesions at 12 wk = progression — Actually: may be flare; confirm.
  • Trap: abiraterone for nmCRPC — Actually: not approved; use apa/enza/daro.
  • Trap: radium-223 + abiraterone — Actually: ↑ deaths and fractures.
  • Trap: second ARPI after first — Actually: cross-resistance; cabazitaxel wins (CARD).
  • Trap: cabazitaxel first line — Actually: not superior to docetaxel (FIRSTANA).
  • Trap: mitoxantrone prolongs survival — Actually: palliation only.
  • Trap: PARPI for ATM — Actually: weak benefit; rucaparib BRCA only.
  • Trap: sipuleucel-T with visceral disease — Actually: not indicated; expect no PSA fall.
