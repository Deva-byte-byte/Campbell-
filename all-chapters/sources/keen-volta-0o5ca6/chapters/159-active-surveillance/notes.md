# Chapter 159 — Active Surveillance of Prostate Cancer

- **A two-decade reversal:** 10–15 years ago over 90% of men in the United States, Canada and Europe with favourable-risk prostate cancer were treated radically; in some regions over 90% now start with **active surveillance (AS)**.
- **Most centres manage at least half of low-risk patients with AS**, plus growing numbers of intermediate-risk patients; variation between regions, practice types and clinicians remains large.
- **Drivers:** overtreatment concern (notably the United States Preventive Services Task Force, USPSTF); the **favourable molecular genetics of Gleason pattern 3**; long-term prospective surveillance data; better detection of aggressive disease by **imaging and biomarkers**; modelling favouring AS; above all, **patient acceptance**.

## THE OVERDIAGNOSIS AND OVERTREATMENT PROBLEM

- **Sensitive testing breeds incidental diagnosis and overdiagnosis**, often through screening, in many organs.

<p class="tcap">Overdiagnosis across organs if the entire reservoir were detected</p>

| Organ | Estimated overdiagnosis | Microfocal prevalence in healthy population |
|---|---|---|
| Prostate | 87–94% | 30–70% |
| Thyroid | 99.7–99.9% | 36–100% |
| Breast | 43–90% | 7–39% |

- **Pattern:** overdiagnosis tracks the **size of the latent reservoir**, amplified by screening's bias towards **slower-growing cancers**.
- **Why prostate is especially exposed:** a **large latent reservoir** growing with age; **prostate-specific antigen (PSA)** is a cheap, simple blood test, so the **threshold to test is low**; treatment has **significant transient and permanent side effects** on quality of life (QoL).
- **PSA trade-off:** finds potentially lethal cancer at a **more curable stage** than clinical diagnosis and has **likely lowered mortality**, at the cost of diagnosing and treating **clinically insignificant cancer** before its natural history is clear.
- **USPSTF on PSA screening:** **2012 Grade D** (harms outweigh benefits: overdiagnosis, overtreatment) → **2018 Grade C (neutral)**, largely because **AS uptake reduced overtreatment**.

<p class="tcap">Active surveillance versus watchful waiting</p>

| | Active surveillance | Watchful waiting |
|---|---|---|
| Candidate | **Fit for radical therapy if needed** | **Limited life expectancy** (age, comorbidity) |
| Intent | **Curative, deferred** | **Palliative** |
| Monitoring | **Close:** serial PSA, digital rectal examination (DRE), MRI, biopsy | **Limited:** infrequent PSA and clinical review; **no biopsy or imaging** |
| Action | **Definitive therapy on reclassification** to higher risk | **Palliation** of symptomatic or advanced disease |
| Transition | **To watchful waiting** once life expectancy is <5–8 years (typically ~80 years, earlier with comorbidity) | — |

- **Trap:** outcomes from older randomised trials and registries of simple observation reflect **watchful waiting**, not contemporary AS, in which many are reclassified and treated, probably with better results.
- **Rationale of AS:** **spares radical-therapy side effects** in indolent, stable or regressive disease; treats those reclassified, usually on **higher grade at later biopsy**. It rests on two premises: a **long lead time to clinical progression**, and treatment still **within the window of curability** at the first sign of higher risk.

<p class="concept"><b>Concept —</b> Active surveillance defers curative intent; watchful waiting abandons it. Every difference in monitoring and trigger follows from whether the patient could still benefit from cure.</p>

## CHARACTERISTICS OF CLINICALLY INSIGNIFICANT PROSTATE CANCER

- **Autopsy prevalence of microfocal cancer ≈ age as a percentage:** 30% in the 30s, 50% in the 50s, **≥80% over 80**.
- **PSA plus systematic biopsy detects cancer in 15–25%** (threshold-dependent); **lifetime risk of prostate cancer death is 3%** (North America).
- **Harbouring : death ≈ 20:1; diagnosis : death ≈ 6:1.** Most diagnosed men will not die of it and would never have been diagnosed without PSA: these **"clinically insignificant" cancers** constitute **overdiagnosis**.
- **Detection is procedure-driven:** 50–60% of men of biopsy age harbour microfocal cancer, so a **lower PSA threshold, more cores and more repeat sessions** find more insignificant cancer.
- **The challenge** is assigning an individual cancer to low risk (→ **AS**) or higher risk (→ **definitive therapy**). PSA, stage and grade help; **Gleason grade group is by far the most powerful predictor**.

<p class="tcap">Grade terminology</p>

| Category | Grade group | Gleason score |
|---|---|---|
| Low | **1** | 3+3 = 6 |
| Intermediate | 2 / 3 | **3+4 / 4+3 = 7** |
| High | 4 / 5 | 8 / 9–10 |

- **AS manages insignificant disease conservatively and reserves treatment for significant disease**, usually **grade group 1**; higher grades may still be insignificant given health and life expectancy. **Once cured by treatment, a cancer's significance can never be known.**

### Molecular Genetics of Gleason Pattern 3 Versus Patterns 4 and 5

- **Gleason grading separates genetically near-normal from abnormal cells** remarkably well; the hallmarks of cancer differ profoundly between **pattern 3 and pattern 4**, explaining their different potential.
- **Six hallmarks:** sustained proliferative signalling, invasion and metastasis, angiogenesis, evasion of growth suppressors, resistance to cell death, unlimited replication. **Pattern 3 is molecularly near-normal; patterns 4 and 5 are significantly altered**, especially in key oncogenic pathways.

<p class="tcap">Table 159.1 Gleason 3 vs. 4 and Hallmarks of Cancer</p>

| Hallmark | Pathway or marker | Gleason 3 | Gleason 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proliferative signalling | EGF/EGFR (epidermal growth factor/receptor) | No | **Overexpressed** |
| | AKT, MAP2 kinase | No | **Aberrant** |
| | HER2/neu | No | **Amplified** |
| Invasion and metastasis | Invasion/metastasis markers | Normal | **Overexpressed** |
| Insensitivity to growth inhibition | Cyclin D2 | Expressed | **Absent** |
| Resisting apoptosis | BCL2 | Negative | **Strong** |
| Angiogenesis | VEGF (vascular endothelial growth factor), microvessel density | Low | **Increased** |
| Metabolism | Reverse Warburg effect | Absent | **Present** |
| Absence of senescence | TMPRSS2-ERG | ERG normal | **Increased** |
| Tumour suppression | PTEN | **Present in 90%** | **Deleted in 70–90%** |
| Clinical | Metastasis/mortality after surgical grading | **Absent** | **Present** |

<p class="tsrc">Data from Ross et al., 2011, 2012; Skacel et al., 2001; True et al., 2006; Susaki and Nakayama, 2007; Padar et al., 2003; Fleischmann et al., 2012; West et al., 2001; Erbersdobler et al., 2010; Georgescu et al., 2016; Bismar et al., 2012; Wang et al., 2006; Trock et al., 2016; Hubbard et al., 2016.</p>

- **What it is for:** pattern 3 **lacks almost every hallmark**. **Pattern:** every row normal in 3, abnormal in 4; cyclin D2 is the one abnormality of **loss**.
- **Trap — PTEN deletion and TMPRSS2-ERG are not exclusive to pattern 4:** rarer in pattern 3 (PTEN deleted in ~10%) but **commoner in pattern 3 cells coexisting with pattern 4** (Gleason 7): a **field defect** or **rapid dedifferentiation**. **PTEN loss alone is not metastatic; MYC amplification plus PTEN deletion** drives genomic instability and metastasis, and **both may be required**.

*Natural history of grade group 1.*

- **Metastatic potential effectively zero:** in large surgical series of Gleason 6, **every nodal metastasis had higher grade on re-review**; with surgically confirmed pure Gleason 6 the nodal rate was **zero**, and 20-year mortality negligible, the few deaths again showing **higher grade on re-review**.
- **But local invasion occurs:** occasional **extraprostatic extension**, **seminal vesicle invasion exceptional**; grade group 2 shows substantially more. This is **one argument for keeping the word "cancer"**.
- **Spontaneous grade progression is uncommon (~1–2% per year)**, mostly in **high-volume Gleason 6**; **10–20% progress biologically after 10 years**, so follow-up is **long term**. Progression need not mean intervention: 3+4 at 80 after surveillance from 65 may never threaten life.
- **Metastases in low-risk series = occult higher grade missed at biopsy**, present in **~30%** diagnosed on 10–12-core systematic biopsy: **85% 3+4, 10% 4+3, 5% ≥8**.
- **MRI, initial vs confirmation biopsy:** MRI finds more higher-grade cancer at **initial** biopsy, but not necessarily at **confirmation**: in the **ASIST trial** (grade group 1, MRI-targeted plus systematic vs systematic alone), upgrading to grade group ≥2 was **33% vs 27%, not significant**.
- **The 2005 Gleason revision** regraded many 6s as 7s: **stage migration** improved cancer-specific survival (**~26%**) and helps explain metastases in older watchful-waiting series.

<p class="kpt">Key points</p>

- Microfocal pattern 3 occurs in most men with age, in all races and regions.
- Pattern 3 has a very favourable mutational burden: oncogenic pathways normal in 3, aberrant in 4 and 5.
- Gleason score 6 (grade group 1) may invade locally but has no metastatic potential.
- Deaths in conservatively managed low-risk men reflect higher-grade cancer missed at diagnostic biopsy.

### Natural History

- **Evidence spans pre-PSA and PSA eras**, so read it against its diagnostic pathway: PSA-screened cohorts carry **5–10 years' lead-time bias**; most lacked **modern AS** (close monitoring, serial biopsy); apparent low-risk progression reflects **25–30% occult higher grade**.
- **Longest follow-up, a Swedish cohort over 32 years** (mostly >70 at diagnosis, nearly all now dead): progression rate **stable for many years**; the **post-15-year rise in mortality is a small-numbers artefact**; no close monitoring.

<p class="tcap">The three randomised trials of intervention versus observation</p>

| | SPCG-4 (Scandinavian Prostate Cancer Group 4) | PIVOT (Prostate Cancer Intervention versus Observation Trial) | ProtecT (Prostate Testing for Cancer and Treatment) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Setting | Scandinavia, **pre-PSA** | US Veterans Administration, 1994–2002 | UK, **all PSA-detected** |
| Population | 695, mean age 65; **5% PSA-detected, 88% palpable**, median PSA 13: **intermediate–high risk** | 731 (15% Black); median PSA 7.8, 50% T1c: **more favourable** | 1643; **>⅓ intermediate/high risk** |
| Arms | Prostatectomy vs watchful waiting | Prostatectomy vs observation | **Active monitoring vs prostatectomy vs radiotherapy** |
| Follow-up | 23 yr | Median 18.6 yr | 15 yr |
| Result | Prostate cancer death **19.6% vs 31.3%**, relative risk **0.55**; **+2.9 life-years** | All-cause death 68% vs 73%, hazard ratio (HR) **0.84**; absolute reduction 5.7 points (CI crosses zero); **~1 year gained** | **No difference in cancer mortality (all <3%) or survival**; metastases **9.4% vs 4.5% vs 5.0%** |
| Verdict | **Benefit in higher grade and age <65** | **Modest**, mainly intermediate risk | **Mortality-safe at 15 yr, more metastases** |

- **SPCG-4:** after prostatectomy, 3+3 and 3+4 had similar cancer mortality, **4+3 over five times** higher. Benefit was **concentrated under 65** (all-cause relative risk 0.62, cancer-specific 0.50; vs 0.86 [not significant] and 0.63 at ≥65); **absolute benefit more than doubled** from years 0–10 to 10–23; **confined to grade group ≥3, none in groups 1–2**, supporting surveillance for favourable intermediate risk.
- **PIVOT:** benefit mostly in **intermediate-risk, White, more-cores, healthier** men; low-risk observation cancer mortality **only 2.7%** (intermediate 11%, high 17%).
- **Cross-trial trap:** SPCG-4 *low-risk* and PIVOT *intermediate-risk* cancer mortality were equal (11%): **PSA stage migration, the 2005 Gleason change and lead time**, not biology.
- **ProtecT, closest to modern AS:** metastasis excess appeared by **10 years** (6.3 vs 2.4 and 3.0 per 1000 person-years) and **held at 15** (7.1 vs 3.5 and 3.7), yet **did not reach survival**, perhaps owing to **better systemic therapy**. Explanations: **23% intermediate/high grade** plus high PSAs (the **>⅓** suffices to explain it); **77% low grade** left it **underpowered** for mortality; **active monitoring ≠ AS** (no scheduled biopsy, no defined triggers). At 15 years **61%** of the monitoring arm had been treated and **24.4%** were alive and untreated; **QoL same or better in every domain**.

<p class="concept"><b>Concept —</b> Treatment benefit concentrates where grade is higher and the patient younger; the lower the risk, the smaller the benefit. That gradient is the evidential basis for surveillance.</p>

## OUTCOMES OF ACTIVE SURVEILLANCE

- **Ten prospective phase II series, ~10,000 men**, several over 15 years; the **Global Action Plan Prostate Cancer Active Surveillance (GAP3)** database pools **25 centres, >15,000 patients**.
- **Cancer mortality is rare in every cohort; cardiovascular disease is the commonest cause of death**; in Toronto (median 9 years) **non-prostate death was 10× prostate death**.

<p class="tcap">Inclusive versus restrictive selection</p>

| | Toronto (inclusive) | Johns Hopkins (restrictive) |
|---|---|---|
| Entry | **All Gleason 6 + selected intermediate risk** (Gleason 7 or PSA >10) | **Epstein criteria:** Gleason 6, **≤2 positive cores, no core >50%, PSA density <0.15** |
| Eligible at diagnosis | **~50%** | **~20%** |
| 15-yr cancer mortality | **5% (actuarial)** | **0.5%** |
| Risk signal | Intermediate vs low metastasis HR **3.75**; **baseline Gleason 7 → ≥20% metastasis at 15 yr** despite radical treatment on "progression"; PSA >10 barely predictive | — |
| Intervention | **~25% at 5 yr** | **~25% at 5 yr** |

- **Lesson:** inclusion criteria set mortality; **intervention is constant at ~25% at 5 years**.

- **What it is for:** cancer mortality **≤5% at 15 years** whatever the entry rules. **Pattern:** **other-cause mortality far exceeds cancer mortality**; freedom from treatment falls steadily, **about half untreated at 7–15 years**.
- **Trap — paired figures:** Toronto **5% vs 1.5%** is actuarial vs actual; Hopkins **0.1% vs 0.5%** is the table's 2020 data cut vs the text's 2015 report. Compare like with like, and adjust for **inclusion criteria**.
- **Consensus lies between the extremes:** **most Gleason 6 belong on AS** (not only Epstein-eligible); offer AS **selectively and cautiously for Gleason 7**. All mature cohorts **predate MRI and biomarkers**, which are broadening indications and may lower metastasis further.

- Three randomised trials show little or no benefit of intervention in low risk.

### Patient Selection

- **Most grade group 1 (3+3 = 6) should be managed with AS:** consensus of Cancer Care Ontario, the American Urological Association (AUA), European Association of Urology (EAU), American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO) and National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE).

<p class="tcap">Very low risk versus low risk</p>

| | Very low risk (Epstein) | Low risk |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | Grade group 1; **1–2 positive cores; no core >50%; PSA density <0.15** | **Any grade group 1, PSA <10** |
| Progression to treatment | **Lower** | Higher; tracks with intermediate risk |
| Progression to metastasis | **Exceptionally low** | **Exceptionally low** |

- **The split reflects the likelihood of occult higher grade** (and possibly later progression), **not volume-dependent metastatic potential**.
- **High-volume Gleason 6** (higher PSA density, more cores, greater core involvement; nomograms quantify it) raises the risk of coexistent higher grade. It **remains an AS candidate but needs scrutiny:** **multiparametric MRI (mpMRI) with targeted biopsy**, repeat extended biopsy and/or a **tissue genomic assay**.

- **What it is for:** **AS for low risk is unanimous**; divergence lies in **intermediate risk** and **MRI**. **Pattern:** older guidance reserves MRI for discordance; newer **builds it in** at enrolment or before biopsy. **Traps:** **only NICE** offers radical treatment and AS as **equal options** for intermediate risk; Cancer Care Ontario's "5-alpha-reductase inhibitors may have a role" is **not supported** by the chapter (see Pharmacologic Intervention).

### Watchful Waiting

- **AS presupposes fitness for radical therapy** (usually **life expectancy ≥10 years**), deferred only because disease is indolent.
- **Over 5 years only very aggressive high-grade disease kills**, so in elderly or comorbid men **watchful waiting** is preferred: infrequent PSA and clinical review, **no biopsy or imaging**, treatment only for **symptomatic progression** (rarely metastasis). Often used for **elderly or comorbid men with low–intermediate risk**.
- **Switch AS → watchful waiting when life expectancy is <5–10 years**, aided by age–comorbidity nomograms.
- **Pitfall — neuroendocrine dedifferentiation:** progression **without PSA rise**; rare with an intact gonadal axis, seen in elderly men with **unrecognised hypogonadism**. Treat **PSA as a guide**, **measure testosterone** if hypogonadism is possible, and obtain **interval imaging**.
- **No hard rule for the switch:** some with ~10 years' life expectancy still want treatment for significant disease; **"modus mortis"** (manner of death) motivates avoiding a cancer death; survival prediction is **inexact**; **improving systemic therapy** extends life after metastasis, which may weaken the case for definitive therapy. **Preference is central.**

<p class="concept"><b>Concept —</b> Life expectancy, not grade, chooses between surveillance and watchful waiting; the switch is a judgement because both life expectancy and the salvage value of systemic therapy keep moving.</p>

### Management of Patients on Active Surveillance

<p class="tcap">The two hazards of surveillance</p>

| | Misattribution of grade | True biological progression |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | **Occult higher grade missed by systematic biopsy** | Pattern 3 **becomes** 4 or 5 |
| Frequency | **Commonest:** ~**30%** of biopsy Gleason 6 upgraded at prostatectomy; similar with annual biopsy over 10 years | **Much less common** |
| Usual result | Mostly **grade group 2**; beyond group 3 rare | Many **3+4 remain indolent** |
| Remedy | **MRI, targeted biopsy**, clinical predictors | **Long-term surveillance** |

- **Predictors of occult high grade ("wolf in sheep's clothing"):** **PSA density**, PSA, **free-to-total ratio**, **biopsy extent** (cores, core involvement), age, race; and **restricted diffusion on MRI**, a powerful predictor.
- **Proof most upgrading is benign:** despite undergrading, pathology variability and progression, **low-risk AS mortality is extremely low**; selected **grade group 2** men are increasingly surveilled.
- **These data predate MRI.** **Targeted (± systematic) biopsy undergrades far less** than systematic alone, promising **earlier detection** of aggressive cancer, **reassurance** after a negative MRI or targeted biopsy and a **more accurate intervention signal**.

<p class="concept"><b>Concept —</b> The main hazard of surveillance is sampling error at entry, not biology over time; the protocol's first job is to get the entry grade right.</p>

## OTHER RISK FACTORS

### Race

- **Racial differences in incidence and mortality are substantial**, from **sociodemographic factors and biology** combined. Results for Black men on AS are **inconsistent**:

<p class="tcap">Black race and active surveillance</p>

| Setting | Finding |
|---|---|
| Single-institution cohort | **More intervention and progression** |
| Meta-analysis, 11 studies of AS-eligible men at prostatectomy | **8:** adverse pathology (margins, upgrading, upstaging); **3:** no association |
| GAP3 consortium | Upgrading and progression worse; **definitive therapy rate the same** |
| Canary Prostate Active Surveillance Study (PASS), protocol-driven | Adverse pathology at deferred surgery **similar**; race **not linked to reclassification** |
| Veterans Affairs (VA), 2004–2013 | **Less AS use**; more progression to definitive therapy |
| VA | **More progression and treatment** |
| VA, intermediate risk | **No association** with treatment, progression, metastasis, cancer or all-cause mortality |

- **What it is for:** genuine **inconsistency**. **Pattern:** differences shrink under a **protocol-driven** strategy (PASS) versus GAP3's **heterogeneous** criteria and triggers. **Trap:** **equal access (VA) did not reliably erase disparity**; methods and outcome definitions differ, and **biology and sociodemography** are hard to separate.
- **Black men are unequivocally AS candidates:** consider **more intense surveillance**, ensure **social factors do not impede compliance**; **MRI and genomics** should bring safety comparable to White men.

<p class="concept"><b>Concept —</b> Race shifts the probability of occult higher grade, not eligibility: the answer is closer scrutiny and better detection, not exclusion.</p>

### Family History

- **Family history should rarely drive radical treatment** because of **"family history inflation":** PSA-era diagnoses of insignificant cancer give relatives a "family history", so they are screened more and diagnosed with insignificant disease. **Relatives with low-grade disease remain AS candidates.**
- **Exception:** **metastatic disease <60 years in a first-degree relative** suggests germline predisposition → **germline testing of family, especially DNA repair genes**.
- **BRCA1, BRCA2, ATM** accelerate mutation acquisition and cause an **aggressive phenotype**; **BRCA2** localised cancers are genomically unstable, **resembling metastatic disease**. On AS, **BRCA2 carries the highest upgrading risk** (especially to grade group ≥3), **most upgraded within 10 years**; **BRCA1 and ATM** less so.
- **Contrast:** a small series of mismatch repair or BRCA carriers, largely diagnosed by MRI/ultrasound fusion, showed **little upgrading at 12-month confirmation biopsy**.
- **BRCA carriers: consider definitive therapy even for low grade**; initial AS is reasonable only with **very close follow-up and excellent compliance**. **Consider DNA repair testing in many patients.**
- **National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) germline testing:** **metastatic or high risk**; localised with **strong family history of early-onset prostate cancer** or cancers suggesting **mismatch repair syndrome**; **consider** for intermediate risk with **intraductal or cribriform histology**.
- **Polygenic risk:** a genome-wide association study of >5000 AS men found **18 variants predicting conversion to treatment**, and a **269-variant risk score** also predicted it; risk extends **beyond DNA repair genes**.

<p class="concept"><b>Concept —</b> A family history of diagnosis is usually a screening artefact; a family history of early metastatic death, or a germline repair defect, is biology — and only biology should move a patient off surveillance.</p>

### Monitoring

- **No universal protocol; common principles:** **early confirmation biopsy**, ideally **high-quality MRI** with initial or confirmation biopsy, and **serial biopsy**.
- **Sequence:** MRI (if not done before diagnosis and quality MRI is available) → **confirmation biopsy** → serial PSA, DRE and MRI → **repeat biopsy while stable**.

<p class="tcap">Surveillance schedule — body text versus key points</p>

| Element | Body text | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| Confirmation biopsy | **Within 1–2 years** | **Within 1–3 years**, targeting undersampled areas; omit if initial biopsy MRI-targeted |
| PSA | **6-monthly** | **6-monthly** |
| DRE | **Every 1–2 years** | — |
| MRI | **No more than every 18–24 months** | — |
| Repeat biopsy | **Every 2–5 years** | **Every 3–5 years** |

- **Why they differ:** the key points incorporate a modelling study allowing **confirmation biopsy to be delayed to year 3 without harm**, and a less intensive rebiopsy interval.
- **Tailor frequency, especially of biopsy, to risk. Compliance is paramount:** compliers (especially with confirmation biopsy) had **better cancer mortality and metastasis-free survival**; published outcomes **may not apply to non-compliers**.

<p class="tcap">Triggers for intervention</p>

| Prompts definitive therapy | Prompts evaluation, not immediate treatment |
|---|---|
| **Grade group ≥3**, unless life expectancy significantly shortened | **Increased Gleason 6 volume** → MRI or molecular biomarker |
| Traditionally **any Gleason ≥7** (now relaxed) | **Small-volume grade group 2**, otherwise acceptable → may continue |
| | **PSA rise alone** → repeat MRI or biopsy |

<p class="concept"><b>Concept —</b> Grade triggers treatment; volume and PSA trigger investigation.</p>

### Magnetic Resonance Imaging

- **mpMRI (T2-weighted, diffusion-weighted, contrast-enhanced) is sensitive and specific for higher grade.** **Diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI):** higher grade → **denser, disorganised architecture** → impeded water diffusion, so **diffusion correlates inversely with Gleason score** → MRI **tends not to show low grade** but defines the location, size and severity of higher grade.
- **Two uses, a pair:** **initial risk stratification** (strong evidence) vs **serial monitoring as sole tool** (less compelling).
- **Before confirmation biopsy:** pooled series found mpMRI **positive in most** and **~⅓ upgraded**, on systematic only, targeted only and both, so **both biopsy types are needed**.
- **ASIST, a pair:** **no significant difference at confirmation biopsy**, but **~50% less upgrading and AS failure at 2 years**, with large **site variation** (MRI quality and reader experience matter). Another randomised trial: **MRI 3 months after diagnosis** plus targeted biopsy → **far less upgrading at 1 year**.
- **Practice:** **mpMRI before confirmation biopsy, systematic plus targeted cores**; **recommended by NICE, an option in NCCN**.
- **Serial MRI alone is insufficient:** some centres link lesion change or new lesions to upgrading or treatment, but meta-analysis shows **only moderate sensitivity and specificity** for progression to grade group ≥2 (negative predictive value, NPV, ~0.8–0.9): **MRI must not be the sole means of excluding progression**.
- **MRI-invisible cancers may be particularly indolent:** **visibility genes confer higher metastatic potential**, and invisible cancers have a **more favourable natural history**, supporting **"image-based" management** (target lesions as they emerge). The **value of systematic biopsy is controversial**.

<p class="concept"><b>Concept —</b> Diffusion restriction tracks grade, so MRI sees what matters and misses what does not: its strength at entry, why invisibility reassures, and why it alone cannot exclude progression.</p>

### Biomarkers

- **Prospective AS cohorts exceed 10,000 men**, thousands beyond 10 years. **Clinical parameters** (PSA, PSA density, biopsy extent, race, T-stage) already stratify; clinical progression is only **0.2–5% at 15 years**, so **incremental biomarker benefit is modest**.
- **Above-average-risk patients may benefit:** **reassurance** from a favourable score, and **earlier identification** of progressors. **Access to genomic testing increases conservative management.**

<p class="tcap">The three FDA-approved tissue genomic assays</p>

| | Genomic Classifier (GC; Decipher) | Genomic Prostate Score (GPS; Oncotype DX) | Cell Cycle Progression (CCP; Prolaris) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basis | **22-marker RNA expression** | **12 cancer genes, 4 pathways:** androgen receptor, cellular organisation, stromal response, proliferation | **31 cell-cycle + 15 housekeeping genes**, quantitative RT-PCR |
| Validated for | **Metastasis after prostatectomy** (HR 1.5 per 10% rise; two validation cohorts); mortality and progression after **salvage radiotherapy**; on biopsy, metastasis and mortality in **intermediate/high risk**; **adverse pathology in favourable intermediate risk** | **High-grade and/or non-organ-confined disease** (odds ratio 1.9 per 20 points) | **Death in conservatively managed men** (worse if CCP >2; HR 1.7 per unit); **no discrimination among low scores** |
| AS evidence | Predicts **upgrading in grade group 1, not 2**; small model gain | **Mixed:** one cohort predictive; larger **Canary PASS** cohort **no association** with upgrading or adverse pathology; adds only with **PSA density and PI-RADS** | None specific |

<p class="tsrc">FDA, United States Food and Drug Administration; PI-RADS, Prostate Imaging Reporting and Data System; RT-PCR, reverse-transcription polymerase chain reaction.</p>

- **What it is for:** none was **built for AS**; each was validated mainly in **higher-risk or treated** men. **Pattern:** AS evidence is **weakest where the tests are most requested**. **Trap:** a favourable score does not override adverse clinicopathology.

<p class="concept"><b>Concept —</b> A biomarker adds most where clinical parameters leave most uncertainty; in a population already at very low risk, little uncertainty is left to resolve.</p>

## CAVEATS OF TISSUE-BASED GENOMICS

- **Genetic heterogeneity:** microdissected multifocal cancers from one gland gave **markedly different GC, GPS and CCP scores**.
- **The Bayesian problem:** a **3%** prior risk of metastasis at 15 years → a **90%-specific** test labels **10%** significant → **false positives ≈ three times the true risk**.
- **Hence test only higher-risk candidates:** **high biopsy volume, high PSA density, grade group 2**.
- **Repeat testing is unhelpful:** serial GPS barely changed; **only the first test predicted upgrading**.
- **Use it to set surveillance intensity** (biopsy timing, interpretation of concerning findings, triggers) — **never as the sole basis for AS**; weigh it with clinicopathology, priorities and **ability to comply**.

<div class="kp" markdown="1">
<p class="kpt">Key points</p>

- Misattribution of grade (25–30%) far exceeds true progression from pattern 3 to 4 or 5 (1–2.3% per year).
- Higher-grade risk factors: PSA density >0.15, Black race, biopsy volume (positive cores, core involvement).
- Confirmation biopsy within 1–3 years, targeting undersampled areas; may be omitted after MRI-targeted initial biopsy.
- Then PSA 6-monthly and biopsy every 3–5 years.
- MRI and targeted biopsy may replace serial systematic biopsy in very low risk; MRI NPV depends on underlying risk, so higher-risk men still need systematic biopsy.
- A visible mpMRI lesion predicts progression to treatment.
</div>

<p class="concept"><b>Concept —</b> A test's value depends on the prior. In grade group 1 the prior is so low that a good assay raises more false alarms than true signals, so genomics belongs with the higher-risk candidate.</p>

## PSYCHOLOGICAL ASPECTS

- **No evidence of significant psychological harm from AS:** anxiety follows the **diagnosis, not the management**; in SPCG-4, psychological function, anxiety and depression **did not differ** between prostatectomy and watchful waiting.
- **Living with untreated cancer is still a burden**, affecting adherence: **10–15%** of large cohorts choose treatment **without a predefined indication**.
- **PRIAS:** anxiety and decisional uncertainty **lower on AS than after definitive therapy**, **not rising over time**; confidence grows with uneventful years. A **systematic review of >12,000 men** found **no adverse effect on well-being**. A small **exercise trial reduced cancer-specific anxiety and fear of progression**.
- **Poor copers:** **chronic anxiety disorders, social isolation**. **Better physical health means less distress.**

<p class="concept"><b>Concept —</b> Distress follows the word "cancer", not the decision to watch it; those who struggle are predicted by prior vulnerability, not by the strategy.</p>

## LIFESTYLE MANAGEMENT

- **The urologist is often the only doctor seen regularly** — an opportunity to counsel on overall health.

### Smoking Cessation

- **The single most cost-effective intervention in medicine.** Smoking may promote **aggressive disease and higher cancer mortality**; quitting appears to reduce this. The newly diagnosed patient is **uniquely receptive**; **two minutes of counselling** may change behaviour, with a longevity gain exceeding **any prostate cancer intervention**.

### Exercise

- **Mechanisms:** modulated **growth factors and sex steroids**, better **myokine/adipokine** profiles and **immunity**, **androgen receptor** adaptation, **less inflammation and oxidative stress**, altered **tumour vascularisation**, **gene regulation** and **telomeres**.
- **Strenuous exertion (≥6× resting metabolic rate) works best.** In treated patients, **>3 hours/week vigorous activity** markedly reduced cancer mortality, **≥7 hours/week brisk walking** similarly; a long-term Canadian cohort agreed.
- **On AS, the ERASE randomised trial:** **high-intensity interval training lowered PSA and PSA velocity**. **Counsel all AS patients to exercise.**

### Weight Control

- **Mechanisms:** altered **leptin and adiponectin** (possible tumour suppressors); **↑ insulin-like growth factor (IGF)-1, ↓ IGF-binding proteins 1 and 2** (mitogenic); **↓ sex hormone-binding globulin, ↑ oestrogen**; **inflammation, oxidative stress**; **hyperinsulinaemia**.
- **Obesity → more high-grade cancer and cancer mortality**, independent of diet, activity and clinical factors; mortality rises with each **5 kg/m² of body mass index (BMI)** and each **0.1 of waist-to-hip ratio**, perhaps **most in Black men**.
- **On AS:** obesity **did not** affect progression at confirmation biopsy but **did at later biopsies**, implicating **true progression, not initial misclassification**. In **Asian men, BMI ≥27.5 kg/m²** predicted **adverse pathology despite low-risk criteria** and more **transition zone tumours**.
- **Chain:** obesity → cancer cells take up **essential free fatty acids** → **CD8+ cytotoxic T cells** are starved → **impaired immunity** → faster progression.

### Diet

- **Diet influences carcinogenesis, but post-diagnosis change is unproven**; data are mostly epidemiological or from treated men.
- **AS evidence conflicts:** **Mediterranean diet** adherence → **less grade progression** (especially non-diabetic and non-White men) vs **Canary PASS: no difference** across three diets; the **Men's Eating and Living (MEAL) randomised trial** raised vegetable intake **without changing progression at 24 months**.
- **Advise:** varied diet **rich in vegetables** (especially **cooked tomatoes, cruciferous**), **whole grains, skinless poultry, fish**; **limit meat, especially processed**. **Prostate-healthy = heart-healthy.**
- **Comprehensive lifestyle change** (vegan diet, exercise, stress management) **lowered PSA, improved treatment-free survival** and favourably altered gene expression and **telomere length**. Since **heart disease is the leading Western cause of death**, benefit may be as much cardiovascular.
- **"Holistic surveillance"** (vegan diet plus many micronutrients) is **unproven**; **high-dose micronutrients may harm**.

### Micronutrients

- **No supplement is of proven value on AS**; most professional bodies do not recommend them. The American Cancer Society (2012): supplements are **unlikely to improve prognosis and may increase mortality**.

<p class="tcap">Micronutrients on active surveillance</p>

| Agent | Evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Vitamin D** | Deficiency common in elderly and **northern latitudes**; low levels linked to prostate cancer; **slower PSA rise in summer**; **4000 IU/day D3 for a year** possibly slowed progression; combined vitamin D, omega-3, curcumin, B complex lowered PSA, most with higher baseline vitamin D; phase II **ProsD** trial ongoing | **The exception:** **1000–2000 IU/day**, especially with little sunlight |
| **Capsaicin** (red chilli) | Hits several cancer pathways; **growth inhibition, apoptosis, cell-cycle arrest in animals** | **Unproven on AS** |
| **Selenium** | **≥140 µg/day** after diagnosis → **higher cancer mortality** | **Contraindicated** |
| **Vitamin E** | **SELECT** → **more prostate cancer** than placebo | **Contraindicated** |

- **What it is for:** **plausible micronutrients can harm** — the **cautionary tale**. **Pattern:** only vitamin D has a favourable signal. **Trap:** "natural" is not "safe".

<p class="concept"><b>Concept —</b> What protects the heart plausibly protects the prostate at no oncological cost; isolated high-dose micronutrients have repeatedly proved harmful when properly tested.</p>

## PHARMACOLOGIC INTERVENTION

<p class="tcap">Drugs studied for secondary prevention on active surveillance</p>

| Agent | Mechanism | Evidence | Adverse effects | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **5-alpha-reductase inhibitors** (dutasteride) | Lower androgenic load; **5-alpha-reductase deficiency → no prostate cancer** | **REDEEM** (Reduction by Dutasteride of Clinical Progression Events in Expectant Management): **38% less "progression"**, **entirely less grade group 1 volume progression** (cytoreduction); **no difference in upgrading** | **↓ libido, erectile dysfunction** | **Not to prevent "progression" or lower PSA**; if a **benign prostatic hyperplasia/lower urinary tract symptom indication** exists, low-grade cancer should not alter the decision |
| **Statins** | HMG-CoA (3-hydroxy-3-methylglutaryl-coenzyme A) reductase inhibition; **fewer lipid rafts** → less AR, EGFR, luteinising hormone receptor signalling, ↓ Akt, apoptosis; anti-inflammatory, antiangiogenic; may inhibit androgen synthesis | Mostly epidemiological/treated cohorts: **↓ cancer mortality, ↓ PSA recurrence** post-diagnosis; some negative | — | **Reasonable in many** for cardiovascular benefit; **consider low dose** |
| **Metformin** | ↓ hepatic glucose output, ↑ insulin sensitivity; **AMP-activated protein kinase**-dependent and -independent; ↓ insulin/IGF signalling; **↓ androgen signalling via cyclin D1** | Diabetics: ↓ cancer mortality in some studies, not others; **MAST** (Metformin Active Surveillance Trial), placebo-controlled, results due 2025 | — | **Not routine** |
| **Apalutamide** | AR antagonist | Phase II, **240 mg/day × 90 days:** many biopsies negative immediately, **not sustained at 1–2 years** | Fatigue, gynaecomastia | Investigational |

<p class="tsrc">AR, androgen receptor.</p>

- **What it is for:** **no drug is yet indicated** for secondary prevention; statins are justified by the **heart**. **Trap:** "progression" included **grade group 1 volume increase**, which 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors and AR-targeted drugs reduce by **cytoreduction** — **expected, not clinical benefit**; only **upgrading** counts.
- **Why 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors are avoided:** **libido** effects and **lingering uncertainty about slightly more high-grade cancer in REDUCE** (Reduction by Dutasteride of Prostate Cancer Events) **and the Prostate Cancer Prevention Trial (PCPT)**.

<p class="concept"><b>Concept —</b> Any agent that shrinks prostatic epithelium will appear to "reduce progression" if progression includes volume; only grade end points separate disease modification from cytoreduction.</p>

## SUMMARY

- **Use surveillance visits to counsel** (also benefiting general and cardiovascular health):
    1. **Exercise regularly**, vigorously if possible.
    2. **Maintain a healthy weight.**
    3. **Stop smoking.**
    4. **Vegetable- and grain-based diet**; moderate red and especially processed meat; prefer **fish and skinless poultry**.
    5. **Limit saturated fat.**
    6. **Vitamin D 1000–2000 IU/day**, especially with reduced sunlight.
    7. **Consider a low-dose statin.**

## GAP3

- **Global Action Plan Prostate Cancer Active Surveillance:** a global AS database **funded by the Movember Foundation**, linking **>250 researchers**.
- **Questions:** defining **low risk** and the diagnosis; **comorbidity and life expectancy**; **monitoring tools**; **triggers** for treatment or switch to watchful waiting; **criteria of AS efficacy**.
- **Aim: international consensus** (partly achieved), with comparison across **races and geographies**. **Protocol heterogeneity limits conclusions**; longer follow-up with imaging and biomarkers promises an **international standard**.

## BARRIERS TO ACTIVE SURVEILLANCE

- **Uptake is relatively low in many constituencies**; US use has risen but **varies widely**.
- **Barriers:** **preconceptions about "cancer"**; **misinformation** from friends, family or practitioners; **provider bias**; **psychological factors**.
- **Remedy — shared decision-making:** (1) **inform** about the reasonable options; (2) **discuss** them against the patient's values. **Better-informed patients prefer conservative management**; **decision aids** for low risk are invaluable.
- **Four requisites of shared decision-making** (each rests on communication): **both** parties involved → **both share information** → **both express preferences** → **a decision both agree**.
- **The physician is navigator, not driver;** AS is **a journey, not a destination**, shaped by internal (**values**) and external (**pressure**) factors.
- **QoL effects:**
    - *Biopsy:* mostly **no or minimal erectile effect**; an association with serial **transperineal sector biopsy of 24–38 cores** suggests **more cores, more erectile dysfunction**; GAP3 shows a **small International Index of Erectile Function (IIEF) fall per serial biopsy**, of uncertain significance.
    - *Anxiety:* **low**, not increased by conservative management; exercise lowered it; **limitation: self-selection**.
    - *Genitourinary QoL:* **as good as or better than after treatment** on validated instruments (EORTC QLQ-PR25, IIEF-15, FACT-P, EPIC), **comparable to men without cancer**; limited long-term data, and **late effects vs ageing** are hard to separate.
- **Self-discontinuation without criteria occurs in every cohort (7–15%)**; one systematic review attributed **20%** of discontinuation to **choice or anxiety**. QoL follow-up then often stops, **obscuring true QoL impact**.

### Nomenclature

<p class="tcap">Renaming Gleason score 6</p>

| For renaming | Against renaming |
|---|---|
| Proposals: **indolent lesion of epithelial origin (IDLE)**; **prostatic neoplasm of low malignant potential (PNLMP)** | **Meets traditional pathological criteria for cancer**; **invades locally** |
| **Less anxiety** (patients fear a lethal disease) | **Mutational landscape is a continuum**; some Gleason 6 carry significant mutations |
| **Eases conservative management** | With **undergrading**, men may **abandon follow-up** — especially worrying for **Black and Native American men** |
| **Better retention** on AS | **Metastasis is not required** to define cancer (**basal cell carcinoma, gliomas**) |

- **Verdict:** "cancer" is **unlikely to be dropped**; the **grade group system** (low grade = **1 of 5, not 6 of 10**) delivers many of the benefits.

### Economics

- **Management is costly** (high prevalence × costly therapy). **AS costs less than treatment** despite long follow-up; overtreatment costs **money and QoL**. For low risk, **first-year definitive therapy costs substantially more** than no active treatment; a Canadian model (including other-cause mortality and recurrence) projected **large 10-year savings** versus treating all.
- **Costs follow test frequency and intervention rate:** **fewer biopsies, lower cost**; **biannual biopsy matches surgical cost by 10 years**. **Savings need retention:** 15-year retention **43% and 55%** (Toronto, Johns Hopkins).
- **MRI and biomarkers add cost but may be cost-effective** by improving selection and reducing treatment and rebiopsy; one model favoured **annual MRI with biopsy only for PI-RADS ≥4**. Results depend on **local, especially MRI, costs**.

<p class="concept"><b>Concept —</b> Surveillance saves money only if biopsies are few and patients stay on it; every cost lever points to imaging-triggered rather than calendar-driven biopsy.</p>

## RESEARCH QUESTIONS

- **Focus has moved from overtreatment to undertreatment:** low-risk AS is supported by genetics, trials (PIVOT, ProtecT) and prospective series; with expanded criteria, the worry is now **higher-than-expected risk**. Open: **who qualifies, how and how often to test, with what — and on progression, definitive therapy for all or focal therapy for some?**
- **Molecular events of progression**, for example **circulating exosomes** carrying mRNA from aggressive into low-grade cells.
- **Germline repair defects:** localised cancers in BRCA carriers carry a mutational load **resembling castration-resistant prostate cancer**, making them **unreliable AS candidates**; offer AS **with caution** to **BRCA1, BRCA2 and ATM** carriers.
- **Finding the "wolf":** MRI reduces the **25–30% misattribution** substantially but not completely. Uncertain: **mpMRI reliability**, **need for systematic biopsy when MRI is normal** (by risk), **biomarker role**. Emerging: **PSMA-PET** (prostate-specific membrane antigen positron emission tomography), **high-resolution micro-ultrasound**.
- **Nomograms integrating MRI and biomarkers are urgently needed.** Unresolved: **restricted diffusion with a grade group 1 targeted biopsy** (aggressive, missed higher grade, or false-positive MRI?); **microfocal Gleason 6 with a mildly raised Prolaris or Oncotype DX score**; **radiomics** is in its infancy.
- **Intermediate risk:** **≥20% metastasis by 15 years** despite monitoring, yet **80% metastasis-free** — the task is identifying the indolent; **liquid biopsy** shows promise.
- **Interventions to test:** exercise, weight loss, less animal protein or carbohydrate, micronutrients (**pomegranate, capsaicin, lycopene**); promising micronutrients **often prove harmful**, whereas **statins and metformin** look promising.
- **Leaner follow-up:** can a **negative MRI replace systematic biopsy**; **2–4 targeted cores replace 12–14 systematic**; a **negative biomarker reduce biopsy**; how does **NPV vary with risk**? Can follow-up **stop** in predictably indolent disease despite **15–20 years' life expectancy**? Is **focal therapy** safe long term?
- **Can widespread AS rehabilitate PSA screening** for policy makers? This needs **modelling on recent data**.

## CONCLUSION

- **AS is a safe, effective, widely adopted strategy** for low-risk disease, **preferred for most by the AUA and ASCO**, and the **antidote to screening-driven overtreatment**. The roles of **serial imaging and biomarkers** are still being defined.
