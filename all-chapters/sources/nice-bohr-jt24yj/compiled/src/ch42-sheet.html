Chapter 42 — Bladder Anomalies in Children — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else

  • Fetal bladder empties every 15–20 min → re-scan before calling it absent.
  • First-trimester megacystis: ≤6 mm normal; ≥8 mm abnormal.
  • Later: SL (sagittal length, mm) = GA (weeks) − 5 (±7); >GA+2 enlarged, >GA+12 megacystis.
  • 7–15 mm → most resolve; >15 mm → none, all obstructive.
  • Early (<18 wk) + resolved <23 wk → no postnatal surgery.
  • Megacystis commonest cause: PUV (posterior urethral valves); M:F 8:1.
  • MMIHS (megacystis–microcolon–intestinal hypoperistalsis): female, normal/↑ fluid, acontractile bladder, ACTG2.
  • Bladder agenesis: survival only with ectopic ureters.
  • Symptomatic urachal remnant → excise with bladder cuff.
  • Nephrogenic adenoma recurs 80% → surveillance cystoscopy.

Congenital Bladder Anomalies

  • Embryology: cloaca divided wk 4–6; urine from wk 10; bladder descends, urachus forms months 4–5.
  • Urachus → median umbilical ligament.
  • Female urethra: entirely pelvic urogenital sinus.
  • Fetal bladder: wall ≤3 mm; diameter first trimester ≤6–8 mm.
  • Visible: ~half at 10 wk, almost all by 13 wk; transvaginal > transabdominal.
  • Amniotic fluid = fetal urine only from 16 WGA (weeks' gestational age).
  • Fetal sex: reliable after 14 wk; use testes, not phallus.
  • Cord: 2 arteries + 1 vein, no fluid-filled urachus.
  • Agenesis: ~1:600,000, female majority; ectopia into müllerian structures (F) / rectum (M).
  • Agenesis rescue: ureterosigmoidostomy or stoma.
  • Exstrophy vs agenesis: exstrophy = abdominal-wall template + normal fluid.
  • Covered cloacal exstrophy: omphalocele + diastasis with bladder filling.
  • Hypoplasia: epispadias, urogenital sinus, bilateral renal dysplasia, ectopia → augmentation often.
  • Megacystis cut-offs: <10% CRL (crown–rump length) normal, ≥13% abnormal; others use >6 or ≥7 mm.
  • After first trimester: no emptying in 45 min = enlarged.
  • Aneuploidy: ~one-fifth, mainly trisomy 13/18 → uniformly poor outcome.
  • Early megacystis ≤12 mm → likely resolves.
  • Late (>18 wk) + resolution >23 wk → most still need surgery.
  • Persistent megacystis + oligohydramnios → LUTO (lower urinary tract obstruction).
  • Table 42.1 (pooled): PUV ~50%, atresia/stenosis 7%, PBS (prune-belly) 4%, MMIHS 1%, cloacal 0.7%, undefined 36.5%.
  • MMIHS survival: ~20% → ~55% recently; TPN (total parenteral nutrition) complications commonest death.
  • MMIHS gold standard: exclude Hirschsprung by ganglion cells on colon biopsy.
  • MMIHS first-line: CIC (clean intermittent catheterisation) / vesicostomy; lifelong CIC.
  • Duplication: commonest complete sagittal; genital dup. ≤90%, lower GI ≤42%.
  • Sagittal > coronal for non-urological anomalies.
  • Bladder without urethra → ipsilateral renal dysplasia.
  • Operate if: complete duplication → both sphincters competent: join urethras; one incompetent: close neck, excise urethra.
  • Don't operate: incomplete duplication draining well; asymptomatic.
  • Diverticula gold standard: VCUG (voiding cystourethrography).
  • Commonest congenital: paraureteral.
  • Hutch: primary = smooth, single, intermittent, no obstruction; secondary = trabeculated, multiple, constant, obstruction.
  • Operate if: primary paraureteral + VUR (vesicoureteral reflux) → excise.
  • VUR resolution: females > males (higher male voiding pressure).
  • Syndromic diverticula: Ehlers–Danlos, Williams, Menkes; lax wall; can exceed bladder.
  • Operate if: syndromic + UTI (urinary tract infection)/stones → resect; postop urodynamics.
  • Urachal cyst: distal; adults > children; S. aureus commonest.
  • Patent urachus: in-utero obstruction ≤14% only.
  • Diagnosis: patent urachus → ultrasound / fistulogram / VCUG; sinus → sinugram; cyst → ultrasound.
  • Infected remnant: drain + antibiotics → excise.
  • Don't operate: asymptomatic incidental remnant (trend; discuss with family); wide-necked urachal diverticulum.
  • Laparoscopic excision: feasible <6 months.

Acquired Bladder Anomalies in Children

  • Acquired diverticulum: treat obstruction first; remodelling may avoid excision.
  • Operate if: still symptomatic → excise ± ipsilateral reimplant.
  • Waldeyer sheath damage → UVJ (ureterovesical junction) failure → reflux/obstruction.
  • Haemangioma: Klippel–Trénaunay, Sturge–Weber; solitary <3 cm; gross haematuria.
  • Haemangioma treatment: small → fulguration / Nd:YAG (neodymium:YAG) laser; large → partial cystectomy.
  • Fibroepithelial polyp: 2nd commonest tumour after rhabdomyosarcoma; endoscopic resection.
  • Nephrogenic adenoma: F:M 5:1; recurrence 80%, latency ~4 yr; distal tubular origin.
  • Cystitis cystica: almost only female → long-term antibiotic prophylaxis.
  • Eosinophilic cystitis: male; biopsy mandatory; TUR (transurethral resection) + steroids/antihistamines/antibiotics.
  • Don't operate: eosinophilic cystitis in neonates — may self-limit.
  • Haemorrhagic cystitis: cyclophosphamide/ifosfamide; ↑ with BK, CMV (cytomegalovirus), adenovirus; prevent with mesna.
  • Haemorrhagic cystitis adjunct: oral pentosan polysulfate; avoid catheters.

Classic traps

  • Trap: bladder not seen = agenesis — Actually: re-scan after 15–20 min.
  • Trap: megacystis = PUV — Actually: over one-third undiagnosed.
  • Trap: female megacystis → PUV — Actually: MMIHS, cloacal, atresia.
  • Trap: prenatal resolution reassuring — Actually: late resolution still needs surgery.
  • Trap: megacystis reflux from obstruction — Actually: reflux can cause dilation.
  • Trap: early VCUG in MMIHS — Actually: VUR uncommon, low yield.
  • Trap: paraureteral diverticulum bars Deflux — Actually: injection still possible.
  • Trap: excise acquired diverticulum first — Actually: fix outlet first.
  • Trap: sinugram separates urachal sinus/omphalomesenteric — Actually: only histology.
  • Trap: eosinophilic cystitis looks typical — Actually: no typical appearance, biopsy.
