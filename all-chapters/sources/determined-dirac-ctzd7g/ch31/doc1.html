Chapter 31 — Tuberculosis and Parasitic Infections of the Genitourinary Tract — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else

  • Urogenital (UG) tuberculosis (TB) gold standard: urine acid-fast bacilli (AFB) culture, 3–5 first-void samples
  • Sterile pyuria ± haematuria → think TB (> 90% in endemic series)
  • Kidney 80% of UG TB; epididymis = commonest genital site
  • Standard UG TB: 6 months — 2 months isoniazid (INH) + rifampicin + pyrazinamide + ethambutol (RIPE) → 4 months INH + rifampicin
  • Operate 4–6 weeks after starting anti-TB drugs
  • Tuberculin skin test (TST) / interferon-γ release assay (IGRA) = infection only; cannot diagnose or exclude active TB
  • Schistosoma haematobium: eggs in urine = gold standard; collect 09:00–15:00
  • Praziquantel (PZQ) 40 mg/kg once or 2 × 20 mg/kg — only World Health Organization (WHO)-recommended drug
  • Schistosomal bladder cancer: squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) 60–90%, onset 40–50 years
  • Genital filariasis = Wuchereria bancrofti; diethylcarbamazine (DEC) = drug of choice

Urogenital tuberculosis

  • Cut-offs: ¼ of world latently infected; UG TB in 15–20% (developing) vs 2–10% (developed) of pulmonary TB
  • Primary progression < 5%; lifetime reactivation 5–15%; UG latency up to 46 years
  • Male : female ≈ 2 : 1; bacille Calmette–Guérin (BCG) instillation → UG TB ≈ 1%
  • Constitutional symptoms < 20%; renal colic < 10%
  • Autonephrectomy up to ⅓; end-stage renal failure ≈ 7%
  • Epididymis 10–55% (bilateral ⅓); prostate 22–49%; urethra 1.9–4.5%
  • Bladder < 100 mL → symptomatic; thimble bladder < 20 mL
  • Gold standard: urine AFB culture; sensitivity 80–90% ideal vs ≈ 10% real-world
  • Ziehl–Neelsen urine stain < 50% sensitive
  • Culture time: Löwenstein–Jensen 4–6 weeks; Middlebrook 7H10 ≈ 1 week sooner; Mycobacteria Growth Indicator Tube (MGIT, liquid) ≈ 10 days
  • Always one solid medium alongside liquid culture
  • Nucleic acid amplification test (NAAT): 1–2 days; adjunct only; cannot monitor treatment
  • GeneXpert rifampicin resistance = surrogate for multidrug-resistant TB (MDR-TB) (> 90% also INH-resistant)
  • Urine lipoarabinomannan (LAM) lateral-flow — WHO 2019: people with HIV + symptoms, advanced HIV, CD4 < 200
  • TST, read 48–72 h: ≥ 5 mm contacts / immunosuppressed / fibrotic chest film; ≥ 10 mm high-risk groups; ≥ 15 mm no risk
  • IGRA preferred ≥ 5 years from endemic country; unaffected by BCG
  • Sensitivity: T-SPOT.TB > TST > QuantiFERON (third generation)
  • Chest radiograph for every patient — coexistent pulmonary TB ≤ 20%
  • Imaging now: computed tomography (CT) urography; former gold standard intravenous urography (IVU)
  • Commonest IVU finding: obstruction; stricture commonest at distal ureter / vesicoureteric junction
  • Putty / cement kidney (lobar calcification) = pathognomonic of end stage
  • Kerr's kink: hiked-up pelvis, sharp pelviureteric junction (PUJ) angulation
  • Signs: phantom calyx, moth-eaten calyx, pipestem / beaded-corkscrew ureter, golf-hole orifice
  • Children / pregnancy: ultrasound (US) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI); US tracks hydronephrosis on treatment
  • First line: RIPE by directly observed therapy
  • Continuation: daily or 3× weekly — never twice weekly
  • Doses: INH 5 mg/kg (300 mg); rifampicin 10 mg/kg (600 mg); pyridoxine 25–50 mg
  • Ethambutol: only non-hepatotoxic first-line drug → check acuity and red–green vision
  • Complicated UG TB: 12–24 months, or ≥ 6 months after surgery
  • Pregnancy: INH + rifampicin + ethambutol + pyridoxine, 9 months
  • HIV not on effective antiretrovirals → 9 months, daily dosing
  • Transplant: rifamycin-free regimen ≥ 18 months; mortality up to 36%
  • Rifabutin = rifamycin of choice with antiretrovirals
  • Pyrazinamide in pregnancy: omitted in United States; WHO recommends
  • MDR = INH + rifampicin resistance; cure 50–60% vs 94–97% susceptible; XDR 30–50%
  • Pre-XDR: MDR + fluoroquinolone or injectable aminoglycoside
  • BPaL (bedaquiline + pretomanid + linezolid) 6–9 months; not approved for extrapulmonary TB
  • Operate if: uraemia / sepsis → retrograde stent first (succeeds 41%) → antegrade → nephrostomy
  • Operate if: non-functioning kidney + recalcitrant TB (relapse < 1%) or resistant hypertension (65% improve)
  • Operate if: stricture no better after 6 weeks of drugs → open repair
  • Nephrectomy: laparoscopic preferred; ligate renal artery and vein separately
  • Nephrostomy removed without fixing cause → cutaneous TB fistula
  • Gap bridging: mobilisation 2–3 cm; psoas hitch ≤ 5 cm; Boari flap 10–15 cm; beyond → ileal ureter
  • Bladder: < 100 mL → augmentation (ileocaecum / sigmoid); < 20 mL → orthotopic substitution
  • Epididymis only → epididymectomy; ligate vas at external ring
  • Do not operate: non-functioning kidney without recalcitrant TB or hypertension
  • HIV → 15–20× risk of active TB; UG TB less fibrotic, more lethal
  • Relapse: UG 6.3–22% vs pulmonary 2–6%; mean 5.3 years → follow up 10 years

Parasitic infections of the urogenital tract

Schistosomiasis

  • S. haematobium: Bulinus snail; vesical venous plexus; egg ≈ 140 µm, terminal spine
  • Bilharz (1852): worms in mesenteric veins linked to urinary eggs
  • Cercariae penetrate intact skin; none shed < 15 °C or > 35 °C
  • Egg-laying 8–12 weeks; antibodies 4–6 weeks
  • Single exposure (visitors): worms die within 3–5 years
  • Katayama fever 2–8 weeks; mainly S. japonicum; add steroids
  • Obstruction: juxtavesical ureter commonest (20–60%); vesicoureteric reflux in 25%
  • Tonic hydroureter (hypertrophied) reversible vs atonic (atrophic)
  • Tubular concentrating function lost before glomerular
  • Symmers fibrosis: pipestem periportal fibrosis from eggs
  • Salmonella in worm tegument → treat both infections
  • Contracted bladder ≤ 50 mL; trigone spared
  • 40–60% of obstructive uropathy presents at inactive end stage
  • Cancer: International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) Group 1; SCC 60–90%, adenocarcinoma 5–15%; posterior wall
  • Urine peak noon; rectal snip before bladder biopsy; squash preparation > histology
  • Serology (FAST-ELISA + Western blot) > 90%; stays positive after cure
  • Active infection: eggs or circulating anodic / cathodic antigen (CAA / CCA); most sensitive: polymerase chain reaction (PCR)
  • Plain film: calcified bladder = "fetal head in pelvis"; earliest IVU sign: striations
  • PZQ 2 × 20 mg/kg 6–8 h apart; efficacy 60–90%; misses schistosomulae → repeat in weeks
  • Artemether / artesunate: 90–97% prophylaxis; not in malaria co-endemic areas
  • Active polypoid obstruction → PZQ; renal function recovers in 1–2 months
  • Operate if: severe haemorrhage; failed medical therapy; contracted bladder; chronic ulcer → partial cystectomy
  • Residual stenosis: Politano–Leadbetter reimplant variants; dilatation restenoses
  • Aswan Low Dam (1930s): prevalence < 11% → > 75%
  • Egyptian mass treatment: fewer bladder cancers; SCC → transitional cell shift
  • WHA65.21 (May 2012): morbidity control → elimination; eliminated in 10 countries
  • Kill cercariae: water > 125 °F (≈ 52 °C) for 5 minutes, or stand > 2 days

Filariasis

  • W. bancrofti > 90%; nocturnal microfilaraemia; mosquito vector
  • Bancroftian: whole limb + genitals; brugian: below knee, genitals spared
  • Filarial lymphangitis retrograde (peripheral spread)
  • Filarial dance sign on US (≥ 80%); midnight Giemsa smear
  • Antigen test for W. bancrofti only; burned-out cases antibody-positive
  • DEC 2 mg/kg three times daily; 1 day = 12 days; tropical pulmonary eosinophilia 2–3 weeks
  • Albendazole 400 mg twice daily 21 days: micro- + macrofilaricidal; ivermectin mainly microfilaricidal
  • Tropical pulmonary eosinophilia: nocturnal wheeze, high IgE, microfilaraemia absent
  • Doxycycline 200 mg 4–8 weeks: anti-Wolbachia, sterilises adults
  • Hydrocele: excise intact sac
  • Lymph scrotum: ruptured scrotal lymphatic vesicles, whitish discharge
  • Chyluria precedes genital elephantiasis
  • Mass drug administration: albendazole + ivermectin (Africa) / + DEC elsewhere
  • Onchocerciasis: ivermectin 150 µg/kg every 6–12 months; "hanging groin"
  • Loa loa: DEC; encephalopathy risk > 2500–8000 microfilariae/mL; no Wolbachia → no doxycycline

Other parasites

  • Hydatid kidney 2–3% (third site): albendazole 400 mg twice daily 1–6 months; no puncture–aspiration–injection–re-aspiration (PAIR)
  • Pinworm: albendazole 400 mg or mebendazole 100 mg once; repeat at 2 weeks; treat contacts
  • Amoebiasis: metronidazole / tinidazole, then luminal agent (paromomycin / iodoquinol)

Classic traps

  • Trap: BCG / M. bovis = M. tuberculosis — Actually: innately pyrazinamide-resistant
  • Trap: quinolone-responsive prostatitis excludes TB — Actually: relapse after short course suggests TB
  • Trap: negative NAAT excludes UG TB — Actually: adjunct only; culture needed
  • Trap: ureteric calcification = TB — Actually: TB intraluminal; schistosomiasis intramural, dilated ureter
  • Trap: healing relieves obstruction — Actually: fibrosis worsens it; monitor with US
  • Trap: 4-month rifapentine regimen for UG TB — Actually: not for extrapulmonary TB
  • Trap: schistosomal serology confirms cure — Actually: stays positive; use eggs or CAA / CCA
  • Trap: egg-negative schistosomiasis = mild — Actually: inactive stage has worst kidneys
  • Trap: DEC for any filaria — Actually: contraindicated in onchocerciasis and heavy Loa
  • Trap: funiculoepididymitis = tumour → orchidectomy — Actually: filarial inflammation; preserve testis
