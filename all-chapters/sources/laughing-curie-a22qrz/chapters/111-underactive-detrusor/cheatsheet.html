Chapter 111 — Underactive Detrusor — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else

  • Detrusor underactivity (DUA) = urodynamic dx; underactive bladder (UAB) = symptoms
  • DUA on urodynamics (UDS): men <50 → 9–28%; men >70 → 48%; women → 12–45%
  • Bladder contractility index (BCI) = Pdet@Qmax + 5Qmax: >150 strong, 100–150 normal, <100 weak
  • Only accepted diagnostic test: invasive UDS; no consensus criteria
  • Preferred drainage: intermittent self-catheterisation (ISC); long term, suprapubic catheter (SPC) > urethral catheter
  • No effective drug; parasympathomimetics avoided — risk of cardiac arrest
  • Sacral neuromodulation (SNM) → DUA with sphincter / pelvic floor disorder
  • Outlet surgery, low PVR, symptoms only → unlikely to help
  • Catheter-dependent man, young, fit → outlet surgery reasonable
  • Preserved bladder sensation = best predictor of treatment response

Terminology, Definitions, Symptoms

  • International Continence Society (ICS) 2002 DUA: contraction of reduced strength and/or duration → prolonged or incomplete emptying; no numeric thresholds
  • No contraction → acontractile detrusor
  • ICS UAB (2018): slow stream, hesitancy, straining ± incomplete emptying ± storage symptoms
  • No pathognomonic symptom (vs overactive bladder [OAB] = urgency)
  • Significant postvoid residual (PVR) (one proposal): >40% of functional capacity (voided + PVR)
  • Sensation absent → infrequent voider, nocturnal incontinence; preserved → frequency, nocturia

Epidemiology

  • Population prevalence unknown — no non-invasive marker
  • Female bladder outlet obstruction (BOO): only 2.7–8% of UDS referrals → low flow + high PVR in woman ≈ DUA
  • Men >65–70: DUA 40–48%; institutionalised elderly ≈ 40%
  • Detrusor hyperactivity with impaired contractility (DHIC): DO + DUA; frail/institutionalised elderly
  • Coexistence: ~half of men with DUA also DO/BOO; ~¾ of women also DO/stress urinary incontinence (SUI) → UDS before LUTS surgery in elderly
  • Untreated DUA (Qmax <15, Pdet@Qmax <40): minimal progression at 10 yr

Etiopathogenesis

  • Confirmed causes: neurological disease/injury, diabetes
  • Probable only: BOO, ageing, atherosclerosis/pelvic ischaemia
  • Mechanisms: myogenic (myocyte/extracellular matrix) vs neurogenic (afferent, efferent, central)
  • Elbadawi degeneration pattern: disrupted myocytes + axonal degeneration
  • Smith hypothesis: stiff bladder → wall stress collapses early in void → reflex stops → PVR
  • BOO animal model: compensation → decompensation (fibrosis) — irreversible if not relieved first
  • Mechanism: Laplace → ischaemia–reperfusion → oxidative injury → denervation
  • Most men in acute retention: detrusor preserved
  • High-pressure chronic retention: preserved contractility, poor compliance, upper-tract change
  • Diabetic cystopathy: early storage symptoms → late poor sensation + voiding failure
  • Acute stroke: ~½ retain ("cerebral shock"); chronic = DO
  • Parkinson disease: DUA <20%, often from anticholinergic drugs
  • Multisystem atrophy: DUA 52–95% → retention
  • Multiple sclerosis: DUA ~20% (lumbosacral plaques)
  • Radical hysterectomy: LUTD ~72%; rectal surgery now <5%; many recover by 1 yr
  • Reversible: Guillain–Barré, herpes zoster; permanent: AIDS, tabes dorsalis

Diagnosis

  • Pdet@Qmax + Qmax underestimates strength: bladder outlet relation (BOR) → Pdet@Qmax is lowest-pressure point
  • Projected isovolumetric pressure (PIP, Schäfer) = Pdet@Qmax + K·Qmax; K 5 (men with BPH), 1 (older women)
  • PIP bands: >150 strong; 100–150 normal; 50–100 weak; <50 very weak
  • Detrusor contraction coefficient (DECO) = PIP/100; <1 weak
  • Watts factor (WFmax): <7 W/m² reduced (some 10); volume- and BOO-independent; unvalidated
  • Voluntary stop test reads ~20 cm H₂O lower than mechanical
  • Continuous occlusion: most reliable, measures sustainability; painful, research only
  • Detrusor contractility parameter (DCP) (t20–80): invalid with BOO
  • Shortening velocity falls before WF — possible early marker
  • Criteria disagree: DUA diagnosed in 5–55% depending on definition
  • No void on UDS? → ambulatory UDS: >80% then contract

Management

  • Assess: diary, DRE, urinalysis, flow, ultrasound PVR, sacral neuro exam, spinal MRI if deficit, drug history, constipation
  • Credé / Valsalva: only DUA + incompetent sphincter (else reflux)
  • ISC: urethral bleeding ~⅓; fewer infections than indwelling catheter
  • Parasympathomimetic works only if stimulus lacking, not muscle lost; needs intact sensation
  • α-Blocker + bethanechol/distigmine → better flow than α-blocker alone
  • Tamsulosin 0.2 mg: some women with DUA improve
  • Prostanoids: PGE → urethral relaxation; PGF₂α → urethral contraction; uterine side effects
  • ASP8302 (M₃ allosteric modulator) negative; TAC-302 (neurite growth) ↑ BCI
  • Brindley sacral anterior root stimulator: complete spinal cord injury (SCI); needs reflex contractions preop; + rhizotomy (Sauerwein)
  • Intravesical electrotherapy (IVE): Aδ afferents; 10–15 sessions trial; only sham RCT negative
  • Urethral botulinum toxin A (BoNT-A) 50 U/4 mL: trial-only; best with poor sphincter relaxation
  • Poor outlet-surgery outcome: Pdet <45, age >80, PVR >1500 mL
  • BOO index (BOOI) = Pdet@Qmax − 2Qmax
  • Women: no bladder neck incision/resection (incontinence, stenosis)
  • Continent catheterisable stoma: young woman, retention refractory to SNM, declines SPC
  • Ileal conduit: renal deterioration from thick-walled bladder
  • Latissimus dorsi detrusor myoplasty (LDDM): inferior epigastric pedicle, intercostal nerve; investigational
  • Reduction cystoplasty: abandoned
  • Tissue-engineered bladder: storage only

Classic traps

  • Trap: UAB has a defining symptom — Actually: none is pathognomonic
  • Trap: low flow + high PVR in man = DUA — Actually: could be BOO; needs UDS
  • Trap: Pdet@Qmax = contraction strength — Actually: lowest point on BOR; underestimates
  • Trap: BOO inevitably decompensates — Actually: long-term stable in most men
  • Trap: acute retention = failed detrusor — Actually: detrusor usually preserved
  • Trap: Parkinson disease → DUA — Actually: DO; DUA usually drug-induced
  • Trap: stroke → DUA — Actually: only acute; chronic = DO
  • Trap: bethanechol treats DUA — Actually: unproven; cardiac arrest risk; avoided
  • Trap: failed void on UDS = acontractile — Actually: bashful bladder; ambulatory UDS
  • Trap: TURP helps symptomatic low-PVR DUA — Actually: no better than conservative
