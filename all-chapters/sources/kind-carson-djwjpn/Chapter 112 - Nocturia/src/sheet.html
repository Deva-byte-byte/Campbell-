Chapter 112 — Nocturia — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else
  • Nocturia = voids during the main period of intended sleep (patient's schedule, not the clock)
  • Aetiology: Sleep CALM — Sleep, Comorbidities, Actions, LUT dysfunction, Medications
  • Bladder diary = gold standard for severity; guideline standard of care
  • Nocturnal polyuria (NP): NPi >20–33% (age-dependent) or NUP >90 mL/h
  • Global polyuria >40 mL/kg/24 h → always investigate systemic disease
  • Ni = NUV/MVV; >1 = mismatch; strongest diary predictor of severity
  • NBCi >1.3 → investigate small nocturnal capacity; >2 = severe
  • Desmopressin: only US-approved nocturia drug — NP + ≥2 voids/night
  • Hyponatraemia: age ≥65, Na <135, CrCl <60, excess dose; mostly first 2–3 weeks
  • Lower urinary tract drugs: statistically significant, clinically modest for nocturia

Nocturia—What, When, and Why?

  • ICS 2002 vs 2019: "wake … to void" → cause-neutral count
  • Counts: single void/night counts; bother not part of definition
  • First morning void: volume → NUV; void → daytime frequency
  • NPi = NUV/TUV; NUP = NUV/hours of sleep
  • Modified Ni = NUV/NMVV — night-only data; surrogate for Ni
  • PNV = Ni − 1 (last volume leaves as first morning void)
  • NBCi = ANV − PNV; 0 = voids at full capacity
  • FUSP = bed → first nocturnal void
  • Convenience voids: little urgency, smaller volumes; uncommon
  • Example: NUV 800, MVV 200 → Ni 4, PNV 3; 6 voids → NBCi 3
  • Capacity: normal >300–400 mL; MVV/NMVV <150–300 mL small
  • Other NP cut-offs: NUV >6.4 mL/kg; >0.9 mL/min; NPi >53% (not ICS)
  • Global polyuria per hour: 90 / 117 / 167 mL/h at 54 / 70 / 100 kg

Rationale for Evaluation and Management

  • Prevalence ≥2 voids: 1 in 5–6 aged 20–40; up to 3 in 5 aged >70
  • Strongest correlate: older age
  • BACH (landmark): higher in Black, Hispanic adults vs White
  • Commonest cause of adult sleep disruption: urge-driven waking
  • Timing: first void within 2–3 h → hits slow-wave sleep (early night)
  • Sleep: NREM ≈75%; 4–6 cycles; slow-wave 10–25%, not recovered
  • Falls/hip fractures: nocturia among most strongly associated LUTS
  • Mortality: ≥2 voids → worse survival vs 0–1, esp. <65 years
  • Clinically significant: any nocturia prompting consultation

Causes of Nocturia: "Sleep CALM" and the Bladder Diary

  • Gold standard: diary-counted ANV; questionnaires usually overestimate
  • Key sleep measure: FUSP — prolongation = treatment success
  • Duration: EAU Female 3 days; EAU Male ≥3 days; ICS no consensus
  • 3–7 days more reliable than 24 h; "diary fatigue" ↓ adherence
  • Night-only diary: cannot diagnose global polyuria; not for women (EAU)
  • FVC vs diary: diary adds intake, pads, leakage
  • Commonest mechanism: NP (most adults); global polyuria uncommon
  • Krimpen (landmark): NPi >33% in 92% with nocturia, 70% without

Evaluation and management

  • Basic tests: metabolic panel, urinalysis, PVR; PSA only if changes management
  • UTI on urinalysis: treat → repeat urinalysis
  • First-line: optimise all Sleep CALM factors, together
  • STOP-BANG: ≥3 yes = high OSA risk; BMI >35, age >50, neck >40 cm
  • OSA: hypoxia → ↑ right atrial pressure → ANP → natriuresis
  • CPAP: up to ≈2 fewer voids — among largest effects
  • OSA history (women): sleep-clinic referral for CPAP (EAU strong)
  • Non-bothersome nocturia still merits evaluation (EAU)
  • Non-dipping hypertension: normal dip 10–20%; 40–50% of hypertensives
  • Non-dipping mechanism: aldosterone → nocturnal natriuresis
  • HCTZ vs loop: more antihypertensive, less diuretic
  • Heart failure: third-spaced leg fluid mobilised supine → ↑ NUV
  • AVP: supraoptic/paraventricular → posterior pituitary → V2, collecting duct
  • Polyuria–polydipsia: central DI, nephrogenic DI, primary polydipsia
  • Diagnosis: arginine-stimulated copeptin > water deprivation test
  • Melatonin: consider in older adults; evidence limited
  • Evening fluid: restrict 3–4 h before bed; water diuresis onset ≈1 h
  • Caffeine/alcohol: diuresis within 2–3 h; caffeine half-life 2.5–4.5 h
  • Protein meal: 2–3 h hyperfiltration
  • Other conservative: ↓ sodium, exercise/weight loss, compression stockings
  • Isolated nocturia is not a LUTS — LUT drugs not expected to work
  • OAB trials: nocturia in up to 85%
  • VA Cooperative: only terazosin beat placebo (2.5 → 1.8)
  • MTOPS: doxazosin, combination beat placebo; finasteride not
  • CombAT: combination −0.5 vs tamsulosin −0.3, dutasteride −0.4
  • REDUCE: dutasteride −0.15/−0.24 voids; no sleep benefit
  • 5ARI: only if prostate >40 mL (EAU)
  • PDE5I: do not offer for nocturia (EAU)
  • Surgery: TURP slightly > drugs; no new modality beats TURP
  • Operate if: BPH-LUTS refractory to medical therapy; counsel on nocturia
  • Antimuscarinics: best for severe urgency, small night volumes
  • Only nocturnal-urgency primary trial: fesoterodine — positive
  • Tolterodine ER nocturia trial: primary negative; urge-related voids fell
  • Behavioural ≥ drugs for nocturia; step-up trial → behavioural first-line
  • Third-line: onabotulinumtoxinA, PTNS > placebo; PTNS ≈ tolterodine
  • EAU grades: male LUTD drugs all weak; female antimuscarinic, PFMT strong
  • β3-agonists: no trial with nocturia as primary outcome
  • OAB first-line: behavioural therapy before drugs
  • GSM: vaginal oestrogen; ospemifene
  • Silodosin: 4 mg if CrCl 30–50; contraindicated <30
  • Antimuscarinic CI: urinary/gastric retention, narrow-angle glaucoma
  • Beers list: antimuscarinics ≥65 years; mirabegron → hypertension
  • 5ARI: PSA halves in 3–6 months; pregnancy category X
  • Drug causes: CCBs (oedema), SGLT-2 (glycosuria), SSRIs, lithium, ketamine

Additional treatment considerations

  • Refractory: Sleep CALM → conservative → drugs (selected)
  • Target: nocturnal volume — helps even without NP
  • DDAVP: selective V2 agonist, no pressor effect; >80 countries
  • DANS (FDA March 2017): 1.66 mcg <65; 0.83 mcg ≥65/at risk; 30 min before bed
  • ODT (FDA June 2018): women 25, men 50 mcg; sublingual, no water, 60 min
  • Sodium (FDA): baseline → ≤7 days → ≈30 days → periodic
  • Sodium (EAU): baseline → day 3 → day 7 → 1 month → periodic
  • DDAVP contraindications: low baseline sodium (EAU avoid); loop diuretics (US label)
  • Old high-dose tablets: Na <130 in ≈5%
  • EAU Male: desmopressin <65; low dose >65 with ≥2 voids from NP
  • Furosemide: diuresis 6–8 h; give >6 h before bed
  • Timed diuretic: morning/early afternoon; level 1b for NP

Antidiuretic and Diuretic Therapy: Key Trials

  • Old tablets: 50% response 34% vs 3% (men), 46% vs 7% (women)
  • DANS pooled (n 1333, ≥50 y): −1.4/−1.5 vs −1.2; Na event 0/1.1%
  • ODT dose-ranging: women respond at lower doses
  • ODT women 25 mcg: −1.46 vs −1.24; Na <130 2.2%
  • ODT men 50/75 mcg: −1.25/−1.29 vs −0.88; Na <130 1.9% vs 7.4%
  • Japan women 25 mcg: not significant (only negative primary)
  • Responder cut-off: 50% (tablets, DANS) vs 33% (ODT)
  • Men 75 mcg: little extra efficacy, more hyponatraemia → 50 mcg
  • Proposed monitoring: stop if Na <135 → prevented all <130
  • Why women lower dose: V2 gene on X chromosome
Classic traps
  • Trap: first morning void is a nocturia episode — Actually: daytime void; volume counts in NUV
  • Trap: one void/night is not nocturia — Actually: counts; bother drives care
  • Trap: NPi >33% diagnoses NP reliably — Actually: poor discrimination (Krimpen)
  • Trap: night-only diary is complete — Actually: misses global polyuria
  • Trap: small 24-h capacity raises NBCi — Actually: NBCi may be normal
  • Trap: nocturia is a LUTS — Actually: only when LUTD contributes
  • Trap: significant P = useful nocturia effect — Actually: fractions of a void
  • Trap: diuretics cause nocturia — Actually: timed early, they treat NP
  • Trap: afternoon furosemide + desmopressin — Actually: loop diuretic contraindicates DDAVP (US)
  • Trap: same desmopressin dose both sexes — Actually: women lower; hyponatraemia at higher
