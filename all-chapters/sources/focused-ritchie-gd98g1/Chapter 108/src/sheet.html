Chapter 108 — Urinary Incontinence and Pelvic Prolapse: Epidemiology and Pathophysiology — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else

  • POP-Q (Pelvic Organ Prolapse Quantification) = recommended staging; hymen = 0, cm, stage by most distal point
  • Stage 2 = −1 to +1; stage 3 > +1 to < +(tvl − 2); stage 4 ≥ +(tvl − 2)
  • Asymptomatic stage 1–2 POP (pelvic organ prolapse) = normal; symptoms start at the hymen
  • Most specific POP symptom: seen or felt vaginal bulge
  • Anterior commonest; apical almost always present with significant anterior POP → add vault suspension
  • CS (caesarean section) NNT (number needed to treat) 13 to prevent 1 moderate–severe UI (urinary incontinence); lost with any vaginal birth
  • OPUS: prophylactic MUS (mid-urethral sling) at vaginal POP repair → UI 27.3% vs 43%, NNT 6.3, more harms
  • VLPP (Valsalva leak point pressure) <60 cm H₂O → ISD (intrinsic sphincteric deficiency)
  • Post-RP (radical prostatectomy) UI: 8–60% at 1 year; ISD >70% on UDS (urodynamic studies)
  • Male remission up to 40% vs female ≈12%/year; male UUI (urgency UI) > MUI (mixed) > SUI (stress)

Definition and classification of UI

  • Treat for bother: QoL (quality-of-life) impact = commonest reason to intervene
  • Sign = clinician-observed; symptom = patient report; observation = diary or UDS

Terminology of LUTS and incontinence

  • Standard: ICS/IUGA (International Continence Society / International Urogynecological Association) joint report, 2010
  • Frequency: ≥8 voids/24 h
  • UD (urodynamic) SUI: leak with ↑ abdominal pressure, no detrusor contraction
  • UUI needs no DO (detrusor overactivity) on UDS
  • Continuous UI → fistula (vesicovaginal, ureterovaginal)
  • Increased sensation: early desire, still deferrable; may be <50 mL filling

Epidemiology of UI in females

  • UI in pregnancy ≈40%; SUI ≈two-thirds
  • Primiparous: ≈3× UI in pregnancy; ≈2.5× at 1 year
  • Hysterectomy resolves UI in 13%: uterus >300 g, BMI (body mass index) <25, coexisting POP
  • Vaginal/laparoscopic hysterectomy → more UI than open
  • Annual incidence: 1–10% at 40–60 (≈3% per subtype); 10–20% older
  • Annual remission up to ≈12%; MUI remits least
  • Menopause (48–54) → ↑ SUI incidence, not UUI

Risk factors for UI in females

  • Age: strongest non-modifiable; SUI peaks 40–59 → UUI/MUI >60
  • LTCF (long-term care facility) UI >40%; ADL (activities of daily living) impairment strongest link
  • Nonagenarians: UI = independent risk factor for death
  • Vaginal delivery → SUI, not UUI; baby ≥4 kg → OR ≈1.5
  • Single delivery → OR ≈1.5; strongest for SUI
  • First pregnancy >30 → OR ≈3 (vs other data: near 20 worst)
  • BMI ≥30 → >2× UI; reversible with weight loss
  • Oral oestrogen ± progestogen → ≈2× incident UI; vaginal oestrogen not
  • White > Black/Asian (↑ urethral closure pressure in Black women)
  • Caffeine → OAB (overactive bladder)/UUI, not SUI; smoking → urgency
  • Type 2 DM (diabetes mellitus) → up to 2× UI; macroalbuminuria, neuropathic pain

Epidemiology of UI in males

  • Prevalence 11–34% older men; up to 11% daily
  • Incidence ≈1–10%/year at 60–70
  • Male SUI = radical pelvic surgery or neurological disease
  • TURP (transurethral resection of the prostate) UI ≈1%
  • Robotic vs open RP: comparable UI

Definitions and classification of POP

  • Name the segment (anterior, apical, posterior), not cystocele/rectocele
  • Baden–Walker: halfway system, grades 0–4, hymen reference, high interobserver variability
  • POP-Q: developed 1993, published 1996 (Bump), updated 2002, recommended 2016
  • Aa/Ap: 3 cm inside hymen; range −3 to +3
  • Ba/Bp = −3 normal; = C in total eversion
  • D omitted without cervix; gh, pb, tvl measured at rest
  • Stage 1: >1 cm above hymen (< −1)
  • Simplified POP-Q: 4 points (Ba, Bp, C, D)
  • Postmenopausal bleeding never from POP → gynaecological evaluation
  • Pessary trial positive → surgery likely to help
  • Imaging usually unnecessary; MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) defecography for enterocele or mismatch

Epidemiology of POP

  • Symptomatic POP 3–12%; support loss on examination 43–90%
  • POSST (Pelvic Organ Support Study): no POP 24%; stage 1 38%, stage 2 35%, stage 3 2%
  • WHI (Women's Health Initiative): anterior 34%, posterior 19%, uterine 14%
  • WHI incidence/100 woman-years: cystocele 9.3, rectocele 5.7, uterine 1.5
  • Progression: age >60, Ba ≥4 cm, gh ≥5 cm
  • Oxford Family Planning Study: RR (relative risk) 8.4 (2 births), 10.85 (≥4)
  • Each extra delivery (up to 5) → +10–20%
  • Forceps > vaginal > CS only > nulliparous
  • Labored CS ≈ vaginal; elective CS ≈ nulliparous
  • Age: ≈+40% prevalence per decade; surgery peaks in 7th decade
  • Hysterectomy for POP → 5.5× POP; 29 vs 16/1000 woman-years
  • Constipation → 2.5×; sibling of severe POP ≈5×
  • Hispanic highest, Black lowest; oestrogen status not consistently linked
  • Recurrence: preoperative stage 3–4

Relationship between UI and POP

  • SUI → anterior POP in 40–55%; OAB → anterior POP ≈20%
  • OAB improves in 60–80% after POP repair; anterior/apical > posterior
  • Occult SUI: seen only after POP reduced; up to ¼ of advanced POP
  • De novo SUI risk: age >66, DM, low urethral closure pressure
  • TOMUS (Trial of Mid-Urethral Slings): stage 2 POP 49%, stage 3 6%
  • CARE (Colpopexy and Urinary Reduction Efforts): Burch at sacrocolpopexy → postoperative SUI 38% vs 58%
  • OPUS harms: perforation 6.7%, UTI (urinary tract infection) 31% vs 18.3%, bleeding 3.1%

Consequences of UI and prolapse

  • UI costs >$50 billion/year (United States); female SUI >$12 billion, >70% out-of-pocket
  • OAB: ≈42 million Americans, ≈$25 billion/year
  • Most bothersome LUTS (lower urinary tract symptoms): urgency and UUI
  • Lifetime surgery risk for POP/UI 11% (Olsen 1997); reoperation 29%
  • POP = commonest hysterectomy indication >54
  • 38% with POP avoid intercourse

Physiology of urinary continence

  • Storage: parasympathetic (pelvic) off; sympathetic (hypogastric) on
  • Onuf's nucleus: sacral pudendal motor nucleus → tonic external sphincter
  • Compliant bladder: type I collagen; poor detrusor cell coupling
  • Rhabdosphincter: type I slow-twitch; >40 cm H₂O continuously
  • Male bladder neck competent; female often incompetent
  • Female mucosal seal: oestrogen-sensitive; up to 30% of continence force

Pathophysiology of UI: general principles

  • MSA (multiple system atrophy): ≥20% UI after TURP
  • RP = commonest surgical cause of male UI
  • Suprapontine lesion (CVA (cerebrovascular accident), MS (multiple sclerosis), Parkinson disease) → NDO (neurogenic DO) → UUI
  • Conus/lumbosacral lesion, neuropathy → overflow
  • Cervical/upper thoracic SCI (spinal cord injury) → DSD (detrusor–sphincter dyssynergia) + NDO

Pathophysiology of SUI in females

  • Pressure transmission: Enhorning 1961 — urethral descent out of abdominal pressure zone
  • Hammock: DeLancey 1994 — urethra compressed against firm vaginal backing
  • Integral theory: Petros and Ulmsten 1990 — PUL (pubourethral ligament) laxity → basis of MUS
  • ISD: McGuire and Lytton 1978 — pubovaginal fascial sling
  • Type III UI = ISD (Blaivas and Olsson 1988); "pipestem" urethra
  • Burch is less effective in severe ISD

Pathophysiology of insensible incontinence

  • Post-void loss → diverticulum → MRI; continuous → ectopic ureter → upper tracts

Pelvic floor support mechanism

  • DeLancey level I (upper third; uterosacral–cardinal) → apical prolapse
  • Level II (mid; ATFP (arcus tendineus fasciae pelvis)) → anterior wall prolapse
  • Level III (distal; perineal membrane) → urethral hypermobility, distal rectocele, perineal descent
  • Richardson 1981: commonest cystocele defect = ATFP detached from ischial spine

Pathophysiology of POP

  • Levator defects on MRI in up to 20% of primiparous women, 0% of nulliparous
  • Levator nerves S3–S5; pudendal nerve → sphincters and perineum
  • Ehlers–Danlos/Marfan → ↑ POP

Classic traps

  • Trap: UUI needs DO on UDS — Actually: no UD finding required
  • Trap: CS always protects — Actually: lost with one vaginal birth; labored CS ≈ vaginal for POP
  • Trap: Isolated cystocele → anterior repair alone — Actually: apex almost always involved
  • Trap: Constipation → posterior repair — Actually: only if bulge or splinting
  • Trap: POP causes pain or bleeding — Actually: look for other causes
  • Trap: Vaginal oestrogen causes UI — Actually: oral does; vaginal does not
  • Trap: Stage 1–2 POP needs repair — Actually: normal if asymptomatic
  • Trap: Hammock = reposition urethra — Actually: restore backing
  • Trap: Burch works in ISD — Actually: sling/bulking; Burch fails
  • Trap: Pessary removed → measure — Actually: underestimates prolapse
