Chapter 104 — Pathophysiology and Classification of LUT Dysfunction

If you read nothing else
  • All LUT dysfunction = bladder or outlet, failing to store or empty (Wein)
  • Enhorning: stress continence needs ≥ equal pressure transmission to proximal urethra
  • UUI → assume detrusor overactivity (DO); urgency alone often no DO on UDS
  • BOO hallmark: high detrusor pressure + low flow
  • Polyuria >40 mL/kg/24 h (≈2.8 L at 70 kg)
  • Nocturnal polyuria: NPI >20% young, >33% elderly
  • ISD alone can cause SUI; hypermobility alone cannot
  • Suprasacral → DO ± DSD; sacral → acontractile; suprapontine → synergic
  • Male sphincteric UI ≈ female ISD, never hypermobility
  • Collagen-driven low compliance → drug-unresponsive

Normal LUT function

  • Storage passive: elastic/viscoelastic wall, no parasympathetic drive
  • Voiding centre: rostral brainstem; parasympathetic outflow sacral (pelvic nerve)
  • Guarding reflex: pudendal → ↑ striated sphincter activity on filling
  • α1 → ↑ bladder neck tone; β3 → detrusor relaxation
  • Smooth sphincter: involuntary, physiological not anatomical
  • Extramural striated sphincter = voluntary external sphincter (male membranous, female mid-urethra)
  • Bladder neck relaxation: NANC (non-adrenergic non-cholinergic), probably NO
  • Funnelling: base–urethra longitudinal muscle continuity
  • Most important afferents: pelvic nerve → sacral cord
  • Normal filling sensation: fullness — never urgency or pain
  • Klevmark: cystometric pressure rise = supraphysiological filling artefact
  • Tanagho (1978): active reflex rise in closure pressure on straining
  • Mucosal seal: atrophic/scarred mucosa seals poorly
  • Oestrogen for postmenopausal UI: controversial

Pathophysiology

  • Storage failure: bladder overactivity (DO or ↓ compliance), ↓ outlet resistance, altered sensation
  • Emptying failure: ↓ contractility or ↑ outlet resistance
  • OAB (ICS): urgency ± UUI, usually frequency + nocturia
  • Low compliance: sacral/infrasacral neurology; long-standing BOO (collagen)
  • Genuine SUI: competent at rest, fails on strain; ISD: incompetent at rest
  • ISD overt: open bladder neck, low ALPP and closure pressure; after surgery, scarring, neurology
  • ISD → coaptation/compression; hypermobility-only repair fails
  • DeLancey hammock: unstable suburethral layer → SUI
  • Continence point in ~half of continent females: mid-urethra
  • Hypermobility alone ≠ diagnosis: needs demonstrated UI
  • Underactivity causes: neurogenic, iatrogenic pelvic surgery, overdistension, drugs, diabetes
  • ICS BOO: ↓ flow and/or ↑ PVR + ↑ detrusor pressure; commoner in males
  • Male BOO: prostate, bladder neck contracture, stricture
  • Female BOO: prolapse, diverticulum, post-incontinence-surgery
  • Striated sphincter dyssynergia: common functional obstruction (neurological)
  • True smooth sphincter dyssynergia: unusual, except autonomic dysreflexia
  • Bladder neck dysfunction: young/middle-aged, normal anatomy
  • Obstruction → intervene promptly: hypertrophy, collagen, diverticula, DO
  • Emptying failure fallback: intermittent catheterisation
  • Flow + PVR integrate bladder and outlet; DLPP bladder, VLPP outlet
  • Storage drugs: antimuscarinics, β-agonists, onabotulinumtoxinA (intradetrusor); outlet: α-agonists
  • Striated sphincter relaxants: baclofen, benzodiazepines, dantrolene, botulinum

Pain, nocturia, NLUTD

  • IC/BPS urgency: driven by pain worsening with filling
  • Aδ = myelinated, normal; C = unmyelinated, silent, sensitised
  • Central sensitisation → OAB–IBS crosstalk
  • NPI = night volume (incl. first morning void) ÷ 24-h
  • Polyuria causes: DM, central/nephrogenic DI, primary polydipsia
  • Elderly: lost nocturnal vasopressin rise
  • OSA → ↑ ANP → ↓ vasopressin → nocturnal polyuria
  • NLUTD (ICS): needs confirmed relevant neurological disorder

Classification systems

  • Four: function, symptoms, signs, urodynamics
  • Functional: no exact diagnosis or UDS needed to treat
  • Symptoms cannot alone diagnose
  • T10 paraplegia: DO + dyssynergia; treat one as primary
  • T10 UDS: neurogenic DO, absent sensation, low capacity, DSD
  • Stroke UUI: neurogenic DO, normal sensation, normal voiding
  • Cystometric capacity: “permission to void”; maximum: cannot delay
  • Underactive = inadequate magnitude/duration; acontractile = none seen
  • Normal urethral storage: positive closure pressure
Classic traps
  • Trap: raised intravesical pressure = voiding — Actually: cough needs no contraction
  • Trap: hypermobility = SUI — Actually: common in continent females
  • Trap: urgency without leak excludes DO — Actually: suggests DO
  • Trap: low flow = obstruction — Actually: flow integrates bladder and outlet
  • Trap: underactivity only an emptying failure — Actually: also overflow storage failure
  • Trap: DSD from suprapontine lesion — Actually: suprasacral; suprapontine synergic
  • Trap: terminal dribble post-micturition — Actually: voiding; after-dribble is post-micturition
  • Trap: C fibres large-calibre — Actually: small, unmyelinated
  • Trap: NPI excludes first morning void — Actually: includes it
  • Trap: nocturia needs ≥2 wakings — Actually: one or more
