Chapter 46 — Neuromuscular Dysfunction of the Lower Urinary Tract in Children

IF YOU READ NOTHING ELSE

  • Detrusor leak point pressure (DLPP) >40 cm H₂O → vesicoureteric reflux (VUR) / upper-tract damage (McGuire 1981)
  • Hostile ≥40; intermediate 25–39; safe <25 cm H₂O
  • Clean intermittent catheterisation (CIC) + antimuscarinic early = standard for hostile neonatal bladder
  • Expectant vs early: end-stage renal disease (ESRD) ~30% vs 0–1.6%
  • Urodynamics (UDS) within 3 months; after spinal cord injury (SCI) only once spinal shock resolves (3–4 months)
  • Folic acid: 400 µg all women; 4–5 mg if affected; start ≥4 weeks pre-conception
  • Dilating VUR (III–V): 7% of spina bifida (SB) neonates; avoid Credé
  • Botulinum toxin A (BTA): 10 U/kg, max 360 U, ~30 sites, spare trigone
  • Dilating VUR at augmentation → reimplant simultaneously
  • Autonomic dysreflexia: lesion ≥T6 → monitor blood pressure (BP), α-blocker ready during UDS

NEURAL TUBE DEFECTS

  • Commonest cause of paediatric neuropathic lower urinary tract (LUT) dysfunction: neural tube defect (NTD); myelomeningocele is commonest and most severe
  • Cut-offs: ~10% of SB deaths urological; survival ~90% at 10 years; first-year mortality ~5%
  • Strongest infant mortality predictors: preterm (RR ~4.5), low birth weight (RR ~4.8)
  • Neural tube closure: day 18 → 35, cephalad → caudal
  • Endoscopic third ventriculostomy + choroid plexus cauterisation: >70% shunt-free
  • Myelomeningocele level: lumbosacral 47%, lumbar 26%, sacral 20%, thoracic 7%
  • Bony level ≠ neurological level (±1–3 vertebrae) → UDS, not anatomy
  • Sacral dimple, hair tuft, vascular malformation, abnormal cleft in ~90% occult dysraphism
  • SB occulta up to 15% population — normal variant
  • Recurrence: 5% after one affected pregnancy; ≤10% after two
  • Arnold–Chiari II: almost all open NTDs
  • Folic acid: supplements ↓50–70%; fortification ↓20–50%
  • Folate antagonists: valproate, carbamazepine
  • MOMS (Management of Myelomeningocele Study):
    • prenatal repair <26 weeks: death/shunt RR (relative risk) 0.70; 40% still shunted
    • 34.1 vs 37.3 weeks; 13% <30 weeks; ⅓ uterine dehiscence/thinning

NEUROPATHIC LOWER URINARY TRACT DYSFUNCTION

  • Management priority: kidneys first → continence → urinary tract infection (UTI) → sexual function
  • Normal compliance >15 mL/cm H₂O
  • UDS timing:
    • safe from 3 months
    • SBA (Spina Bifida Association): annually to 36 months
    • 2–3 months after changing antimuscarinic
    • closed NTD/tumour: before + 3–4 months after surgery
  • Gold standards:
    • video UDS for cystography
    • needle electromyography (EMG) for detrusor–sphincter dyssynergia (DSD)
    • dimercaptosuccinic acid (DMSA) for cortical defects
    • clearance study for glomerular filtration rate (GFR)
  • Patch EMG unreliable → DSD dropped from UMPIRE high-risk category
  • Newborn needle EMG: ~40% intact / ~25% partial / ~35% complete denervation
  • UDS after SCI: ≥6 weeks (International Children's Continence Society, ICCS)
  • UMPIRE (CDC urologic and renal protocol) baseline:
    • ultrasound 56% normal / 40% mild / 4% moderate–severe
    • DMSA defects 8%
  • Ultrasound: 6, 12, 18, 24 months, then yearly
  • Spinal ultrasound before posterior arch ossification (first months)
  • Cystatin C > creatinine in SB (muscle-independent); creatinine baseline still advised
  • Vitamin B12 yearly from 2 years after reconstruction
  • Post-augmentation cystoscopy: EAU (European Association of Urology) annual from 10–15 years
  • No antibiotic prophylaxis on CIC; treat symptomatic UTI only
  • Vesicostomy = temporary bridge; augmentation = definitive; ileum preferred (British Association of Paediatric Urologists, BAPU)
  • SBA UTI (<3 years): urinalysis + catheter culture + fever ≥38.0 °C
  • Receptors: M3 mediates contraction; M2 more numerous
  • First-line: CIC (Lapides 1971) + antimuscarinic; oxybutynin commonest
  • Oxybutynin:
    • oral 0.2 mg/kg/dose up to three times daily, max 5 mg/dose
    • patch 3.9 mg/day, ~20% stop (skin)
    • intravesical 0.1–0.9 mg/kg/day
  • FDA (US Food and Drug Administration) paediatric approval:
    • oxybutynin ≥5 years; solifenacin ≥2 years; mirabegron ≥3 years
    • fesoterodine ≥6 years and >25 kg
    • vibegron: no
  • Trospium: quaternary, half-life 8 vs 2 hours (oxybutynin), few CNS effects
  • Fesoterodine = tolterodine metabolite (5-hydroxymethyl tolterodine); less variability
  • β3 agonist side effect: hypertension
  • Sympathomimetics: bladder-neck α-receptors; unlicensed; retention if voiding
  • Early vs expectant:
    • scarring 4–12% vs 27–50%
    • hydronephrosis 0–15% vs 18–80%
    • UTI 0.3/year vs ~50% by 15 months
    • 18–24% fewer augmentations
  • No benefit of early intervention if empties spontaneously
  • BTA:
    • continence 65–87%; lasts 6–10.5 months
    • minimum age 2 years
    • ~90% avoid augmentation
    • dose reduction → relapse
  • VUR:
    • 30–40% by 5 years untreated
    • resolves 30–55% with CIC + antimuscarinic
  • Grade I–III and emptying → prophylaxis only; IV–V or not emptying → CIC
  • Bacteriuria on CIC: harmless unless high-grade VUR
  • Credé: harmful with reactive sphincter; harmless if denervated
  • Operate (antireflux) if:
    • febrile UTI despite prophylaxis + CIC
    • persistent hydroureteronephrosis despite emptying
    • ureterovesical junction anomaly
  • Antireflux success ~95% since CIC (Jeffs 1976); unilateral VUR → unilateral surgery
  • Endoscopic vs open: 61–73% vs 84–96%; 40% recur at 4.5 years; ~95% with BTA

SEXUAL FUNCTION

  • Central precocious puberty: ~50% girls, 10–30% boys
  • Erectile dysfunction (ED) 75% (≥18 years); sildenafil ~80% effective (randomised controlled trial)
  • Male reproductive function: S1 or lower → normal; above → ~50%
  • Female sexual dysfunction 55.5%
  • 70–80% of women conceive, uneventful pregnancy; caesarean common
  • Offspring SB 3.7%; both parents ~15%
  • Absolute contraindications: intrauterine device; latex with allergy
  • Relative: combined oestrogen–progestin → thromboembolism if immobile

OTHER CONDITIONS ASSOCIATED WITH NEUROPATHIC LOWER URINARY TRACT DYSFUNCTION

  • Cerebral palsy (CP):
    • commonest childhood physical disability; ~3.3/1000
    • continence late
    • median continence 3.6–4.1 years (diplegia/hemiplegia, good intellect) vs 10–13 years (tetraplegia)
    • upper tract <5%; UDS ⅓ normal; DSD ~12%
  • SCI: adolescents 6× children; spinal shock → underactive bladder, non-reactive sphincter
  • Suprasacral SCI → overactivity + DSD; cauda equina → no return
  • Transverse myelitis (TM):
    • retention >95% acute; areflexia ~70% acute
    • spinal shock ≤6 weeks
    • 57–73% need CIC
    • >½ on CIC still need it at 1 year
    • ~1400/year (USA), 28% children; peaks 10–19 years
    • acute treatment: steroids, plasma exchange, intravenous immunoglobulin
  • Anorectal malformation (ARM):
    • 1 in 5000
    • urinary anomalies high 70% vs low 35%
    • cord anomaly 18–50%; neuropathic 18–35%
    • spinal ultrasound <3 months
    • UDS only if bony spine anomaly, cord defect, or VCUG/ultrasound signs
    • UDS before definitive surgery → after pull-through; ESRD ~2%
  • Syndromic ARM: VACTERL, MURCS, OEIS, Currarino
  • Currarino: ARM + sacrococcygeal defect + presacral mass; tethered >80%
  • Peña posterior sagittal anorectoplasty (PSARP): nerve-sparing; not the cause of neuropathic bladder
  • Krickenbeck: ARM classification by fistula site
  • Sacrococcygeal teratoma: 35–45% neuropathic → UDS before + after surgery, all
  • Rhabdomyosarcoma:
    • frequency–volume chart all survivors
    • radiotherapy → high risk
    • ~20% reconstruction/diversion
  • Hirschsprung: UDS only if symptomatic, 6 months post-op
    • unstable detrusor ≤45%, often resolves after resection
  • Nerve injury:
    • parasympathetic (pelvic splanchnic) → underactive
    • sympathetic (hypogastric) → poor compliance

CLASSIC TRAPS

  • Trap: bony level predicts bladder — Actually: unpredictable; do UDS
  • Trap: normal creatinine = normal GFR in SB — Actually: low muscle mass masks loss; use cystatin C
  • Trap: high-grade VUR with "safe" pressures — Actually: VUR pops off pressure; falsely reassuring
  • Trap: patch EMG diagnoses DSD — Actually: unreliable (UMPIRE); needle EMG
  • Trap: repeat BTA causes fibrosis or tachyphylaxis — Actually: neither; fibrosis falls
  • Trap: augmentation alone cures dilating VUR — Actually: persists 20–47%; reimplant simultaneously
  • Trap: antimuscarinics impair cognition — Actually: no effect (randomised double-blind trial)
  • Trap: PSARP causes ARM neuropathic bladder — Actually: associated cord anomaly
  • Trap: MOMS abolished shunting — Actually: 40% still shunted; more prematurity
  • Trap: female fertility impaired in SB — Actually: hormonal control, preserved
