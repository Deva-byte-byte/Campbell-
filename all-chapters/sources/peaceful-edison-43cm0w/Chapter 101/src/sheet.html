Chapter 101 — Surgical, Radiographic, and Endoscopic Anatomy of the Female Pelvis

IF YOU READ NOTHING ELSE

  • Ureter under uterine artery ("water under the bridge") → hysterectomy injury site.
  • Sacrospinous ligament (SSL) fixation → risks pudendal nerve (near ischial spine) and sacral plexus (just posterior).
  • Sacrocolpopexy sutures → anterior longitudinal ligament (ALL) over S1, not L5/S1 disc.
  • Presacral venous plexus: valveless; 1–2 mm vein → >1 L/min bleeding.
  • Lithotomy foot drop → common peroneal nerve at fibular head.
  • Distal ⅓ urethra → inguinal nodes; proximal ⅔ → iliac and obturator.
  • Left ovarian vein → left renal vein; right → inferior vena cava (IVC).
  • Bladder dome injury → intraperitoneal → operative repair.
  • T2-weighted MRI = best test for urethral diverticulum.
  • Internal iliac ligation tolerated (collaterals); external iliac ligation → limb emergency.

TRANSVAGINAL APPROACH

  • Vulva = mons, labia majora and minora, clitoris, vestibule.
  • Meatus 1 cm anterior to vaginal opening.
  • Skene's glands = prostate homologue; ventral to meatus.
  • Bartholin's gland under posterior vestibular bulb → cyst or abscess.
  • Crura → ischiocavernosus; bulbs → bulbospongiosus.
  • Perineal membrane = old "urogenital diaphragm"; pierced by urethra and vagina.
  • Deep space: striated sphincters, deep transverse perinei. Superficial: bulbs, clitoris, Bartholin's.
  • Colles fascia = female dartos; roof of superficial space.
  • Perineal body = bulbospongiosus + external anal sphincter + superficial and deep transverse perinei.
  • Episiotomy angled laterally → spares external anal sphincter.
  • Female pelvis: oval inlet (male heart-shaped); obtuse subpubic angle; short wide sacrum.
  • Standing: ASIS and symphysis vertical; inlet faces anteriorly → load onto bone.
  • Ischial spine ≈ S5; landmark for apical suspension.
  • Interspinous line divides true pelvis anterior vs posterior compartments.
  • True vs false pelvis: below vs above pelvic brim (promontory → symphysis).
  • Sacral promontory neurovascular bed: avoid in mesh fixation.
  • S1 foramina fan lateral to S4 → finding S3 for neuromodulation.
  • SSL: ischial spine → lower sacrum and coccyx.
  • Greater sciatic foramen: piriformis, sacral plexus. Lesser: pudendal nerve and vessels, obturator internus.
  • Cooper's ligament → Burch urethropexy anchor.
  • Obturator canal superolateral → transobturator trocar: groin pain, bleeding.
  • Levator ani = puborectalis + pubococcygeus + iliococcygeus; S3–S5 (mainly S4).
  • Puborectalis = anorectal sling; pubococcygeus = pubovaginal (urethrovaginal) sling.
  • Wide levator hiatus → prolapse.
  • Levator plate = horizontal visceral shelf; loses orientation with lost tone.
  • Coccygeus: ischial spine → sacrum and coccyx, on the SSL.
  • Arcus tendineus: ischial spine → pubis.
  • Martius flap: labial fat; dual supply — external pudendal (femoral) + internal pudendal.
  • Sacral plexus L4–S4. Pudendal S2–S4 → Alcock's canal.
  • Pudendal block: SSL near ischial spine, transvaginal.
  • Pudendal branches: inferior rectal, perineal, dorsal nerve of clitoris.
  • Pelvic floor: direct S3–S5; pelvic somatic nerves (L4–S4) → levator and striated urethral sphincter.
  • Ilioinguinal injury: hernia repair, appendicectomy, caesarean, abdominal hysterectomy.
  • Genitofemoral injury: herniorrhaphy, lymphadenectomy.
  • Vulval lymph: superficial inguinal → deep inguinal → pelvic.
  • Ilioinguinal L1, genitofemoral L1–L2: mons and labia.
  • Posterior femoral cutaneous S1–S3: perineum.
  • Post-lithotomy leg pain and swelling → compartment syndrome: surgical emergency.
  • Fascia lata harvest: avoid near lateral condyle (narrow band).

TRANSABDOMINAL APPROACH

Cut-offs:

  • Urethra 4.7–5.1 cm; cervix 2.5 cm; tubes 10–12 cm; rectum 12–15 cm.
  • Vagina: anterior 7.5 cm vs posterior 9 cm.
  • Pelvic ureter ≈ half ureteric length.
  • Aorta: T12 → bifurcates L4 (left anterolateral).
  • Common iliac artery: right ≈ 5 cm, left ≈ 4 cm.
  • Common iliac veins unite ≈ L5.
  • ALL 1.3–2.5 mm thick.
  • Mid-promontory → right ureter and left common iliac vein ≈ 2.7 cm each.

Urethra and bladder:

  • Internal sphincter: smooth, involuntary, bladder neck. External: striated, voluntary, distal ⅔.
  • Closure pressure = mucosa + submucosa; oestrogen-dependent.
  • Diverticulum = infected or ruptured periurethral gland through periurethral fascia.
  • Ventral = vaginal side; dorsal = pubic side.
  • Pubovaginal sling → bladder neck; mid-urethral sling → mid-urethra.
  • Bladder base injury → vesicovaginal or vesicouterine fistula.
  • Striated sphincter complex: urethral compressor (closes on vaginal wall) + urethrovaginal sphincter (narrows hiatus).
  • Proximal urethra + bladder neck = female internal sphincter.
  • Epithelium: transitional → squamous distally.
  • Uterus anteverted, anteflexed; posterior fornix deeper than anterior.
  • Tube: interstitial → isthmus → ampulla → infundibulum.
  • Ovarian fossa: obliterated umbilical artery, ureter, internal iliac artery.
  • Vesicovaginal space: divide bladder–vaginal smooth muscle fibres (fistula repair).
  • Rectum: retroperitoneal, no taeniae or haustra.

Ureter danger points:

  • Pelvic brim over common iliac bifurcation.
  • Under uterine artery; beside cardinal ligament near cervix.
  • Anterior vaginal fornix (transvaginal surgery).
  • Lateral to uterosacral ligament, closest at cervix.
  • Left ureter: base of sigmoid mesocolon; landmark intersigmoid fossa.

Ligaments:

  • Infundibulopelvic: ovary → side wall; carries ovarian vessels.
  • Ovarian ligament: ovary → uterus.
  • Round ligament = gubernaculum homologue; ends in labium majus.
  • Cardinal (Mackenrodt's): side wall → cervix; uterine vessels.
  • Uterosacral: sacrum S2–S4 → cervix; apical suspension anchor.
  • Pubourethral = puboprostatic homologue; weakness → stress urinary incontinence (SUI).
  • Vesicopelvic fascia defect → lateral cystocele.
  • Most support = intermediate fascial stratum (parametrium, paracolpium).
  • Cardinal and uterosacral carry pelvic plexus → hysterectomy → bladder dysfunction.
  • Uterosacral suspension → sacral nerve (S1–S4) entrapment.

Vessels and nodes:

  • Internal iliac divides at upper border of greater sciatic foramen.
  • Posterior division = parietal only: iliolumbar, lateral sacral, superior gluteal.
  • Middle sacral artery behind left common iliac vein.
  • Obturator and uterine artery origins variable.
  • Uterine collaterals: contralateral uterine + ovarian (aorta, below renals).
  • Internal iliac vein posteromedial to artery; often duplicated.
  • External iliac vein = femoral vein continuation behind inguinal ligament.
  • Most visceral lymph → internal iliac (presacral, obturator, internal pudendal).
  • Bladder and vagina → middle external iliac chain.
  • Upper ⅔ vagina → pelvic; lower ⅓ and vulva → inguinal.
  • Ovary and tube → along gonadal vessels (para-aortic).
  • Common iliac nodes → lateral aortic nodes.
  • Leissner levels: 1 iliac, obturator, perivesical → 2 common iliac, presacral → 3 paracaval, para-aortic, interaortocaval.

ENDOSCOPIC ANATOMY

  • 30° lens at bladder neck → orthotopic orifices on trigonal ridge.
  • Patulous orifice → reflux; ectopic → duplication.
  • Prolapse obscuring orifices → vaginal sponge stick.
  • Impressions: symphysis anterior; bowel lateral; uterus posterior.
  • Squamous metaplasia, cystitis cystica: biopsy if in doubt.
  • Post-radiation mucosa: friable, hypervascular.
  • Urethroscopy: coaptation at mid-urethra and bladder neck; look for mesh, stones, diverticular ostia.

RADIOGRAPHY

  • Translabial ultrasound: mesh, slings, bulking agents; bladder neck mobility on Valsalva.
  • Endoanal ultrasound: internal sphincter defect = echogenic break; external = hypoechoic lesion.
  • MRI = most comprehensive radiation-free pelvic imaging.
  • Dynamic pelvic floor MRI: T2 HASTE or SSFSE (fast single-shot sequences).
  • Uterine T2: endometrium high, junctional zone low, myometrium intermediate–high.
  • Benign vaginal cysts: müllerian, epidermal inclusion, Gartner duct, Bartholin, Skene.
  • Pubococcygeal line: inferior symphysis → last coccygeal joint.
  • MR defecography: descent below line → mild, moderate or severe per compartment.
  • MR defecography if defaecatory dysfunction or symptoms > examination findings.

CLASSIC TRAPS

  • Trap: infundibulopelvic ligament joins ovary to uterus — Actually: ovary to side wall; ovarian ligament joins uterus.
  • Trap: lithotomy foot drop is a pelvic nerve injury — Actually: common peroneal compression at fibular head.
  • Trap: both ovarian veins drain to IVC — Actually: left → left renal vein.
  • Trap: whole urethra drains to pelvic nodes — Actually: distal ⅓ → inguinal.
  • Trap: posterior internal iliac division supplies viscera — Actually: parietal branches only.
  • Trap: pubovaginal sling sits mid-urethra — Actually: bladder neck; synthetic sling mid-urethra.
  • Trap: sacrocolpopexy sutures into L5/S1 disc — Actually: ALL over S1 (discitis risk).
  • Trap: dome injury heals with catheter — Actually: intraperitoneal → repair.
  • Trap: internal sphincter tear is hypoechoic — Actually: echogenic break; external tear hypoechoic.
  • Trap: coccygeus is part of levator ani — Actually: separate muscle on the SSL.
