Chapter 123 — Sphincteric Incontinence in the Male: AUS and Slings — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else

  1. Artificial urinary sphincter (AUS) = gold standard, every stress urinary incontinence (SUI) severity; works after radiotherapy (RT) and without detrusor contraction.
  2. 24-h pad weight: mild <150 g, moderate <400 g → slings for mild–moderate.
  3. Severe UI or any RT → AUS first-line (AUA/SUFU guideline).
  4. MASTER trial: sling non-inferior overall; AUS better if >250 g/24 h or prior RT.
  5. Bulbar cuff proximal to corporal fusion; usually 4.0–4.5 cm.
  6. Pressure-regulating balloon (PRB) 61–70 cm H₂O bulbar; 51–60 fragile; 71–80 bladder neck; fill 23 mL.
  7. Deactivated 4–6 weeks after implant (tandem: 6 weeks).
  8. Erosion 2–15% (to 35% with multiple risks); after prior erosion ~50% at 5 years.
  9. Infection or erosion → explant all components; reimplant 3–6 months, new site.
  10. Failed AUS >3 years old → replace whole device (PRB); <3 years → failed part.

Classification, pathophysiology, aetiology

  • Commonest cause: radical prostatectomy (RP) → external sphincter alone left.
  • Male SUI requires both sphincters impaired (internal + rhabdosphincter).
  • Prevalence: 11% at 60–64; 31% at >85 years.
  • Post-RP UI (urinary incontinence): 2.5–87%; improves for up to 2 years.
  • Nerve sparing → faster return, not better final continence.

Mechanism of continence

  • Transobturator sling repositions, does not compress → needs functioning sphincter.
  • AUS cuff: ~2 cm circumferential; void by straining or Credé.
  • Virtue: prepubic compression + transobturator elevation.
  • ATOMS, ProACT: saline-adjustable via scrotal port(s).

Evaluation and diagnosis

  • Gold standard severity: 24-h pad weight; pad counts unreliable.
  • Voiding diary: 2–7 days.
  • Cough test: Grade 0 ≈ 57 g → Grade 4 ≈ 513 g; drops → stream.
  • Urinalysis ± culture mandatory; eradicate urinary tract infection first.
  • Cystoscopy mandatory: suspected stricture/anastomotic stenosis; after erosion explant.
  • Poorly coapting sphincter → AUS, not transobturator sling.
  • Urodynamics (UDS) not routine: mixed, undemonstrable, overflow, low compliance.
  • Hypocontractile detrusor → AUS over sling.
  • Check manual dexterity before AUS.
  • Leak during sleep → severe SUI rather than urgency.
  • SUFU Pad Test: tracks loss; not validated to predict surgery.

Indications for surgery

  • Operate if: irreversible sphincter incompetence + bothersome UI.
  • Operate without delay: severe/gravitational SUI, no gain by 6 months.
  • Pelvic floor training: no benefit beyond 12 months.
  • First-line: AUS or perineal sling. Bulking agents: not in post-RP algorithm.
  • Absolute (need alternative): kidney-threatening low compliance; low capacity; unusable tissue.
  • Unusable tissue → clamp, catheter, bladder neck closure, diversion.
  • Relative: bladder cancer, refractory stenosis, radiation cystitis.
  • Radiation cystitis: hyperbaric oxygen + 6 months bleeding-free before AUS.
  • No AUS: poor dexterity/cognition, poor urethra, severely low compliance.
  • AUS: social continence 55–77%; satisfaction ~90%; revision 10–29% at 3 years.
  • AUS life: 7–10 years (text) vs 8–10 years (guideline).
  • Sling cure ~60%; ProACT dry ~60% (more erosion); ATOMS dry ~67%.
  • Sling failure predictors: RT, severity, previous surgery.
  • Counsel: one thin pad/day; failed sling → AUS.
  • AUS success predictors: surgeon experience, volume, intact compliance.
  • Advance XP durable; I-STP TOMS decays over 5 years.
  • Sling in severe SUI only if AUS refused or unable to use pump.

High-risk patient

  • Fragile urethra: RT, prior failed AUS, urethroplasty, hypogonadism.
  • Also: coronary artery disease; age >80 years.
  • Erosion after RT: 3.4–33% vs 1–13%; erode sooner.
  • Urethroplasty > RT as erosion risk.
  • Diabetes, smoking: not proven independent — optimise anyway.
  • Transcorporal (TC) cuff — Webster, 2002: tunica interposed dorsally; benefit unproven.
  • Mitigation: 51–60 PRB; cuff 0.5 cm over measured.
  • RT predicts first explant, not later revisions.
  • Low testosterone = risk; replacement not proven protective.

Technique of implantation

  • Antibiotics ≤60 min: aminoglycoside 5 mg/kg + cephalosporin or vancomycin.
  • Skin: chlorhexidine–alcohol.
  • Cuff 2 cm wide; sizes 3.5–11 cm; bladder neck ≥8 cm.
  • Cuff tighter than tape; looser cuffs → better continence.
  • 3.5-cm cuff after RT → more erosion → TC and/or 51–60 PRB.
  • No ring/ectopic PRB: mesh hernia repair, cystectomy, major abdominal surgery.
  • Contrast diluted with sterile water; contrast allergy → saline.
  • Closed cuff = slight urethral blanching on urethroscopy.
  • Tandem: ≥1.5–2 cm distal, Y-connector, +3 mL; not first-line.
  • TC cuff: urethra <3.5 cm; more transient retention.
  • Transscrotal — Wilson, 2003: single incision; inferior continence (smaller cuff).
  • Bladder neck AUS: absolute contraindication after RP; for intact prostate (e.g. myelomeningocele).
  • "Gullwing": complete tunical wrap; no long-term data.
  • Cuff around bulbospongiosus: erosion rare, revision high.
  • DuraPrep: fewer skin cultures, same infection rate.

Male slings

  • Rehder–Gozzi, 2007: first transobturator male sling.
  • Indications: SUI after prostate cancer treatment, TURP, laser enucleation.
  • Stab ~1 fingerbreadth below adductor longus; outside-in; two "pops".
  • Tension to cystoscopic coaptation; bulb rises 3–4 cm.
  • ATOMS: bulbospongiosus not opened; port 9 mL.

Persistent incontinence after AUS or sling

  • Sudden → deactivation or leak; gradual → atrophy.
  • Reactivate: hold button ≥30 s.
  • PRB >20 mL on imaging → cuff problem; little/no fluid → leak.
  • Erosion: urgent explant + catheter ~3 weeks.
  • Failed sling → AUS (gold standard), results as first-time.
  • Advance loosened ≤6 weeks → re-explore, replace.
  • Worsening SUI after sling → cystoscopy (mesh erosion).
  • Second Advance sling: ~half succeed; only if AUS barred/refused.
  • PRB refills passively, changes size → atrophy, not leak.
  • Exclude PRB herniation: needs deep pelvic/submuscular site.

AUS complications

  • Retention: catheter 24–48 h → failed trial at 48 h → image-guided suprapubic tube.
  • Retention lasting weeks → undersized cuff → replace.
  • Infection 1–3% (≤10% high risk); S. epidermidis, S. aureus; late >4 months.
  • Salvage contraindicated: sepsis, ketoacidosis, necrotising infection, erosion, immunosuppression, pus.
  • Post-erosion stricture 8–35%; <50% urethral loss → catheter.
  • Re-erosion after reimplant: up to 35%.
  • Atrophy → downsize or relocate cuff; 4.0-cm cuff → move proximal, or distal TC.
  • Early infection sign: scrotal pain.
  • Erosion >50% urethral loss: best repair unresolved.
  • Catheterising an AUS patient = erosion risk; delayed activation lowers erosion.
  • Reimplant after erosion: nightly deactivation.
  • Early failures (<3 years): pump or cuff, median ≤1 year.

Special circumstances

  • ROBUST III: paclitaxel balloon 83.2% vs 21.7% (1-year); 67% at 3 years.
  • 22–24 Fr scope past cuff → uncouple cuff first.
  • Stenosis <12 Fr dilated with AUS → no excess erosion.
  • Neobladder SUI 33–95%; PRB ectopic behind rectus.
  • AUS + inflatable penile prosthesis (IPP): reoperation ~25% either timing; staged → AUS first.
  • Climacturia 20–68%; mini-jupette = 3-cm corporotomies + graft.
  • Paclitaxel balloon: bulbar data only; none for stenosis.
  • Refractory anastomotic stenosis → robotic reconstruction (spares cuff).
  • Stricture before AUS → non-transecting urethroplasty.
  • Neobladder AUS: most improve; most need revision.

Sling complications

  • Non-adjustable: commonest pain/wound; erosion/explant 0–0.4%; chronic pain 1.3%.
  • Inguinal pain: self-limiting; median VAS 0 at 3 months.
  • ATOMS: erosion + infection 10%; explant 10–15.8%.
  • ProACT: commonest perforation 5.3%; revision 22%.

Summary

  • ~90% improved urinary QoL after surgery; >150,000 men implanted.

Classic traps

  • Trap: transobturator sling compresses — Actually: repositions; needs working sphincter.
  • Trap: mild UI after RT → sling — Actually: any RT → AUS first-line.
  • Trap: raise PRB pressure for atrophy — Actually: erosion; downsize or relocate.
  • Trap: bladder neck AUS after RP — Actually: contraindicated.
  • Trap: InhibiZone cuts AUS infection — Actually: shown for penile implants only.
  • Trap: keep clean PRB at erosion explant — Actually: remove all components.
  • Trap: salvage infected eroded AUS — Actually: erosion bars salvage.
  • Trap: adjustable devices safer — Actually: ATOMS, ProACT erode/revise more.
  • Trap: large scope through active cuff — Actually: uncouple cuff first.
  • Trap: hypocontractile detrusor → sling — Actually: AUS (opens to void).
