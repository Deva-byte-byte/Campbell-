Chapter 78 — Anatomy of the Retroperitoneum — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

IF YOU READ NOTHING ELSE

  • Upper poles: left 11th rib, right 12th; hila L1; lower poles L3–L4.
  • Supracostal access > subcostal complications; supra-12th may cross pleura.
  • Intercostal bundle, top → bottom: vein → artery → nerve; nerve least protected.
  • Perirenal space open inferiorly → perinephric collections track to the pelvis.
  • Anterior lamina = Toldt; posterior lamina = Zuckerkandl (thicker).
  • Marginal artery of Drummond → IMA (inferior mesenteric artery) ligation safe.
  • Left gonadal vein → left renal vein at 90° → left varicocele.
  • New right varicocele → image for renal cell carcinoma with IVC (inferior vena cava) thrombus.
  • Left testis → left para-aortic; right testis → interaortocaval; crossover only right → left.
  • Obturator nerve + lateral-wall TURBT (transurethral resection of bladder tumour) → adduction → perforation.

Definition, Boundaries and Organs

  • Boundaries: peritoneum (front), abdominal wall (back), diaphragm (top), extraperitoneal pelvis (bottom).
  • Retroperitoneum ≠ extraperitoneal space (latter also encircles the abdomen).
  • Primarily retroperitoneal: adrenals, kidneys, ureters, aorta, IVC, anal canal.
  • Secondarily retroperitoneal: ascending and descending colon, duodenum, pancreas.
  • Exceptions (intraperitoneal): duodenum D1, pancreatic tail.
  • Lobstein 1829: first used “retroperitoneal tumour”.
  • Gerota 1895: anterior renal fascia. Zuckerkandl 1883: posterior.
  • Wickham 1979: endoscopic ureteric stone removal in pneumoretroperitoneum.
  • Gaur 1992: retroperitoneal videoscopic renal surgery.

Body Surface Landmarks

  • Right kidney lower than left (upper pole 12th vs 11th rib).
  • Hila L1; lower poles L3–L4.

Posterior Abdominal Wall

  • External oblique: ribs 5–12, fibres inferomedial; aponeurosis forms inguinal ligament.
  • Internal oblique: fibres superomedial.
  • Order: external oblique → internal oblique → transversus → transversalis fascia.
  • Psoas major: T12–L5 → lesser trochanter; anterior rami L1–L3.
  • Psoas minor: may be absent; inserts at pelvic brim.
  • Quadratus lumborum: posterolateral to psoas; stabilises 12th rib.
  • Vertebrae: 7 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar.
  • Sacral cord segments at vertebral T12–L1; specify vertebral vs segmental level.
  • Lower rib fracture → suspect retroperitoneal injury.
  • 11th rib: no neck; 12th rib: no angle, shortest; both floating.
  • Intercostal bundle: costal groove, lower border of rib above; between internal and innermost intercostals.
  • Superficial fascia: Camper = fatty layer; Scarpa = membranous layer.
  • Costovertebral ligament tethers 12th rib to L1–L2; divide it → more exposure.

Lumbodorsal Fascia

  • Three layers fuse laterally; access at 12th rib tip.
  • Posterior layer: covers erector spinae. Middle layer: erector spinae / quadratus lumborum. Anterior layer: front of quadratus lumborum.
  • Anterior layer continuous with psoas fascia.
  • Dorsal lumbotomy: vertical, lateral to erector spinae and quadratus lumborum; no muscle cut.

Retroperitoneal Fasciae and Spaces

  • Strata: outer → transversalis; intermediate → renal fascia; inner → gut fusion fascia.
  • Posterior pararenal space: posterior lamina (front); transversalis (back and side).
  • Transversalis–posterior lamina fusion: incise to reach hilum.
  • Posterior lamina thicker, seen more often on imaging.
  • Lateroconal fascia separates anterior and posterior pararenal spaces.
  • Perirenal contents: adrenal, kidney, ureter, fat, pedicle, gonadal vessels.
  • Perirenal fat fine, pale yellow vs pararenal coarse, yellow-orange.
  • Perinephric collections: urine, blood, pus; contained, but track caudally.
  • Perirenal spaces may communicate across midline below hilum.
  • Anterior pararenal space: colon, pancreas, duodenum D2–D3.
  • White line of Toldt: lateral edge of colonic mesentery fusion; reflect colon here.

Gastrointestinal Viscera and Mesentery

  • Right renal surgery: duodenum D2 at hilum → Kocher manoeuvre; pancreatic head over IVC.
  • Left renal surgery: pancreatic tail, spleen, stomach at the upper pole.
  • Duodenum 20–25 cm; D3 between SMA (superior mesenteric artery) in front, aorta behind.
  • Ampulla of Vater: common bile duct + pancreatic duct into D2.
  • Hepatocolic and splenocolic ligaments: divide sharply; retraction tears liver and spleen.
  • Bogros space: caudal extraperitoneal extension towards pelvis and bladder.
  • Close small mesenteric defects after bowel isolation; internal hernia can be lethal.

Vasculature

  • Aorta enters T12; bifurcates L4. IVC exits T8.
  • Levels: celiac T12; SMA L1–L2; renal arteries L1–L2; IMA L3–L4.
  • Branch order: inferior phrenic → celiac → middle adrenal → SMA → renal → gonadal → IMA.
  • Lumbar arteries: usually four pairs.
  • Adrenal supply: superior (inferior phrenic, constant), middle (aorta), inferior (renal).
  • Supernumerary renal arteries ≥ 1 in 4; no side predominance.
  • Splenic vessels on cephalad pancreatic edge: at risk lifting inferior pancreas off renal fascia.
  • SMA injury in left surgery → severe bowel ischaemia despite collaterals.
  • Testicular artery → internal ring; ovarian artery → infundibulopelvic ligament.
  • Safe to ligate: IMA, gonadal arteries, median sacral. Not safe: SMA.
  • Ureteric supply: abdominal ureter from medial; pelvic ureter from lateral.
  • Renal vein anterior to renal artery; enters IVC at L1.
  • Right renal vein: short, no tributaries, duplicated in one-sixth.
  • Left renal vein receives gonadal, adrenal and ≥ 1 lumbar vein.
  • Right gonadal vein → IVC; into right renal vein up to 10%.
  • Right adrenal vein: short, single, into posterolateral IVC.
  • Right ureter: dissect lateral to gonadal vein; medial plane → IVC injury.
  • IMV (inferior mesenteric vein) joins SMV (superior mesenteric vein) in 40% (not splenic vein).
  • Median sacral vein → left common iliac; avoid at sacrocolpopexy.
  • Ascending lumbar veins behind psoas → azygos (right), hemiazygos (left).
  • Hepatic veins → anterior IVC below diaphragm.
  • Budd–Chiari: hepatic vein occlusion → jaundice, ascites, pain, hepatomegaly.

Lymphatic System

  • Lymph flows cephalad, right → left → left innominate vein.
  • Lumbar nodes drain lateral-branch organs: kidney, adrenal, ureter, gonad.
  • Exception: head, neck, right thorax, right arm, right heart → right innominate.
  • Left para-aortic: aortic midline → left ureter. Right paracaval: IVC midline → right ureter.
  • Interaortocaval: IVC midline → aortic midline.
  • Three testicular zones: left para-aortic, interaortocaval, right paracaval.
  • Right testis sends small but real drainage to left para-aortic.
  • Cisterna chyli: behind right aorta, in front of L1–L2 → thoracic duct.

Nervous Structures

  • Parasympathetic: cranial nerves III, VII, IX, X + S2–S4 (pelvic splanchnics).
  • Sympathetic: cord segments T1–L2; thoracic, lumbar and sacral splanchnics.
  • Adrenal medulla = modified ganglion; preganglionic synapse directly.
  • Pelvic splanchnics = only parasympathetic splanchnics.
  • Sympathetic trunk medial to psoas; lumbar vessels cross it at 90°.
  • Celiac plexus first and largest; supplies kidney, adrenal, ureter.
  • Superior hypogastric plexus: aortic bifurcation → L5.
  • Hypogastric plexus injury → loss of emission / retrograde ejaculation.
  • Roots: subcostal T12; iliohypogastric and ilioinguinal L1; genitofemoral L1–L2.
  • Roots: lateral cutaneous nerve of thigh L2–L3; obturator and femoral L2–L4; sciatic L4–S3.
  • Upper plexus nerves pierce psoas; lower nerves run medial to it.
  • Lateral cutaneous nerve of thigh: purely sensory.
  • Pectineus: dual supply, femoral + obturator.
  • Genitofemoral: on psoas; motor to cremaster; injured in psoas hitch, laparoscopic varicocelectomy.
  • Femoral: retractor on inguinal ligament or low lithotomy → no knee extension.
  • Sciatic: high lithotomy hyperflexion.

CLASSIC TRAPS

  • Trap: right kidney higher — Actually: right lower (12th rib).
  • Trap: all duodenum and pancreas retroperitoneal — Actually: not D1 or the tail.
  • Trap: Gerota described both laminae — Actually: anterior only; posterior is Zuckerkandl.
  • Trap: perirenal space closed below — Actually: open cone into pelvis.
  • Trap: intercostal nerve protected in groove — Actually: lowest, often exposed.
  • Trap: sympathetic outflow from vertebrae T1–L2 — Actually: cord segments T1–L2.
  • Trap: IMV always joins splenic vein — Actually: joins SMV in 40%.
  • Trap: vessel in reoperative left mesocolon is gonadal vein — Actually: may be IMV.
  • Trap: left testis crosses to right paracaval — Actually: crossover only right → left.
  • Trap: ilioinguinal is motor to cremaster — Actually: genital branch of genitofemoral.
