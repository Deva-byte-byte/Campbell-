# CAMPBELL-WALSH-WEIN CHAPTER NOTES — STANDING INSTRUCTIONS

These are the user's standing instructions for every chapter session in this repository. Follow them in full.

## WORKING METHOD

I paste a chapter in instalments, approximately six pages at a time. Summarise each as it arrives.

Number the instalments sequentially within the chapter for working purposes only. Instalment numbering is a scaffold for the live session — it NEVER appears in the delivered documents.

Give each instalment a subheading listing the topics it actually covers: "Instalment 4 — Obesity Thresholds, Frailty, Infection Screening, Algorithm".

If a paste nonetheless arrives containing more than roughly six Level B sections or more than four numbered tables, split it: summarise it as two or more consecutive instalments within the one reply, each with its own numbers block, rather than as one compressed pass. Say that the split has been made.

## COVERAGE

Work through the paste in source order and cover everything in it: every heading at Level A, B and C; every numbered table, box and figure, including any whose number or caption is truncated; and any unheaded opening or bridging prose — chapter preamble, era summaries, transitional paragraphs. Unheaded prose carries epidemiology and framing; it is content, not roadmap.

Anything in the paste that is not covered is named explicitly in the closing appendix, with the reason — truncated, not pasted, absent from the paste — and carried forward as pending until it arrives.

Silent omission is the single worst failure mode. An acknowledged gap is acceptable; an unacknowledged one is not.

## SUMMARY STYLE

- Bullet points throughout. Concise but concept-carrying — keep the reasoning, drop the padding.
- Omit specific study data: no sample sizes, percentages, or raw figures. Give only the bottom line of what a study showed. Exception: landmark trials important enough to know by name — those keep their identity and key numbers.
- Omit author–year citations for studies that are not landmark ("Stacey, 2014", "Capogrosso, 2013", "Quam, 1989"): state the finding and, where it matters, the study type ("meta-analysis of randomised trials"), without the citation. Only landmark trials keep their names — for example, the Massachusetts Male Aging Study (MMAS), the Prostate Cancer Prevention Trial (PCPT) and REINVENT. This rule governs citations in the body text only; the source attribution printed with a numbered table or figure ("Data from Francis et al., 2007") is retained under the TABLES rule.
- Expand every abbreviation in full on first use, then abbreviate.
- No slang, no informal register. Keep the language medical throughout.
- Bold the operative phrase in each bullet so the eye catches it on revision.
- British spelling and standard medical usage, even where the source is American.
- Where the source gives a technique as a sequence, summarise it as a sequence (step → step → step), not as unordered bullets.
- Where two facts are the examinable contrast (rigid vs flexible, active vs passive deflection), state them adjacently as a pair.
- Preserve figure and table legends as their own subsection when pasted; name the source study and year where the legend gives it.
- Chapter opening prose is content. Epidemiology, incidence, mortality, subtype proportions, sex ratio and the framing of why a disease is treated as it is are all summarised. Only the chapter's own roadmap sentences are stripped — "this chapter will cover", and nothing else.
- Close every major section with a conceptual takeaway — see CONCEPTUAL TAKEAWAYS below.

## TABLES

Every numbered table and box in the paste is rebuilt or bulleted. None is skipped, none is folded silently into prose, none is deferred to a later instalment. A table referred to in the text but pasted elsewhere is flagged as pending and picked up when it arrives.

NEVER reproduce a source table as raw or dumped rows. Every table is handled one of two ways, whichever serves the material better:

- Bulleted and summarised, or
- Rebuilt as a clean, legible table — rows collapsed and grouped into the categories that actually matter (by sex, age band, setting, criterion), not one row per individual study.

Tables of drugs, agents or devices are always rebuilt as tables, never prose. Mechanism, target, line of therapy, risk group, comparator, endpoint and route are columns, not sentences.

Either way, follow the table with the concepts it is testing: what the table is FOR, the pattern across its rows, and the trap it sets.

Drop study-by-study granularity (individual author rows, sample sizes) unless the study is landmark. Keep the criteria, thresholds, cut-offs and ranges.

Retain the table number and full caption ("TABLE 17.2 Patient Risk Factors…") and the source study and year.

Where the body text and a table give different values for the same endpoint, tabulate both and name the reason — independent versus investigator review, different data cut, different denominator. Do not silently select one.

## MANDATORY COMPARISONS

Build a comparison table, unasked, wherever the source presents parallel entities:

- Two or more competing models, classifications, scores or criteria → one table, shared rows, differences bolded.
- Three or more drugs of one class → one table of targets, one of signature toxicities.
- Two or more guideline bodies covering the same decision → one table, not two lists.
- A series of trials answering the same question → one table: trial, comparator, population, endpoints, verdict.
- A governing contrast or asymmetry (normoxia vs hypoxia, hereditary vs sporadic, immediate vs deferred) → a two-column pair, adjacent.

Prose is for reasoning and mechanism. Anything enumerable and parallel goes in a table.

## DENSITY DISCIPLINE

Depth must not decay across an instalment. The last section gets the same treatment as the first — same table handling, same conceptual takeaway, same bolding. Before closing, reread the final third against the first third and correct any thinning.

Never compress by dropping. If length is a problem, tighten phrasing; the set of facts covered does not shrink. (This applies to the session and to Document 2. Document 1 is deliberately selective — see DOCUMENT 1.)

Reading-time budget. Chapter Notes (Document 2) run to no more than about 360 words per source page, so that a 20-page chapter reads in about 60 minutes at 120 words per minute (my measured speed on dense technique pages, read cold). A chapter that exceeds this is brought back by tightening phrasing and removing duplication between tables and prose — each fact stated once, in the table where one carries it — never by dropping facts. Full sentences for reasoning and mechanism, the post-table notes and the conceptual takeaways are not cut to meet the budget. Completeness and understanding outrank the cap: the notes must let me understand why each fact is true, not merely memorise it, so the reasoning that connects facts is never stripped to save words. If a chapter cannot meet the cap without losing a fact or the reasoning behind it, exceed the cap and say so in the delivery report, with the overrun.

## ORDER OF OUTPUT

Summary first, source flags last. The chapter notes open the instalment; truncations, duplicates, resequencing and source errors close it, under their own heading.

Flags are a closing appendix, never a preamble. A long error block at the top buries the material it is meant to protect.

Corrections themselves still appear inside the relevant bullet, in corrected form, at the point the fact is taught — the appendix records that a correction was made, not the teaching.

## HANDLING THE SOURCE TEXT — IN THE LIVE SESSION ONLY

Flag all of the following, in the closing appendix. None of it survives into the PDFs.

- Where a passage is truncated mid-sentence, say so and name the exact words it breaks at. When a later instalment completes it, open by closing the loop.
- If I paste something already covered, say it is a duplicate and where to resume.
- If one paste contains an internal duplicate, summarise once and note it.
- If a paste arrives out of textbook order (e.g. text below an attachment precedes it), restore the correct order and say you have done so.
- If the source contains an error, an internal inconsistency, or an ambiguous expansion, flag it and give the correct version — never silently reproduce and never silently fix. This includes corrupted symbols (a comma printed for "<", a full stop for ">", "P 5" for "P ="), split numerals, and dropped letters.
- Where a systematic character substitution is running through a paste, state the substitution key once rather than flagging each instance.
- If I paste material unrelated to the chapter, answer it but keep it out of the chapter notes and ask where it should be filed.
- Anything in the paste that was not covered, and why.

## CONCEPTUAL TAKEAWAYS

Close each major section (Level A, or a Level B section substantial enough to stand alone) with a short conceptual takeaway.

- One to three sentences, or a single bullet. Never a paragraph.
- It states the organising idea — why the facts in that section hang together, what one principle generates the rest, or what the section is really testing. NOT a recap, not a list, not a repetition of the bullets above it.
- Where a section has a governing asymmetry, contrast or causal chain, name it: "transfeminine therapy is two-component, transmasculine one-component, and every downstream difference follows from that."
- Add it only where a genuine organising idea exists. A purely enumerative section (a list of definitions, a list of instruments) gets none — do not manufacture one.
- Same register as the rest: medical, no slang, bolded operative phrase.

## END OF EVERY INSTALMENT

Close with a block titled "The numbers to lock in", covering as applicable:

- Key facts, figures, thresholds and cut-offs
- Gold standards, commonest, and first-line options
- Eponyms, each with its single distinguishing feature
- Landmark trials by name, with their key numbers
- Dates and legislation, each with its single distinguishing feature
- Absolute versus relative contraindications, kept as two separate lists
- "When to operate" triggers, and when not to operate
- Classic traps — the specific errors this material sets up

These blocks are written in full during the session. They are the raw material from which Document 1 is distilled at compile time; they are not printed as they stand.

Then the source-flag appendix. In that order.

## COMPILATION — WHAT I GET WHEN I SAY "COMPILE"

Two PDFs per chapter. No Word file, no third variant. Both are for black-and-white printing on A4.

Restructure before rendering. The delivered documents follow THE CHAPTER'S OWN heading hierarchy, not the paste order:

- Level A: the chapter's own section heads, in caps (HISTORY OF ENDOSCOPY, CYSTOURETHROSCOPY, UPPER TRACT ENDOSCOPY, CONCLUSIONS)
- Level B: the chapter's second-level heads (Indications, Equipment, Patient Preparation, Technique, Special Circumstances, Ureteroscopy Technique)
- Level C: the chapter's third-level heads (Rigid Cystourethroscopes, Guidewires, Dilation Devices, Flexible Ureteroscopy Technique)
- Level D: italic run-in subheads where a section needs finer division

Headings get larger going up; body text is the smallest thing on the page.

Strip completely from both documents:

- Instalment numbering and instalment cross-references
- Paste-order, resequencing, duplication and truncation notes
- Source-error, discrepancy and terminology flags
- The chapter's own roadmap sentences ("this chapter will cover…")
- Figure legends that merely duplicate the body text
- Author–year citations of non-landmark studies, if any survived the session

Where the source was wrong, carry the CORRECT value silently in the documents — the correction was already flagged in the session, and the flag does not print.

Add nothing new at compile time. Reorganising, merging duplicated passages and deleting collation commentary is expected; writing new content is not. Conceptual takeaways written during the session are carried through as written; they are not treated as new content, and none are composed at compile time. Comparison tables built during the session are likewise carried through; none are composed at compile time. Selecting and shortening for Document 1 is permitted and expected; introducing a fact that never appeared in the session is not.

Before rendering, run a final coverage check across every instalment in the chapter — every heading, numbered table, box and figure summarised in the session — and report any item that never reached Document 2. (Document 1 is selective by design and is not held to this check.)

Before rendering, check Document 2 against the reading-time budget in DENSITY DISCIPLINE, and tighten if it exceeds it.

Rasterise a page of each document in greyscale before delivering and confirm that every box, table and takeaway remains distinguishable from body text.

## DOCUMENT 1 — "Chapter NN — Title — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps.pdf"

A cheat sheet for last-look revision. Readable end to end in 10–15 minutes.

### Length

- Target: one A4 sheet printed on both sides (two sides). Hard ceiling of three sides, used only for an exceptionally long chapter.
- Word budget approximately 1,200–1,600 words. Report the word count on delivery.

### Selection — the high-yield test

An item enters Document 1 only if it is one of the following:

- An examinable number, threshold or cut-off
- A gold standard, first-line option or "commonest"
- A when-to-operate or when-not-to-operate trigger
- An absolute contraindication
- A landmark trial, with its single key result
- An eponym, with its single distinguishing feature
- A classic trap

Excluded: mechanism, rationale, history (unless a date is itself examinable), background epidemiology, relative contraindications unless classically examined, conceptual takeaways, and anything a urology trainee would already know without revising.

If over budget, cut in this order until within it: eponyms of historical interest only → dates → secondary thresholds → relative contraindications. Never cut traps, gold standards or operative triggers.

### Wording

- One fact per line, one line per fact. Telegraphic: no articles, no full sentences, no connecting prose. Aim for 12 words or fewer per bullet.
- Use arrows and symbols: "→", ">", "<", "≥", "vs". Example: "Stone >10 mm lower pole → PCNL (percutaneous nephrolithotomy) over SWL (shock-wave lithotripsy)."
- Merge related cut-offs into one line wherever possible.
- Collapse duplicates across instalments; each fact appears once in the sheet.
- Abbreviations expanded on first use in the sheet, then abbreviated.
- Bold the operative phrase in each bullet.
- Tables are carried as single-line cut-offs only. No gridded tables.

### Structure

- Open with a box titled "IF YOU READ NOTHING ELSE": the ten highest-yield lines in the chapter, spanning both columns.
- Then the chapter's Level A sections, in chapter order, each followed by its bullets. Bold run-in labels ("Cut-offs:", "Gold standard:", "Operate if:") replace category subheadings, to save space.
- Close with a single chapter-wide CLASSIC TRAPS box, with a maximum of ten traps, each one line: "Trap: X — Actually: Y."

### Layout

- A4 portrait, two columns, 7 mm gutter, black and white only.
- Margins 12 mm top and bottom, 13 mm left and right.
- Carlito, falling back to DejaVu Sans. Body 9.4 pt, line height 1.25, pure black (#000). Space between bullets 1 pt.
- Chapter title 14 pt bold with a 1 pt rule beneath, spanning both columns.
- Level A section heads 10.4 pt bold caps with a 0.5 pt rule.
- "IF YOU READ NOTHING ELSE" box: 1 pt black border, no fill, spanning both columns.
- Classic traps box: #ececec fill, 5 pt double black left rule.
- Left-aligned, ragged right, hyphenation off. Never justify.
- Boxes may flow across columns and pages; do not set break-inside: avoid on them.
- break-after: avoid on all headings. Orphans and widows 2.
- Page numbers bottom centre, 8 pt, black.

## DOCUMENT 2 — "Chapter NN — Title — Chapter Notes.pdf"

For printing and reading on paper. The body notes, flowing as proper study notes.

- A4 portrait, single column, black and white only. Nothing may depend on colour.
- Margins 18 mm top and bottom, 20 mm left, 18 mm right.
- Carlito, falling back to DejaVu Sans. Body 11 pt, line height 1.4, pure black (#000). No grey body text.
- Space between bullets 2.5 pt. At least 8 pt of space before Level B heads and at least 12 pt before Level A heads.
- Headings are distinguished by size, weight, caps and rules, never colour:
  - Chapter title 20 pt bold over a 1.5 pt black rule.
  - Level A 14 pt bold caps over a 1 pt rule.
  - Level B 12.5 pt bold.
  - Level C 11.5 pt bold.
  - Level D 11 pt italic run-in.
- KEY POINTS boxes: 0.75 pt black border on all four sides, no fill, 6 pt padding, title in bold caps.
- Tables:
  - No fill and no vertical rules. Use a 1 pt rule above and below the table and a 0.5 pt rule under the header row. Optional alternate-row banding at #f2f2f2, no darker.
  - Title in bold caps, keeping the table number and full caption ("TABLE 17.2 Patient Risk Factors…").
  - The table rendered is the REBUILT one from the TABLES rule, never the raw source rows. Follow each with its summary bullets.
  - Table body 10 pt minimum, full text width, header row repeated across page breaks.
- Comparison tables built under MANDATORY COMPARISONS take the same treatment, titled for what they compare rather than by source number.
- Conceptual takeaway: a closing run-in at the end of its section, body size, italic, black, opening with a bold "Concept —". No box, no rule. break-before: avoid, so it never orphans from its section.
- Running header, 8.5 pt, black: chapter title on the left, current Level A section on the right, with a 0.3 pt rule beneath.
- Left-aligned, ragged right, hyphenation off. Never justify.
- break-after: avoid on all headings. Orphans and widows 2.
- Page numbers bottom centre, 9 pt, black, in the form "page X of Y".

## RENDERING

- WeasyPrint, never wkhtmltopdf — wkhtmltopdf silently ignores print CSS, giving no page numbers and no page-break control.
- Carlito throughout. Serif faces have been tried and rejected: Minion Pro is licensed and unavailable, and Source Serif 4, Bitstream Charter, Caladea and DejaVu Serif were all worse. Do not propose a serif again.
- All text in pure black. Grey text is halftoned by the printer and prints fuzzy.
- Legibility floor:
  - Document 2 never goes below 10.5 pt body or 1.35 line height.
  - Document 1 never goes below 9.4 pt body or 1.25 line height.
  - If Document 2 must come down in page count, tighten bullet gaps and heading spacing first.
  - If Document 1 exceeds its budget, cut by the selection order — never by shrinking the type.
- Report on delivery:
  - The page count of both documents.
  - The word count of Document 1.
  - For Document 2: the source page count, the notes word count and the estimated reading time at 120 words per minute, and whether the cap was exceeded and by how much.

### Environment setup (cloud container)

The container starts without WeasyPrint or Carlito. Before compiling:

```
pip install weasyprint
apt-get update && apt-get install -y fonts-crosextra-carlito poppler-utils
```

`poppler-utils` supplies `pdftoppm` for the greyscale rasterisation check (`pdftoppm -gray -r 100 -f N -l N in.pdf out`).

The container is ephemeral: commit session working notes (instalment summaries, numbers blocks) to the repository as the chapter progresses so that compilation does not depend on a single session's memory.

## Notes on using it

The instalment subheading is the quick check. It lists the topics the instalment actually covers, so reading it against the paste takes a few seconds and shows immediately whether anything has been skipped. If the subheading is missing, or names fewer topics than the paste contains, that is the signal that the instruction set has slipped and the reply should be regenerated.

Six pages holds only if the pages are ordinary. A stretch that is mostly tables — as Chapter 144's drug section was — is denser than six pages of prose. If a six-page paste contains four or more numbered tables, expect the split, and say so if it does not come.
