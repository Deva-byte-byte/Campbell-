Chapter 51 — Pathophysiology of Urinary Tract Obstruction — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else

  • Posterior urethral valves (PUV) + urethral atresia ≈ 50% of lower urinary tract obstruction (LUTO)
  • Ureteropelvic junction obstruction (UPJO) = commonest upper tract cause; 1 in 1500, male 2:1
  • Second-trimester oligo/anhydramnios in LUTO → 60–80% mortality
  • Anteroposterior diameter (APD) ≥ 7 mm (16–27 wk), ≥ 10 mm (≥ 28 wk) → consider obstruction
  • Favourable fetal urine: Na < 100, Cl < 90 mEq/L, osm < 200 mOsm/L
  • Voiding cystourethrogram (VCUG) = gold standard for vesicoureteral reflux (VUR)
  • Mercaptoacetyltriglycine (MAG3) = drainage and level; dimercaptosuccinic acid (DMSA) = scar
  • Bladder > 40 cm H₂O = hostile, threatens kidneys
  • Postobstructive diuresis (POD): ≈ 2% unilateral vs ≈ 50% bilateral
  • Pyeloplasty: re-anastomose anterior to crossing vessel

Pathophysiology of Paediatric Obstruction

  • Incidence: ≈ 1 in 500 live births
  • LUTO: 2–3 per 10,000 fetuses; 1 in 5000–25,000 pregnancies
  • High-grade obstruction: lifetime end-stage renal disease (ESRD) risk up to 30%
  • Governing risk: permanent damage to developing kidney
  • Axes: acute/chronic · partial/complete · intrinsic/extrinsic · uni/bilateral

Clinical Presentations

  • Prenatal detection: earliest 11–14 wk; most 18–20 wk
  • Commonest prenatal finding: hydronephrosis
  • Prenatal US signs: megacystis, keyhole, echogenic/cystic kidneys, thick bladder, ascites, oligohydramnios
  • Keyhole sign = dilated posterior urethra → LUTO
  • Other LUTO causes: anterior valves, megalourethra, prolapsing ureterocele, cloaca, megacystis–microcolon–intestinal hypoperistalsis syndrome (MMIHS), trisomy 18/21
  • LUTO kills via ESRD + pulmonary hypoplasia
  • Extrinsic causes: crossing vessel, retroperitoneal fibrosis, nodes, pelvic/spinal tumour, faecal impaction
  • Intraluminal causes: calculi, sloughed papillae, clot, upper tract bleeding
  • Other upper tract causes: ureterovesical junction obstruction, ureterocele, ectopic ureter, fibroepithelial polyp, calyceal diverticulum
  • Functional lower tract cause: neurogenic bladder, myogenic failure
  • Lower tract tumour: prostatic rhabdomyosarcoma

Definition of Obstruction

  • Partial → may resolve; high-grade → intervene
  • Prenatal obstruction → pulmonary hypoplasia, limb deformity

Diagnosis

  • Central task: surgery-needing vs spontaneously resolving
  • CAKUT (congenital anomalies of kidney and urinary tract): 1 in 250–1000 pregnancies
  • Fetal kidneys 9 wk; bladder 12 wk; absent at 15 wk = abnormal
  • Urinary tract dilation (UTD) system: 8 societies; split at 28 wk
  • Most reliable prenatal parameter: APD (ERKNet consensus, 2022)
  • Below APD threshold → obstruction only if megacystis or low fluid
  • Primary prenatal modality — ultrasound (US): cost-effective, part of anomaly screening
  • Fetal MRI: adjunct — oligohydramnios, maternal obesity, pre-intervention
  • Megacystis: fails to empty over 40 min at ≥ 18 wk
  • Fetal urine also: Ca < 8 mg/dL, β₂-microglobulin < 6 mg/L
  • Vesicocentesis: 3 serial samples; last = current function
  • Fetal urine uses: obstructive vs non-obstructive; monitor LUTO intervention
  • 12PUV peptide signature: ESRD < 2 yr; sensitivity 88%, specificity 95%
  • Urinalysis: initial study; low specific gravity = concentrating defect
  • Fractional excretion of sodium (FENa): < 1% prerenal; > 1% tubular damage/acute tubular necrosis
  • FENa in obstruction: low acutely; high once tubules damaged
  • FENa = (urine Na × serum creatinine) / (urine creatinine × serum Na) × 100
  • Best paediatric glomerular filtration rate (GFR) estimate: cystatin C
  • Postnatal biomarkers: neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin, kidney injury molecule-1, cystatin C, transforming growth factor (TGF)-β1, retinol-binding protein → expedite surgery
  • Imaging tiers: primary US, scintigraphy, VCUG, MR urography → secondary retrograde/antegrade pyelography → tertiary intravenous pyelography (IVP), CT urography (CTU), Whitaker
  • First-line postnatal imaging: US
  • Technetium-99m DTPA: function + perfusion only, no drainage
  • PUV on VCUG: dilated posterior urethra, voiding phase
  • Caecoureterocele: filling defect at trigone
  • VUR grades 1–5
  • High-grade VUR → secondary UPJO
  • MR urography: anatomy + function, no radiation; ectopic ureter
  • Retrograde = cystoscopic cannulation; antegrade = percutaneous
  • IVP: seldom used; bowel prep improves accuracy
  • CTU: preferred over IVP despite higher radiation
  • Whitaker indications: equivocal tests, poor function, intermittent, gross dilation + positive renogram
  • Whitaker obstructed: > 12 cm H₂O or continuous rise
  • Uroflowmetry: voided volume, maximum flow rate, curve

Pathological Changes

  • Bladder remodelling: hypertrophy → compensation → decompensation
  • Compliance = extracellular matrix (ECM); interleukin-6, tumour necrosis factor-α, monocyte chemoattractant protein-1 → collagen
  • Immature kidney: apoptosis, impaired nephrogenesis
  • Mature kidney: proliferation → fibrosis
  • Fibrosis marker: α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA)
  • Master fibrotic pathway: TGF-β → Smad
  • miR-34a → epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition
  • Worse disease: bilateral obstruction or solitary kidney
  • Ischaemic injury within 2 h of complete obstruction
  • Early wasting: Na, K, HCO₃, Mg, Ca, PO₄
  • Late retention: K, H⁺, Cl, NH₄⁺
  • Acid–base: distal renal tubular acidosis
  • Aquaporins 1–4 + Na transporters downregulated

Clinical Impact

  • Renal parenchymal disease = 60–80% of paediatric secondary hypertension
  • Hypertension: ≈ 77% bilateral vs ≈ 20% unilateral
  • Unilateral: renin lateralises to affected kidney
  • Compensatory growth: insulin-like growth factor-1, TGF-β1, hepatocyte growth factor; glomerulomegaly
  • End-stage renal failure: days to months of obstruction
  • Acute-on-chronic: obstruction on chronic kidney disease (CKD) → rapid acute kidney injury
  • Recovery: often incomplete after relief
  • POD mechanism: aquaporin loss, medullary washout, transporter dysfunction

Treatment

  • Shunt/fetal cystoscopy: LUTO with preserved function
  • Amnioinfusion: renal agenesis or LUTO with renal failure; serial
  • Pain sources: baroreceptors, stent, infection, decompression haematuria
  • Avoid non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs if renal function compromised
  • Timed voiding: every 1–3 h; double void for high residual
  • Continuous overnight catheter drainage: post-PUV nocturnal polyuria; delays renal progression
  • Suprapubic tube: urethral route impossible; short-term
  • Vesicostomy: infant complex LUTO, unstable, unable to perform clean intermittent catheterisation; temporising
  • Surgery for LUTO: valve excision, stricture repair, outlet reconstruction
  • Stent: best for extrinsic ureteral obstruction
  • Nephrostomy: needs some hydronephrosis
  • Pyeloplasty: correct all intrinsic + extrinsic causes
  • Paediatric reimplant: non-refluxing
  • CKD/ESRD: blood pressure, diet, drugs → dialysis or transplant
  • POD duration: resolves < 24 h; rarely > 48 h
  • POD risk after valve ablation: oligohydramnios, prematurity, high creatinine

Classic traps

  • Trap: dilation = obstruction — Actually: partial may resolve spontaneously
  • Trap: detrusor hypertrophy is lower tract only — Actually: causes secondary ureterovesical junction obstruction
  • Trap: crossing vessel is intrinsic — Actually: extrinsic cause of UPJO
  • Trap: first fetal urine sample predicts function — Actually: the third does
  • Trap: visible fetal ureter is normal — Actually: always dilated
  • Trap: CTU gives less radiation than IVP — Actually: more
  • Trap: compensatory growth = new nephrons — Actually: glomerulomegaly
  • Trap: overnight drainage replaces catheterisation/augmentation — Actually: adjunct only
  • Trap: amnioinfusion relieves obstruction — Actually: replaces fluid only
  • Trap: generous fluid replacement in POD — Actually: overcorrection perpetuates it
