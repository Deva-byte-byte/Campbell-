Chapter 106 — Evaluation and Management of Males With Urinary Incontinence — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else

  1. AUS (artificial urinary sphincter) = gold standard, moderate–severe SUI; ≈80% continent.
  2. Wait ≥6–12 months after radical prostatectomy before SUI surgery.
  3. PFMT (pelvic floor muscle training) ≥3 months, supervised → first line SUI.
  4. UUI conservative: ≥6 weeks, ideally 3 months; cut fluid 25% if >1 L/day.
  5. Antimuscarinics + β3-agonists = first-line drugs for UUI.
  6. UDS (urodynamics) only if it changes management — before invasive therapy.
  7. OnabotulinumtoxinA 100 U idiopathic OAB; 200 U neurogenic DO; retention ≈10%.
  8. SNM (sacral neuromodulation): S3; proceed if ≥50% improvement.
  9. Bulking agents: do not offer for post-prostatectomy incontinence.
  10. MASTER: sling = AUS at 12 months; AUS better on secondary outcomes.

Urinary incontinence

  • UI (urinary incontinence): ICS (International Continence Society) — any involuntary loss of urine; a storage symptom.
  • Most important continence determinant: EUS (external urethral sphincter).
  • Major independent risk factor: age.
  • DIAPPERS: Delirium, Infection, Atrophic urethritis, Pharmacological, Psychiatric, Endocrine, Reduced mobility, Stool impaction.
  • Male:female ≈ 1:2; prevalence 1–40%; 11–34% in older men.
  • Commonest male type: UUI (urgency) > SUI (stress) > MUI (mixed).
  • EpiLUTS 46% — inflated by broad definition.

Types of urinary incontinence

  • UUI → DO (detrusor overactivity) on UDS in ≈90% of men.
  • Male SUI → almost always post-prostatectomy.
  • Adult new nocturnal enuresis → exclude high-pressure chronic retention.
  • Enuresis = wet asleep, unaware; nocturia = wakes to void.
  • Significant residual: ≥300 mL.
  • Chronic retention + SCI (spinal cord injury) ≥T6 → autonomic dysreflexia.
  • Continuous UI → fistula, exstrophy/epispadias, ectopic ureter.
  • Enuresis prevalence: ≈10% at age 7; persists in 2–3%.

Evaluation

  • Essential: history, exam, BMI, dipstick, diary, questionnaire, flow, PVR (postvoid residual).
  • Optional: PSA, bloods, imaging, endoscopy, invasive UDS.
  • Cough test: supine, then standing.
  • AUS balloon 61–70 cm H2O; abdominal pressure above → leak.
  • Only fully validated diary: ICIQ-BD (ICI Questionnaire Bladder Diary).
  • Diary length: 3 days = 7 days; include weekend.
  • ICIQ-UI-SF (short form): score items 3+4+5 = 0–21; separates SUI vs UUI.
  • IPSS (International Prostate Symptom Score) does not measure incontinence.
  • Pad count: 1 mild; 2–4 moderate; >4 severe.
  • Pad weight/day: <200 g mild; 200–400 g moderate; >400 g severe — better measure; unvalidated.
  • Pad test not recommended by most guidelines; cannot separate UUI/SUI.
  • Flow: fast rise → OAB (overactive bladder); slow rise, long tail → BOO (outflow obstruction).
  • Flow cannot separate BOO vs detrusor underactivity.
  • Flows: ≥2 traditionally; 1 if representative (ICS 2017).
  • PSA: if life expectancy >10 years and result changes management.
  • Bloods: high PVR or diabetes → renal function, glucose.
  • Cystourethroscopy if: stricture, BOO, haematuria, high PVR.
  • Imaging only if: upper-tract concern, high PVR, stones.
  • UDS gold standard for LUT (lower urinary tract) function.
  • Stress test in UDS: Valsalva/cough upright at 200 mL, repeat at capacity.
  • USI (urodynamic stress incontinence) vs cough-associated DOI → refill supine, cough.

Management — urgency incontinence

  • Antimuscarinics: 7 agents; all level 1, grade A; similar efficacy; ER > IR.
  • Old rule: ≥2 agents, ≥4 weeks each; now one then move on.
  • Solifenacin > tolterodine: efficacy, less dry mouth.
  • Trospium: quaternary amine → no cognitive effects.
  • Anticholinergic ≥3 months → dementia risk ↑ ≈46%.
  • Cognitive impairment → β3-agonist first or early surgery.
  • Mirabegron: monitor blood pressure; superior to tolterodine.
  • Vibegron 75 mg daily; no CYP interaction, no blood–brain barrier crossing.
  • Combination: mirabegron 50 + solifenacin 5 > solifenacin escalation.
  • Tadalafil: only FDA-approved PDE5I, voiding LUTS + erectile dysfunction.
  • UDS before toxin/SNM: not mandatory; FUTURE trial results awaited.
  • Botox repeat every 6–9 months; must accept self-catheterisation.
  • Abobotulinum (Dysport) 2.5:1 conversion; more retention.
  • SNM test: temporary lead 1–2 weeks; tined lead 2–4 weeks.
  • SNM battery: old 5–7 years; new ≥15 years; Axonics first MRI-safe rechargeable.
  • SNM = most effective, durable, most expensive.
  • PTNS (tibial): behind medial malleolus, S2–S4; 30 min weekly × 12.
  • PTNS = least invasive; better for dry OAB.
  • Last resort: augmentation enterocystoplasty or ileal conduit.
  • Autoaugmentation: no longer recommended.

Management — stress incontinence

  • Duloxetine: off-label in men; Weak offer, Strong inform.
  • Before surgery: exclude stricture, bladder dysfunction.
  • AUS first described: Scott, 1973.
  • Mild–moderate: fixed transobturator sling (Weak).
  • Moderate–severe: AUS (Strong).
  • AUS/ProACT → expert centres only.
  • Sling outcome worse: severe UI, pelvic radiotherapy, prior TURP (transurethral surgery).
  • ProACT contraindicated after pelvic radiotherapy.
  • AdVance: transobturator, under membranous urethra.
  • ATOMS: silicone cushion, noncircumferential; 2–4 adjustments; > ProACT.
  • Fixed vs adjustable sling: neither superior.
  • MASTER: only 15% never leaked after either operation.
  • Interim containment: avoid long-term urethral catheter (traumatic hypospadias).

Mixed and other types

  • MUI: treat most bothersome first; treat DO → repeat UDS.
  • Retention + enuresis: catheterise first, then treat BOO.
  • Postmicturition dribble: no drug; urethral milking + pelvic squeeze.
  • Milking: 3 fingerbreadths behind scrotum; repeat twice.

Algorithms

  • Specialist referral: haematuria, pain, recurrent UTI, pelvic radiotherapy, abnormal DRE, voiding dysfunction.
  • ICI: AUS grade A (AMS 800 only); sling C.
  • EAU 2020 algorithm: pad test Weak; toxin or SNM Strong.
  • Underactive detrusor + UUI → intermittent catheterisation + drug.

Classic traps

  • Trap: "overflow incontinence" — Actually: incontinence associated with chronic retention.
  • Trap: SUI = USI — Actually: SUI symptom; USI urodynamic finding.
  • Trap: cough leak = SUI — Actually: may be cough-associated DOI.
  • Trap: terminal dribble postmicturition — Actually: voiding symptom.
  • Trap: IPSS grades incontinence — Actually: it does not.
  • Trap: pad count grades severity — Actually: pad weight better.
  • Trap: UDS before everything — Actually: only if it changes management.
  • Trap: early SUI surgery post-RP — Actually: wait 6–12 months.
  • Trap: bulking for PPI — Actually: do not offer.
  • Trap: ProACT after radiotherapy — Actually: contraindicated.
