### Gonadal Descent

#### Starting position and the two ligaments

- **Starting position.** The urogenital ridge is **identical in both sexes at 7–8 weeks**. The undifferentiated gonad lies **near the kidney in the upper lumbar region**.
- **Two ligaments hold it:**
  - **cranially**, the **cranial suspensory ligament (CSL)**;
  - **caudally**, a ligament that becomes the **gubernaculum**.
- **Weeks 10–15:**
  - the **testis reaches the future inguinal region** as the abdominal cavity enlarges;
  - the **ovary descends only into the pelvis**.
- **Male.** **Androgen causes the CSL to regress**, and the gubernaculum swells into a **plump ligament that anchors the testis at the groin** while the kidney moves cranially. This tethering was first observed in the 1700s.
- **Female.** The **CSL persists** and keeps the ovary near the kidney; the **gubernaculum stays rudimentary**.

#### Transabdominal phase

- **Swelling reaction (gubernacular outgrowth).** The gubernaculum enlarges caudally.
- **Shortening of the gubernacular cord.** The cord is **taken up into the enlarging bulb**. This positions the testis over the inguinal ring so that **abdominal pressure can push it out**.
- **Cord division misdirects the testis.** Transecting the gubernacular cord → **descent into the contralateral inguinal canal, or an aberrant intra-abdominal position**.
- **Role of intra-abdominal pressure:** **controversial in this phase**, but **important in the inguinal and scrotal phases**.

#### Inguinoscrotal phase

- **Timing.** From the **seventh month**, the gubernaculum bulges beyond the external ring toward the scrotum.
- **Processus vaginalis.** At the same time, the gubernaculum is **hollowed out by the processus vaginalis**, a peritoneal diverticulum. This lets the testis leave the abdomen.
- **The gubernaculum must migrate 3–5 cm** to the scrotum, and the processus vaginalis lengthens with it.
- **Driving force:** intra-abdominal pressure, transmitted
  - **directly** through the lumen of the processus vaginalis;
  - **indirectly** through the gubernacular cord.
- **Direction of migration:** controlled by **androgen acting mostly indirectly, via the genitofemoral nerve**, which releases **calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP)**.
- **After descent:**
  - the **gubernacular bulb is resorbed**;
  - the **processus vaginalis closes, then involutes completely**.

**The two phases of testicular descent**

| | Transabdominal phase | Inguinoscrotal phase |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | Weeks 10–15 (ends at about 15 weeks) | From the 7th month |
| Key events | **CSL regression**; gubernacular swelling; cord shortening | Gubernacular migration 3–5 cm to the scrotum; elongation of the processus vaginalis; bulb regression |
| Androgen dependence | **CSL regression is androgen-dependent**. **Gubernacular swelling is androgen-independent**: it occurs in androgen resistance | **Androgen-dependent**: absent in gonadotrophin deficiency and complete androgen resistance. **Bulb regression is also androgen-dependent** (the bulb stays enlarged in androgen resistance) |
| Other mediators | **INSL3** (Leydig cell) | **CGRP from the genitofemoral nerve** |
| Intra-abdominal pressure | Controversial | Important |
| Failure → | **Intra-abdominal testis** | **Inguinal or ectopic testis** |

#### INSL3

- **What it is:** **insulin-like peptide 3**, a Leydig-cell product identified in **1993**.
  - It is structurally related to **relaxin and insulin**.
  - It is expressed in **fetal and adult Leydig cells**, depending on their differentiation.
- **Knockout phenotype:** **Insl3-null mice have intra-abdominal cryptorchidism with no other reproductive defect**.
- **Early orchidopexy restores fertility potential** in these mice.
- **Why it matters:** this is the **phenotype of classic human cryptorchidism**.
- **Androgen's overall role in descent is still unclear.** Defective androgen production or metabolism gives variable cryptorchidism.

#### Ovarian descent

- **A female gubernaculum exists.** It runs from the lower pole of the ovary to the labioscrotal folds, and later passes through a fully formed inguinal canal.
- **It does not shorten**, but it still **anchors the ovary in the pelvis** → **descent in the third month**, onto the **broad ligament**.
- **Week 7:** the gubernaculum attaches to the Müllerian ducts. As those ducts fuse into the uterovaginal canal, the gubernaculum is **tethered to the uterus** and divided into:
  1. **ovary → uterus: the ovarian ligament**;
  2. **uterus → inguinal canal → labia majora: the round ligament**.
- **Processus vaginalis in females** is normally obliterated. **If it stays patent → indirect inguinal hernia.**

**Concept —** *Descent is a **tug between two ligaments followed by a migration**. Androgen removes the cranial restraint (CSL), INSL3 builds the caudal anchor (gubernaculum), and androgen via CGRP steers the inguinoscrotal migration. So the site of an undescended testis tells you which step failed.*

### Clinical Correlation: Cryptorchidism

- **Incidence:** about **3% of newborn males**. **Hydrocele and hernia are even more common.**
- **Each anomaly maps to a failed step of descent:**

| Failed step | Result |
|---|---|
| **CSL retained** | **Intra-abdominal testis** |
| **Gubernacular attachment abnormal** | Testis in the **inguinal canal or just outside it** |
| **Gubernacular attachment ectopic** | **Ectopic testis**: perineum, femoral region, or near the penis |
| **Step 1 fails** (gubernacular migration to the scrotum) | **Congenital cryptorchidism** |
| **Step 2 fails** (closure of the processus vaginalis) | **Hydrocele or inguinal hernia** |
| **Step 3 fails** (complete involution of the processus vaginalis) | **Acquired cryptorchidism (ascending testis)**: a fibrous remnant stops the cord lengthening after birth |

### Clinical Correlation: Streak Gonad

- **Three genotypes:**
  1. **45,X (Turner syndrome)**;
  2. **46,XX gonadal dysgenesis**;
  3. **46,XY gonadal dysgenesis**.
- **Mechanism — both testicular hormones are absent:**
  - no testosterone → the **Wolffian ducts regress**;
  - no MIS → the **Müllerian ducts form the tubes, uterus, cervix and upper vagina**.
- **Phenotype:** **female-like internal and external genitalia**.
- **Treatment:**
  - **hormone replacement** for secondary sexual characteristics, because the streak is non-functional;
  - **46,XY:** risk of **gonadoblastoma** and later malignancy → **streak gonads are typically removed**.

### Clinical Correlation: Ovo-testicular Syndrome

- **Definition.** An ovotestis is a gonad containing **both testicular and ovarian tissue**. It is **extremely rare**.
- **Function.** Both tissues can function hormonally, before and after birth.
  - **Female fertility with normal oocytes is possible**;
  - **functioning adult spermatogenesis has not been reported**.
- **Ducts follow the gonad on the same side:**
  - **Müllerian structures** on a side **without testicular tissue**;
  - **Wolffian structures** on a side **with functioning testicular tissue**.
- **Genitalia:** often **atypical**.

### Clinical Correlation: Persistent Müllerian Duct Syndrome

- **Definition.** A **46,XY male** with a **uterus and fallopian tubes**, known as **hernia uteri inguinale**.
- **Usual cause:** an **abnormal MIS receptor**, so MIS has no action → female internal genitalia retained, with a **relatively normal male phenotype**.
- **Typical presentation:** found at **exploration for bilateral abdominal testes**.
- **Alternative cause:** **immature testis**, where MIS production is **out of phase** with the Müllerian ducts' period of sensitivity.
  - That sensitivity window is normally **9–13 weeks**.

**Disorders of gonadal and ductal development — comparison** *(adds CAH from Instalment 2)*

| Condition | Karyotype | Gonad | MIS | Testosterone | Internal ducts | External genitalia | Key management point |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Streak gonad** | 45,X / 46,XX / 46,XY | Streak, non-functional | Absent | Absent | **Müllerian retained**, Wolffian regressed | Female-like | Hormone replacement; **remove the streak if 46,XY** (gonadoblastoma) |
| **Ovotesticular** | (not specified) | **Ovotestis** | Present on the testicular side | Present on the testicular side | **Follow the ipsilateral gonad** | Often atypical | Female fertility possible; no spermatogenesis reported |
| **Persistent Müllerian duct syndrome (PMDS)** | **46,XY** | Testes (often abdominal) | **Produced but ineffective** (receptor defect) or mistimed | Normal | **Müllerian retained** and Wolffian present | **Male** | Found at exploration for bilateral abdominal testes |
| **Congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH)** | 46,XX | Ovaries | Absent | **Excess (adrenal)** | Female | **Virilised**; can be male-identical | Impalpable gonads and pigmentation are the clues |

- **The organising pattern:**
  - **MIS decides the Müllerian ducts**;
  - **testosterone decides the Wolffian ducts and the external genitalia**;
  - each condition is **a different combination of the two signals**.

**MIS action window — two values in the source**

| Statement | Value | Why they differ |
|---|---|---|
| Müllerian regression under MIS (testicular formation, Instalment 3) | Weeks **8–10** | When **regression is observed** |
| Müllerian sensitivity to MIS (PMDS section) | Weeks **9–13** | **Window in which the ducts can respond**; relevant to mistimed MIS |

### KEY POINTS: GONADAL DEVELOPMENT

- **SRY** makes the indifferent gonad of the ambisexual embryo **differentiate into a testis**.
- **Leydig-cell androgens** drive male genital differentiation, **including the prostate**.
- **Sertoli-cell MIS** makes the Müllerian structures (uterus and fallopian tubes) **degenerate**.
- **Both gonads reach the pelvis by the third month.** The **testis enters the scrotum at about the seventh month**, guided by the gubernaculum.

**Concept —** *Every correlation in this section follows from **two independent testicular signals acting on two independent duct systems**. Remove both (streak gonad), block one (PMDS), give them unilaterally (ovotestis), or add androgen without MIS (CAH), and the phenotype can be predicted.*

## The numbers to lock in

**Key facts, figures and cut-offs**
- **Urogenital ridge identical:** 7–8 weeks. **Gonad starts:** upper lumbar, beside the kidney.
- **Transabdominal descent:** weeks 10–15. **Inguinoscrotal descent:** from the 7th month.
- **Gubernacular migration to the scrotum:** 3–5 cm.
- **Both gonads in the pelvis:** by the 3rd month. **Testis in the scrotum:** about the 7th month.
- **Ovarian descent:** 3rd month. **Gubernaculum tethers to the Müllerian ducts:** week 7.
- **Cryptorchidism:** about 3% of newborn males. **Hydrocele and hernia:** more common.
- **Müllerian sensitivity to MIS:** 9–13 weeks.
- **INSL3 identified:** 1993.

**Commonest / management**
- **Streak gonad in 46,XY → gonadectomy** (gonadoblastoma risk).
- **Streak gonad (any karyotype) → hormone replacement.**
- **PMDS** is found at exploration for **bilateral abdominal testes**.

**Eponyms and named structures**
- **Hernia uteri inguinale:** PMDS; XY with a uterus and tubes.
- **Turner syndrome:** 45,X; streak gonads.
- **Swelling reaction:** gubernacular outgrowth in the transabdominal phase.

**Classic traps**
- **Trap:** gubernacular swelling needs androgen. **Actually:** it is androgen-independent; CSL regression and the inguinoscrotal phase are androgen-dependent.
- **Trap:** androgen acts directly on the gubernaculum to steer migration. **Actually:** it acts mostly indirectly, via CGRP from the genitofemoral nerve.
- **Trap:** an ascending testis is congenital. **Actually:** it is acquired; a remnant of the processus vaginalis fails to involute and stops the cord lengthening.
- **Trap:** hernia and hydrocele are failures of migration. **Actually:** they are failures of processus vaginalis closure (step 2).
- **Trap:** a retained CSL gives an inguinal testis. **Actually:** it gives an intra-abdominal testis.
- **Trap:** PMDS means MIS is absent. **Actually:** usually the MIS receptor is defective; less often MIS is mistimed.
- **Trap:** ovotesticular patients can father children. **Actually:** functioning spermatogenesis has not been reported; female fertility is possible.
- **Trap:** all streak gonads are removed. **Actually:** removal is for 46,XY, because of the gonadoblastoma risk.
