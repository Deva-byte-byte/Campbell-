### Chapter preamble (unheaded opening prose)

- **Two interrelated systems.** The urogenital system has a urinary part and a reproductive part, and the two are **most closely linked early in development**.
- **Timing.** Development **begins in the first trimester and is mostly complete by the end of the second trimester**.
- **Carnegie collection.** It documents human embryos **from fertilisation to the indifferent stage, at about 8–9 weeks' gestation**.
- **Sexual divergence after the indifferent stage.** After 8–9 weeks, **androgens drive male and female urogenital structures apart**.
- **Why this matters clinically.** Understanding normal development is the **basis for understanding congenital anomalies**, and it is the first step in planning reconstruction.

### DEVELOPMENT OF THE EXTERNAL GENITALIA

#### Genetic and hormonal control

- **The indifferent stage.** At 8–9 weeks the external genitalia consist of an **indifferent genital tubercle**.
- **The gonad comes first.** Differentiation of the external genitalia **depends on prior gonadal differentiation**.
- **SRY.** Gonadal sex is set by the **SRY (sex-determining region Y) region on the short arm of the Y chromosome**, and its downstream gene cascade directs testicular development.
- **Genes for the accessory structures.** Genes for the male and female accessory sexual structures are carried on the **X chromosome and the autosomes**, not only on the Y.
- **Leydig cells.** They appear at **about 8 weeks** and begin secreting testosterone.
- **Targets of testosterone and DHT.** Testosterone, and the more potent **dihydrotestosterone (DHT)**, masculinise three targets:
  - the genital tubercle;
  - the Wolffian duct;
  - the urogenital sinus (UGS).
- **Type 2 5α-reductase.** It converts testosterone to DHT and is **expressed in the UGS and the genital tubercle**. It is **required for normal penile and prostatic development**.

#### Masculinisation sequence

- **Order of events:** **increased anogenital distance** (first sign) → **elongation of the genital tubercle** → **formation of the urethral plate** within the tubercle.
- **The genital tubercle** is the precursor of **both the penis and the clitoris**, and contains all three germ layers.

**Germ-layer origin of the genital tubercle**

| Germ layer | Derivative |
|---|---|
| Ectoderm | Skin of the phallus and the prepuce |
| Mesoderm | Corporal bodies (erectile tissue) |
| Endoderm | Urethral plate → penile urethra, reaching to near the distal tip of the future glans |

- **Pattern across the rows.** Each layer gives one tissue compartment. The **urethra is endodermal along its whole length**, and this is the basis of the modern view of the glanular urethra (below).

#### Penile urethra — the "double zipper"

- **Opening zipper (canalisation).** The urethral plate **canalises from proximal to distal** → a **wide, diamond-shaped urethral groove** bounded by the urethral folds.
  - Canalisation is driven by **cell proliferation (Ki67-positive)**.
  - **No apoptosis** is seen (caspase-3-negative).
- **Closing zipper (fusion).** The **medial epithelial edges of the urethral folds fuse in the ventral midline** → the tubular penile urethra plus the **median penile raphe**.
  - A dense **vascular and neural network** lies at the site of fusion.
- **Timetable:**
  - **11–12 weeks:** the coronal sulcus separates glans from shaft.
  - **16 weeks:** the urethral folds are completely fused along the shaft.
  - **18 weeks:** penile and urethral development is essentially complete.
  - **20 weeks:** the normal fetal ventral curvature (**chordee**) has resolved.

**Shaft urethra vs glanular urethra — two mechanisms**

| | Penile shaft urethra | Glanular urethra |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | **Canalisation, then fusion of the urethral folds** | **Direct canalisation of the urethral plate**, with epithelial remodelling inside the glans |
| Origin of epithelium | Endoderm (UGS / urethral plate) | Endoderm (UGS / urethral plate) — **for most of its length** |
| Clinical corollary | Failure of fusion → shaft hypospadias | The shaft–glans junction, where the mechanism changes, is **the commonest site of the ectopic meatus** |

**Glenister vs the endodermal view — origin of the glanular urethra**

| | Ectodermal intrusion theory (Glenister) — outdated | Current (endodermal) view |
|---|---|---|
| Glanular urethra formed by | Skin (ectoderm) growing into the glans to meet the endodermal urethra | Canalisation of the endodermal urethral plate within the glans |
| Epithelium of the whole urethra | Mixed ectoderm and endoderm | **Endoderm of the UGS throughout** |

- **Common ground between the two views.** On either view, **the meatus marks the ectoderm–endoderm (skin–urethral epithelium) junction**.

#### Prepuce

- **Development is tied to the urethra.** The prepuce forms at the same time as the glanular urethra and **depends on normal urethral development**.
- **Sequence:**
  1. At about 8 weeks, low **preputial folds** appear on each side of the shaft.
  2. They join dorsally to form a **flat preputial ridge** at the corona. Ventrally, the ridge is **blocked by the not-yet-formed glanular urethra**.
  3. The folds extend **distally** to cover the glans, and **ventrally** to fuse in the midline.
  4. The epithelial **preputial lamina** is laid down, separating the mesenchyme of the glans from the mesenchyme of the prepuce.
  5. **Postnatally, the lamina delaminates** → preputial space → the prepuce becomes retractable.
- **The prepuce is richly innervated.**
- **Proof of the urethral link.** In hypospadias, preputial tissue is **absent ventrally and excessive dorsally** (the **dorsal hooded foreskin**).

#### Penile innervation

- **Origin.** At **week 5**, neural crest cells give rise to the peripheral and autonomic nervous systems.
- **Somatic supply.** Comes from **S2–S4 via the pudendal nerve**. Its course:
  - over the sacrospinous ligament;
  - through **Alcock's (pudendal) canal**;
  - through the transverse perineal muscle;
  - onto the dorsum of the penis as the **dorsal nerve of the penis**.
- **Autonomic supply.** Comes from the **vesical and prostatic plexuses**:
  - **sympathetic from L1–L2**;
  - **parasympathetic from S2–S4**.
- **Cavernous nerves.** They leave the pelvis **between the transverse perineal muscles and the membranous urethra**, then pass beneath the pubic arch to each corpus cavernosum.
- **Distribution of the dorsal nerves:**
  - They are grouped in bundles at **11 and 1 o'clock**.
  - They fan out around the tunica **as far as the junction of the corpora cavernosa with the corpus spongiosum**.
  - They leave a **nerve-free zone at 12 o'clock**.

**Concept —** *The penile urethra is built by two different mechanisms (**fusion in the shaft, canalisation in the glans**), and the prepuce depends on both. So the junction between the mechanisms predicts the commonest site of hypospadias, and failed ventral urethral closure predicts the hooded foreskin.*

### Clinical Correlation: Hypospadias

- **Incidence:** about **1 in 250 newborn males**.
- **Definition — four components:**
  1. **ectopic urethral meatus**;
  2. **incomplete prepuce (dorsal hooded foreskin)**;
  3. **ventral skin deficiency**;
  4. possibly **penile curvature**.
- **Commonest meatal site:** the **junction of the penile shaft and the glans**. This fits the switch from fold fusion (shaft) to direct canalisation (glans).
- **Anatomy.** The hypospadiac penis is **normal apart from the urethra, spongiosum, overlying skin and ventral prepuce**.
- **Arrest, not deformation.** Abortive elements of the urethra and spongiosum (epithelium, connective tissue, smooth muscle, vessels) are found in the adjacent tissue. So hypospadias is an **arrest of development, not a deformation anomaly**.
- **Aetiology:**
  - It is **unknown in the overwhelming majority** of cases.
  - Working hypothesis: **genetic susceptibility plus maternal exposure to endocrine disruptors**.
  - A known genetic defect is found only rarely. The example is **type 2 5α-reductase deficiency**, which causes **severe hypospadias**, because the enzyme is expressed at the site of urethral fold fusion and is needed to fuse the edges of the groove.

### Clinical Correlation: Correction of Penile Curvature

- **Nerve map.** Along the shaft, the nerves run on the tunica albuginea and **surround the corpora ventrally, up to the junction with the spongiosum**. From the glans to the point where the crura separate to attach to the pubic rami, they are **bundled tightly at 11 and 1 o'clock**.
- **Where to place plication sutures:** **12 o'clock**. This site is suitable for **dorsal plication in mild-to-moderate curvature**, for two reasons:
  - it is the **nerve-free zone**;
  - it is where the **tunica albuginea is thickest**, so the suture anchors well.
- **Scope.** The same principle applies to curvature **with hypospadias** and to **congenital curvature without hypospadias**.

### Clinical Correlation: Duplicated Urethra

- **Rare**, and occurs in both **hypospadiac and epispadiac** forms.
- **Embryology** — longitudinal splitting of the urethral plate:
  - **Complete duplication:** the plate splits from the bladder distally.
  - **Y-duplication:** the plate splits along the shaft of the genital tubercle.
- **Signalling pathway:** **Sonic hedgehog (Shh)** appears to control this process.

**Concept —** *All three correlations apply the same developmental map. **Hypospadias is arrested development** at the fusion–canalisation boundary, **the safe zone for plication is the 12 o'clock nerve-free zone** on the nerve map, and **duplication is abnormal splitting of the single endodermal plate**.*

## The numbers to lock in

**Key facts, figures and cut-offs**
- **Indifferent stage:** 8–9 weeks. Development is **mostly complete by the end of the second trimester**.
- **Leydig cells and testosterone:** appear at about 8 weeks.
- **Neural crest → peripheral and autonomic nerves:** week 5.
- **Coronal sulcus:** 11–12 weeks.
- **Urethral folds fully fused:** 16 weeks.
- **Penile development complete:** 18 weeks.
- **Fetal chordee resolves:** by 20 weeks.
- **Hypospadias:** about 1 in 250 newborn males.
- **Somatic nerve supply:** S2–S4 via the pudendal nerve.
- **Sympathetic supply:** L1–L2. **Parasympathetic supply:** S2–S4.
- **Dorsal nerve bundles:** 11 and 1 o'clock. **Nerve-free zone:** 12 o'clock, where the tunica is also thickest.

**Commonest / first-line**
- **Commonest meatal site in hypospadias:** junction of the shaft and glans.
- **Aetiology of hypospadias:** unknown in most cases; best hypothesis is genetic susceptibility plus endocrine disruptors.
- **Plication for mild-to-moderate curvature:** dorsal, at 12 o'clock.

**Eponyms**
- **SRY:** short arm of the Y chromosome; triggers testis formation.
- **Glenister:** the outdated ectodermal-intrusion theory of the glanular urethra.
- **Alcock's canal:** the pudendal canal that carries the pudendal nerve.
- **Carnegie collection:** the reference series of embryos from fertilisation to the indifferent stage.

**Classic traps**
- **Trap:** the glanular urethra is ectodermal. **Actually:** the whole urethral epithelium is endodermal (UGS).
- **Trap:** the urethral groove opens by apoptosis. **Actually:** by proliferation-driven canalisation; there is no apoptosis.
- **Trap:** the dorsal nerves are confined to 11 and 1 o'clock. **Actually:** they fan ventrally to the spongiosal junction along the shaft. Only 12 o'clock is nerve-free.
- **Trap:** hypospadias is a deformation. **Actually:** it is a developmental arrest; abortive urethral elements are present.
- **Trap:** type 1 5α-reductase is the relevant enzyme. **Actually:** type 2 converts testosterone to DHT in the genital tubercle and UGS.
