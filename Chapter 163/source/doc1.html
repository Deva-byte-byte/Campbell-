Chapter 163 — Focal Therapy of Prostate Cancer — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else

  • Focal therapy (FT) = image-defined, biopsy-confirmed lesion ablation + safety margin.
  • Sweet spot: Gleason 3+4, completely ablatable; maximum 4+3 (2016 consensus).
  • Index lesion (IL) = largest/highest-grade focus → drives natural history.
  • Stamey insignificant cancer: <0.5 mL.
  • PROMIS (multiparametric MRI, mpMRI): sens 93%, spec 41%, positive predictive value (PPV) 51%, negative predictive value (NPV) 89%.
  • Targeted + systematic biopsy in every consensus; never mpMRI alone.
  • Margin: 5 mm → >95%; 6 mm → 98.6% of significant cancer ablated.
  • Erectile dysfunction is NOT a contraindication to FT.
  • Vascular-targeted photodynamic (VTP) therapy vs active surveillance (AS), randomised controlled trial (RCT): progression 28% vs 58%; only level 1 FT evidence.
  • Outfield cancer: short term = selection failure; long term = de novo.

Opening and biological basis

  • Incidence 30.6/100,000/yr; 2nd most incident cancer in men.
  • USA: commonest solid malignancy in men; 2nd cancer death.
  • Best mortality predictors: grade, prostate-specific antigen (PSA), clinical stage; grade earliest.
  • Gleason 1973 = most practical prognosticator.
  • Hanahan–Weinberg hallmarks (2000/2011): more in Gleason 4/5 than 3.
  • Pattern 4: aneusomy 7, 8, 17 + HER-2/neu; epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) copy number NOT raised.
  • Deletions 6q, 16q = commonest; size ∝ Gleason.
  • Multifocality 50–91% at prostatectomy (table range 33–99%).
  • Unifocal 12.4–25% → 52–93.4% if satellites <0.5 mL discounted.
  • IL ≈ 80–90% of tumour volume; source of most extraprostatic extension (EPE).
  • Aggressive feature in satellite, not IL: 12–38%.
  • Modern significance thresholds: >1.09 mL (Charing Cross); >1.31 mL (Rotterdam arm, European Randomised Study of Screening for Prostate Cancer).
  • First FT = hemiablation ("male lumpectomy").
  • Metastases monoclonal; lethal clone once traced to small pattern 3 focus.
  • PIVOT: prostatectomy survival benefit in intermediate risk only.
  • Strategy: ablate grade 4 foci + AS for grade 3 remainder.

Patient selection

  • Selection = fundamental tenet; patient must accept close surveillance.
  • Biopsy confirmation mandatory; mpMRI alone insufficient.
  • Transrectal ultrasonography (TRUS) biopsy unilaterality: high NPV, poor specificity/PPV; ceiling even at 24 cores.
  • Transperineal mapping biopsy (TMB): 5-mm grid; >half of TRUS-unilateral disqualified.
  • Unilateral predictors: PSA ≤4 ng/mL + unilateral biopsy; >2 positive cores → more failure after FT.
  • Anterior cancers 15–33%; missed by TRUS; ideal FT targets (both neurovascular bundles spared).
  • mpMRI for EPE: spec 73–91%, sens 35–63%; beats Partin tables.
  • mpMRI best for >1 cm, Gleason ≥7, index tumours.
  • mpMRI underestimates volume 5–30%.
  • 3 T > 1.5 T; endorectal coil at 1.5 T; reader experience critical.
  • Prostate Imaging–Reporting and Data System (PI-RADS) v2 (2015): 5-point Likert; score ∝ Gleason.
  • In-bore biopsy: most accurate, costly. Cognitive fusion = software fusion in experts.
  • Transperineal = transrectal MRI-targeted detection of clinically significant cancer (csPCa).
  • ~90% outfield csPCa within 1 cm of target ("penumbra").
  • Prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA) 100–1000× overexpressed; ¹¹¹In-capromab pendetide failed.
  • PSMA positron emission tomography (PET) vs mpMRI: PET more specific, mpMRI more sensitive; PET unaffected by biopsy haemorrhage.
  • Shear-wave elastography cut-off 50 kPa.

Consensus cut-offs:

  • Residual 3+3: ≤5 mm core (2013) → ≤1 mm (2016).
  • Max size (2016): 1.5 mL or 3 mL (not crossing midline); 20–25% gland (crossing).
  • Life expectancy (2013): >10 yr, not <5 yr; 2016: no limit.
  • 2016: ≥12-core systematic + MRI–TRUS fusion biopsy.
  • 2019: no cribriform/ductal; retreat to delay radical therapy.
  • Risk group: intermediate.

Ablation patterns and technologies

  • FT = lesion, quadrant, hemiablation; hockey stick = partial ablation, not FT.
  • Transrectal → posterior; transperineal → anterior.
  • Cryotherapy and irreversible electroporation (IRE): transperineal (metal probes, 17 G+); high-intensity focused ultrasound (HIFU) transrectal; laser either.

Cryotherapy:

  • Joule–Thomson probes, 17 G; variable ice length; visible ice edge; urethral warmer.
  • Thermocouples: sphincter, neurovascular bundles, Denonvilliers fascia.
  • Biochemical progression-free survival (BPFS) 71–98%; 3-yr 56–62.5%; 5-yr 62–70%.
  • Continence 95.1–100%; potency 46.8–100% (half on phosphodiesterase type 5 inhibitors).
  • Clavien ≥3 ≤4%; retention commonest → catheter a few weeks; recto-urethral fistula (RUF) ≤0.8%.
  • Cryo On-Line Data (COLD) registry: partial = whole-gland BPFS; better erections (~2×, intermediate risk).

HIFU:

  • ≥55 °C; posterior lesions; anterior hard (dissipation, oedema).
  • Limits: size, height, calcification; transurethral resection of prostate (TURP) beforehand by some.
  • Infield 16.7–54% (csPCa 0–21.8%); outfield 7–42.8% (csPCa 2–7.5%).
  • Continence 90–95%; potency 50–78%.
  • HEAT registry (1379): failure-free 68% intermediate, 65% high risk; 7-yr 69%.
  • MRI-guided transurethral ultrasound ablation (TULSA): MR thermometry; precision 1.3 mm; anterior gland reachable; grade 3 ~3%, no grade 4–5.

Laser:

  • ≥60 °C; ablation >5 mm beyond lesion; 14 G introducer, 17 G applicator.
  • Edge: neovascularisation + mitoses → margin essential.
  • Infield 11–50%, outfield 16.7–60%; continence 95–100%.
  • Laser RUF healed with catheter in 4–6 weeks.

IRE:

  • Non-thermal; probes 10–20 mm apart; >1600 V/cm.
  • 20 test pulses → deep anaesthesia → 70 pulses/pair.
  • csPCa infield 1–9.8%, outfield 5–16%.
  • vs robot-assisted radical prostatectomy: better continence and erections; some need salvage.

VTP:

  • TOOKAD (padeliporfin) 4 mg/kg IV; 753 nm, 150 mW/cm, 200 J/cm.
  • RCT, 415 low risk: progression 28% vs 58% AS.
  • RCT limits: Gleason 6, short follow-up, surrogate end points, reclassification.
  • Perineal pain 15% VTP vs <1% AS.
  • Radical therapy VTP vs AS: 2 yr 7 vs 32%; 3 yr 15 vs 44%; 4 yr 24 vs 53%.

Other energies:

  • Radiofrequency ablation: ~100 °C; ≤100 W; 450–1200 kHz; fat limits conduction.
  • High-dose-rate (HDR) brachytherapy: stricture up to 8.2% in 2 yr (apex, bulbar urethra).
  • Focal brachytherapy biopsy: 24 months.
  • Focal HDR: outfield csPCa 33%; new impotence 50%.
  • Stereotactic body radiotherapy tumour boost: 5–10 fractions.
  • Urethral-sparing intensity-modulated radiotherapy: no toxicity gain; worse oncology.
  • PRX302: aerolysin activated by PSA.
  • Androgen deprivation + cryo: no benefit.

Salvage (post-radiotherapy):

  • Cryo: 5-yr BPFS ~50%; RUF ~1.4% = most feared.
  • HIFU: 3-yr BPFS 48%; continence 64–87.5%; fistula ~2%; grade ≥3 ~quarter.
  • IRE: progression-free 60%, metastasis-free 91%; continence 93%.
  • Focal ≈ whole-gland salvage control; better function.

Post-focal therapy follow-up

  • PSA fall after FT: 50–80%, variable.
  • Nadir + 1.0 ng/mL at 12 mo; nadir + 1.5 at 24–36 mo → biopsy.
  • Biopsy: mpMRI + 4–6 targeted cores at 3–6 mo; mpMRI + systematic at 1–2 and 5 yr.
  • Prostate Imaging after Focal Ablation (PI-FAB): dynamic contrast-enhanced (DCE) read first; enhancement >3 mm → biopsy.
  • Success, infield: short term eradication; long term no csPCa.
  • PI-FAB 1 monitor; 2 (DCE+) PSA kinetics; 3 (DCE+, diffusion-weighted imaging+) biopsy.
  • PI-FAB 2 → 1 if linear, off original site; 2 → 3 if >3 mm or growing.
  • Contrast-enhanced ultrasound = mpMRI for early ablation success; grey-scale TRUS cannot see zone.

Conclusions

  • Whitmore: "cure possible when necessary, necessary when possible?"
  • Up to half on AS eventually have radical treatment.
  • No trial of focal ablation + adjuvant whole-gland radiotherapy.

Classic traps

  • Trap: multifocal → no FT — Actually: IL + satellites <0.5 mL qualifies.
  • Trap: MRI-negative → skip systematic biopsy — Actually: NPV 89%; biopsy still needed.
  • Trap: MRI shows true size — Actually: underestimates 5–30%; add margin.
  • Trap: impotence contraindicates FT — Actually: not a contraindication.
  • Trap: transrectal HIFU for anterior tumour — Actually: posterior tool; anterior → transperineal/TULSA.
  • Trap: PSA undetectable after FT — Actually: 50–80% fall; nadir + 1.0 at 12 mo.
  • Trap: read T2 first after FT — Actually: PI-FAB reads DCE first.
  • Trap: IRE spares bundles and urethra — Actually: fibrinoid necrosis, urethral denudation.
  • Trap: VTP RCT shows survival gain — Actually: Gleason 6, surrogate end points.
  • Trap: residual 3+3 tolerance loosened — Actually: tightened 5 mm → 1 mm.
