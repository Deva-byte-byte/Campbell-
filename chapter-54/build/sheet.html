Chapter 54 — Management of Paediatric Kidney Stone Disease — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else

  • Ultrasound (US) first; low-dose non-contrast CT (NCCT) only if US non-diagnostic + high suspicion.
  • Every stone-forming child → full metabolic evaluation: stone analysis + 24-h urine.
  • Commonest abnormality: hypercalciuria (30–50%), mostly idiopathic; >4 mg/kg/day.
  • Uncomplicated ureteric stone ≤10 mm → observation ± α-blocker (off-label, ≤6 weeks).
  • Renal ≤20 mm → SWL or URS first-line (shockwave lithotripsy, ureteroscopy).
  • Renal >20 mm → PCNL (percutaneous nephrolithotomy), clearance about 90%.
  • No routine pre-stenting before URS.
  • Low-dose CT before PCNL — strong recommendation, grade C evidence.
  • Do not restrict calcium; restrict sodium; fluid >2–2.5 L/day (adolescents).
  • PH1 (primary hyperoxaluria type 1) → pyridoxine trial in all; lumasiran FDA-approved.

Epidemiology

  • Recurrence about 50% within 5 years (3–5 years in children).
  • US prevalence 5% → 11%; youth incidence +5–10%/year.
  • Females 15–19 y: 75 → 120/100,000; 52% > males.
  • Sex ratio: adult M : F 3 : 1 vs early-onset female-predominant.
  • Early-onset = onset <24 years.
  • Commonest stone: calcium oxalate (CaOx) about 80%, as in adults; children more CaP, less uric acid (alkaline urine).
  • Randall's plaque = papillary hydroxyapatite; platform for idiopathic CaOx.
  • Antibiotic-linked stones: risk persists up to 5 years; greatest in children.
  • Drug-crystal stones 1–2%.
  • Spina bifida post-augmentation: about 1%/year, ≥10 × baseline.
  • Fracture risk peaks at 10–19 years.

Evaluation

  • Gold standard: NCCT, about 100% sensitivity/specificity.
  • US: >70% sensitive, >95% specific; ureteric stones poorly seen.
  • US stone = hyperechoic focus + twinkle; maximise PRF (pulse repetition frequency).
  • No shadow for stones <4 mm; US overestimates size 1–2 mm.
  • Low-dose CT: 40 mA for ≤50 kg.
  • Image Gently Alliance: 2007.
  • Metabolic abnormality → up to 5 × recurrence.
  • Stone analysis: infrared spectroscopy / X-ray diffraction; diagnostic — cystine, struvite, 2,8-DHA (2,8-dihydroxyadenine), xanthine.
  • 24-h urine: Ca, oxalate, urate, Na, citrate, creatinine, volume, pH, cystine.
  • Not toilet-trained → spot solute : creatinine ratios.
  • PTH (parathyroid hormone) not routine — only if ↑Ca + ↓phosphate.
  • CYP24A1: ↑1,25-(OH)₂D mimics vitamin D intoxication; screen 25-OH-D : 24,25-(OH)₂D.
  • Dent screen: urine β2-microglobulin / retinol-binding protein.
  • Crystals: hexagonal = cystine; coffin-lid = struvite; brown spiculated = 2,8-DHA.

Urinary Metabolic Abnormalities

  • Hypercalciuria: >4 mg/kg/day (>2 y) or >200–250 mg/day.
  • Spot Ca : Cr: ≤0.21 school-age; <0.8 at 0–6 mo; <0.6 at 7–12 mo.
  • Idiopathic hypercalciuria: 4% of healthy children; autosomal dominant, incomplete penetrance.
  • Hypercalciuria work-up: serum calcium first → high / low / normal bins.
  • Oxalate: 80–85% endogenous; normal <40 (text) / <50 (table) mg/1.73 m².
  • PH markers: PH1 glycolate · PH2 glycerate · PH3 4-hydroxy-2-oxoglutarate.
  • PH genes: AGXT (AGT, peroxisomal) · GRHPR · HOGA1 (mitochondrial).
  • All PH markers normal + ↑ oxalate → secondary hyperoxaluria.
  • Hypocitraturia: <128 mg/g (M), <300 mg/g (F); table male normal >180.
  • Cystinuria: SLC3A1/SLC7A9; normal <60, homozygote >400 mg/day/1.73 m².
  • Uric acid: FE (fractional excretion) 30–50% neonate → 8–12%; stones <5%.
  • Hyperuricosuria: >815 mg/day/1.73 m²; spot >0.56 mg/dL GFR (>2 y).
  • Uric acid stone risk: hyperuricosuria + low urine pH.
  • B6 deficiency mimics PH1; cystinuria can present in infancy (staghorn).
  • Hereditary renal hypouricaemia: SLC22A12/SLC2A9; exercise-induced renal failure.
  • Eponyms: Dent — X-linked CLCN5, low-molecular-weight proteinuria. Bartter — metabolic alkalosis. Lesch–Nyhan — HPRT deficiency, X-linked. Williams — dysmorphism, hypercalcaemic hypercalciuria.
  • X-linked stone genes: Dent, HPRT, PRPS → boys.

Management — MET and Guidelines

  • MET (medical expulsive therapy) = α1A/α1D blockade; tamsulosin.
  • Spontaneous passage: best <5 mm, distal.
  • Up to 60% of children need surgery.
  • AUA/EUS 2016 (American Urological Association/Endourological Society): 8 paediatric recommendations, half expert opinion.
  • Failed observation/MET → URS or SWL (strong).
  • Asymptomatic non-obstructing → surveillance with US.
  • No routine open/laparoscopic/robotic surgery unless anatomic abnormality.
  • Prophylaxis: all URS/PCNL; SWL if high-risk; stop ≤24 h.
  • Residual fragment cut-point: 4 mm on US (2 mm overestimate + 2 mm CT).
  • Radiation limits: 50 mSv/12 months; <20 mSv/year over 5 years.

Ureteroscopy

  • Clearance approaching 90%; <6 y or <20 kg → more complications.
  • Dusting: >50 Hz, <0.4 J → <1 mm; fragmentation → 1–4 mm.
  • Scopes: semirigid 4.5–6 Fr; flexible 7.5–9 Fr, 270° deflection.
  • 4 Fr catheter / 3.7 Fr stent → 0.025-inch wire only.
  • 8/10 Fr won't pass → 4.6–4.8 Fr stent ≥14 days.
  • Fibres: 150–200 µm lower pole; 365 µm elsewhere.
  • Stent on string 5–7 days.
  • Stent if: long procedure, many passes, trauma/oedema.
  • Stricture: distal → reimplant; proximal → pyeloplasty; mid → excision + anastomosis.

Shockwave Lithotripsy

  • First paediatric SWL: 1986.
  • Favourable: <10 mm, <1000 HU (Hounsfield units), non-lower-pole.
  • Unfavourable: cystine, brushite; infundibulopelvic angle >45°.
  • Settings: <20 kV; ≤2500–3000 shocks (<2000 very young); 80 > 120/min.
  • Ungated shocks safe in children.
  • Absolute CI (contraindications): bleeding diathesis, untreated UTI (urinary tract infection), pregnancy.
  • SWL >20 mm → stent or nephrostomy (AUA); authors: no SWL >15 mm.
  • URS = SWL for clearance; SWL fewer anaesthetics and ED visits.
  • Steinstrasse — ureteric column of post-SWL fragments.

Percutaneous Nephrolithotomy

  • First-line >20 mm; clearance about 90%.
  • Child 24–30 Fr ≈ adult 72 Fr; mini <20–24 Fr (1998); micro <10 Fr.
  • Culture 2–3 weeks pre-op; bladder urine ≠ pelvic/stone culture.
  • Sheath 18 Fr (24 Fr staghorn); nephrostomy 7 Fr infant → 16 Fr adolescent.
  • Lower-pole posterior calyx: one above most inferior on AP (>90%).
  • Upper pole: below 11th rib, medial calyx.
  • Complications: intraoperative 10%, postoperative 20%; ≥Clavien III ≤16%; transfusion <10%.
  • Operate only where paediatric interventional radiology can embolise.
  • Tubeless PCNL not established in children.
  • Complication drivers: operative time, sheath size, mid-calyceal puncture, partial staghorn.

Secondary Prevention

  • Fluid: ≥ maintenance (child); >2–2.5 L (adolescent); 1.5–2 L/m² cystinuria/PH.
  • Sodium: <2–3 mEq/kg/day (child); <2.4 g/day (adolescent).
  • Protein: 100% of daily allowance; avoid excess animal protein.
  • Hydrochlorothiazide 1–2 mg/kg/day; add potassium citrate.
  • Potassium citrate 2–4 mEq/kg/day.
  • Allopurinol 4–10 mg/kg/day (max 300 mg).
  • Pyridoxine 2–5 → 8–10 mg/kg/day; 10–30% of PH1 respond.
  • pH targets: uric acid >6.5; cystine >7; CaP risk >6.5.
  • FDA-approved (children): thiols + lumasiran only.
  • Commonest thiol: tiopronin.
  • Sodium citrate less ideal — sodium → calciuria.
  • Grapefruit ↑ risk; orange juice, lemonade ↓ risk.
  • Stop vitamin C in hyperoxaluric CaOx formers.
  • ILLUMINATE-A (lumasiran): oxalate ↓ 65%; 84% ≤1.5 × ULN.
  • PUSH trial: incentives + coaching for hydration.
  • Elastosis perforans serpiginosa — D-penicillamine.

Classic traps

  • Trap: childhood stones mostly genetic — Actually: mostly idiopathic CaOx.
  • Trap: no shadow = no stone — Actually: stones <4 mm rarely shadow; use twinkle.
  • Trap: check PTH routinely — Actually: only if ↑Ca + ↓phosphate.
  • Trap: low-calcium diet — Actually: raises urinary oxalate; don't restrict.
  • Trap: "strong" = strong evidence — Actually: CT before PCNL is grade C.
  • Trap: pre-stent routinely — Actually: not recommended; more ED visits.
  • Trap: balloon-dilate the ureter — Actually: passive stent dilatation only.
  • Trap: allopurinol first for uric acid stones — Actually: fluid + alkali.
  • Trap: genotype predicts pyridoxine response — Actually: trial in all PH1.
  • Trap: more alkali always better — Actually: pH >6.5 → CaP stones.
