Chapter 56 — Hypospadias — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else

  • Incidence 1 in 150–300 male births; distal 70–85%, proximal 10–25%.
  • Hypospadias + undescended testis → DSD (disorder of sex development) work-up (karyotype, ultrasound, hormones).
  • Repair at 6–12 months (AAP, American Academy of Pediatrics, 1996); genital awareness about 18 months.
  • Curvature <30° → dorsal plication; >30° → divide plate → ventral lengthening (usually staged).
  • TIP (tubularised incised plate): >90% of distal repairs; stop 2–3 mm short of meatus.
  • Commonest complication: fistula (just under 10%); repair ≥6–12 months.
  • Meatal stenosis: symptoms + <8 Fr prepubertal or <12 Fr postpubertal.
  • Buccal mucosa = graft of choice; lip within the glans.
  • 25–75% need extra procedures after planned two-stage repair.
  • Follow up through puberty: only about half of complications appear in year 1.

Embryology

  • Triad: ventral meatus, ventral curvature, dorsal hood.
  • Critical window weeks 8–16; DHT (dihydrotestosterone) key at 8–12 weeks.
  • SRY 7–8 weeks; 5α-reductase type 2 → DHT locally.
  • Two-zipper (endodermal): opening zipper both sexes; closing zipper male, androgen-dependent.
  • Ventral prepuce fuses about 20 weeks → incomplete foreskin in 95%.
  • MIP (megameatus with intact prepuce) 5%: retract foreskin before every circumcision.

Diagnosis

  • Severity: meatus + curvature after degloving; distal-looking can be proximal.
  • GMS (glans–meatus–shaft) score: each 1–4, summed; curvature <30 / 30–60 / >60°; higher = more complications.
  • Hernia or hydrocele 9–16%; cryptorchidism about 7% (about 10% proximal).
  • Non-palpable testis → DSD about 50%; proximal vs distal DSD 64% vs 7%.
  • Severe hypospadias = 46,XY DSD (since 2006); normal karyotype can mislead.
  • Curvature without hypospadias → defer circumcision.

Aetiology

  • Risks: prematurity (<37 weeks), SGA (small for gestational age, <10th centile), IUGR (intrauterine growth restriction), small placenta (2×), assisted reproduction.
  • Twin concordance 9–27%; first-degree relative 9–15%; chromosomal defect up to 30%.
  • Syndromes: WAGR, Denys–Drash, Smith–Lemli–Opitz (about 200 syndromes).
  • TDS (testicular dysgenesis syndrome): poor semen, testis cancer, cryptorchidism, hypospadias.
  • Short AGD (anogenital distance) ∝ severity; EDC (endocrine-disrupting chemical) exposure.

Surgical assessment

  • No routine VCUG (voiding cystourethrogram) or renal ultrasound (non-syndromic).
  • Stretched penile length (micropenis) + both groins.
  • Glans <14 mm → more complications (disputed).
  • Testosterone cypionate 2 mg/kg IM at 5 and 2 weeks pre-op if glans <15 mm; +4 mm with two doses.
  • Androgen side effects resolve within 6 months.
  • Caudal commonest block; fistula link disputed; pudendal for older children.
  • Ketorolac 0.5 mg/kg IV at end.
  • Cystoscopy only for severe proximal or redo.
  • POST (plate objective scoring tool): low AB/BC → inlay graft rather than TIP.
  • Curvature grading: mild <30°, moderate 30–45°, severe ≥45°; goniometer.
  • Artificial erection: tourniquet + saline (Gittes–McLaughlin); tourniquet hides basal curve.
  • Plication: Nesbit (1965) excision; Baskin midline 12 o'clock nerve-free zone.
  • Recurrence: plication 28% vs grafting 9%.
  • Dermal graft 20% larger than defect; corporotomy 9 to 3 o'clock.
  • Congenital curvature (normal urethra): recurrence 2–15%.
  • Sutures: chromic or polyglactin > PDS (polydioxanone); 6-0 to 8-0 infants.
  • Never hair-bearing skin for urethroplasty.
  • Belman flap = de-epithelialised preputial dartos barrier.
  • Tourniquet released every 30 min; dressing 72 h.

Advancement and tubularisation

  • MAGPI (meatal advancement and glanuloplasty incorporated; Duckett 1981): glanular meatus only.
  • MIV (M inverted V; Decter 1991): coronal or proximal glanular; avoids retraction.
  • Urethromeatoplasty: Heineke–Mikulicz for stenotic distal meatus.
  • GAP (glans approximation procedure; Zaontz 1989): deep wide groove, fish-mouth meatus (MIP).
  • TD (Thiersch–Duplay): U-incision sized to 12 Fr.
  • TIP (Snodgrass 1994): incise plate to 12 mm total width.
  • Mathieu: flip-flap; narrow base → fistula; SLAM (slit-like adjusted Mathieu) → slit meatus.
  • OIF (onlay island flap): narrow plate; diverticulum if flap too wide; less stenosis.

Proximal repair

  • TPIF (transverse preputial island flap, Duckett tube, 1980): oval meatus; Duckett moved to OIF.
  • OIF proximal: plate + flap ≤12 mm.
  • Proximal TIP abandoned (stricture, recurrent curvature).
  • Bracka / STAG (staged tubularised autograft): free graft, 1–3 ungrafted corporotomies.
  • STAC: three stages; avoids graft over corporotomy.
  • Byars (1951): pedicled preputial flaps; highest staged complications.
  • Ulaanbaatar: distal tube first; second stage at 6 months.
  • Stage interval ≥6 months (8–12); repeat artificial erection.
  • Poiseuille: resistance ∝ length, 1/r⁴ → long neourethra = stricture.

Postoperative care and complications

  • Diversion 3–21 days; oxybutynin for spasm; Foley must be midline.
  • Table 56.1: curvature or torsion >30°; glans fusion <2 mm; skin surplus ≥2 cm.
  • Fistula: original meatus or corona; recurrence about 20%; coronal → revise urethroplasty.
  • Fistula work-up: bougie à boule → urethroscopy → stress test.
  • Routine meatal dilatation unnecessary.
  • Stricture <1 cm: urethrotomy rarely durable.
  • Diverticulum: preputial flap, two-stage, proximal repairs.
  • BXO (balanitis xerotica obliterans): excise, staged buccal; recurrence up to 40%; invades buccal.
  • Utricle + recurrent UTI (urinary tract infection) → excise or fulgurate.

Outcomes

  • Pfistermuller TIP meta-analysis: complications 10.6%, reoperation 4.5%.
  • TIP: fistula 5.7% > stenosis 3.6% > stricture 1.3%.
  • TIP vs Mathieu: more stenosis with TIP.
  • Distal success 83–95%; proximal complications 23–68%.
  • STAG <25%; Byars 30–70%.
  • Recurrent curvature after two-stage 0–10%.
  • Recurrent UTI after repair about 2%.
  • HOPE (Hypospadias Objective Penile Evaluation): photo panels; 1–10 per item.

Reoperation

  • Devine: no "small operations"; always artificial erection.
  • Buccal: 10% oversize; fenestrate; wait ≥6 months; staged success 30–75%.
  • Bladder mucosa: prolapse up to 38%.
  • Tissue expander: sufficient 95%; complications 41%.
  • Cecil–Culp: release at 6–8 weeks (Cecil) vs 9–12 months (modified).

Classic traps

  • Trap: meatal position = severity — Actually: assess after degloving.
  • Trap: circumcise newborn with MIP or curvature — Actually: retract first; save foreskin.
  • Trap: normal 46,XY excludes DSD — Actually: consider 5α-reductase and AIS (androgen insensitivity).
  • Trap: routine VCUG — Actually: not indicated.
  • Trap: plicate severe curvature — Actually: recurs; lengthen ventrally.
  • Trap: tourniquet artificial erection is complete — Actually: hides basal curvature.
  • Trap: MAGPI for coronal meatus — Actually: retraction; glanular only.
  • Trap: excise a coronal fistula simply — Actually: revise the urethroplasty.
  • Trap: meatotomy for BXO stenosis — Actually: excise all and stage.
  • Trap: most complications in year 1 — Actually: about half; strictures late.
