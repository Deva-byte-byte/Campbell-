Chapter 56 — Hypospadias — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else

  • Commonest complication: urethrocutaneous fistula (just under 10%).
  • Fistula repair: wait ≥6–12 months; exclude distal obstruction first.
  • Meatal stenosis: symptoms + calibration <8 Fr prepubertal or <12 Fr postpubertal.
  • Recurrent curvature or torsion: >30°.
  • Buccal mucosa = graft of choice; lip within the glans.
  • Severe proximal hypospadias → staged: curvature first, urethroplasty second.
  • 25–75% need extra procedures after planned two-stage repair.
  • Distal success 83–95%; proximal complications 23–68%.
  • BXO (balanitis xerotica obliterans): excise all, staged buccal; recurrence up to 40%.
  • Follow up through puberty: only about half of complications appear in year 1.

Proximal repair and postoperative care

  • Poiseuille: resistance ∝ length, ∝ 1/radius⁴ → long neourethra = stricture risk.
  • Glans non-distensible + no spongiosum → diverticulum.
  • TIP (tubularised incised plate) long neourethra → flattened flow; onlay more elastic.
  • Modified Ulaanbaatar: Duckett tube + Byars flaps; second stage at 6 months.
  • Diversion 3–21 days; soft stent in complex repairs → less stenosis and fistula.
  • Oxybutynin until stent removal (bladder spasm).
  • Foley must be fixed in the midline; lateral pull → glans dehiscence.
  • Ibuprofen and ketorolac: no added bleeding; paracetamol + ibuprofen regular for 48–72 h.

Complications

Definitions (Table 56.1):

  • Glans dehiscence: fusion <2 mm or coronal or more proximal meatus.
  • Skin surplus ≥2 cm; torsion >30°.
  • Non-retractable reconstructed prepuce: acceptable before puberty, abnormal after.

Fistula:

  • Commonest at the original meatus or coronal margin.
  • Small early fistula rarely closes spontaneously.
  • Work-up: bougie à boule → urethroscopy → saline or methylene blue stress test.
  • Small shaft → excise tract, multilayer offset closure.
  • Coronal fistula → revise the urethroplasty (implies distal narrowing).
  • Recurrence about 20%; reduce with tunica vaginalis or dartos layers.
  • Diversion: none if simple; 7–14 days if complex.

Meatus and glans:

  • Glans dehiscence: reoperate only if symptomatic; inlay graft.
  • Postoperative meatus ≥8 Fr; "laser-beam" stream.
  • Routine dilatation unnecessary; refractory → dorsal meatotomy or redo.

Stricture:

  • Sites: native–neourethral junction, coronal margin, glans.
  • Characterise by cystoscopy.
  • Urethrotomy or dilatation rarely durable, even <1 cm.
  • Refractory → revision urethroplasty (flap or buccal; often staged).
  • Hair + stones + UTI (urinary tract infection) → resect and replace.
  • Enlarged utricle with recurrent UTI → excise or fulgurate.

Diverticulum and curvature:

  • Diverticulum: commoner after preputial flap, two-stage, proximal repairs; excise + multilayer.
  • BXO: topical betamethasone or clobetasol, tacrolimus → usually fails → cutback, staged buccal.
  • BXO invades buccal mucosa.
  • Curvature check: deglove + artificial erection; tourniquet hides basal curvature.
  • Buried penis: anchor Buck's fascia to dermis; scrotoplasty for penoscrotal transposition.

Hypospadias outcomes

  • Pfistermuller TIP meta-analysis: complications 10.6%, reoperation 4.5%.
  • TIP complications: fistula 5.7% > meatal stenosis 3.6% > stricture 1.3%.
  • Extra dartos layer → fewer complications.
  • TIP vs Mathieu: equal fistula; more meatal stenosis with TIP.
  • Recurrent UTI about 2% → anatomical imaging (narrowing, VUR, diverticulum, utricle).
  • TIP → raised PVR (post-void residual); OIF (onlay island flap) → diverticulum or fistula.
  • Fistula and dehiscence early; stricture late.
  • Proximal fistula 3–45%; recurrent curvature after two-stage repair 0–10%.
  • STAG (staged tubularised autograft) <25%; Byars two-stage 30–70% (43–81% across centres).
  • Proximal TIP: no longer recommended (recurrent curvature).
  • Staged repair → longer penis, better cosmesis.
  • Glans width <14 mm, proximal meatus, no barrier flap, previous surgery = risk.
  • Instruments: HOSE (Hypospadias Objective Scoring Evaluation); PPS (Penile Perception Score: patient and parents); GPS (Genital Perception Score); HOPE (Hypospadias Objective Penile Evaluation: independent panels, photographs).
  • HOPE: 1–10 per item; torsion and curvature 0–30° = 10, >70° = 1.
  • HRQoL (health-related quality of life) domains: appearance, sexual, psychological, social, voiding.
  • Mild undiagnosed distal → minimal sexual, fertility or voiding problems.

Reoperation

  • Devine: avoid "small operations"; resect scar, major tissue shifts.
  • Always artificial erection; low threshold for cystoscopy.
  • SOS (split-onlay skin) flap: about 1 cm pedicled skin, split for onlay + cover.
  • Nitroglycerin topical for 24 h → flap flow (no RCT, randomised controlled trial).
  • Tissue expander: skin sufficient 95%; complications 41%.
  • Bladder mucosa: prolapse up to 38%; superseded.
  • Buccal: harvest 10% oversize; fenestrate 18–21 G; quilting sutures.
  • Wait ≥6 months (to 12) before tubularising the graft.
  • Graft massage from 2 weeks; topical steroid from 1 month.
  • Staged buccal > single-stage; dorsal inlay best in redo.
  • Buccal staged success 30–75%; meatal stenosis commonest failure.
  • Cecil–Culp: penis buried in scrotum; release at 6–8 weeks (Cecil) or 9–12 months (modified).
  • Donor site: leave open (heals by secondary intention).

Classic traps

  • Trap: small early fistula will close — Actually: rarely; repair at 6–12 months.
  • Trap: excise a coronal fistula simply — Actually: revise the urethroplasty.
  • Trap: repair a fistula without checking distally — Actually: distal obstruction causes recurrence.
  • Trap: calibration <8 Fr alone = stenosis — Actually: symptoms are also required.
  • Trap: routine meatal dilatation — Actually: unnecessary.
  • Trap: assess curvature with a tourniquet — Actually: it hides basal curvature.
  • Trap: meatotomy for BXO stenosis — Actually: fails; excise and stage.
  • Trap: cheek graft for the glans — Actually: lip (thinner, fewer dehiscences).
  • Trap: most complications appear in year 1 — Actually: about half; strictures late.
  • Trap: small redo procedures after failure — Actually: Devine; radical reconstruction.
