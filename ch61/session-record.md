## The numbers to lock in

### Instalment 1
- **Thresholds**: transplant age most often **13–17 years**; safe detrusor pressure **<40 cm H₂O**; algorithm "small capacity **<30%**"; small child **<15 kg** → aorta/IVC and possibly transperitoneal access.
- **Commonest**: congenital = hypoplasia/aplasia/dysplasia, then obstructive uropathy; acquired = FSGS; early ESKD bladder = low capacity, overactivity, poor compliance; ileum or sigmoid = commonest augment segments.
- **First line**: antimuscarinic (oxybutynin, titrated); augmentation is second line.
- **Landmark registry**: NAPRTCS.
- **Eponyms**: Gibson incision (retroperitoneal graft site).
- **Traps**: routine VCUG is not needed; high pressure harms the kidney without reflux; volitional emptying is never assumed; the gastric augment has been abandoned (haematuria–dysuria syndrome).

### Instalment 2
- **Thresholds**: augment at **<75%** capacity, **>40 cm H₂O**, CIC every **3–4 h** (text) or **≤3 h** (box); **6–12 weeks** healing after enterocystoplasty; transplant in the **first 2 years** → defer continent diversion.
- **Absolute indications for native nephrectomy**: refractory hypertension, nephrotic syndrome with malnutrition or hypercoagulability, massive polyuria, recurrent upper-tract infection, large stone burden, high malignancy risk, very large kidneys. **Relative**: massive VUR.
- **When not to operate**: no concurrent procedures in infants; postpone augmentation in a PUV bladder; do not reconstruct intraperitoneally on PD without planning haemodialysis.
- **Eponyms**: Barry technique (4-cm tunnel between parallel incisions).
- **Traps**: never use the allograft to test the bladder; metabolic problems are not an absolute contraindication to diversion; keep the normal ureter and remove the dilated refluxing one.

### Instalment 3
- **Thresholds**: double-J removal at **4–6 weeks**, early removal at **5–7 days**; complications: stenosis **6%**, leak **3%** (<5%), stones **2%**, reflux **2%**; obstruction **up to 8%**, more than half within **100 days**; catheter trial **1–2 weeks**; focal stenosis → balloon + stent **4–6 weeks**; stones **>1 cm** and growing → treat; endoscopic graft-VUR resolution about one-third.
- **Preferred**: extravesical non-refluxing ureteroneocystostomy; intravesical reimplant for graft VUR; the catheter as the first diagnostic step.
- **Traps**: hydronephrosis grade does not track impairment; obstruction and rejection coexist; isolated VUR does not cause progressive hydronephrosis; a graft mass is PTLD until proven otherwise; avoid the appendix as a ureteric substitute.

**Substitution key**
3. In the text layer, `!` → α, `"` → β, `#` → <, `'` → >, `%` → ≤, `&` → ≥. Confirmed against the rendered page ("α-blockers", "<15 kg", ">1 cm", "≤1 and ≥2 UTIs"). These are extraction artefacts; the printed page is correct.

**Source errors and inconsistencies**
4. **Dropped numeral**: "low-pressure storage for up to hours" (p. 1281). A number is missing before "hours". In context it matches the "every 3–4 hours" catheterisation interval. The notes say "an adequate interval" instead of guessing a number.
5. **Augmentation catheterisation interval**: the body says "no more frequently than every 3 to 4 hours"; the Key Points box says "every 3 hours or less". Both are carried, and both are tabulated in the numbers block.
6. **Complication rates**: the Key Points box gives stenosis 6%, leak 3%, stones 2%, reflux 2%. The body gives obstruction up to 8%, leak <5%, and stones up to 5% / <1% / 2% at 3 years. These are **different series and denominators**. Both are tabulated in the notes, with the reason.
7. **Algorithm "small bladder capacity (<30%)"** (Fig. 61.1) versus the **75%** augmentation threshold in the text. These are different decision points (a screening trigger versus an augmentation trigger), not a contradiction. The unit is ambiguous: presumably the percentage of expected capacity for age.
8. **Fig. 61.22 legend versus its plot**: the extracted text-layer data under Fig. 61.22 belong to Fig. 61.23 (the PUV/non-PUV survival curve). The rendered page confirms that Fig. 61.22 is a box plot of GFR by UTI count.
9. **"Kim et at., 2023"** should read **et al.**
10. **NAPRTCS citation**: the body says "Chua et al., 2018"; Selected References lists 2019 (the 2018 report, published 2019). The citation is dropped from the notes.

**Style decisions recorded**
11. **Drug table**: MANDATORY COMPARISONS asks for both a target table and a toxicity table for three or more drugs. The chapter gives **no agent-specific toxicities**, only "side-effect profile" for oxybutynin. A toxicity table would require facts not in the source, so only the target/role table was built.
12. Non-landmark citations were removed from the body. NAPRTCS was kept as a landmark registry; the figure-legend attributions for Figs 61.22 and 61.23 were retained.
