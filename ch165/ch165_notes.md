# Chapter 165 — Management of Recurrent and Newly Metastatic Prostate Cancer

- **Biochemical recurrence (BCR)** is progression **unique to prostate cancer**: after curative-intent therapy a **prostate-specific antigen (PSA) rise precedes clinical recurrence** (recurrence **detectable on imaging**).
- Management depends on **prior therapy, patient goals, and risk of future clinical recurrence** — **further curative therapy** for some, **systemic control** for others; progression to metastasis is managed with **combination systemic therapy**.

## CURATIVE INTENT APPROACHES TO BIOCHEMICAL RECURRENCE

### After Radical Prostatectomy

#### Definition of Biochemical Recurrence After Radical Prostatectomy

- **About a quarter to two-fifths** develop PSA recurrence **within 10 years** of radical prostatectomy (RP); **high-risk** disease approaches **60% at 5 years**. The **2012 US Preventive Services Task Force grade D** recommendation has increased **high-risk disease at diagnosis**, and hence potential relapse.
- **A detectable PSA is not destiny**: about **half** of intermediate/high-risk men with detectable PSA ever reach **clinical failure** (metastasis, second-line treatment, or cancer death). **Why:** **residual benign tissue** (the usual explanation after radiotherapy), **non-prostatic PSA**, or **indolent low-grade residual cancer**.
- **The governing asymmetry:** **not every detectable PSA precedes clinical recurrence, but clinical recurrence is almost always preceded by a rising PSA** — hence the search for a threshold that predicts progression early enough to maximise benefit from further therapy.
- **PSA ≥0.4 ng/mL followed by a further rise** was most associated with **metastasis**, secondary therapy, further PSA progression and rapid doubling time (Memorial Sloan Kettering [MSK] series).
- Any fixed threshold is complicated by **ultrasensitive assays**, **clinical risk features**, and the **effect of PSA on salvage outcome**.

##### Ultrasensitive Prostate-Specific Antigen

- Assays reach **0.01 ng/mL**; earlier detection brings **anxiety and uncertainty**, without high-level evidence that treating on these values helps.
- **Prognostic, not diagnostic:** an **undetectable nadir predicts freedom from BCR**; **each month undetectable lowers risk**; the value **at 3 years** predicts eventual BCR.
- **Pathology modifies meaning:** discrimination is sharp in **pT3** disease but **negligible in low-risk disease** → relevant mainly to **intermediate/high risk**.
- About **half of early (1–3 month) detectable values fall** on retesting → **closer follow-up, not treatment**.

##### Clinical Factors Associated With Recurrence

- Context changes meaning: **0.1 ng/mL** differs **8 years after grade group 1** versus **6 months after grade group 5**.
- A **single PSA ≥0.1 ng/mL** predicts further therapy in **most** men with **high-risk pathology (pT3b, grade group 4–5)** but **few** with **favourable pathology (pT2, grade group 1–2, negative margins)**.
- **Positive margins, grade group, T stage, PSA doubling time** and **pre-salvage PSA** predict metastatic recurrence (meta-analysis) — effect sizes too heterogeneous to pool.

##### Effect of Prostate-Specific Antigen on Salvage Radiation

- **Lower PSA at salvage radiotherapy (RT) predicts freedom from recurrence**, even below 0.5 ng/mL.
- **Adverse pathology amplifies the PSA effect:** with **≥2 adverse features** (positive margin, pT3b, grade group 4–5) **each 0.1 ng/mL costs ~10%** in recurrence risk; with **0–1**, ~**1.5%**.

##### Guideline-Based Definitions of Recurrence

<p class="tt">Comparison — guideline definitions of BCR after radical prostatectomy</p>

| Body | Definition | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| American Urological Association (AUA) | **PSA ≥0.2 ng/mL** | **Single threshold** |
| National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) | **Failure to reach undetectable**, **two consecutive rises**, or **PSA ≥0.1 ng/mL** | **Any of three** triggers |
| European Association of Urology (EAU) | **No absolute threshold**. **Low-risk BCR:** PSA doubling time **>1 year AND grade group <4**. **High-risk BCR:** doubling time **≤1 year OR grade group 4–5** | **Risk categories** |

- **What it tests:** AUA and NCCN define recurrence **by a number**; EAU **by kinetics and grade**. **Trap:** AUA (0.2) is **higher** than NCCN (0.1); EAU low risk needs **both** favourable features, high risk **either** adverse one.
- Whatever the definition, the aim is to find **men who will benefit**, weighing biochemistry, pathology, goals and **overtreatment**.

### Prediction of Postprostatectomy Biochemical Recurrence

- Needed for **counselling, early salvage and trial selection**. **Established factors:** **pre-treatment PSA, clinical stage, pathological grade group, pathological stage, margin status**.
- **Positive margin — the only modifiable factor** and one of the strongest.
    - Raises BCR at every pT stage; **pT3a margin-negative** carried **lower** risk than **pT2 margin-positive**; confirmed by meta-analysis.
    - **Beyond binary status:** **length >1 mm**, **multifocality**, **location**, and **Gleason ≥4 at the margin**; after adjustment for PSA, stage and grade, **each 1 mm of margin length** and **Gleason 4–5 at the margin** independently predict BCR and metastasis → **meticulous dissection**.
- **Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI):** tumour volume, Gleason score, stage and margin status predict BCR; **tumour contact length >14.1 mm** with **PSA >10** and **grade group 3–5** identifies the highest risk.
- **Prostate Health Index (PHI)** (**total PSA, free PSA, [–2]proPSA**) predicts BCR.
- **Genomic classifiers** — **Decipher, Genomic Prostate Score (formerly Oncotype DX GPS), Prolaris** — validated for **metastatic recurrence**; **prognostic**, but their role in **selecting early salvage** is unproven.
- **Nomograms:** a **preoperative** tool (stage, PSA, Gleason, systematic biopsy) predicts progression (PSA ≥0.4 confirmed, secondary therapy, clinical recurrence, or positive frozen-section nodes) with **concordance 0.76–0.79**; a **postoperative** tool (adding extracapsular extension, seminal vesicle, nodal and margin status, adjuvant RT) predicts **10-year BCR-free survival** with **concordance 0.79–0.81**; University of California, San Francisco (UCSF) **CAPRA** (pre-op) and **CAPRA-S** (post-op) are validated for **5-year BCR**.

### Natural History of Biochemical Recurrence Postprostatectomy

- **BCR precedes metastasis and death — slowly and variably.**
- **Pound series (1999, seminal):** median **8 years BCR → metastasis**, **5 years metastasis → death**; risk depended on **time to BCR, Gleason score, PSA doubling time**. An update: **median metastasis-free survival (MFS) 10 years**, reflecting better selection and management.
- **Most men with BCR do not die of prostate cancer**; even at intermediate/high risk about **half never fail clinically**. When they do, **5-year disease-specific survival falls with site: local > nodal > distant**. Even **node-positive at RP**, only about half of those with BCR fail clinically.
- **Nomogram BCR-risk quartile predicts death**: 15-year disease-specific mortality is several-fold higher in the least favourable quartile.
- **PSA follow-up is lifelong** — up to about **a quarter recur after 5 years**, still at risk of progression.

<div class="kp" markdown="1">
**KEY POINTS**

- BCR is the **intermediate state** of recurrence, **defined by PSA**, and guides further treatment in selected patients.
- **AUA: PSA ≥0.2 ng/mL**; **NCCN: failure to reach undetectable PSA, two consecutive rises, or PSA ≥0.1 ng/mL**.
- Risk is assessed by **clinicopathological features** (PSA, stage, grade group, margins), **genomics** and **nomograms**.
- BCR **does not universally lead to metastasis or death**; act in the context of **patient goals and longevity**.
</div>

### Evaluation of Postprostatectomy Biochemical Recurrence

- The curative option is **salvage RT to the pelvis**, so evaluation aims to **exclude metastatic (non-local) recurrence**, where local therapy is **risk without benefit**.
- **Conventional imaging fails at low PSA:** **bone scan** and **computed tomography (CT)** rarely positive **below PSA 10 ng/mL**; **¹⁸F-sodium fluoride positron emission tomography/CT (¹⁸F-NaF PET/CT)** beats planar bone scan but **equals bone scan plus single-photon emission CT (SPECT)**; **transrectal ultrasonography (TRUS)** detects local masses (especially at the **vesicourethral anastomosis**) but **cannot exclude metastasis** → use only if local confirmation would change salvage.
- **Historical only:** **indium-111 capromab pendetide (ProstaScint)** and **¹¹C-acetate**. **Current:** **choline, ¹⁸F-fluciclovine, prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA)**. **Choline improved on fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG) PET**, which is limited by prostate cancer's **low glucose metabolism**.

| **¹⁸F-choline** | — | **Higher urinary excretion**, **longer half-life → no cyclotron needed** | Similar to ¹¹C |
| **¹⁸F-fluciclovine** | **2016** | **LOCATE**: recurrence found in a quarter at median PSA 0.57; **EMPIRE-1** (phase II/III): fluciclovine-guided salvage **improved 3-year control vs conventional imaging (75.5% vs 63.0%)** | **Poor below 0.5**; **none below 0.2** |

- **Pattern:** detection **rises with PSA for every tracer**; pooled detection **below 0.5 ng/mL: PSMA > fluciclovine**. → **PET/CT with an approved agent before curative salvage**, to avoid **irradiating the pelvis in metastatic disease**.
- **MRI for local recurrence:** endorectal MRI is highly accurate — recurrence **isointense to muscle on T1, slightly hyperintense on T2**; **dynamic contrast enhancement (DCE)** raises accuracy at lower PSA; **DCE + magnetic resonance spectroscopic imaging (MRSI)** stays sensitive at **PSA 0.4–1.4**. **Multiparametric MRI (mpMRI) beats PET/CT for local recurrence**, especially at low PSA, and **a visible local recurrence predicts benefit from salvage RT**.

- Imaging in BCR aims to **rule out metastasis before further local therapy**.
- **CT and bone scan** have **limited utility at low PSA** after prostatectomy.

### Salvage Radiation Therapy

- About **two in five** men develop BCR after surgery; observation and hormones are options, but **salvage RT is the only curative-intent therapy**.
- **Outcomes:** roughly **a third to a half BCR-free at 4–5 years**, even with high-risk features. **Predictors of durable response:** **Gleason grade, pre-treatment PSA, margin status, PSA doubling time, seminal vesicle invasion**. Salvage RT sharply reduces **local recurrence**, **later androgen deprivation therapy (ADT)** and **systemic progression**.
- **CSS benefit (~threefold vs observation)** was confined to men treated **within 2 years of BCR** with **doubling time <6 months** — the highest metastatic risk. Median overall survival (OS) after a **second failure** still exceeds a decade.
- **Predicting failure:** a salvage nomogram (PSA, pathological Gleason, doubling time, margins, nodes, ADT) has **concordance 0.69**; **shorter time to failure** predicts metastasis and death. **Margin location and number do not predict failure**, but **size and grade do** — in **grade group 3–5**, a **margin >3 mm** roughly halves survival.
- **Harms:** **genitourinary (GU)** — cystitis, stricture; **gastrointestinal (GI)** — proctitis, urgency, tenderness; **erectile dysfunction**.
- **When to start:** older data supported **PSA ≤0.5**; **lower is better** — **each 0.1 ng/mL costs ~2.6% recurrence-free survival**.
- **Early salvage vs adjuvant (landmark):** **RADICALS, RAVES, GETUG-AFU 17** — **early salvage at PSA 0.1–0.2** vs **adjuvant** RT in high-risk pathology; pre-planned meta-analysis (**ARTISTIC**): **equivalent biochemical control**, **≥60% spared RT** → **PSA 0.1–0.2 is a reasonable trigger** in high-risk men.
- **Imaging and timing:** **no visible lesion on mpMRI predicts salvage failure** (possible occult metastases); **⁶⁸Ga-PSMA-11 PET/CT** finds disease **outside the planned field** in about one in five at low PSA (commonest **bone, perirectal**). **The inverse relationship:** imaging detection **rises**, and salvage efficacy **falls**, as PSA rises.

#### Salvage Radiation Therapy Dose

- Borrowed logic from primary RT (**higher dose, better control**); **intensity-modulated radiotherapy (IMRT)** replaced conformal RT. Series and a meta-analysis favour doses **>66 Gy** (~**2% recurrence-free survival per Gy**), escalation to 75–76 Gy with acceptable toxicity — but the randomised **SAKK 09/10** found **no gain from 70 Gy** (table below). **ASTRO (American Society for Radiation Oncology)/AUA: 64–65 Gy minimum.**

#### Whole Pelvis Versus Prostatic Bed Radiation Therapy

- **Prostate bed only**, or **bed + pelvic nodes (whole pelvis)**. Retrospective data were mixed: whole pelvis helped **high-risk features** (Gleason ≥8, PSA >20, seminal vesicle, capsular or nodal involvement), **Gleason ≥7**, and men with **pre-RT PSA ≥0.4**. **Best evidence: RTOG 0534 (SPPORT)** (table below) — **Figs. 165.2 and 165.3** show the two fields.

#### Concurrent Androgen Deprivation Therapy With Salvage Radiation

- **Rationale:** **sterilise micrometastases, shrink tumour, radiosensitise**.

- **AUA:** **offer hormonal therapy** to men with PSA recurrence who are **salvage RT candidates**.

#### Follow-up After Salvage Radiation Therapy

- **No guideline.** Roughly **one in eight to one in three** suffer a **second BCR** → continue **PSA surveillance** with **restaging on recurrence**.

<div class="kp" markdown="1">
**KEY POINTS**

- **Salvage RT is the only curative-intent therapy** for post-prostatectomy BCR.
- **Earlier RT gives better outcomes**; **early salvage (PSA 0.1–0.2) may equal adjuvant** therapy.
- Dose **≥64–65 Gy to the fossa** and **40–45 Gy to pelvic nodes**.
- **Adding ADT improves survival.**
</div>

<p class="concept"><b>Concept —</b> <i>After prostatectomy two curves run in opposite directions: salvage radiotherapy works best at the lowest PSA, but imaging and the certainty that recurrence is real improve only as PSA rises. Risk features — grade, margins, kinetics — are what let the clinician act early without overtreating.</i></p>

## AFTER DEFINITIVE RADIATION THERAPY

### Definition of Biochemical Recurrence After Radiotherapy

- After radiotherapy a **remnant gland keeps making PSA**, so surgical thresholds fail.
- **Phoenix definition (ASTRO/Radiation Therapy Oncology Group [RTOG], 2005): PSA ≥2 ng/mL above nadir ("nadir + 2")** — designed to apply **with concurrent ADT**; validated as the **strongest predictor of metastasis, disease-specific and overall mortality** after adjustment; used in modern trials.
- **Brachytherapy (BT) exception:** BT gives **≥2-fold the dose** of external beam radiotherapy (EBRT), so Phoenix may **flatter BT**. After **low-dose-rate (LDR) BT**, a **≥0.4 ng/mL** threshold detects more failures **~12 months earlier**; **ASCENDE-RT** (EBRT vs EBRT + **LDR-BT boost**) retained its BT advantage under a **surgical definition (≥0.2)**; **PSA <0.2 at 4 years** after LDR-BT defines **cure** in almost all.

<p class="tt">Comparison — BCR definitions by primary treatment</p>

| | Radical prostatectomy | EBRT (± ADT) | LDR brachytherapy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Why it differs | **No gland** — any PSA suspect | **Remnant gland** makes PSA | **≥2× dose** → very low nadir |
| Definition | **AUA ≥0.2**; NCCN ≥0.1 / non-nadir / two rises; EAU risk groups | **Phoenix: nadir + ≥2** | **≥0.2** may suit better; **<0.2 at 4 years = cure** |

### Postradiation Prostate-Specific Antigen Bounce

- **Transient rise of 0.1–0.5 ng/mL** (definitions vary) **then fall**, at a **mean ~9 months**; attributed to **post-radiation prostatitis** or **delayed death of sublethally irradiated cells**.
- **Favourable:** associated with **better BCR-free survival**, and after dose-escalated EBRT with **lower PSA recurrence, metastasis and all-cause mortality** → **monitor closely; do not salvage**.

### Natural History of Prostate-Specific Antigen Recurrence After Definitive Radiation

- PSA-only recurrence leads to **local failure, distant metastasis (more often than local) and cancer death** in a substantial proportion; after Phoenix failure, median **~5.4 years to metastasis** and **~10.5 years to cancer death**.
- **Predictors:** **short PSA doubling time, clinical stage, Gleason score, short interval to BCR**.
- Unlike surgery, a **remnant prostate exists to guide further local therapy**.

### Evaluation of Postradiation Biochemical Recurrence

- **First ask whether he would benefit from any therapy**: many chose RT because of **limited life expectancy or unfitness**, and post-failure survival is long — evaluate only if **actionable**.

#### Imaging After Biochemical Recurrence Following Radiotherapy

- **Step 1 — exclude metastasis** (if present → **systemic therapy**; local therapy would not prolong survival). Principles mirror post-RP imaging, except the **residual gland** alters fossa assessment.
- **Fibrosis and shrinkage defeat conventional imaging:** **TRUS ≈ digital rectal examination**; **CT** useful only at **PSA >20**.
- **⁶⁸Ga-PSMA PET** was **positive in all** Phoenix failures in one series (mostly **distant or local + distant**); **¹¹C-choline PET/CT** positive in most, a third extrapelvic — **PSA rise above nadir, time since RT and NCCN risk group** predicted distant disease (nomogram index **0.79**).
- **Step 2 — confirm local recurrence: only biopsy proves it.** **PET lacks spatial resolution**; **MRI maps extent and guides biopsy**.
- **MRI after RT:** **T2 is poor** (diffusely **low T2 signal**; low sensitivity and positive predictive value) → **DCE** (vascularity, angiogenesis, permeability) outperforms T2; **diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI)** improves biopsy accuracy and matches **template mapping biopsy** per patient; **full mpMRI (T2 + DWI + DCE)** reaches **area under the curve ~0.84**; **3-Tesla endorectal mpMRI** before salvage RP stages **extraprostatic extension, seminal vesicle invasion and nodes** reasonably.

#### Biopsy After Radiotherapy

- **Goals:** presence/absence of residual disease and its **grade**. **Sequence:** **targeted lesions → seminal vesicles → systematic whole-gland sampling** (ultrasound- or MRI-guided). **Only when local salvage is contemplated.**
- **Caveat:** radiation **alters architecture** (possible **dedifferentiation**); **post-RT Gleason grading is unreliable** and controversial.

<div class="kp" markdown="1">
**KEY POINTS**

- **Phoenix (nadir + 2 ng/mL)** identifies men at risk of metastatic progression after **EBRT ± ADT**.
- After **brachytherapy**, **PSA ≥0.2 ng/mL** may be the more appropriate definition.
- Evaluate with **metastatic assessment (PET/CT)**, then — if local therapy is possible — **MRI** and **biopsy** for histological confirmation.
</div>

<p class="concept"><b>Concept —</b> <i>Radiotherapy leaves the organ in place, and every difference from the post-prostatectomy pathway follows: recurrence is measured from a nadir rather than from zero, a benign bounce must be told apart from failure, and local recurrence can — and must — be proven by biopsy before salvage.</i></p>

## TREATMENT OF POSTRADIATION BIOCHEMICAL RECURRENCE

- **Prerequisites for every salvage option:** **histologically confirmed local recurrence**, **imaging-confirmed non-metastatic disease**, **excellent performance status**, **≥5–10 years' life expectancy**, and **shared decision-making** — morbidity is **high** and control **lower than for primary treatment**.

### Salvage Radical Prostatectomy

- **Longest-studied** but **infrequently used**; **high 10-year disease-specific survival** in selected series. **Outcome tracks pathological stage:** **organ-confined and isolated extracapsular extension good**; **seminal vesicle invasion and nodal disease poor**.
- **Historically avoided** because of initial preference for RT (reluctance or contraindication to surgery) and **fibrosis-related morbidity** — major complications in up to half, **rectal injury**, frequent **bladder neck contracture**, high **incontinence**, near-universal **impotence**.
- **Contemporary (including robotic):** about **two-thirds recurrence-free**, fewer complications; but men are selected for **radio-resistant** disease, so 5-year biochemical control is **moderate**, predicted by **stage and grade**. **Robotic vs open: better continence, fewer contractures and rectal injuries.**
- **EAU selection:** **life expectancy ≥10 years; pre-salvage PSA <10 ng/mL; biopsy grade group ≤3; no nodal disease/metastasis on imaging; initial cT1–T2**. The minority meeting all criteria had **better functional and oncological outcomes**.

### Salvage Brachytherapy

- **Re-irradiation for local failure after EBRT** — dose to the gland while **sparing previously irradiated neighbours**.
- **High-dose-rate (HDR):** about **half BCR-free at 5 years**, **negligible grade ≥3 GU and no grade 3–4 GI** toxicity. **LDR:** about half free at 10 years; **higher grade and PSA** predict failure; grade ≥3 urinary toxicity ~**1 in 6**, **GI rare**. **LDR ≈ HDR** for control.
- **Bottom line:** control and GU toxicity **comparable to salvage surgery**.

### Salvage Stereotactic Radiation

- **Stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT)** after prior EBRT: **modest short-term control** (2-year BCR-free **40–76%** in systematic review; about half at 3 years in the largest multicentre series) with **low toxicity**.

### Salvage Cryotherapy

- **Selection:** **PSA <10 ng/mL (ideally <4)** and **PSA doubling time ≥16 months**.
- **Success lacks a single definition** — mostly **Phoenix**; Cryo On-Line Data (**COLD**) Registry and series give **roughly two-thirds control at 3–5 years** (whole gland); **focal** control **declines progressively** to 5 years. **Freedom from systemic hormones** is an alternative endpoint — most avoid hormones at 5 years, and **post-cryotherapy nadir <0.2** strongly predicts avoidance.
- **Morbidity then vs now:** incontinence once **most** (mostly moderate–severe, low satisfaction), now **single-digit** thanks to **universal urethral warming** protecting the sphincter; **erectile dysfunction** remains very common; **urethrorectal fistula — the most dreaded** — now **1–3.4%**; also **obstruction, urethral sloughing, stricture, rectal pain, scrotal oedema, haematuria**.

### Salvage High-Intensity Focused Ultrasonography

- **High-intensity focused ultrasound (HIFU) ≥2 years after EBRT.** Prospective trial endpoints: **PSA nadir ≤0.5 ng/mL + negative 12-month biopsy** — most biopsied were cancer-free; notable grade 3 toxicity (**rectal fistula, osteitis pubis, refractory haematuria**). Largest series: **~half BCR-free at 5 years (Phoenix)**, tied to **pre-treatment risk group**; risks **re-treatment, incontinence, stricture, fistula, pubic osteitis**.

|---|---|---|---|
| **Salvage RP** | EAU criteria (above) | **Longest data; pathological staging** | **Incontinence, contracture, rectal injury, impotence** |
| **Salvage BT** | **After EBRT only** | Spares irradiated neighbours | **GU** (stricture, incontinence, haematuria) |
| **Salvage SBRT** | After EBRT | **Low toxicity** | Modest control |

| **Salvage HIFU** | ≥2 years after EBRT | **Focal or whole-gland** | **Fistula, osteitis pubis**, stricture, incontinence |

### Follow-up After Salvage Treatment of Radiation Recurrence

- **Recurrence in more than half** after maximal local therapy. **No guidelines**: periodic **PSA**, with a **modality-appropriate biochemical definition** triggering **restaging imaging**.

### After Focal Therapy

- **Treat as a radiation failure:** **advanced imaging → MRI → targeted biopsy → salvage** — **surgery**, **radiation** (EBRT, SBRT, LDR- or HDR-BT) or **ablation** (repeat HIFU or cryotherapy).

<div class="kp" markdown="1">
**KEY POINTS**

- Curative options for radiation recurrence: **salvage prostatectomy, re-irradiation (BT or SBRT), ablation (cryotherapy or HIFU)**.
- Well selected: **grade group <4** and **pre-salvage PSA <10**.
- **Salvage prostatectomy** is harder than primary surgery (**fibrosis**), with more **incontinence, impotence and complications**.
- **Salvage BT** only after **external RT**; **LDR ≈ HDR**.
- **Ablation** may be **focal or whole-gland**.
- **Focal therapy recurrence** is managed **as radiation recurrence**.
</div>

<p class="concept"><b>Concept —</b> <i>After radiotherapy every salvage modality buys a similar, moderate chance of cure at the price of morbidity in irradiated tissue; selection — proven local-only disease, low PSA, lower grade, long life expectancy — predicts success far more than the choice of modality.</i></p>

## NONCURATIVE INTENT APPROACHES TO BIOCHEMICAL RECURRENCE

<p class="tt">Pair — the governing trade</p>

| Observation | Androgen deprivation therapy |
|---|---|
| **Preserves quality of life** | **Delays metastatic recurrence** |
| Risks **metastatic progression and symptoms** | Risks **side effects** and **castration resistance** (from prolonged suppression) |

- **AUA:** **observation or trial**; **ADT only for higher metastatic risk** — **doubling time <12 months** or **high grade**; if used, **intermittent over continuous**.

### Observation

#### Natural History

- Most men **will not die of prostate cancer** even after recurrence. **Pound (untreated post-RP BCR):** only about **a third metastasised** (median **8 years**), dying a median **5 years** later — and fewer than half of those died of prostate cancer. **Pound predictors: time to BCR <2 years, doubling time <10 months, Gleason 8–10.** → For many, treating BCR alone **does more harm than good**.
- **AUA:** **observation over hormones** after maximal local therapy. **EAU:** observation if **unwilling** to have local therapy, **limited life expectancy**, or **EAU low-risk BCR** — **post-RP: doubling time >1 year and grade group <4**; **post-RT: interval to failure >18 months and grade group <4**.

#### Follow-up and Evaluation

- **PSA surveillance and periodic imaging** — AUA prefers **PET/CT**, especially if **doubling time <12 months**.

<div class="kp" markdown="1">
**KEY POINTS**

- **Observation** is reasonable because **most men will not die of biochemically recurrent disease**.
- **AUA: observation over hormonal therapy**; **EAU: observation for low metastatic risk**.
- Observed men need **routine surveillance imaging**.
</div>

### Androgen Deprivation Therapy

#### Historical Background

- **1893 — White:** **castration for urinary obstruction** (ageing dogs). **1941 — Huggins and Hodges:** **castration improves far-advanced prostate cancer; injected androgens aggravate it** → **Nobel Prize 1966**.
- **Enabling discoveries:** **testosterone isolated, characterised, synthesised**; **prostatic acid phosphatase** linked to prostatic epithelium and advanced cancer; castration's effect on **benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH)**.
- **"Studies on Prostate Cancer I–III":** **adult prostate = source of acid phosphatase**; carcinoma = **malignant adult-type epithelium**; **orchidectomy** → **prompt, sustained fall in acid phosphatase**, longer survival, weight gain, rising red cells, pain relief, less leg oedema — at the cost of **lost sexual capacity and hot flushes**.
- **Castration resistance** made it **rarely curative**; Huggins suspected **adrenal androgens** → **bilateral adrenalectomy (1945)**: modest, brief responses, severe morbidity.
- **1960s — Veterans Administration Cooperative Urological Research Group (VACURG):** **diethylstilboestrol (DES)** vs orchidectomy — effective but **cardiovascular and thromboembolic toxicity**.
- **Hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal (HPG) axis:** **oestradiol inhibits luteinising hormone (LH)**; **LHRH isolated (1971)** → agonists and antagonists. **Agonist → testosterone surge ("flare") → loss of pituitary stimulation → ↓LH → ↓Leydig testosterone**; **depot** formulations made agonists **dominant**.

##### The Androgen Receptor

- The **androgen receptor (AR)** is the **key oncogenic driver**; antiandrogens **interrupt ligand binding**. **Trap:** many antiandrogens **block central feedback**, so **alone they raise LH and testosterone**.
- **AR:** **nuclear receptor superfamily** (sex and adrenal steroids, thyroid, vitamin D, retinoids) — **ligand-inducible transcription factors** with **three domains: ligand-binding (LBD), DNA-binding, transactivation**. **Steroid diffuses in → binds LBD → activates DNA binding → transactivation domain recruits co-regulators → gene repression or activation.** **Gene on the X chromosome**, mapped through cancer and **androgen insensitivity** mutants. **All ADT blocks AR activity** — by **cutting ligand** or **inhibiting the receptor**.
- **Castration resistance** (formerly "androgen-independent"/"hormone-refractory") was thought non-AR-mediated, but **rapid ("warm") autopsies** showed **AR signalling remains necessary and sufficient** → renamed **"castration-resistant"**.
- **Mechanisms:**
    1. **AR overexpression/amplification** (~**20–30%** of castration-resistant prostate cancer [CRPC] metastases) → **hypersensitivity to low androgen**.
    2. **Activating LBD mutations** → **altered specificity**: other steroids and **antiandrogens activate AR** — basis of **antiandrogen withdrawal**.
    3. **Splice variants lacking the LBD** → **ligand-independent** AR; **AR-V7** predicts **castration resistance and poor survival**.
    4. **Bypass pathways**, e.g. **glucocorticoid receptor** co-option.
- **Fig. 165.4:** **normal — testosterone → 5α-reductase → dihydrotestosterone (DHT) → AR–DHT dimer → nuclear translocation → androgen response element (ARE) → PSA, growth, survival**; **resistance — amplification; mutation and splicing (AR-ΔLBD); intratumoural steroidogenesis; bypass (GR, MAPK, SOX2, WNT, BCL2, AKT)**.

### Molecular Biology and Sources of Androgen

- **Testis:** **Leydig cells under LH**; testosterone **diffuses freely**. In cells with **AR and 5α-reductase**, **testosterone → DHT**, which binds AR **~13-fold** more avidly (**preferred ligand**) → nucleus → **AREs**.
- **Adrenal:** **androstenedione and dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA)**, driven by **adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH)** ← corticotropin-releasing factor — **too weak alone to sustain prostatic epithelium after castration**.
- **Tumour:** **autocrine steroidogenesis** (raised intratumoural androgens, upregulated enzymes) → rationale for **synthesis inhibitors**.

<p class="concept"><b>Concept —</b> <i>Castration removes the ligand, not the receptor. Because the AR remains the driver even in "castration-resistant" disease, every resistance mechanism — amplification, mutation, splicing, local synthesis, bypass — keeps AR signalling alive, and every later drug hits the receptor or its ligand supply harder.</i></p>

## METHODS OF ANDROGEN DEPRIVATION THERAPY

- **Four approaches:** **orchidectomy; AR antagonism; LHRH/LH inhibition; androgen synthesis inhibition**. Classic ADT = **chemical or surgical castration**; **castrate testosterone <50 ng/dL**. Some agents are licensed only in **metastatic or castration-resistant** disease.

### Orchiectomy

- **~95% of testosterone is testicular**: orchidectomy gives **castrate levels often within 6 hours**, **<20 ng/dL within 12 hours**, median **~15 ng/dL**, nearly all **<30 ng/dL**; VACURG showed **pain relief and better performance status**.
- **Simple** (all tunical tissue removed) **vs subcapsular** (non-functional tunica kept — avoids an **empty scrotum**; **operator-dependent**, must remove all Leydig tissue; done properly, **equivalent**).
- **Underused and declining** despite **lower cost and equal survival**; versus LHRH agonists, **fewer cardiac events, peripheral arterial disease and fractures**, and with **≥35 months** of therapy **less thromboembolism and diabetes** → **offer for lifelong ADT**.

### Antiandrogens

- **AR antagonists competing at the LBD**: **steroidal** or **nonsteroidal** (first, second, third generation).

#### Steroidal

- **Cyproterone acetate** (no longer used for cancer): **AR blockade + progestational central suppression (testosterone ↓70–80%)**; 100 mg two to three times daily; **inferior to goserelin**; **severe cardiovascular events (up to 10%)**, gynaecomastia, rare **fulminant hepatotoxicity**; still used **50–100 mg/day for hot flushes**. **Medroxyprogesterone** and **megestrol acetate** are the same class — not routine.

#### Nonsteroidal First and Second Generation

- **Alone they raise LH and testosterone (~1.5× normal)** — antiandrogen action **without hypogonadism**; potency preservation is theoretical, **uncommon in practice**.
- **Class toxicity:** **gynaecomastia/mastodynia** (**aromatisation** of excess testosterone to oestradiol); **hepatotoxicity** in all (**monitor liver tests**); **interstitial pneumonitis → fibrosis** in all, **highest with nilutamide (1–2%)**.
- **Role: combined androgen blockade (CAB)** with LHRH agonists — above all **blocking the flare** for ~**30 days**.

| Agent | Half-life | Dosing | Signature | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|

#### Nonsteroidal Third Generation

- **AR-targeting agents (ARTAs)** block **ligand binding, nuclear translocation and DNA binding**; **more potent** than earlier agents.

#### Antiandrogen Withdrawal Syndrome

- On **CAB** with biochemical progression, **stopping the antiandrogen can drop PSA** (sometimes objective responses) — **AR mutations turn the antagonist into an agonist**.
- **Bicalutamide:** response **within 6 weeks**; **15–30%** get **>50% PSA fall**, lasting **3.5–5 months**. With **enzalutamide-class** agents **0–10%**, brief, no radiographic response (less agonism). **No OS benefit.**

### Inhibition of Luteinizing Hormone–Releasing Hormone

#### Luteinizing Hormone–Releasing Hormone Agonists

- **Leuprolide, goserelin, triptorelin, histrelin**: **chronic receptor exposure → desensitisation → ↓LH → ↓testosterone**; potency and half-life from **amino acid substitution at position 6**; **depots (28 days) to osmotic implants (1 year)**; **survival equals orchidectomy**.
- **Flare:** LH up to **10-fold** for **10–20 days** → **life-threatening exacerbation** almost only with **spinal/weight-bearing bone metastases**; prevented by **antiandrogen before or with the agonist** (simultaneous start is as good as a week's lead-in), needed only **21–28 days**.

#### Luteinizing Hormone–Releasing Hormone Antagonists

- **Abarelix, cetrorelix, degarelix, ganirelix, relugolix**: **immediate competitive blockade** — **LH ↓84% in 24 hours**, **no flare** (the **major advantage**) → preferred in **eugonadal men with impending cord compression or severe bone pain** when orchidectomy is unsuitable.
- **Abarelix:** rapid, effective castration; early antagonists caused **histamine reactions**; abarelix can cause **severe allergy even after uneventful doses** → **observe ≥30 minutes**; US use restricted to men **unable to take other hormones who refuse orchidectomy**.
- **Degarelix:** **no systemic allergy**; vs leuprolide **castrate (<50 ng/dL) by day 3 in >95% vs none**, equal maintenance to 12 months (**non-inferior**); more **injection-site reactions, chills**, fewer **urinary infections, arthralgia**; pooled data favour it, but **no randomised survival comparison**.
- **Relugolix — oral, FDA 2020. HERO** (BCR or newly metastatic, vs leuprolide, 48 weeks): **castrate at day 4 56% vs 0%**; **sustained castration 97% vs 89%**; **testosterone recovery 90 days after stopping 54% vs 2%**. About **half** of men on LHRH agents **never recover baseline testosterone by 5 years**.

| Agonist | Antagonist |
|---|---|

| **Flare** → antiandrogen **21–28 days** | **No flare** → **cord compression** |
| **FSH rises** | **FSH falls** |

## TIMING OF THERAPY

- In non-metastatic BCR, ADT aims to **prolong life and prevent symptomatic progression**, but not all progress and ADT is **lifelong and toxic**. **AUA, EAU and NCCN set no firm start point**. Questions: **immediate vs delayed**; **continuous vs intermittent** (metastatic data now largely **historical**).

### Immediate Versus Delayed

#### Results in Clinically Localized Disease

- **Primary ADT** (no local therapy, non-metastatic) is **not recommended**: in elderly men it gave **lower cancer-specific survival and no OS gain** versus conservative management, and elsewhere **no change in mortality** except in **high-risk** disease.

#### Results in Lymph Node Metastatic Prostate Cancer as Adjuvant Therapy

- **Messing (ECOG) trial** showed immediate ADT benefit after node-positive RP, but: **pre-dates modern imaging**; **small**; delayed arm treated **only at clinical progression**; a fifth had **detectable PSA** (persistent disease); **bulky nodes** (up to 20). **Low-volume N+ with undetectable PSA: 20–40% cured by surgery alone.**

#### Results in Locally Advanced or Asymptomatic Metastatic Disease or Disease Not Suitable to Local Treatment

- **MRC trial:** the delayed arm had **more progression-related symptoms**; the OS gain faded with follow-up. **EORTC 30891:** **30% of the delayed arm died without ever needing ADT**; benefit concentrated in **PSA >50 ng/mL** or **doubling time <12 months**.

#### Results in Biochemically Recurrent Prostate Cancer

- **Observational data:** ADT timing after RP (adjuvant, PSA 0.4/1.0/2.0, or metastasis) **did not change OS**; deferred ADT gave **similar CSS**; salvage ADT vs observation **no survival difference** — **except doubling time <9 months**.
- **TOAD:** delayed arm waited **≥2 years** unless metastasis, symptoms or **doubling time <6 months**.
- **EMBARK — high-risk BCR:** **doubling time ≤9 months** and **PSA ≥1 ng/mL after RP (± RT)** or **≥2 ng/mL above nadir after RT**; **1:1:1** **leuprolide / leuprolide + enzalutamide / enzalutamide**; **paused at week 37 if PSA <0.2**, **restarted at 2 (post-RP) or 5 ng/mL (post-RT)**; **MFS 87.3% / 80.0% / 71.4%**; almost half the combination arm stayed **off treatment >24 months**; 5-year freedom from PSA progression **97.4%** → **enzalutamide approved for high-risk nmHSPC**.

### Supportive Care on Androgen Deprivation Therapy

- Discuss side effects at the outset; **mitigate proactively**.

#### Osteoporosis

- **Androgen suppression (not AR antagonism)** lowers **bone mineral density (BMD)** via **loss of peripheral oestrogen**. **Over half of older men are osteopenic/osteoporotic before ADT** (osteoporosis: **T-score >2.5 SD below the young-adult mean**). Fracture risk **↑20–45%**, rising with **duration**.
- **Baseline hip dual-energy x-ray absorptiometry (DEXA)** for anticipated long-term ADT; **FRAX** (WHO) for risk; testing is **badly under-used**.
- **All:** **calcium 1000–1200 mg/day, vitamin D₃ 400–1000 IU/day, weight-bearing exercise, stop smoking**.
- **Drug treatment** if **T-score –1.0 to –2.5** with **10-year hip fracture >3%** or **major fracture >20%**: **denosumab 60 mg SC 6-monthly**, **zoledronic acid 4–5 mg IV yearly**, or **alendronate 70 mg orally weekly**; **repeat DEXA at 1 year**.

#### Hot Flashes (Vasomotor Symptoms)

- **Commonest side effect (50–80%)**: upper-body warmth then sweating, from disturbed **hypothalamic oestrogen signalling → adrenergic thermoregulatory activation**; triggered by **posture, hot drinks, ambient temperature**; **wane** but may persist. **Treat only if bothersome.**

| Class | Agent and dose | Evidence / caveat |
|---|---|---|
| **Oestrogens** | Low-dose DES; transdermal oestradiol | **Most effective (up to 90%)**; **gynaecomastia, thromboembolism** |
| **Progestogens** | **Megestrol 20 mg twice daily** (→ 5 mg twice daily); **cyproterone 50 → 300 mg/day**; medroxyprogesterone 20 mg/day | Effective; megestrol **stimulates appetite** |
| **Non-hormonal** | **Venlafaxine** (serotonin–noradrenaline reuptake inhibitor) **12.5 mg twice daily**, 37.5 → max **225 mg/day** | Helps over half; **less effective than hormonal agents** head-to-head; **taper — never stop abruptly** |
| | **Gabapentin** up to **900 mg** | **Moderate** |
| | **Clonidine** (central α-agonist), transdermal | **Ineffective** |
| **Behavioural** | Acupuncture / soy / cognitive–behavioural therapy | **Uncontrolled** / **failed** / **short-lived** |

- **Pattern:** efficacy and toxicity both run **oestrogen > progestogen > non-hormonal** → progestogens only when flushes **significantly impair quality of life**.

#### Sexual Dysfunction (Erectile Dysfunction and Loss of Libido)

- Profound (Huggins: desire and erection "absent") but **not inevitable**: **~1 in 10 to 1 in 6** keep erections; **libido suffers more** (~**1 in 20** keep high interest). **Penile shortening, volume loss, loss of nocturnal tumescence**.
- **Loss of libido is very hard to treat**; **phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitors usually fail**; **intraurethral therapy, vacuum device, intracavernosal injection, penile prosthesis** work in selected men; **exercise** raised sexual interest; **sex therapy for couples**.

#### Cognitive Function

- Hypogonadism worsens **memory and visuospatial** performance (verbal knowledge, mental status, mood spared). Evidence **conflicts**: a small randomised study showed decline in half at 6 months; a **larger controlled study showed none** to 3 years; functional MRI shows **reduced right parieto-occipital activation**. **ADT–dementia link is methodologically weak.** **Monitor and refer** if decline.

#### Changes in Body Habitus

- **↑Appetite**, ↑insulin and **abdominal girth**; after a year **weight ↑~2–4%**, mostly **fat (↑~9–24%)**, with progressive **sarcopenia**; **endurance and grip strength fall within 3 months**.

#### Diabetes and Metabolic Syndrome

- **Metabolic syndrome in more than half** on long-term ADT — but **atypical**: **subcutaneous (not visceral) fat and raised HDL cholesterol**, unlike classic visceral, low-HDL syndrome. **↓Insulin sensitivity**; diabetes risk ↑ with **GnRH agonists (HR 1.44)** and **orchidectomy (HR 1.34)**; diabetics need **more drugs**, worse **HbA1c**. A recent meta-analysis found the metabolic-syndrome link **lost after adjustment**.

#### Cardiovascular Morbidity and Mortality

- **Raised risk, especially in lower-risk disease**: GnRH agonists ↑**coronary disease, myocardial infarction, sudden cardiac death**, plus **peripheral arterial disease and venous thromboembolism**; **≥6 months** lowers **cardiorespiratory fitness**. **Exception:** no cardiovascular mortality excess with **RT + ADT for locally advanced disease**.

#### Gynecomastia

- **Gynaecomastia/mastodynia**, together or apart: **DES ~half vs leuprolide rare**; **orchidectomy/agonists ≤5%**; **bicalutamide 150 mg or enzalutamide ~half** (aromatisation).
- **Prevent:** **prophylactic breast RT 10–15 Gy single fraction** (**useless once established**); **tamoxifen 10 mg/day** (non-standard). **Established:** **liposuction** or **endoscopic subcutaneous mastectomy**.

#### Anemia

- **Normochromic normocytic, very common** (most on CAB lose **≥10% haemoglobin**; risk ~**tripled**, within **6 months**, chronic), even **without marrow disease**; **pre-ADT haemoglobin <12 g/dL predicts shorter survival**; symptomatic in few; **reversible within about a year** of stopping.

<p class="concept"><b>Concept —</b> <i>The case for early ADT strengthens only as the risk of cancer death rises — rapid doubling, high PSA, high burden — because every month of ADT carries a fixed metabolic, skeletal, cardiovascular and sexual cost that the low-risk man pays without a matching gain.</i></p>

## FOLLOW-UP ON ANDROGEN DEPRIVATION THERAPY (NONMETASTATIC)

- **Aims:** monitor response/progression and **supportive care**; review **3–6-monthly** with **blood count, chemistry, PSA**.
- **Magnitude and speed of PSA response predict durability:** rapid **pre-ADT** rise predicts cancer death; **faster time to undetectable (<0.2 ng/mL)** predicts better survival; undetectable nadir is prognostic in metastatic disease too.
- **HSD3B1 1245A>C** (germline, **15–35%**): encodes **3β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase-1**, converting **adrenal androgens toward DHT**; the variant **resists degradation → excess intratumoural DHT**; **PFS, MFS and OS worsen with each variant allele**; **homozygous ~doubles metastasis rate** — a **predictive biomarker of ADT resistance**, **no clinical test yet**.
- **Rising PSA → check testosterone (<50 ng/dL)**; lower levels within the castrate range may help. **Inadequate suppression → switch LHRH agent, add oestrogen, antiandrogen or synthesis inhibitor, or orchidectomy.**
- **Rising PSA with suppressed testosterone = castration resistance** → image: **axial soft tissue (PET/CT, CT or MRI) + bone (bone scan or PET/CT)**.

<div class="kp" markdown="1">
**KEY POINTS**

- ADT: **orchidectomy, AR antagonism, LH suppression, or synthesis blockade**.
- **Rapidity and magnitude of PSA fall** predict **duration and efficacy**.
- PSA change on ADT → **repeat PSA and testosterone**.
- **Rising PSA with castrate testosterone = CRPC** → **metastatic evaluation**.
- ADT's **quality-of-life impacts must be managed**.
</div>

<p class="concept"><b>Concept —</b> <i>On ADT the testosterone level is the referee: a rising PSA means nothing until testosterone is known — inadequate suppression is a drug problem; adequate suppression means the disease has become castration-resistant.</i></p>

## HORMONE SENSITIVE METASTATIC PROSTATE CANCER

- **Metachronous** (after local treatment) vs **synchronous** (at diagnosis). **Metastasis arising on hormonal suppression = CRPC** (next chapter, with nmCRPC). **mHSPC** = metastatic, **no prior hormones**.
- **CHAARTED high volume: ≥4 bone metastases with ≥1 outside the axial skeleton, or any visceral metastasis.** Care is **multidisciplinary**.

### Diagnosis

- **Conventional (CT, MRI, bone scan) or PET/CT.** Treat what is found — but trials used **conventional imaging**, so **PET-only metastatic disease** is largely unstudied.
- **Histology** not always needed (especially metachronous); **biopsy prostate or metastasis** if never proven.
- **Germline and somatic testing for all metastatic men (AUA, NCCN)** — **>10%** carry **DNA-repair mutations** with therapeutic implications.

| Germline | Somatic |
|---|---|
| **BRCA1, BRCA2, ATM, PALB2, CHEK2** | **BRCA1, BRCA2, ATM, PALB2, CHEK2** |
| **HOXB13** | **FANCA, RAD51D, CDK12** |
| **MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2** | **Microsatellite instability–high (MSI-H)**, **mismatch repair deficiency (dMMR)** |

- **Pattern:** **homologous-recombination core in both**; **HOXB13 and individual mismatch-repair genes germline-only**; **FANCA, RAD51D, CDK12, MSI-H/dMMR somatic-only**.

### Prognosis

- Median OS **~30 months historically → >48 months** with docetaxel, advanced hormones and combinations.
- **Metachronous low-volume (~92 months) vs synchronous or high-volume (~43 months).**
- **Site (pre-combination era): nodes only > bone > visceral > bone + visceral.**
- **SWOG 9346 PSA nadir at 7 months: ≤0.2 → 75 months; 0.2–4 → 44; >4 → 13.**

### Management of Metastatic Hormone Sensitive Prostate Cancer

#### Systemic Therapy

- **Backbone: orchidectomy or LHRH agonist/antagonist.** **Emergency (impending cord compression) → antagonist or orchidectomy.** **Bone metastases + agonist → add nonsteroidal antiandrogen** for the flare. **Antiandrogen alone — not recommended**; **long-term CAB — AUA against** (no meaningful survival gain; better combinations exist).
- **Unless contraindicated, add docetaxel, an androgen synthesis inhibitor, or an ARTA**; growing evidence for **"triplet therapy"** (ADT + docetaxel + abiraterone or ARTA).
- **Observation not recommended** except **asymptomatic men with life expectancy <5 years**, or while receiving **metastasis-directed therapy** for oligometastases.

##### Docetaxel

- **Microtubule inhibitor, 75 mg/m² every 21 days** (6 cycles; up to 9 in GETUG-AFU 15). Meta-analysis of the three trials: **HR 0.77 — 94 fewer deaths per 1000**. **Toxicity:** grade 3–4 **neutropenia**, **febrile neutropenia**, **liver dysfunction**. Benefit **greatest in high volume**.

##### Androgen Synthesis Inhibitor

- **Abiraterone 1000 mg + prednisone 5 mg** — **LATITUDE** (≥2 of: **grade group 4–5 [Gleason ≥8], ≥3 bone lesions, visceral**) and **STAMPEDE arm G** (M1 plus selected high-risk locally advanced).

##### Androgen Receptor–Targeting Agents

- **Enzalutamide and apalutamide** approved with ADT (**ARCHES, ENZAMET, TITAN**); benefit **regardless of volume or prior docetaxel**.

##### "Triplet Therapy"

- **PEACE-1** (synchronous; standard care evolved from ADT to **ADT + docetaxel**; **± abiraterone ± prostate RT**, 2 × 2) and **ARASENS** (**ADT + docetaxel ± darolutamide 600 mg twice daily**): both **improved OS** with **similar toxicity** across arms.

#### Local Therapy to the Primary

- In **synchronous** disease the untreated primary may seed metastasis.

<p class="tt">Comparison — radiotherapy to the primary in mHSPC</p>

| Trial | Overall OS | Low volume | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **HORRAD** (ADT ± prostate RT) | No difference (HR 0.9) | Suggested benefit **<5 sites** (HR 0.68, not significant) | Hypothesis-generating |
| **STAMPEDE arm H** (pre-specified low-volume analysis) | No difference (HR 0.92) | **Improved: HR 0.68; 3-year OS 81% vs 73%** | **RT to primary in low-volume synchronous disease** |

- **Caveat:** these men had **ADT only**; benefit **on top of modern combinations is unknown**.
- **Cytoreductive prostatectomy:** **feasible** (low morbidity, good continence) — **trials or palliation of local symptoms only**.

#### Metastasis-Directed Therapy

- **PET/CT** detects **oligometastatic disease (≤3 sites)** targetable by **surgery or stereotactic RT** — a **non-hormonal** strategy.
- **Salvage lymph node dissection:** about half free of clinical recurrence at 2–3 years, but **long-term BCR-free survival is low** while **OS stays high**.

<div class="kp" markdown="1">
**KEY POINTS**

- **ADT + docetaxel** improves survival, driven by **high-volume** disease.
- **ADT + abiraterone, enzalutamide or apalutamide** improve survival **regardless of volume or prior docetaxel**.
- **ADT + docetaxel + abiraterone or darolutamide** improves survival; **triplets** are for the **chemotherapy-fit**, by shared decision on **volume** (high benefits more), **synchronous vs metachronous** (synchronous benefits more) and preference.
- **Local therapy to the primary** may help **low-volume synchronous** disease.
- **MDT** may prolong **systemic-therapy-free survival** in oligometastatic disease.
</div>

<p class="concept"><b>Concept —</b> <i>In mHSPC, ADT is the floor, never the ceiling: every randomised addition has improved outcomes, and what remains is judging burden — chemotherapy where volume and synchronous presentation justify it, radiotherapy to the primary only where volume is low, metastasis-directed therapy only where the lesions are few enough to count.</i></p>

## FOLLOW-UP WHILE ON THERAPY FOR METASTATIC HORMONE SENSITIVE PROSTATE CANCER

- **AUA, EAU, NCCN:** review **3–6-monthly** — **PSA** and treatment-relevant bloods (**liver, blood count**). **Testosterone with any PSA rise** (**rising PSA + castrate testosterone = CRPC**). **Image only if management will change or CRPC is suspected.** Maintain **bone health (baseline and periodic DEXA)** and **cardiovascular health**.
