Role of Androgen Suppression Combined With Radiation

Background and Potential Mechanisms

  • Huggins and Hodges (1941) showed that prostate cancer is androgen-dependent. This provided the rationale for adding hormonal therapy to RT.
  • From 1967, hormonal therapy was added to RT for stage C (T3) tumours. These were often large, and cytoreductive ADT gave better geometry for irradiation and less tumour burden. Its use remained controversial through the 1970s–1980s, when early series were often negative.
  • RTOG 75-06, an early favourable report: patients with histologically unfavourable tumours given androgen suppression + EBRT had disease-free and overall survival similar to patients with favourable tumours who did not receive it.
  • Mechanisms of synergy:
    • Apoptosis: androgen-responsive cells undergo programmed cell death when deprived of androgen. Like radiation-induced death, this is p53-independent.
    • Radiosensitisation: castration → less Ku70 repair protein → impaired nonhomologous end-joining → more unrepaired double-strand breaks after RT.
    • RT upregulates androgen receptor (AR) signalling durably. Patients with a post-RT rise in AR-regulated hK2 were about threefold more likely to fail biochemically. Adjuvant ADT after RT works by blocking this AR upregulation.

Androgen Suppression Therapy and Locally Advanced Disease

  • Meta-analysis of ADT in nonmetastatic disease: relative reductions in both disease-specific and all-cause mortality.

Benefit of Radiation in Addition to Androgen Suppression for Locally Advanced Disease

  • SPCG-7 and NCIC PR.3/MRC PR07 (see the ADT trials table above) show that adding RT to lifelong ADT reduces prostate cancer death and improves overall survival.
  • STAMPEDE (abiraterone comparison), in newly diagnosed locally advanced or metastatic disease, or relapsing disease with poor features: 24 months ADT + abiraterone + prednisolone vs ADT alone gave a 71% relative improvement in time to treatment failure and a 37% difference in overall survival. RT from 6–9 months after randomisation was mandatory for node-negative and encouraged for node-positive nonmetastatic disease.

  • RT + ADT improves survival over either alone. Unfavourable intermediate: 4–6 months. Locally advanced high risk: 28–36 months beats 4–6 months with conventional RT. Shorter courses may be considered with dose escalation.

Concept — ADT and RT are mutually sensitising: ADT impairs DNA repair, and RT switches on AR signalling that ADT then blocks. The required duration rises with risk and falls as radiation dose rises, which is why an HDR boost made extra ADT redundant in RADAR.

Evaluating the Response to Radiation Therapy

Prostate-Specific Antigen Follow-up: Definition of Failure

  • Serum PSA is the accepted surrogate endpoint. Unlike after RP, residual benign glands keep producing some PSA after RT, but the "acceptable" level is far below age-matched normal because non-malignant acini atrophy.
  • Nadir depends on dose: median nadir ~0.6 ng/mL after 70 Gy, ~0.3 after 79 Gy, ≤0.1 after EBRT + BT boost.
  • No absolute nadir defines cure. Meeting a definition of failure is for trials and reporting; it does not by itself justify intervention.

Significance of Time to Nadir

  • Without ADT, PSA falls slowly after RT. Three sources contribute to the nadir: residual benign epithelium, residual local cancer and subclinical micrometastases. Higher dose ablates more benign epithelium, giving a lower nadir.

Significance of Nadir Value and Doubling Time

  • Conventional-dose RT (~66–72 Gy): median nadir 0.4–0.5 ng/mL with NED, >1.0 with local failure, >2 with distant failure.
  • Nadir thresholds after EBRT: nadir >2 ng/mL at 1 year predicts biochemical failure, distant metastasis and death. >1.5 ng/mL at 2 years is an independent risk factor for distant metastasis. >0.5 ng/mL predicts all-cause mortality (unfavourable risk, 70.2 Gy ± 6 months ADT).
  • After BT: nadir <0.5 ng/mL → better long-term freedom from biochemical failure. PSA <0.2 ng/mL at 5 years → excellent 10-year control. PSA ≤0.2 ng/mL at 4 years → disease-free survival beyond 10 years in a very large multi-institutional analysis. With 125I D90 of 160 Gy, only ~10% have PSA >0.2 at 5 years (two-thirds still falling), and the proportion below 0.2 keeps rising for 7–8 years.
  • BT gives a deeper and later nadir than EBRT: mean nadir about ten-fold lower after LDR or HDR + EBRT (~0.05–0.1 ng/mL) than EBRT alone (~0.5 ng/mL). Time to nadir is about 2.5 years after BT vs 1.5 years after EBRT.
  • PSA doubling time (PSADT): <3 months → associated with prostate cancer mortality. <12 months → markedly worse disease-specific survival (RTOG 92-02 analysis; in TROG 96.01 the HR for prostate cancer death was ~23). Practical use: consider earlier intervention if PSADT <12 months, and observation is more comfortable if it is longer.
  • Caveat: PSADT is an imperfect surrogate. Its reliability depends on having many values over a wide range.

Brachytherapy and the Benign Bounce Phenomenon

  • Bounce (spike) = a rise >0.2 ng/mL followed by a durable decline. It occurs after LDR and HDR BT and also after EBRT.
  • Seen in about a third of men after LDR, and up to about two-thirds of younger men. The mean rise is ~1 ng/mL (up to 10 ng/mL); it can last 12–18 months, and double peaks occur.
  • A bounce is associated with better outcome: improved biochemical control and survival compared with no bounce.
  • A biopsy during a bounce may show residual cancer with treatment effect (indeterminate). MR spectroscopy shows a diffuse rise in choline:citrate across the gland, not the focal change expected with tumour. There is no reliable way to tell a bounce from failure in the first 3 years.
  • Management of a rising PSA after BT: within 30 months → monitor. If it approaches 10 ng/mL → systemic staging. If it persists beyond 30 months → prostate biopsy.

Biochemical Failure and Impact of Neoadjuvant Hormones

  • With neoadjuvant ADT, PSA is often already undetectable when RT starts, so a true nadir and time to nadir cannot be determined.
  • When testosterone recovers after EBRT + ADT, PSA often bounces (median ~2 years after treatment, ~0.8–0.9 ng/mL), then falls again as the RT effect continues. Many more of these bounces meet the ASTRO definition of failure than the Phoenix definition.
  • Only about a fifth of bounces occur during testosterone recovery; the rest come after testosterone has normalised. This suggests a delayed testosterone response, inflammation or instrumentation. More frequent testing detects more bounces.
  • Phoenix (nadir + 2) makes fewer false calls of failure than ASTRO (three rises).

Post–Radiation Therapy Biopsy, Timing, and Interpretation

  • PSA cannot distinguish local from systemic failure, so biopsy assesses the local effect. The problems are timing, indeterminate biopsies with marked radiation effect and sampling error.
  • Timing: radiation causes postmitotic cell death, meaning fatally damaged cells survive a few divisions before dying. Biopsies taken as early as 6 months were once called failures. Histological resolution runs parallel to the PSA nadir. The optimal biopsy time is 30–36 months, although 24-month biopsies strongly predicted 7-year disease-free survival.
  • Grade only when radiation effect is absent or minimal. Gleason grading was built on untreated surgical specimens and depends on gland architecture, which RT disrupts. Grading disintegrating glands gives a false-positive, falsely high-grade result and unnecessary salvage. ADT-treated cancer can also mimic Gleason 4 or 5.
  • Radiation atypia in benign glands can mimic cancer. High-molecular-weight cytokeratin labels the basal cell layer, which is present in benign glands and absent in cancer.
  • Radiation-effect scoring systems (Box 162.1) matter because they separate biopsies with no or minimal effect from those with marked effect. Ignoring the distinction dilutes the prognostic value of the biopsy and leads to inappropriate salvage. Proliferation markers (proliferating cell nuclear antigen, Ki-67) help judge residual tumour.
  • Prognostic value: 2-year biopsy status predicts 10-year biochemical NED, freedom from distant metastasis and disease-specific survival. Biopsies showing marked treatment effect behave as negative. Biopsy status and Gleason score are the two strongest determinants of biochemical disease-free survival.

Imaging and Sampling Error

  • TRUS alone is of limited use after RT: fibrosis changes the echogenicity of the irradiated gland.
  • MRI far surpasses CT and ultrasound for soft tissue, and mpMRI detects recurrence better than T2-weighted imaging alone. Adding MR spectroscopy improves accuracy; diffusion weighting improves it; dynamic contrast enhancement added nothing further in one study.
  • Local recurrence arises at the site of the original dominant nodule, as confirmed on whole-mount salvage specimens.
  • MRI–TRUS fusion biopsy is well established, and in-bore MRI-guided biopsy is safe and effective.
Key points: evaluating the response to radiation therapy
  • Judge local therapy by local eradication; lower and later nadir = better; bounces commoner after BT; viable tumour must be proven before radical salvage, with mpMRI to guide biopsy.

Concept — After RT the gland stays in place, so every marker is ambiguous: PSA persists and bounces, and biopsies show dying cells. Interpretation depends on time: nadir kinetics, the 18-month failure interval, the 30-month bounce rule and the 30–36-month biopsy window.
