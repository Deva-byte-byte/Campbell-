Chapter 162 — Radiation Therapy for Prostate Cancer — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else
  1. Prostate cancer α/β ≈1.5 Gy, lower than the rectum (~3) → large fractions favour tumour kill.
  2. Moderate hypofractionation 60 Gy/20 noninferior (CHHiP, PROFIT); SBRT 36.25 Gy/5 standard (PACE-B).
  3. Dose escalation: 6 randomised trials → better biochemical control, no overall survival (OS) gain.
  4. ADT (NCCN): favourable intermediate none; unfavourable intermediate 4–6 months; high risk 24–36 months (12–36 with BT + EBRT).
  5. Phoenix failure = nadir + 2 ng/mL; ASTRO 1996 = three consecutive rises.
  6. LDR quality: D90 >90% of prescription; V100 >85%; imaging at day 14–30.
  7. BT relative contraindications: IPSS >20, Qmax <10 mL/s, TURP <3–6 months, median lobe, pubic arch.
  8. ASCENDE-RT: LDR boost halves biochemical failure (HR 2.04) but grade 3 GU 18.4% vs 5.2%.
  9. Bounce >0.2 ng/mL then falls; monitor to 30 months; biopsy if still rising after 30 months.
  10. Post-RT biopsy at 30–36 months; never Gleason-grade marked radiation effect.

Background and technologic advances

  • Conventional fractionation: 1.8–2.0 gray (Gy)/day; megavoltage linear accelerators 6–20 MV; late toxicity = ≥90 days after treatment.
  • α/β: late normal tissue 2–4 Gy (~3, fraction-sensitive); most tumours ≥8 (~10); prostate 1.0–1.7.

Contemporary techniques and imaging

  • MRI misses cancers <10 mm → systematic ± targeted biopsy; cores from one target count as one.
  • MRI has no electron density → must fuse with CT to plan; most useful with hip prosthesis or small gland.
  • PSMA PET/CT (prostate-specific membrane antigen) before treatment: unfavourable intermediate and high risk.
  • IMRT (intensity-modulated radiation therapy): commonest curative EBRT; no randomised trial vs 3D-CRT (three-dimensional conformal radiotherapy).
  • GTV (gross tumour volume) = dominant lesion on MRI; CTV (clinical target volume) = + whole prostate ± seminal vesicles ± nodes; PTV (planning target volume) = + set-up/motion margin.
  • Margins: IGRT (image-guided RT) 3–7 mm; SBRT (stereotactic body RT) 3–5 mm; MRI-guided 2 mm vs CT-guided 4 mm (MIRAGE).
  • Moderate hypofractionation 2.25–4 Gy/fraction; ultrahypofractionation ≥5 Gy (5–10 Gy).
  • HYPO-RT-PC: 42.7 Gy/7 = 78 Gy/39 (noninferior; late toxicity equal).
  • SBRT ceiling: no gain above 40 Gy/5 (36 Gy/4); more late toxicity.
  • SBRT: every other day; commonest U.S. schedule 725 cGy × 5; CyberKnife needs fiducials.
  • FLAME: boost to dominant lesion → better biochemical progression-free survival (HR 0.45), no added toxicity.
  • Protons: Bragg peak; spread-out Bragg peak (SOBP) for tumour thickness; ↓ low-dose bath, not high-dose volume.
  • Proton trap: femoral head dose higher (lateral beams) → avascular necrosis risk.
  • PROG 95-09: 79.2 vs 70.2 Gy-equivalent → fewer biochemical failures; no photon comparator.
  • No randomised trial of protons vs IMRT; PARTIQoL (EPIC bowel score at 24 months) ongoing.

Brachytherapy

  • Isotope half-lives: 125I 59.6 d; 103Pd 17 d; 131Cs 9.7 d.
  • Monotherapy dose: 125I 140–160; 103Pd 110–125; 131Cs 100–115 Gy.
  • Combined: EBRT 45 Gy + implant at 60–70% of monotherapy dose.
  • LDR: energy ~21–30 keV. Peripheral loading spares the urethra. Intraoperative planning gives best coverage and lowest urethral dose.
  • Rectal RV100: <1 cm³ (125I/103Pd), <0.5 cm³ (131Cs); <1.0 cm³ on day 1 / <1.3 cm³ on day 30.
  • HDR (high dose rate): 192Ir, 380 keV, remote afterloader; no post-implant dosimetry; needs a shielded room.
  • HDR monotherapy: 27–28 Gy/2; 31.5 Gy/3; 34–38 Gy/4.
  • Single 19 Gy HDR fails: 5-year local failure 29% vs 3% with 27 Gy/2.
  • Prerequisites: fit for general anaesthesia (standard) and TRUS.
  • Cytoreduction: ADT up to ~30%; 5α-reductase inhibitor ~20% (less morbid).
  • RTOG 0232: intermediate risk, BT alone = EBRT + BT for control; less toxicity → BT alone standard.
  • UK HDR boost trial: EBRT ± HDR 17 Gy/2 → 21% absolute relapse-free survival gain at 12 years.
  • Gleason 9–10: EBRT + BT lowest 5-year prostate cancer mortality (3% vs 12% RP vs 13% EBRT).

Metastatic disease

  • Oligometastatic ≤5 lesions; oligoprogression → treat sites, keep systemic therapy.
  • CHAARTED high volume: ≥4 bone metastases with ≥1 outside spine or pelvis, or visceral.
  • STAMPEDE (metastatic): prostate RT improves OS only in low burden (HR 0.68).
  • HORRAD: prostate RT, no OS benefit overall.
  • 223Ra (ALSYMPCA): alpha; 50 kBq/kg every 4 weeks × 6; OS +2.6 months; no visceral metastases (nodes <3 cm).
  • 177Lu-PSMA-617 (VISION): post-taxane PSMA+ castration-resistant disease; OS 15.3 vs 11.3 months (HR 0.62).
  • 89Sr: pain relief, rarely pain-free; myelosuppression.
  • SABR-COMET: 1–5 metastases; 5-year OS 42.3% vs 17.7%.
  • ORIOLE: 1–3 metastases; progression at 6 months 19% vs 61%.
  • STOMP: ADT-free survival 21 vs 13 months — not significant.
  • ESTRO-ACROP consensus: metastasis-directed therapy for ≤5 lesions; oligoprogressive castration-resistant disease → do not change systemic therapy.

Androgen suppression with radiation

  • Mechanism: castration ↓ Ku70 → impaired repair; RT ↑ androgen receptor signalling.
  • TROG 96.01: 6 months (not 3) ADT → better OS.
  • RTOG 94-08: + 4 months ADT benefits intermediate, not low risk.
  • DFCI 95-096: + 6 months ADT → OS benefit only with no or minimal comorbidity.
  • EORTC 22863: 3 years goserelin + 70 Gy → better OS.
  • RTOG 86-10: 4 months ADT → better disease-specific mortality, not OS.
  • SPCG-7 / PR.3: adding RT to ADT → OS +8% (7 years) to +10% (10 years).
  • RTOG 92-02: long ADT → OS benefit only in Gleason 8–10.
  • PCS IV: 18 months = 36 months; better quality of life with 18.
  • RTOG 99-10: mostly intermediate risk; longer neoadjuvant ADT no benefit.
  • RADAR: extra ADT benefit falls as dose rises; none with HDR boost.

Pelvic nodal treatment

  • RTOG 94-13 / GETUG-01: intact prostate → no durable benefit.
  • SPPORT (RTOG 0534): postoperative pelvic RT + short ADT → fewer distant metastases.
  • Superior border L4/L5 (RTOG 09-24); old L5/S1 border misses common iliacs.

Evaluating the response

  • Median nadir: ~0.6 ng/mL (70 Gy); ~0.3 (79 Gy); ≤0.1 (EBRT + BT).
  • No evidence of disease (NED) nadir 0.4–0.5; local failure >1.0; distant failure >2.
  • Biochemical failure <18 months → distant metastasis and prostate cancer death.
  • Nadir >2 at 1 year, >1.5 at 2 years, >0.5 → worse outcome.
  • After BT: nadir <0.5; PSA ≤0.2 at 4–5 years → durable cure.
  • Doubling time: distant 3–6 months; local ≥1 year; <3 months → prostate cancer mortality; <12 months → consider intervention.
  • Bounce: ~⅓ after LDR (⅔ if young); lasts 12–18 months; approaching 10 ng/mL → systemic staging.
  • Post-RT recurrence imaging (ACR): bone scan first; CT/MRI if negative. AUA: mpMRI after a negative biopsy.
  • High-molecular-weight cytokeratin = basal cells → benign atypia, not cancer.
  • Box 162.1 score: 0–2 minimal; 3–4 moderate; 5–6 marked (marked = behaves as negative).

Treatment efficacy

  • RTOG 0126: 79.2 vs 70.2 Gy; biochemical failure-free survival 80% vs 65%; OS HR 1.00.
  • MD Anderson: 78 vs 70 Gy; freedom from failure 73% vs 50%.
  • CHHiP: 60/20 noninferior; 57/19 not.
  • RTOG 04-15 (low risk): 70 Gy/28 noninferior; more late toxicity.
  • HYPRO: 64.6 Gy/19 not superior.
  • Postoperative: 62.5 Gy/25 noninferior toxicity to 66.6 Gy/37.
  • SBRT rectal risk: >35% of rectal circumference receiving >39 Gy.

BT outcomes and comparison

  • No randomised trial of BT alone vs EBRT alone; retrospective data favour BT biochemically; EBRT <72 Gy inferior.

Morbidity

  • IMRT vs 3D-CRT: ↓ GI, not GU toxicity.
  • Rectal V70 >25% → more rectal complications.
  • Urinary symptoms peak at 2 months; baseline by 12 months; incontinence uncommon.
  • Pre-BT IPSS >20 → ~29% retention; <10 → ~2%.
  • Proctitis at 6–18 months: steroid suppositories or sucralfate → argon plasma coagulation or hyperbaric oxygen → colostomy.
  • BT rectal complications 1–2%; colostomy ~0.3%.
  • Spacer (Denonvilliers' fascia): late rectal toxicity 2% vs 7% (hydrogel); 2.9% vs 13.8% (hyaluronic acid).
  • ProtecT: RT → more bowel bother, bloody stools; no faecal incontinence difference.
  • Potency best with BT alone (~76% at 5 years); ADT worsens it.
  • PDE5 inhibitors effective in ~¾.

Salvage

  • Prerequisite: biopsy-proven, local-only failure.
  • Salvage prostatectomy (CALGB 9687): ~39% salvaged at 5 years; ~63% incontinent.
  • BT favoured for salvage; NRG 0526: 68% salvaged at 5 years.
  • HDR salvage: 8 Gy × 4; strictures a key toxicity.
Classic traps
  • Trap: dose escalation improves survival — Actually: biochemical control only.
  • Trap: LDR means low dose — Actually: low dose rate; permanent seeds.
  • Trap: protons spare every organ — Actually: femoral head dose higher; high-dose rectum unchanged.
  • Trap: a PSA rise after BT means failure — Actually: probable bounce; monitor to 30 months.
  • Trap: ASTRO "three rises" is current — Actually: Phoenix nadir + 2 ng/mL.
  • Trap: Gleason-grade the irradiated biopsy — Actually: only if radiation effect minimal.
  • Trap: single-fraction HDR 19 Gy suffices — Actually: 29% local failure; multifraction standard.
  • Trap: IMRT lowers GU toxicity — Actually: GI only; urethra inside target.
  • Trap: pelvic nodal RT proven in intact prostate — Actually: RTOG 94-13 and GETUG-01 negative.
  • Trap: biopsy at 6–12 months post-RT — Actually: postmitotic death; wait 30–36 months.
