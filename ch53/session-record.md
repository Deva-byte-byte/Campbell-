## The numbers to lock in

### Instalment 1 — UPJO: Definition to Endoscopic Approach
- **Thresholds**: hydronephrosis = AP pelvis **12 mm**; antenatal scan about **20 weeks**; bilateral UPJO **10–40%**; associated urological anomaly **up to 50%**; contralateral UPJO **10–40%**; VUR with UPJO **up to 40%**; about **one-third** need surgery; non-functioning moiety **≤5–10%** → partial nephrectomy; 21-gauge needle to decompress a tense pelvis; 6-0/5-0 absorbable anastomosis; stent duration **1–4 weeks** makes no difference; endopyelotomy double-J **6 weeks**.
- **Gold standard / commonest**: Anderson–Hynes (about 95%); infant intrinsic, older child crossing vessel; commonest associated anomaly = contralateral UPJO; open surgery preferred in infants; extraperitoneal preferred when open.
- **Eponyms**: Koff (obstruction = progressive damage if untreated); Anderson–Hynes (devised for retrocaval ureter); Foley Y-V (high insertion, no vessel); Heineke–Mikulicz (inferior); spiral flap (length:width ≤3:1).
- **Operate if**: rising AP diameter, low or falling DRF, breakthrough UTI, pain; infant failure to thrive, feeding difficulty, constipation.
- **Do not**: simultaneous reimplant and pyeloplasty for secondary UPJO (two-stage); endopyelotomy as first line in children.
- **Traps**: dilatation ≠ obstruction; endopyelotomy only for failed pyeloplasty; balloon dilatation under 18 years performs poorly.

### Instalment 2 — Laparoscopic and robotic pyeloplasty, stenting, outcomes
- **Thresholds**: transperitoneal **45–60°** vs retroperitoneal **80–90°** flank; retroperitoneal space **200–300 mL**; 12-Fr catheter-and-glove balloon; 5-mm assistant port; Blue stent open **12–48 h**, out at **7–14 days**; Foley **24–48 h**; success **90–100%**; late stenting **4–6 weeks**; first laparoscopic pyeloplasty **1993**; vascular hitch **1951**.
- **Commonest**: bleeding = commonest reason for conversion; transperitoneal = more widely used for robotic repair; transmesocolic on the left.
- **Eponyms**: Hasson (open camera port); Hellström–Vahlquist vascular hitch (anterior vessel, no intrinsic stenosis); Blue stent (Salle; externalised, no anaesthetic for removal).
- **Contraindications to the retroperitoneal route**: horseshoe kidney; retrocaval ureter (robotic). Ideal: robotic lower-moiety duplex repair.
- **Operate (redo)**: falling function or increasing dilatation → dilatation/incision or redo pyeloplasty (transperitoneal); scarred short ureter plus dilated lower calyx → uretero-calicostomy.
- **Traps**: table flexion shrinks the retroperitoneal space; emergency pre-pyeloplasty diversion = nephrostomy, not retrograde stent; Foley needed only with double-J; string-removal stents raise febrile UTI.

### Instalment 3 — Other ureteric lesions; VUR principles and intravesical reimplants
- **Thresholds**: stricture segments **1–3 cm**; Pfannenstiel **2 cm** above the symphysis; bladder opened to within **2 cm** of the neck; **5-Fr** feeding tube; circumscribe **1–2 mm**; length:diameter **5:1**; Glenn–Anderson up to **98%**; Cohen **>98%**; Foley **1–3 days**.
- **Commonest**: Cohen = commonest intravesical procedure; polyps in the upper third, male, left; RPF mostly idiopathic.
- **Eponyms with dates**: Politano–Leadbetter **1958** (suprahiatal); Glenn–Anderson **1967** (infrahiatal); Cohen **1977** (cross-trigonal); Paquin (5:1); Denis Browne retractor.
- **Traps**: Cohen makes later retrograde access hard; the Politano neo-hiatus endangers bowel, vas and vagina; two-layer closure shortens catheter time.

### Instalment 4 — Extravesical reimplantation and minimally invasive surgery
- **Thresholds**: voiding dysfunction **up to 10%** (bilateral worse); pelvic plexus **1.5–2 cm** dorsomedial to the UVJ; bladder **one-third** full; tunnel **5 cm**; Foley **12–48 h**; open benchmark **95.9%** (AUA meta-analysis, Elder 1997: 86 studies, 6,472 patients, 8,563 ureters).
- **Eponyms**: Lich (1961), Gregoir (1964); Zaontz modification (around the UVJ, now abandoned); Waldeyer's sheath.
- **Traps**: dissect hugging the ureter and stop at the UVJ; inguinal incision is acceptable for unilateral repair; early robotic series had leak, obstruction and injury.

### Instalment 5 — RALUR-EV, postoperative care, complications, redo, endoscopic history
- **Thresholds**: 12-mm umbilical and two 8-mm ports; Y-detrusorotomy **4–5 cm**; ultrasound **6–12 weeks** (AUA 2010, amended 2017); contralateral VUR about 1 in 10, risk **<6 years**, capacity **<50%**, duplex; prophylaxis **under 4–5 years**; acute obstruction **within 2 weeks**; Matouschek **1981**; STING **1986**; Deflux about **90%** for low grade.
- **Omit VCUG**: low-grade, normal ultrasound, no dysfunction or UTI. **Do VCUG**: high grade, dysfunctional bladder, new hydronephrosis or UTI.
- **Do not operate**: on secondary reflux before treating the bladder; bilateral psoas hitch.
- **Eponyms**: psoas hitch (one side, before the tunnel); Boari flap (posterior-wall base); nipple valve (Paquin); Monti-type bowel ureter (Pope and Koch).
- **Traps**: "dry reimplant" from prolonged nephrostomy; postoperative dilatation ≤ the reflux dilatation is acceptable; neo-hiatus too lateral or anterior → kink on filling.

### Instalment 6 — Endoscopic technique and materials; megaureter definition and indications
- **Thresholds**: needle **3.7–5 Fr**; mound by **0.1–0.2 mL**; hold **1 min** (not for Deflux); antibiotics **3 months**; ultrasound **2–6 weeks**; **1–2** reinjections; migration **<50 µm** intravascular, **<80 µm** phagocytosis; Teflon **90% <40 µm**; Deflux **80–250 µm**, about **23%** volume loss, FDA **2001**; Vantris **300 µm**; Swedish Reflux Trial **20%** recurrence at 2 years; megaureter **≥7 mm**, normal **5.0–6.5 mm**; POM **10–23%**, bilateral **25%**, contralateral **10–15%**; diameter **<10 mm** vs **≥10 mm**; operate at DRF **<40%** or a fall **≥5%**; renogram false positives **up to 44%**.
- **Commonest / preferred**: Deflux = most used; volcano mound = best predictor.
- **Landmark trial**: Swedish Reflux Trial (20% recurrence at 2 years after Dx/HA).
- **Eponyms**: Caulk 1923 (megaloureter); Cussen and Hellström (normal diameter); interstitial cells of Cajal.
- **Traps**: never cauterise puncture bleeding; inspect the mound empty and full; renogram T½ is unreliable; Teflon is not FDA-approved.

### Instalment 7 — Megaureter surgery, outcomes, dilatation and stenting
- **Thresholds**: plication only **<1.75 cm**; catheter **8–10 Fr**; folding success **90–95%**; Hendren **>90%**; stents **7 days** (feeding tube) or **4 weeks** (double-J); POM outcomes **>90%**; dilatation and stenting **70–80%**, double-J **2–6 months**, complications **up to one-third**, follow-up rarely beyond 5 years.
- **Eponyms**: Starr (interrupted Lembert plication); Kalicinski (two-lumen folding); Hendren 1969 (excisional tapering; running on the proximal two-thirds, interrupted on the distal third).
- **Temporising in infants**: distal cutaneous ureterostomy; refluxing reimplant; nephrostomy (hard to maintain); dilatation and stent.
- **Traps**: abrupt tapering is itself obstructive; POM does better than refluxing megaureter; ischaemic stricture after excisional tapering → redo.

**Source errors and inconsistencies**

9. **Blue stent open period**: the body says **12–48 h**; the Fig. 53.16 legend says **24–48 h**. The notes carry the body value.
10. **Posterior lumbotomy**: the body describes a **vertical** lumbodorsal fascial incision, while the Fig. 53.10 legend describes an **oblique** skin incision. These are different layers, not a true conflict; both are carried.
11. **"Antegrade pyelotomy"** (p. 1068) is an ambiguous term; the context is **antegrade endopyelotomy**. Corrected in the notes.
12. **"Matouscheck"** in the body vs **"Matouschek"** in the citation: the citation spelling is used.
13. **"torturous"** (p. 1064) should read **tortuous**.
14. **Key Points: Pyeloplasty**: "in association with a UPJ" should read **UPJO**; "that is association with" should read **associated with**.
15. **"Heineke–Mickulicz"** should read **Heineke–Mikulicz**; **"Denis Brown"** should read **Denis Browne**.
16. **Farrugia BAPU consensus**: cited as 2014 in the text but listed as 2013 in Selected References. The citation is dropped from the notes, so there is no effect.
17. **Deflux success figures differ** between the endoscopic introduction (about 90% for low-grade after one injection) and the materials section (68–89% across series). The reason is **different populations**: low-grade only versus all grades and series. The notes give the bottom line for each.
