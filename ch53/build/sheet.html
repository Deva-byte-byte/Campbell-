Chapter 53 — Surgery of the Ureter in Children: Ureteropelvic Junction, Megaureter and Vesicoureteral Reflux — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else

  • Gold standard UPJO (ureteropelvic junction obstruction) repair: Anderson–Hynes dismembered pyeloplasty, about 95% success.
  • Paediatric hydronephrosis: AP (anteroposterior) pelvis 12 mm; dilatation ≠ obstruction (Koff).
  • Moiety ≤5–10% function → partial nephrectomy, not repair.
  • Tunnel length:diameter 5:1 (Paquin), for reimplant and megaureter alike.
  • Commonest intravesical reimplant: Cohen cross-trigonal (>98%).
  • Lich–Gregoir: voiding dysfunction up to 10%, especially bilateral.
  • Megaureter ≥7 mm; normal ≤5.0–6.5 mm.
  • POM (primary obstructive megaureter) surgery: DRF (differential renal function) <40% or fall ≥5%, recurrent UTI (urinary tract infection), progressive dilatation.
  • POM <10 mm virtually never operated; ≥10 mm → complications, surgery.
  • Most used bulking agent: Deflux (Dx/HA, dextranomer/hyaluronic acid), FDA (US Food and Drug Administration) approval 2001.

Ureteropelvic junction obstruction

Cut-offs and epidemiology:

  • Antenatal scan about 20 weeks; urogenital = commonest anomaly; dilatation commonest within it.
  • Male, left; bilateral 10–40%.
  • Associated urological anomaly up to 50%; commonest = contralateral UPJO (10–40%).
  • Then dysplasia → MCDK (multicystic dysplastic kidney) → agenesis; also horseshoe kidney, VATER.
  • VUR up to 40% with UPJO; high spontaneous resolution.
  • About one-third need surgery; most resolve.

Cause by age:

  • Infant → intrinsic (circular muscle defect); older child → crossing lower-pole vessel, intermittent pain and vomiting.

Operate if:

  • Rising AP diameter · low or falling DRF · breakthrough UTI · pain.
  • Infant: failure to thrive, feeding difficulty, constipation.

Gold standard and technique:

  • Infant → open (incision ≈ port size); open → extraperitoneal.
  • Anderson–Hynes: originally for retrocaval ureter; preserves vessel, excises adynamic segment.
  • Spatulate laterally; stenotic segment = handle, excised last.
  • 6-0/5-0 absorbable; tense pelvis → 21 G needle decompression first.
  • Bipolar for bleeding; bleeding = commonest conversion reason.
  • Pyeloplasty success 90–100%; “success” definitions vary.
  • Stent duration 1–4 weeks → no outcome difference.

Alternatives:

  • Foley Y-V: high insertion, no crossing vessel.
  • Spiral flap: long stricture; length:width ≤3:1.
  • Vertical flap: box-shaped extrarenal pelvis.
  • Heineke–Mikulicz inferior to dismembered.
  • Vascular hitch (Hellström 1951): anterior lower-pole vessel + NO intrinsic obstruction; normal peristalsing UPJ; no calyceal dilatation.
  • Uretero-calicostomy: scarred short ureter + dilated lower calyx, thin parenchyma.

Access:

  • Posterior lumbotomy: infants and toddlers only; prone.
  • Flank: supine, tip of 11th–12th rib.
  • Transperitoneal 45–60° flank; left → transmesocolic.
  • Retroperitoneal 80–90°; table not flexed; 200–300 mL space.
  • No retroperitoneal route for horseshoe or retrocaval ureter; ideal for duplex lower-moiety robotic repair.
  • Robot: shorter learning curve than laparoscopy.
  • Redo pyeloplasty → transperitoneal.

Endopyelotomy:

  • Balloon under 18 years: poor; secondary (scar) UPJO > primary.
  • Niche = failed pyeloplasty → double-J 6 weeks; redo pyeloplasty still best.

Stents:

  • Blue stent: open 12–48 h, knotted, out at 7–14 days, no anaesthetic.
  • Foley 24–48 h only with double-J (stent-induced reflux).
  • Emergency diversion before pyeloplasty → nephrostomy, not retrograde stent.
  • String on double-J → more febrile UTI.

Other ureteric lesions:

  • Congenital stricture: mid-ureter, 1–3 cm → ureteroureterostomy.
  • Polyp: upper third, male, left; fibroepithelial; confirm by RPG (retrograde pyelography) or ureteroscopy.
  • Retroperitoneal fibrosis: steroids → azathioprine → double-J → ureterolysis.

Vesicoureteric reflux (VUR)

Principles:

  • Treat secondary reflux first; length:diameter 5:1; straight course.
  • Pfannenstiel 2 cm above symphysis; vertical incision for redo.
  • Bladder opened to 2 cm of neck; orifice 5-Fr feeding tube; circumscribe 1–2 mm.

Eponyms:

  • Politano–Leadbetter (1958): suprahiatal, new superomedial hiatus; long tunnel; high grade.
  • Glenn–Anderson (1967): infrahiatal → bladder neck; least kinking, short tunnel.
  • Cohen (1977): cross-trigonal; commonest; trabeculated bladder; hard later retrograde access.
  • Lich (1961)–Gregoir (1964): extravesical; no haematuria or spasm; 5-cm tunnel.
  • Zaontz modification (around UVJ) → nerve injury; abandoned.

Numbers:

  • Pelvic plexus 1.5–2 cm dorsomedial to UVJ (ureterovesical junction) → hug ureter, stop at UVJ.
  • Open reimplant success 95.9%: AUA meta-analysis (Elder 1997).
  • Low-grade primary VUR → reimplant about 100%.
  • Ultrasound 6–12 weeks postoperatively (AUA 2010, amended 2017).
  • RALUR-EV (robotic extravesical reimplant): low lithotomy, 12-mm Hasson umbilical port, two 8-mm ports, Y-detrusorotomy 4–5 cm.

Postoperative:

  • Omit VCUG (voiding cystourethrogram): low-grade, normal ultrasound, no dysfunction or UTI.
  • Do VCUG: high grade, dysfunctional bladder, new hydronephrosis or UTI.
  • Early persistent reflux → usually resolves by 1 year.
  • Contralateral VUR about 1 in 10, mostly resolves; risk age <6 y, capacity <50%, duplex.
  • Asymptomatic contralateral VUR under 4–5 y → prophylaxis.
  • Postoperative dilatation ≤ maximal reflux dilatation = acceptable.
  • Acute obstruction within 2 weeks → double-J or nephrostomy; internalise early (“dry reimplant”).
  • Late angulation: neo-hiatus too lateral or anterior; kinks on filling.

Redo:

  • Psoas hitch one side only, before the tunnel, non-absorbable sutures.
  • Both short → hitch + TUU (transureteroureterostomy).
  • Boari flap: based on the posterior wall.
  • Monti-type bowel ureter: no tapering, no metabolic cost.

Endoscopic treatment of VUR

  • Matouschek 1981; STING (subureteric Teflon injection), O'Donnell and Puri 1986.
  • Teflon never FDA-approved: migration (90% <40 µm).
  • Migration: <50 µm intravascular; <80 µm phagocytosed.
  • Deflux 80–250 µm; loses about 23% volume; low-grade single injection about 90%.
  • Vantris 300 µm, non-degradable; higher resolution; late obstruction; not FDA-approved.
  • Swedish Reflux Trial: 20% recurrence at 2 years after Dx/HA.
  • Needle 3.7–5 Fr, bevel up, 6 o'clock; mound by 0.1–0.2 mL.
  • STING: 2–3 mm below orifice; HIT (hydrodistension implantation technique) intramural > STING; double HIT best.
  • Volcano mound → best success.
  • Bleeding → pressure, never cautery.
  • Follow-up: antibiotics 3 months; ultrasound 2–6 weeks; 1–2 reinjections, then surgery.
  • Recurrence risk: febrile UTIs, BBD (bladder and bowel dysfunction), scarring.

Megaureter

  • “Megaloureter”: Caulk 1923.
  • 4 types: obstructed · refluxing · non-obstructed non-refluxing · refluxing + obstructed.
  • POM: adynamic distal segment; craniocaudal maturation; interstitial cells of Cajal.
  • POM 10–23% of antenatal dilatations; male, left; bilateral 25%; contralateral anomaly 10–15%.
  • Renogram T½ unreliable: false positive up to 44%.
  • Follow into early adulthood (worsening at puberty).
  • Infant temporising: distal cutaneous ureterostomy, refluxing reimplant, nephrostomy, dilatation and stent.
  • Plication or folding (Starr, Kalicinski) only <1.75 cm; 8–10-Fr catheter.
  • Hendren excisional tapering (1969): massive ureter; ischaemia risk; running proximal two-thirds, interrupted distal third.
  • Taper gradually; stent: feeding tube 7 days or double-J 4 weeks.
  • POM outcome >90%; better than refluxing megaureter.
  • Dilatation and stenting: 70–80%; double-J 2–6 months; complications up to one-third.

Classic traps

  • Trap: dilatation = obstruction — Actually: obstruction = progressive damage (Koff).
  • Trap: laparoscopy for infant UPJO — Actually: open preferred in infants.
  • Trap: vascular hitch for any crossing vessel — Actually: only without intrinsic stenosis.
  • Trap: endopyelotomy first-line in children — Actually: reserve for failed pyeloplasty.
  • Trap: retroperitoneal route for horseshoe kidney — Actually: contraindicated (posterior approach).
  • Trap: bilateral psoas hitch — Actually: one side only, plus TUU.
  • Trap: reimplant secondary (neurogenic or valve) reflux — Actually: treat bladder first (anticholinergic + CISC).
  • Trap: routine bilateral reimplant to prevent contralateral VUR — Actually: most resolve spontaneously.
  • Trap: prolonged renogram T½ proves megaureter obstruction — Actually: up to 44% false positive.
  • Trap: plicate any megaureter — Actually: folding only <1.75 cm; excise larger.
