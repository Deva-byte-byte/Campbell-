Chapter 80 — Surgery of Testicular Tumours — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else

  • Suspected cancer → inguinal radical orchidectomy, early cord control, cord to internal ring
  • Partial orchidectomy only if polar + ≤2 cm + contralateral testis compromised/absent
  • PC-RPLND: nonseminoma residual >1 cm + normal markers
  • Complete response (≤1 cm, markers normal) → observe
  • PC-RPLND standard = residual mass + full bilateral infrahilar template
  • Modified PC template: ≤5 cm in landing zone + normal markers + good/intermediate risk
  • PC-RPLND histology: fibrosis 40% · teratoma 45% · viable 15%
  • Seminoma residual: <3 cm observe; >3 cm → FDG-PET; PET-avid → treat
  • Late relapse (>2 years) resectable → surgery first (chemoresistant)
  • Bleomycin → low FiO₂ (SpO₂ 88–90%), restrictive fluids

Management of the testis mass

  • Ultrasound key role: contralateral testis; bilateral synchronous ≈1%
  • Markers: AFP, β-hCG, LDH before and after orchidectomy; trend guides
  • Staging: CT with IV + oral contrast; no role for PET/lymphangiography initially
  • Incision: 3–5 cm inguinal, Langer lines; preserve ilioinguinal nerve
  • Vas and vessels tied separately; non-absorbable, 1–2 cm tail for RPLND
  • Partial: hypothermia unnecessary if <30 min
  • Tunica incision: vertical ventral midline vs horizontal medial/lateral (segmental arteries)
  • Partial adjuvant 18–20 Gy → spermatogenesis lost, Leydig function kept
  • Mass <5 mm, markers negative → partial or surveillance
  • Normal contralateral testis → no elective testis sparing
  • Local recurrence after partial → completion orchidectomy, always
  • Metastatic presentation → chemotherapy first → delayed orchidectomy in all
  • Extragonadal primary: ITGCN up to 42%; metachronous ≈5%; lateralising → orchidectomy

Retroperitoneal lymph node dissection

  • Primary RPLND: high-risk CS I or CS IIA (N1) NSGCT, normal markers
  • Late relapse = >24 months after complete response
  • Pre-op: CT ≤6 weeks; markers ≤7–10 days; no bowel prep; offer sperm banking
  • Bleomycin lung toxicity 11% vs 1.7%; grade 3–4 ×5 → PFTs
  • Exposure: caecum → IMV; Toldt → foramen of Winslow
  • Split at 12 o'clock on aorta below left renal vein → IMA
  • Lumbar arteries: 3 pairs; 2nd pair at IMA level
  • Common lumbar vein: largest, at IMA, may pass behind aorta
  • Sympathetic L1–L4 → anterior aorta → superior hypogastric plexus
  • Right sympathetic trunk posterolateral to IVC — not lymphatic; preserve
  • Drain only for large resections: non-suction; out when serous <100 mL/24 h
  • Post-op: no NG tube; fluids day 0; walk day 1; discharge day 3–5

Auxiliary procedures

  • Auxiliary in 23–45% of PC-RPLND; commonest nephrectomy (5–31%) > vascular
  • Nephrectomy predictor: residual >10 cm, especially left-sided
  • IVC intervention: mass >5 cm ≈20% vs <5 cm ≈3%
  • IVC narrowing <25% → primary repair; >25% → patch or graft
  • IVC ligation acceptable; significant aortic injury → graft always
  • Aortic replacement ≈1%; long duodenal serosal injury → graft (aortoduodenal fistula)
  • Intraluminal thrombus ≈6% (IVC/renal vein)
  • IGCCCG: non-pulmonary visceral metastases = poor risk (≈48% 5-year)
  • Liver discordance ≈50%; RP necrosis → liver necrosis 94%
  • Unresectable liver lesion <3 cm → open microwave ablation
  • Supradiaphragmatic 10–20%; 80% lower/middle mediastinum
  • Anterior mediastinum → think primary mediastinal GCT
  • Thoracic discordance 25–50%; RP necrosis → chest necrosis 89% → RPLND first
  • Resect thoracic residual >1 cm unless RP fibrosis only
  • Retrocrural: transdiaphragmatic; >T8 → partial sternotomy; extensive chest → staged

Surgical decision making

  • Complete response ≈70% after cisplatin (CS ≥II)
  • ≤1 cm residual: teratoma ≈22%, viable ≈5%
  • Necrosis predictors: no primary teratoma, seminoma elements, normal AFP/hCG, raised LDH, >50% shrinkage, <2 cm
  • Einhorn & Donohue 1977: curative cisplatin
  • Donohue 1982 mapping: right → interaortocaval/precaval; left → para-aortic
  • Omit suprahilar + interiliac if non-bulky
  • 3–23% nodes outside modified templates (right > left) → full bilateral argument
  • Primary series: pN+ 20–30%; relapse pN0 7–12%; pN+ alone 23–34%; CSS ≈99–100%
  • pN1–2 cured by RPLND alone ≈70%
  • Williams 1987 RCT: relapse 40/53/60% (micro/<2/>2 cm), not significant
  • Adjuvant: pN1 observe; pN2 2 cycles vs observe; teratoma only → observe
  • Salvage after relapse: ≥1 extra BEP or 2 extra EP
  • Modified PC template results: in-field recurrence 0–4%, CSS 98–100%, ejaculation >85%

Histology and outcomes after chemotherapy

  • Fibrosis CSS/RFS ≈95%; teratoma RFS 80–90%, CSS 85–95%; viable 50–70%
  • Fizazi factors: incomplete resection · ≥10% viable · IGCCCG intermediate/poor
  • Fizazi 0/1/≥2: 5-year OS 100/83/51%; PFS 90/76/38%
  • Adjuvant after viable GCT: ↑PFS, not OS
  • NCCN: incomplete or intermediate/poor; EAU: not good-risk <10% viable
  • No adjuvant after salvage chemotherapy, fibrosis or teratoma

PC-RPLND in high-risk populations

  • Salvage: after ≥7–8 cycles; viable ≥2× first-line; OS 60–75%
  • After HDCT: viable ≈40%; 5-year OS ≈70%
  • Desperation: 5-year OS ≈54%; worse after salvage, raised β-hCG, redo
  • Plateaued markers → >75% fibrosis/teratoma
  • Desperation — operate if: limited, fully resectable, markers falling/plateaued; or chemo exhausted
  • Do not operate: rising markers, rapid doubling, multisite progression
  • Redo: incomplete lumbar division → recurrence; ≈half retroaortic/retrocaval
  • Volume: mean 1/year; >2/year top 25%; 3/year top 10%
  • Late relapse: 2–4%; RP commonest; yolk sac; OS ≈60%

Outcomes, function and complications

  • Abnormal semen at diagnosis 40–60%
  • Ejaculation: template 75–87% → 97%; nerve-sparing 90–100%; paternity ≤75%
  • Complications: primary 10.6–24% vs PC up to 56%
  • Pulmonary PC 3–5%; tachycardia is not a fluid trigger
  • Ileus up to 26%; less with extraperitoneal approach
  • Lymphocele symptomatic ≤1.7%; air/rim → infected → percutaneous drain
  • Chylous ascites: primary 0.2–2.1% vs PC 2–12%
  • Chyle: milky, protein >3 g/dL, TG 2–8× serum
  • Cisterna chyli: L1–L2, behind aorta under left crus; present ≈50%
  • Chyle ladder: tap → low-fat + MCT + octreotide → NPO/TPN → embolise/shunt/surgery
  • Low-fat diet ≥4 weeks after chyle resolves
  • VTE: primary <1%, PC 0.1–3.1%; bleomycin DVT ≈13% → prophylaxis
  • PC mortality <1%: PE, duodenal fistula, MI, chyle
  • Paraplegia risk: bulky post-chemo mediastinal/RP disease

RPLND in unique situations

  • Seminoma CR 70–90%; viable residual ≈10%; no teratoma
  • 3 cm rule: <3 cm all fibrosis; >3 cm ≈30% viable/teratoma
  • SEMPET >3 cm: specificity 100%, sensitivity 80%, PPV 100%, NPV 96%
  • SEMPET ≤3 cm: PPV 37% → PET useless
  • PET-avid >3 cm → PC-RPLND, salvage chemo or HDCT + SCT
  • Residual seminoma at PC-RPLND: 5-year CSS ≈54%
  • HDCT seminoma OS: 92% second-line; 64% third/fourth
  • SCST <5%; malignant if ≥2: age, >4 cm, necrosis, mitoses >3–5/10 HPF, atypia, infiltration, LVI
  • Sertoli: 35% stage II/III, CSM 32% vs Leydig: 98.5% stage I, CSM 2%
  • RPLND in metastatic SCST: no proven survival benefit
  • Overall cure >90%

Classic traps

  • Trap: scrotal biopsy/approach — Actually: inguinal orchidectomy, early cord control
  • Trap: partial with normal contralateral testis — Actually: never elective
  • Trap: PET for seminoma <3 cm — Actually: only >3 cm
  • Trap: adjuvant after teratoma-only RPLND — Actually: observe
  • Trap: Fizazi model guides adjuvant — Actually: prognostic only
  • Trap: modified template standard post-chemo — Actually: full bilateral
  • Trap: fibrosis at desperation RPLND reassuring — Actually: marker source elsewhere
  • Trap: ligate injured aorta like IVC — Actually: graft
  • Trap: post-op tachycardia → fluid bolus — Actually: restrict (bleomycin)
  • Trap: late relapse → chemotherapy first — Actually: resect first
