### PATHOPHYSIOLOGY OF PAEDIATRIC URINARY TRACT OBSTRUCTION

- **Definition:** any **anatomic or functional abnormality that impedes urine flow**, causing renal and lower urinary tract injury.
- Can affect **any level from calyces to urethral meatus**, upper and/or lower tract.
- **Classified along four axes:** acute vs chronic · partial vs complete · intrinsic vs extrinsic · unilateral vs bilateral.
- Clinical importance: **permanent damage to the developing kidney** is the risk that drives timing of diagnosis and treatment.

#### Epidemiology
- **Overall incidence ≈ 1 in 500 live births** worldwide — relatively common.
- **Fetal lower urinary tract obstruction (LUTO): ≈ 2–3 per 10,000 fetuses** (a second figure is given under Lower Urinary Tract Obstruction below).
- Prevalence **varies by population and region**; severity forms a **spectrum** from trivial to lethal.

#### Association With End-Stage Renal Disease and Kidney Transplantation
- Untreated severe obstruction → **chronic kidney disease (CKD) → end-stage renal disease (ESRD)**.
- **High-grade obstruction: lifetime ESRD risk up to 30%.**
- ESRD = **lifelong renal replacement therapy** (dialysis or transplantation), both with significant morbidity and mortality → justifies **early detection**.

**KEY POINTS (source box):** obstruction is **common in children**; severe uncorrected obstruction → **CKD, ESRD, renal failure**.

*__Concept —__ paediatric obstruction matters because it strikes a __kidney that is still developing__: the same pressure that a mature kidney partly tolerates can permanently cap nephron number, so the whole chapter is organised around __detecting early and decompressing before development is lost__.*

### CLINICAL PRESENTATIONS OF OBSTRUCTION

- Presentation depends on **site, severity and developmental stage** at onset (plus duration — Key Points).

#### Timing of Presentation — Prenatal Versus Postnatal

| | **Prenatal** | **Postnatal** |
|---|---|---|
| How found | **Routine anomaly ultrasound (US)** | Symptoms, signs, or incidental imaging |
| When | As early as **11–14 weeks**; most at **18–20 weeks** (second trimester) | Any age |
| Commonest finding | **Hydronephrosis** (collecting-system dilation) | Variable — asymptomatic to life-threatening |
| Key measure | **Renal pelvis anteroposterior diameter (APD)** within the **urinary tract dilation (UTD) classification** — most reliable parameter | — |

- **Prenatal US signs** (any combination): dilated pelvis, ureters, bladder (**megacystis**) or posterior urethra (**"keyhole" sign**); **cystic change or increased parenchymal echogenicity**; **bladder wall thickening**; **ascites**; **oligohydramnios / anhydramnios**.
- Severe LUTO → **impaired pulmonary and limb development** (consequence of absent amniotic fluid).
- Principle: **every structure proximal to the obstruction dilates**, so the pattern of dilation localises the level.
- Prenatal hydronephrosis is a **marker that warrants further evaluation**; early detection allows **fetal intervention or planned postnatal care**.
- **Postnatal manifestations:**
  - **Mass effect:** abdominal distension, palpable abdominal mass
  - **Infection / pain:** unexplained fever, **recurrent urinary tract infection (UTI)**, flank or abdominal pain
  - **Voiding / tubular:** polyuria, urinary retention
  - **Systemic:** emesis, early satiety, anorexia, **failure to thrive**; fluid and electrolyte imbalance with renal dysfunction or failure
  - **Respiratory compromise** (pulmonary hypoplasia)

#### Urinary Tract Sites — Lower vs Upper

| | **Lower urinary tract obstruction (LUTO)** | **Upper urinary tract obstruction** |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | **1 in 5000 – 1 in 25,000 pregnancies**; among the commonest genitourinary congenital anomalies | **Ureteropelvic junction obstruction (UPJO): 1 in 1500 live births** |
| Commonest cause | **Posterior urethral valves (PUV) + urethral atresia ≈ 50%** | **UPJO — the vast majority**; **male : female 2 : 1** |
| Other causes | Anterior urethral valves, megalourethra, prolapsing ureterocele, **cloacal malformation (Fig. 51.1)**, **megacystis–microcolon–intestinal hypoperistalsis syndrome (MMIHS)**, **trisomy 18 and 21** | Ureterovesical junction obstruction (UVJO), pyelocalyceal diverticulum, ureterocele, ectopic ureteral insertion, fibroepithelial polyp, stones |
| Imaging pattern | Megacystis, bilateral dilation, keyhole | Hydronephrosis, hydroureteronephrosis, or isolated calyceal dilation (by level) |
| Lethal mechanism | **ESRD + pulmonary hypoplasia** | Progressive loss of renal function if untreated |

- LUTO mechanism: obstructed flow + **raised intrarenal pressure → compromised glomerular development**; **reduced amniotic fluid → pulmonary hypoplasia** → high fetal, perinatal and postnatal mortality.
- **Oligo- or anhydramnios in the second trimester → combined mortality 60–80%.**
- Both sites: severity spans **clinically insignificant to progressive renal deterioration**, so need and urgency of treatment vary.

**Fig. 51.1** — External female genitalia with **cloacal malformation and pseudophallus**.

**KEY POINTS (source box):** manifestations depend on **location, severity, age of onset and duration**; prenatal detection is by **routine US**; postnatal presentation is **more variable** (asymptomatic → life-threatening); **location dictates symptoms**; **LUTO carries significant long-term morbidity and mortality**; **UPJO is the commonest upper tract cause**.

*__Concept —__ LUTO is a __bilateral, whole-fetus disease__ (kidneys and lungs fail together through loss of amniotic fluid), whereas upper tract obstruction is usually a __unilateral, organ-level problem__ with a normal contralateral kidney — that asymmetry explains why LUTO dominates mortality despite being far rarer than UPJO.*

#### TABLE 51.1 Paediatric Urinary Tract Obstruction Aetiologies

| Mechanism | **Upper tract (kidney and ureter)** | **Lower tract (bladder, prostate, urethra)** | **Extrinsic** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Congenital / structural** | **UPJO**, obstructing megaureter, ectopic ureter, ureterocele, calyceal diverticulum (narrow infundibulum), ureteral stricture | **Posterior and anterior urethral valves**, urethral atresia, caecoureterocele, prostatic utricle, syringocele, urethral stricture, meatal stenosis, phimosis | **Lower pole crossing vessel (UPJO)** |
| **Intraluminal debris** | Calculi, **sloughed papillae** (pyelonephritis, acute tubular necrosis), **bleeding** (infection, trauma) | Calculi, **clot retention** | **Faecal impaction** |
| **Neoplastic** | Tumour, **fibroepithelial polyp** | **Prostatic rhabdomyosarcoma** | **Pelvic and spinal tumours**, retroperitoneal lymphadenopathy |
| **Functional / fibrotic** | — | **Neurogenic bladder and myogenic failure**; **detrusor hypertrophy → secondary UVJO** | **Retroperitoneal fibrosis** |

- **What it tests:** that obstruction is **not only congenital** — intraluminal, neoplastic, functional and extrinsic causes must be considered at every level.
- **Pattern:** calculi appear at **both** levels; the crossing vessel is filed as **extrinsic** even though it produces UPJO.
- **Traps:** **detrusor hypertrophy is a lower tract cause of an upper tract (UVJ) obstruction** — the thick-walled bladder narrows the intramural ureter; **neurogenic bladder is obstruction without an anatomical lesion**; **phimosis and meatal stenosis** count.

### DEFINITION OF OBSTRUCTION

- Obstruction = **impedance of urinary flow** anywhere from kidney to urethra; may involve one or several components.

| **Partial** | **High-grade / complete** |
|---|---|
| Fewer signs and symptoms | More signs and symptoms |
| **May resolve spontaneously** | **Requires intervention** |

- Causes: **congenital malformation, structural abnormality, inflammation, tumour, extrinsic compression** (Table 51.1).
- Spectrum of presentation: **incidental** on prenatal or postnatal imaging → **recurrent UTI, retention, hydronephrosis, impaired renal function**.
- Untreated severe obstruction → **CKD**; if prenatal, also **pulmonary hypoplasia and limb deformity**.
- **Management aims (three):** relieve severe obstruction → preserve renal function → prevent long-term complications; options: **conservative, pharmacological, endoscopic, surgical** — chosen by cause, severity and clinical status.

**KEY POINTS (source box):** obstruction = **impedance of flow**; partial vs high grade, any level, intrinsic or extrinsic, congenital or acquired; treatment aims to **relieve obstruction, preserve function, prevent complications**.

*__Concept —__ "obstruction" is defined by __its effect on flow and function, not by dilation__: a dilated system can be unobstructed and resolve alone, which is why the partial/high-grade distinction, not the image, decides treatment.*

### DIAGNOSIS OF URINARY TRACT OBSTRUCTION

- **The central diagnostic task:** separate patients who **need surgery to preserve renal function** from those who will **resolve spontaneously**.
- Approach combines **prenatal and postnatal** imaging and laboratory studies to grade severity and time treatment.

#### Timing of Obstruction — Prenatal Diagnostic Testing
- **Congenital anomalies of the kidney and urinary tract (CAKUT)** are among the commonest prenatal US anomalies: **1 in 250 – 1 in 1000 pregnancies**.
- Severity assessed by: **degree of hydronephrosis + associated urinary tract changes + amniotic fluid volume**; adjuncts: fetal blood and urine biomarkers, proteomics.

##### Imaging

**Ultrasound** — primary prenatal modality (**cost-effective, widely available, part of standard anomaly screening**).
- Grades obstruction by: **APD**, calyceal dilation, parenchymal thickness and appearance, bladder and ureteral abnormalities.
- **Developmental landmarks:**
  - Fetal kidneys visible from **9 weeks**
  - Bladder filling seen by **12 weeks**; **not seen by 15 weeks = abnormal**
  - **Normal fetal ureters are not visible** — a visible ureter is a dilated ureter
- **UTD classification:** unified prenatal and postnatal grading from **eight societies** (Society for Pediatric Urology, Society for Fetal Urology, American Society of Pediatric Nephrology, Society for Maternal-Fetal Medicine, American College of Radiology, American Institute of Ultrasound in Medicine, Society for Pediatric Radiology, Society of Radiologists in Ultrasound); stratifies by gestation **before vs after 28 weeks**.
- **European Rare Kidney Disease Reference Network (ERKNet) CAKUT–Obstructive Uropathy consensus (May 2022):** **APD is the most reliable parameter**. APD threshold at which obstructive uropathy is considered:

| Gestation | **APD threshold** |
|---|---|
| **16–27 weeks** | **≥ 7 mm** |
| **≥ 28 weeks** | **≥ 10 mm** |

- **Below threshold → do not consider obstruction** unless other signs present: **megacystis and/or reduced amniotic fluid**.

**Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)** — selective adjunct to US (**Fig. 51.2**).
- Indicated when **US is technically limited**: **oligo-/anhydramnios, maternal obesity**; to delineate **complex anatomy** in severe obstruction or before **fetal intervention**.
- Advantages: **not limited by fetal position**, multiplanar; future: **3D reconstruction and functional renal imaging**.

**Fig. 51.2 (A–B)** — T2-weighted fetal MRI of **cloacal malformation**: markedly dilated, ventrally placed urinary bladder; posterior to it **uterus didelphys with massive complex hydrometrocolpos**.

##### Laboratory Studies

**Bladder taps (fetal vesicocentesis)** — US-guided needle aspiration of fetal urine.
- Purpose: **distinguish obstructive from non-obstructive hydronephrosis** and **predict postnatal renal function**.
- Tests: **electrolytes (sodium, chloride, calcium most important)**, **osmolarity**, **low-molecular-weight proteins (β₂-microglobulin**, proteomics).
- Evidence for utility is **still accruing**.

**Urinary markers** — utility **debated**.
- Uses: obstructive vs non-obstructive hydronephrosis and **megacystis**; predict postnatal renal function; **monitor efficacy of fetal intervention for LUTO**.
- **Megacystis:** enlarged bladder **failing to empty over 40 minutes of observation at ≥ 18 weeks**.
- **Sampling: three serial samples; the last reflects current renal function** (earlier samples are stagnant urine).

| **Favourable fetal urine biochemistry** | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Sodium | **< 100 mEq/L** |
| Chloride | **< 90 mEq/L** |
| Calcium | **< 8 mg/dL** |
| Osmolarity | **< 200 mOsm/L** |
| β₂-microglobulin | **< 6 mg/L** |

- Logic: the fetal tubule normally **reabsorbs sodium and chloride and produces hypotonic urine**; **high values = tubular damage** (poor prognosis). Every favourable value is a "less than".

**Biomarkers** — investigational.
- **MicroRNAs (miRs) and protein profiles** discriminate **severe vs partial vs non-obstructive** hydronephrosis; may predict need for surgery.
- **12PUV** (12-peptide urinary proteomic signature) in PUV-related LUTO predicts **ESRD before age 2 years**: **sensitivity 88%, specificity 95%**.

#### Postnatal Diagnostic Testing
- Systematic: **laboratory studies + imaging** → degree of obstruction, renal function, choice of management.

##### Laboratory Studies

**Urinalysis (UA)** — excellent **initial study**; sequences further testing.

| UA finding | Indicates |
|---|---|
| **Low specific gravity** | Concentrating defect |
| Proteinuria, haematuria, casts | Filtration abnormality |
| Crystals | Nephrolithiasis |
| White blood cells | Infection / inflammation |

**Fractional excretion of sodium (FENa)** — tubular handling of sodium; separates **prerenal injury from intrinsic renal disease**.
- **Formula:** FENa = (urine Na × serum creatinine) / (urine creatinine × serum Na) × 100 — urine and serum sampled **simultaneously**.

| **FENa < 1%** | **FENa > 1%** |
|---|---|
| Sodium-avid state — **prerenal azotaemia** | **Tubular damage** — intrinsic renal disease, **acute tubular necrosis** |

- In obstruction: **acute obstruction may give a low FENa** (reduced filtration, preserved tubular avidity); **established obstruction with tubular injury gives a high FENa (salt wasting)** — consistent with the solute wasting described under tubular function. *(Corrected — see appendix.)*

**Glomerular filtration rate (GFR)** — fundamental measure of renal function; guides treatment.
- **Most accurate paediatric estimate: cystatin C–based formula** — **less dependent on muscle mass and age** than creatinine.

**Postnatal biomarkers** — urinary proteins, enzymes, miRs reflecting pathophysiology.
- First-year panel: **serum creatinine, serum and urine cystatin C, neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin (NGAL), kidney injury molecule-1 (KIM-1), transforming growth factor-β1 (TGF-β1), retinol-binding protein (RBP), microalbuminuria (μALB)**.
- Alone and combined, **promising non-invasive tool to identify infants needing expedited surgery**.

### The numbers to lock in — Instalment 1
- **Incidence:** obstruction **1 in 500** live births; UPJO **1 in 1500**, **male 2 : 1**; LUTO **2–3 per 10,000 fetuses** / **1 in 5000–25,000 pregnancies**; CAKUT **1 in 250–1000** pregnancies.
- **Commonest:** prenatal finding **hydronephrosis**; upper tract cause **UPJO**; LUTO cause **PUV + urethral atresia ≈ 50%**.
- **Detection timing:** earliest **11–14 weeks**, most **18–20 weeks**.
- **Fetal landmarks:** kidneys **9 weeks**; bladder by **12 weeks**, **absent at 15 weeks = abnormal**; normal ureters **invisible**.
- **ERKNet APD thresholds:** **≥ 7 mm at 16–27 weeks**, **≥ 10 mm at ≥ 28 weeks**; UTD split at **28 weeks**; **eight societies**.
- **Most reliable parameter:** **APD**.
- **Prognosis:** ESRD lifetime risk **up to 30%** (high-grade); **second-trimester oligo/anhydramnios → 60–80% mortality**.
- **Megacystis:** fails to empty over **40 min** at **≥ 18 weeks**.
- **Favourable fetal urine:** Na **< 100**, Cl **< 90** mEq/L; Ca **< 8 mg/dL**; osmolarity **< 200 mOsm/L**; β₂-microglobulin **< 6 mg/L**; **three samples, last one counts**.
- **12PUV:** ESRD **< 2 years**; **sensitivity 88%, specificity 95%**.
- **FENa:** **< 1% prerenal**, **> 1% tubular / ATN**.
- **Best paediatric GFR estimate:** **cystatin C**.
- **Classic traps:**
  - Detrusor hypertrophy is a **lower tract cause of UVJ obstruction**.
  - Crossing vessel is **extrinsic**, yet produces **UPJO**.
  - **Dilation ≠ obstruction** — partial obstruction may resolve spontaneously.
  - Fetal MRI is **adjunct**, not first line — used when US is limited.
  - **First** fetal urine sample does **not** reflect current renal function.
  - A **visible fetal ureter is abnormal**.

### DIAGNOSIS OF URINARY TRACT OBSTRUCTION (continued) — Postnatal Imaging

#### Imaging tiers at a glance

| Tier | Modalities | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | **US**, renal scintigraphy, **voiding cystourethrogram (VCUG)**, MR urography (MRU) | First-line anatomy and function |
| **Secondary** | Retrograde pyelography, antegrade pyelography | When primary imaging is **inconclusive**; directs management |
| **Tertiary** | Intravenous pyelography (IVP), CT urography (CTU), pressure-flow studies | Selected cases / when others unavailable |

#### Primary Imaging

**Ultrasound** (**Fig. 51.3**) — **standard of care for initial postnatal assessment**: hydronephrosis, ureteral dilation, anatomy, **no radiation**.

**Fig. 51.3** — Postnatal US: **thickened bladder wall and bilateral hydroureter in PUV**.

**Nuclear medicine — technetium-99m renal scintigraphy**

| Agent | **Diethylenetriaminepentaacetic acid (DTPA)** | **Dimercaptosuccinic acid (DMSA)** | **Mercaptoacetyltriglycine (MAG3)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Information | Function and perfusion | Function and perfusion; **cortical scarring** (Fig. 51.4) | Function and perfusion **plus excretion and drainage** |
| Localises level of obstruction | No | No | **Yes** |

- All three assess **presence and severity of obstruction and resulting renal impairment**; **MAG3 is the drainage study**.

**Fig. 51.4** — DMSA: **reduced left renal function and lower pole scarring** in **grade 4 left vesicoureteral reflux (VUR)** with recurrent febrile UTI.
**Fig. 51.5** — MAG3: **delayed left renal drainage = UPJ obstruction**.

**Voiding cystourethrogram (VCUG)** (**Fig. 51.6**)
- Shows bladder, urethra, and (if VUR present) ureter and collecting system.
- **Gold standard for VUR**; identifies **PUV** and other LUTO; delineates trigone, bladder neck and urethra.
- **PUV — leading cause of bladder outlet obstruction (BOO):** **dilated posterior urethra on the voiding phase**.
- **Caecoureterocele:** **filling defect at or near the trigone**, ± extension into bladder neck and proximal urethra.
- **VUR graded 1–5** by level of reflux and ureteric dilation/tortuosity.
- **VUR–obstruction relationships (three):**
  1. **VUR secondary to obstruction** — high bladder pressure in BOO
  2. **VUR causing obstruction** — **secondary UPJ obstruction in high-grade VUR**
  3. **Coexistent** — **refluxing, obstructing megaureter**
- Clinical relevance of VUR: **recurrent UTI, renal scarring, long-term impairment**.

**Fig. 51.6** — (A) **right grade 4, left grade 5 VUR with periureteral diverticula**; (B) **trabeculated bladder, dilated posterior urethra = PUV, no VUR**.

**Magnetic resonance urography (MRU)** — **anatomy + function in one test, no ionising radiation**.
- Quantifies damage via signal change in **perfusion, concentration and excretion** of contrast.
- Uses: CAKUT, hydronephrosis, malformations, **ectopic ureter in the incontinent child**, complex hydroureteronephrosis; functional models advancing.

#### Secondary Imaging

| **Retrograde pyelography** | **Antegrade pyelography** |
|---|---|
| **Cystoscopic ureteric cannulation** (open-ended catheter) via natural orifice | **Percutaneous** direct injection — **no natural orifice** |
| Contrast into ureter and collecting system under fluoroscopy | Contrast into collecting system → pelvis, ureter, ± bladder |

- Both: used when **other imaging is inconclusive**; define anatomy and direct further management.

#### Tertiary Imaging

| **Intravenous pyelography (IVP)** | **CT urography (CTU)** |
|---|---|
| Excretory contrast radiography; **now seldom used** | Non-contrast, nephrographic and excretory phases; **highly specific** |
| Rough identification of **hypo-/non-functioning kidney**; degree and site of obstruction | Detailed anatomy and function; **preferred to IVP for accuracy** and availability |
| Useful **where conventional imaging unavailable**; **bowel preparation improves accuracy** | **Key drawback: higher radiation dose than IVP** → low-dose / split-bolus protocols in development |

#### Pressure-Flow Studies

**Whitaker test** — invasive test of upper tract obstruction.
- **Indications (four):**
  1. **Equivocal** less invasive tests
  2. Suspected obstruction with **poor renal function** (diuretic renogram unreliable)
  3. Suspected **intermittent** obstruction
  4. **Gross dilation with positive diuresis renogram**
- **Technique:** percutaneous access to collecting system → **perfuse renal pelvis at high rate** (saline or dilute contrast) → **serial renal pelvic and bladder pressures**.
- **Interpretation:** unobstructed → drains freely, **no appreciable rise**; obstructed → **pressure > 12 cm H₂O or continuous rise** (source threshold; see appendix for classical Whitaker bands).

**Uroflowmetry** — **volume, speed and duration** of voiding: **voided volume, maximum flow rate (Qmax), curve shape** → lower tract dysfunction or obstruction.

**KEY POINTS (source box — Diagnosis):** timely diagnosis is essential; **prenatal = imaging (US, MRI) + laboratory (bladder taps, urinary markers, biomarkers)**; **postnatal = laboratory (UA, FENa, GFR, biomarkers), primary imaging (US, nuclear medicine, VCUG, MRU), secondary (retrograde, antegrade), tertiary (IVP, CTU, pressure-flow)**.

*__Concept —__ postnatal imaging is chosen by the question asked: __US for anatomy, MAG3 for drainage, DMSA for scar, VCUG for the bladder outlet and reflux__; invasive and irradiating tests are escalations used only when these fail to separate obstruction from simple dilation.*

### PATHOLOGICAL CHANGES OF THE URINARY TRACT WITH OBSTRUCTION

- Drivers: **mechanical stress, inflammation, molecular signalling**.

#### Lower Urinary Tract — Pathological Changes in the Bladder
- BOO → **detrusor hypertrophy** (larger fibres, more mass) **and hyperplasia** (more smooth muscle cells) → **wall thickening, reduced compliance**.
- Changes span **urothelium, suburothelium, detrusor cells, extracellular matrix (ECM) and nerves**.
- **Remodelling stimuli:** cyclic stretch → raised hydrostatic and cyclic hydrodynamic pressure, **hypoxia**.
- **Three-stage remodelling:** **hypertrophy → compensation → decompensation**.
- **Compliance is mediated by ECM:** chronic inflammation → **interleukin-6 (IL-6), tumour necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), monocyte chemoattractant protein-1 (MCP-1)** → ↑ **collagen and fibronectin** → fibrosis → **poorly compliant, "hostile" bladder**.
- **High-pressure bladder: > 40 cm H₂O** → risk of **renal damage and renal failure**.

*__Concept —__ bladder failure in obstruction is a __matrix disease, not only a muscle disease__: muscle hypertrophy compensates, but inflammatory collagen deposition removes compliance, and it is the lost compliance — pressure above 40 cm H₂O — that transmits injury to the kidneys.*

#### Upper Urinary Tract — Immature vs Mature Kidney

| **Immature kidney** | **Mature kidney** |
|---|---|
| **Apoptosis** from raised intrarenal pressure and ischaemia → remodelling, scarring | **Cellular proliferation** (especially tubules) — compensatory |
| **Impaired nephrogenesis / abnormal differentiation** | Persistent obstruction → **collagen deposition and fibrosis** → scarring |
| Loss of **developmental potential** | Loss of **established function** |

#### Regulation of Renal Fibrosis
- Hallmark: ↑ **α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) and ECM in the interstitium**.
- **Renal fibrosis is the predominant cause of function loss** and the **critical pathway to ESRD**.

| Pathway | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Mechanotransduction** | Mechanosensitive proteins/receptors convert **stretch into biochemical signals** → fibrosis |
| **Inflammatory mediation** | **TNF-α, ILs** → immune-cell recruitment, fibrogenic activation, ECM deposition |
| **Growth factors / cytokines** | **TGF-β superfamily (TGF-β, bone morphogenetic proteins [BMPs]) → Smad signalling** → fibrogenesis; plus TNF-α, ILs, **angiotensin II** |
| **MicroRNAs** | Small non-coding RNAs that **negatively regulate mRNA**; **miR-34a** ↑ in tubular cells (human fibrosis, mouse unilateral ureteral obstruction) → **epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition**, suppresses anti-fibrotic proteins; **potential therapeutic target** |

*__Concept —__ every fibrotic pathway converges on __interstitial ECM accumulation__: stretch starts it, inflammation and TGF-β/Smad amplify it, and miRs tune it — which is why fibrosis, not dilation, is the final common path to ESRD.*

#### Haemodynamic Changes With Renal Obstruction
- **Sequence:** urine production continues → **proximal pressure rises** → **net filtration pressure falls** → **reduced renal perfusion → ischaemic injury**.
- **Bilateral obstruction or solitary kidney → more profound disease** (no contralateral compensation).
- **GFR and renal blood flow** fall in direct relation to the high-pressure system; compensatory hypertrophy and smooth muscle stretch perpetuate it → hydronephrosis.
- **Ischaemic injury (inflammation, tubular atrophy, interstitial fibrosis) begins within 2 hours of complete obstruction.**

#### Effects of Obstruction on Renal Tubular Function
- Sustained obstruction → **loss of concentrating ability (hyposthenuria)** and **impaired acidification** → **polyuria and electrolyte derangement**.

| **Early obstruction — wasting** | **Prolonged obstruction — retention** |
|---|---|
| **Sodium, potassium, bicarbonate, magnesium, calcium, phosphate** | **Potassium, hydrogen, chloride, ammonium** |

- **Distal renal tubular acidosis** is the characteristic acid–base lesion.
- **Ion transport mechanism:** obstruction **downregulates aquaporins (AQP1–4)** — proximal tubule, descending thin limb, collecting duct — **and sodium transporters** throughout the nephron → **concentrating defect + salt wasting**; impaired **H⁺ transport → metabolic acidosis** in severe cases.
- Trap: **potassium switches sides** — wasted early, retained late.

#### Egress of Urine From the Obstructed Kidney
- Degree of obstruction (partial vs complete) determines **how much egress is compromised** → retention proximally → **progressive pelvicalyceal dilation (hydronephrosis)** → raised intrarenal pressure → long-term damage.

**KEY POINTS (source box — Pathological Changes):** complex changes in upper and lower tracts driven by **mechanical stress, inflammation, signalling**; lower tract: **detrusor hypertrophy/hyperplasia and ECM change**; upper tract: **impaired nephrogenesis**; chronic obstruction → **scarring, tubular and concentrating dysfunction, ion transport change**; **haemodynamic change**; **reduced perfusion → inflammation, tubular atrophy, interstitial fibrosis**.

*__Concept —__ the __age of the kidney decides the injury__: the immature kidney dies and fails to develop (apoptosis, lost nephrogenesis), the mature kidney proliferates then scars — and in both, falling perfusion and tubular transporter loss explain the polyuria, acidosis and salt wasting seen clinically.*

### CLINICAL IMPACT OF RENAL OBSTRUCTION

#### Hypertension
- Obstruction-related hypertension = **secondary hypertension**; **renal parenchymal disease causes 60–80% of secondary hypertension in children**.

| **Bilateral ureteral obstruction** | **Unilateral ureteral obstruction** |
|---|---|
| **≈ 77%** hypertensive | **≈ 20%** hypertensive |
| — | **Renin secretion lateralises to the affected kidney** in most |

- **Mechanism:** ↑ intrarenal pressure → ↓ perfusion and GFR → **renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system (RAAS) activation** → ↑ angiotensin II and aldosterone → **vasoconstriction + sodium retention** → hypertension. Requires diligent monitoring and treatment.

#### Compensatory Renal Growth
- Significant loss of part or all of a renal unit → **compensatory hypertrophy**, driven by **insulin-like growth factor-1 (IGF-1), TGF-β1, hepatocyte growth factor (HGF)**.
- **Glomerular enlargement + tubular hypertrophy — not new nephrons**: postnatal hypertrophy is **glomerulomegaly, not nephrogenesis**.

#### Renal Recovery
- **Chronic renal insufficiency risk:** injury begins **within 2 hours**; prolonged obstruction → scarring and atony → **end-stage renal failure over days to months**; treatment can reverse damage but **incomplete recovery is common**.
- **Acute-on-chronic injury:** acute obstruction on pre-existing CKD → **rapid deterioration, acute kidney injury (AKI)**.
- **Renal reserve:** capacity to raise function under demand — **compromised by obstruction**, limiting response to further insults.

#### Postobstructive Diuresis (POD)

| **Unilateral obstruction** | **Bilateral obstruction** |
|---|---|
| POD in **≈ 2%** | POD in **≈ 50%** |

- Polyuric phase after **acute relief** of obstruction; body restoring euvolaemia and plasma composition.
- **Mechanism (three):** **aquaporin downregulation**, **loss of medullary tonicity**, **tubular transporter dysfunction from apoptosis**.
- Risk: severe fluid and electrolyte imbalance → **monitor urine output and serum and urine electrolytes closely** (detail in Chapter 90).

**KEY POINTS (source box — Clinical Impact):** obstruction-related hypertension is secondary hypertension (renal parenchymal disease **60–80% of secondary hypertension in children** — corrected, see appendix); compensatory hypertrophy follows significant functional loss; prolonged or recurrent obstruction ↑ CKD risk; **POD follows acute relief** with fluid and electrolyte imbalance.

*__Concept —__ __bilaterality is the multiplier__: with a normal contralateral kidney, hypertension, POD and renal failure are uncommon because the healthy kidney compensates and regulates volume; remove that buffer (bilateral obstruction or solitary kidney) and each complication becomes the rule.*

### TREATMENT OF OBSTRUCTION

- Approach dictated by **location and aetiology**; **multidisciplinary** because decompression carries medical complications.
- Aims: **relieve symptoms, preserve renal function, improve short- and long-term outcomes**.

#### Fetal Interventions

| **Vesicoamniotic shunt / fetal cystoscopy** | **Amnioinfusion** |
|---|---|
| **LUTO with preserved renal function** | **Bilateral renal agenesis**, or **LUTO with fetal renal failure** |
| **Treats the obstruction** → fetal urine restores amniotic fluid | **Does not treat the obstruction** — artificially replaces fluid |
| Regional anaesthesia, US guidance | **Serial** infusions needed through pregnancy |
| Goal: prevent pulmonary hypoplasia | Goal: prevent pulmonary hypoplasia and anhydramnios sequelae |

#### Pain Management
- Sources: **baroreceptor activation by obstruction**, stent discomfort, infection, **decompression haematuria**.
- Agents: **non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) — only if renal function not compromised**, paracetamol (acetaminophen), **α-adrenergic blockers**, anticholinergics, occasionally opioids.

#### Lower Urinary Tract — Drainage (choice by aetiology, age, anatomy, stability)

| Option | Indication | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| **Timed voiding ± double voiding** | Mild or **functional obstruction** (poor bladder neck opening without anatomical lesion or sphincter overactivity) | Void every **1–3 hours** by clock; double voiding for **raised post-void residual** |
| **Clean intermittent catheterisation (CIC)** | Neurogenic bladder, selected urethral abnormalities | Empties bladder; ↓ UTI, stones, retention |
| **Continuous overnight catheter drainage (COCD)** | **Progressive renal disease + dysfunctional bladder (e.g. post-PUV)** with **nocturnal polyuria** | Indwelling Foley at night **delays renal progression**; **does not replace augmentation or daytime CIC** |
| **Suprapubic tube (SPT)** | Urethral catheterisation **not feasible or contraindicated** | Reliable **short-term** drainage |
| **Vesicostomy** | Infants with complex LUTO; **unstable** needing prompt decompression; **unable or unwilling to do CIC** | Bladder dome to lower abdomen; **temporising, reversed at definitive repair** |
| **Surgery** | Severe/complex LUTO | Urethral stricture repair, **valve ablation**, bladder outlet reconstruction |

#### Upper Urinary Tract — Drainage (prompt drainage to preserve function)

| Option | Indication | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| **Ureteral stent** (endoscopic) | Temporising when **unstable** or instruments cannot pass; or **with definitive treatment** (laser lithotripsy, stricture dilation, pyeloplasty) | **Excellent for extrinsic ureteral obstruction** |
| **Percutaneous nephrostomy (PCN)** | Ureteral obstruction, hydronephrosis | Flank → renal pelvis; US-guided; **requires some hydronephrosis** for placement |
| **Cutaneous ureterostomy** (Fig. 51.7) | Bladder drainage not feasible or ineffective | **End or loop**, by aetiology and surgeon preference |

**Fig. 51.7** — **Left loop cutaneous ureterostomy with ureteral stent** in the proximal segment.

#### Upper Urinary Tract — Surgical Intervention (definitive)

| **Ureteropelvic junction — pyeloplasty** | **Ureterovesical junction — ureteral reimplantation** |
|---|---|
| Open, laparoscopic or **robot-assisted laparoscopic** | Technique by anatomy and preference |
| **Identify and correct all intrinsic and extrinsic causes** to prevent recurrence | **Non-refluxing** neo-ureterovesical anastomosis preferred in children |
| **Aberrant (crossing) vessel → re-anastomose anterior to the vessel** | Aim: avoid **postoperative VUR** |

#### Management of Oliguria
- Seen in severe obstruction or AKI → **treat cause, optimise fluid and electrolytes, support renal perfusion**.

#### Management of Postobstructive Diuresis
- **Monitor intake and output, correct electrolytes, replace fluid as needed**.
- **Usually resolves within 24 hours; seldom > 48 hours.**
- **Do not over-replace** — overcorrection **perpetuates** POD.
- **Risk factors after valve ablation (three):**
  - **Oligohydramnios** — prolonged, severe obstruction
  - **Preterm birth** — low aquaporin-2 expression
  - **Raised serum creatinine** — renal impairment

#### Management of Chronic Kidney Disease
- **Blood pressure control, dietary modification, medication** to slow progression; severe disease or ESRD → **peritoneal dialysis, haemodialysis or transplantation**.

#### Emerging Therapeutic Options With Precision Medicine
- **Individualised treatment by patient characteristics and genetics** — future, more targeted interventions.

**KEY POINTS (source box — Treatment):** treatment depends on **location, aetiology and duration**; aims are **relief of obstruction and management of disease- and treatment-related complications**.

*__Concept —__ treatment runs on a __temporise-then-define__ logic: drainage (catheter, SPT, vesicostomy, stent, nephrostomy, ureterostomy) buys renal function and stability, definitive reconstruction follows, and the complications of relief (POD) are managed as carefully as the obstruction itself.*

### The numbers to lock in — Instalment 2
- **Gold standards / first line:** initial postnatal imaging **US**; VUR **VCUG**; drainage and level of obstruction **MAG3**; cortical scarring **DMSA**; best paediatric GFR estimate **cystatin C**.
- **Commonest:** BOO cause **PUV**; its VCUG sign **dilated posterior urethra on voiding**.
- **VUR grades 1–5.**
- **Whitaker:** 4 indications (equivocal tests, poor function, intermittent, gross dilation + positive renogram); obstruction **> 12 cm H₂O (source) or continuous rise**.
- **High-pressure bladder > 40 cm H₂O.**
- **Bladder remodelling:** hypertrophy → compensation → decompensation.
- **Ischaemic injury within 2 hours** of complete obstruction; ESRF over days to months.
- **Hypertension:** bilateral **≈ 77%** vs unilateral **≈ 20%**; renal parenchymal disease **60–80% of paediatric secondary hypertension**; renin lateralises in unilateral.
- **POD:** unilateral **≈ 2%** vs bilateral **≈ 50%**; resolves **< 24 h**, rarely **> 48 h**.
- **Timed voiding** every **1–3 hours**.
- **Eponym — Whitaker test:** percutaneous **pressure-perfusion** test of upper tract obstruction.
- **When to intervene (fetal):** LUTO with **preserved renal function** → shunt/cystoscopy; **renal failure or agenesis** → amnioinfusion only.
- **Relative contraindication:** **NSAIDs when renal function is compromised**.
- **Operative rules:** pyeloplasty — **correct all causes**, anastomosis **anterior to crossing vessel**; reimplantation — **non-refluxing**.
- **Classic traps:**
  - CTU has **higher** radiation than IVP, but is still preferred for accuracy.
  - Compensatory hypertrophy = **glomerulomegaly, not new nephrons**.
  - Potassium is **wasted early, retained late**.
  - COCD **does not replace** augmentation or daytime CIC.
  - Amnioinfusion **does not relieve** obstruction.
  - PCN needs **some hydronephrosis** to place.
  - Over-replacing fluid **perpetuates** POD.
  - Distal (not proximal) **RTA** in obstruction.
