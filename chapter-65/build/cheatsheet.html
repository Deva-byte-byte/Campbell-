Chapter 65 — Adolescent and Transitional Urology — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else
  1. Start transition ~age 12 (American Academy of Pediatrics, AAP); preparation 12–14
  2. Transfer 18–21 → complete 18–23; latest completion commonly 26
  3. Three Ps: Preparation, Plan, named Provider of the future
  4. Paediatric team initiates transition, not the adult team
  5. Transfer ≠ transition: complete = integration confirmed + feedback
  6. Active transition → fewer lost to follow-up
  7. Outcome metric = stability, not improvement (renal function, glycated haemoglobin)
  8. No transition during crisis; exception pregnancy → co-management
  9. Emerging adults 18–25 consent for themselves unless capacity lacking
  10. Spina bifida (SB): normal intelligence; ↓ working memory + executive function

What constitutes adolescent urology?

  • Adult reconstructive urologist: operatively capable; underequipped for whole patient
  • Aim: seamless lifelong care; adolescent urology = bridge

Definition of transition

  • Got Transition: child/family-centred → adult/patient-centred care
  • Three parts: planning → transfer → integration

Why is transition needed?

  • AHRQ (Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality): "no significant difference", but metrics inadequate
  • Avoid "normal": patient = own baseline

Who needs transition?

  • 1. Complex congenital (exstrophy, posterior urethral valves, complex hypospadias, SB) → lifelong
  • 2. Quiescent (vesicoureteric reflux, VUR) → may be discharged, open return
  • 3. Cured, treatment sequelae (pelvic rhabdomyosarcoma) → late-effects surveillance
  • 4. Beyond paediatric scope → fertility, pregnancy

What happens when transition fails?

  • Failure → non-adherence, ↑ emergency department visits, admissions, morbidity, mortality

Transition as a process

  • Six core elements: policy 12–14 → tracking/readiness/planning 14–18 → transfer 18–21 → complete 18–23
  • Readiness tool: TRAQ (Transition Readiness Assessment Questionnaire)
  • Top preparation element: how/where to find valid information
  • Private time alone → sex, sexual function, sexual debut
  • Programmes: Good 2 Go (Toronto), Ready Steady Go (UK), Got Transition (US)
  • Best programme = the one that fits the unit
  • Age 26 = US limit on parent's insurance
  • No independent living → plan medical custodianship

Systems and models of care

  • Routes: transition (1) + new adolescent problem (3) ideal; no-transition referral (2) suboptimal; emergency after discharge (4) worst
  • Lifelong paediatric urologist → suits limited life expectancy; fails when patient outlives provider
  • Integrated paediatric + adult dept → joint clinics; barriers: cost, geography, privileging
  • Direct transfer, no plan → invisible until emergency
  • Multidisciplinary team (MDT) backbone: nursing
  • Most complex MDT needs: neurological disease (SB)

Developmental considerations

  • Genital surgery windows: < 6 months (both); 2–3 y (theory) vs 4th–5th y (AAP 1975)
  • Stages: bonding year 1; individuation 18–30 mo; phallic–oedipal 3–6 y
  • Augmentation cystoplasty: child helps catheterise + wants to be dry
  • Exstrophy closure (non-elective) → as soon as safe
  • Self-management components (4): adherence, responsibility, self-care skills, knowledge
  • Medical post-traumatic stress disorder: no adult congenital urology data; cognitive-behavioural therapy mitigates (cardiac data)

The practicalities: setting up a service

  • Training pathway: none defined; usually paediatric + reconstructive
  • Core MDT: paediatric + transitional urologist, nurses, coordinator, nephrologist, psychologist
  • Biggest adult-provider barrier: unfamiliarity with the condition
  • Paediatric provider: "Am I the best person?"
Classic traps
  • Trap: AHRQ shows transition futile — Actually: poor metrics; stability = success
  • Trap: transfer = transition — Actually: transfer one step; complete 18–23
  • Trap: all need lifelong follow-up — Actually: quiescent (VUR) may be discharged
  • Trap: MDT need = lifelong need — Actually: exstrophy lifelong, urologist-led
  • Trap: new adolescent problem suboptimal — Actually: route 3 ideal
  • Trap: lifelong paediatric urologist best — Actually: fails as patient outlives provider
  • Trap: elective timing follows theory — Actually: school, parental anxiety, surgeon
  • Trap: weak processing → parent consents — Actually: capacity present; support consent
  • Trap: confidentiality reviewed once — Actually: with family, then alone
  • Trap: adult team starts transition — Actually: paediatric team initiates
