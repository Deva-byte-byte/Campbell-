Chapter 95 — Urinary Lithiasis: Etiology, Epidemiology, and Pathogenesis — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else

  • Calcium in ≈ 80% of stones; calcium oxalate 60%, uric acid 7%, struvite 7%, cystine 1–3%.
  • Commonest abnormality in calcium formers → hypercalciuria (35–65%).
  • Hypercalciuria > 200 mg/day; hyperoxaluria > 40; hyperuricosuria > 600; hypocitraturia < 320 mg/day.
  • Uric acid stones: low urine pH (< 5.5) is the key determinant — uric acid excretion usually normal.
  • Calcium and oxalate contribute equally to calcium oxalate saturation.
  • Randall plaque = calcium apatite, origin basement membrane of thin loop of Henle.
  • Distal (type 1) renal tubular acidosis (RTA) → calcium phosphate stones, urine pH > 6.0, profound hypocitraturia.
  • Enteric hyperoxaluria needs an intact colon.
  • Struvite requires urease; commonest organism Proteus mirabilis.
  • Borghi 1996 RCT: high fluid → recurrence 12% vs 27%.

Epidemiology

  • Lifetime prevalence 1–15%; North America 7–13%, Europe 5–9%, Asia 1–5%.
  • US prevalence ≈ 9–10% (NHANES, National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey); annual incidence ≈ 2%; rise partly CT (computed tomography) detection.
  • Females: more stone morbidity (infection, nephrostomy, intensive care).
  • Hot occupations → low volume, low pH → uric acid stones; sedentary → bone turnover, hypercalciuria.
  • Hard water: not proven harmful; net probably protective.
  • Male:female historically 2–3:1 → 1.3; gap narrowing (female rise).
  • Race: White > Hispanic > Black > Asian; White M:F 2.3, Black 0.65.
  • Adult peak 4th–6th decades; female 2nd peak 6th decade (menopause).
  • Paediatric rise 5–10%/year; peak adolescent females.
  • Stone belt = US Southeast; summer peak July–September; lag < 10 days after > 10 °C.
  • Desert deployment → stones at ≈ 93 days.
  • Energy intake > 2200 kcal/day, BMI (body mass index), inactivity → ↑ risk.
  • Diabetes → stones: females only; stones → diabetes: both sexes.
  • Stones ↔ CKD (chronic kidney disease), MI (myocardial infarction), stroke: association, female-driven, not causal.

Physicochemistry and Pathogenesis

  • States: < Ksp (solubility product) undersaturated → metastable → > Kf (formation product) unstable.
  • Heterogeneous nucleation needs less energy than homogeneous — usual in vivo.
  • Therapy targets metastable zone (most urinary salts sit there).
  • RSR (relative saturation ratio) < 1 dissolve; > 1 grow.
  • JESS more reliable than EQUIL 2; EQUIL 2 overestimates (brushite ≈ 80%, calcium oxalate ≈ 50%).
  • Oxalic acid pK ≈ 4 → pH cannot alter oxalate.
  • Calcium oxalate: normal urine 4× solubility; precipitates at 7–11×.
  • Nephron transit 5–7 min.
  • Randall 1937: plaque 17% autopsies; endoscopy 74% formers vs 43% controls.
  • Ductal plugs → suspect secondary cause (bypass, distal RTA, hyperparathyroidism, cystinuria).
  • Most abundant urinary protein: Tamm-Horsfall (uromodulin) — inhibitor in alkaline, promoter in acid urine.
  • Citrate: weak on calcium oxalate growth, potent on calcium phosphate growth.
  • No urinary inhibitor of uric acid or cystine.
  • Nephrocalcin: formers excrete non-inhibitory isoforms (lack γ-carboxyglutamate).
  • Matrix ≈ 2.5% of stone weight.

Mineral Metabolism

  • Calcium absorbed 30–40%; oxalate only 6–14%.
  • Most potent gut calcium stimulus → 1,25(OH)2D3 (calcitriol).
  • PTH (parathyroid hormone): ↑ 1α-hydroxylase; no direct gut action.
  • Renal calcium: excreted 1–3%; proximal 60–65%, TAL (thick ascending limb) 25–30%, DCT 8–10% (TRPV5).
  • Phosphate: gut absorbs ≈ 60%; kidney reabsorbs 80–90%; PTH inhibits reabsorption.
  • Oxalobacter formigenes: oxalate sole carbon source; ↓ with antibiotics.
  • Ascorbate = largest endogenous oxalate source (≤ 40%); hydroxyproline ≈ 15%.

Pathophysiology of Upper Tract Calculi

  • Hypercalciuria definitions: > 200 mg/day on 400 mg calcium/100 mg sodium diet; > 4 mg/kg/day; > 7 (M) / 6 (F) mmol/day.
  • AH (absorptive hypercalciuria): load ≥ 0.2 mg/mg creatinine, fasting < 0.11 mg/dL GF (glomerular filtrate), PTH suppressed; ≈ 30%.
  • Renal hypercalciuria: fasting ≥ 0.11 mg/dL GF + ↑ PTH, normal serum calcium.
  • AH type II corrects with calcium restriction; type I does not.
  • Renal phosphate leak ≤ 2–4% (HHRH, hereditary hypophosphataemic rickets with hypercalciuria).
  • Dent disease: X-linked, ClC-5 — hypercalciuria, proteinuria, renal failure.
  • CYP24A1 loss → high calcitriol; idiopathic infantile hypercalcaemia.
  • PHPT (primary hyperparathyroidism): ≈ 5% of stones; stones in < 5% of PHPT; suspect if calcium > 10.1 mg/dL; thiazide challenge unmasks.
  • Hypercalcaemia: outpatient → PHPT; inpatient → malignancy.
  • Sarcoid: PTH suppressed; corrects with steroids.
  • Primary hyperoxaluria ≥ 100 mg/day; PH3 severe hypercalciuria, rarely ESRD.
  • Nephrocalcinosis in PH → ≈ 70% ↑ ESRD risk.
  • PH1: AGT (alanine-glyoxylate aminotransferase), peroxisome, ESRD (end-stage renal disease) 2nd–3rd decade; pyridoxine-responsive Gly170Arg.
  • PH1 with ESRD → combined liver–kidney transplant; PH2 → kidney alone.
  • Screen relatives for PH before living-related donation.
  • Hyperoxaluria after bypass: malabsorptive only, ≥ 6 months post-op.
  • Dietary oxalate: nuts, chocolate, tea, spinach, potatoes, beets, rhubarb.
  • Hypocitraturia causes: distal RTA, diarrhoea, animal protein, thiazides, ACE (angiotensin-converting enzyme) inhibitors.
  • Ettinger 1986 RCT: allopurinol > 2× recurrence reduction (hyperuricosuric, normocalciuric).
  • Confirm distal RTA: ammonium chloride load, pH not < 5.5; stones ≤ 70%.
  • Incomplete distal RTA: no acidosis, normal electrolytes.
  • SLC4A1 distal RTA: dominant, hearing spared; H⁺-ATPase forms recessive.
  • Proximal RTA: HCO₃⁻ 15–18 mEq/L, urine pH < 5.5, no stones.
  • Type 4 RTA: hyperkalaemic, can acidify; uric acid if any.
  • Uric acid pKa 5.35; solubility ≈ 100 mg/L; > 1200 mg/L soluble at pH 6.5.
  • BMI ≥ 27 → urine pH ≤ 5.5; type 2 diabetes → uric acid stones ↑.
  • Insulin resistance → ↓ ammoniagenesis → low pH.
  • Cystine solubility 300 / 400 / 1000 mg/L at pH 5 / 7 / 9.
  • Cystinuria: autosomal recessive; FE (fractional excretion) 0.4% → 100%; ≤ 10% paediatric stones; first stone ≈ 12 years.
  • ICC (International Cystinuria Consortium): A = SLC3A1 (chr 2), B = SLC7A9 (chr 19).
  • Struvite urine pH 7.2–8.0; F:M 2:1; highest staghorn risk → complete cord transection.
  • Staghorns: majority metabolic (calcium phosphate), not struvite.
  • Xanthine, DHA (2,8-dihydroxyadenine): radiolucent; DHA → febuxostat > allopurinol.
  • Ammonium acid urate ≈ 1%; pH 6.2–6.3; ileostomy, laxatives, obesity.
  • Indinavir: stones 4–13%; ≈ 50% excreted unchanged; soluble in acid urine.
  • Atazanavir–ritonavir ≈ 7% stones; triamterene ≈ 0.4% of stones.
  • Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors → calcium phosphate stones.
  • Topiramate: label 1.2–1.5% underestimates; ≈ 20% asymptomatic on CT.
  • Furosemide: stones ≤ 64% low-birth-weight infants.
  • Anatomy: UPJO (ureteropelvic junction obstruction) ≈ 20%; horseshoe 36% adults; diverticulum ≤ 40%; horseshoe → hypocitraturia over-represented.
  • MSK (medullary sponge kidney): hypercalciuria, hypocitraturia, distal RTA.
  • Pregnancy: 1:204–1:3000; hydronephrosis ≤ 90%, right > left; GFR (glomerular filtration rate) +30–50%; misdiagnosis ≤ 28%.
  • Pregnancy peak 0–3 months post partum; stones mainly calcium phosphate.

Classic traps

  • Trap: uric acid stones = hyperuricosuria — Actually: low urine pH.
  • Trap: oxalate outweighs calcium — Actually: equal contributors.
  • Trap: Randall plaque is calcium oxalate — Actually: calcium apatite.
  • Trap: ileostomy → hyperoxaluria — Actually: no colon, uric acid/ammonium urate.
  • Trap: restrict calcium to cut oxalate — Actually: raises oxalate absorption.
  • Trap: proximal RTA forms stones — Actually: citrate normal; distal does.
  • Trap: E. coli makes urease — Actually: rarely; cultures discordant.
  • Trap: fixing UPJO stops stones — Actually: metabolic recurrence, often contralateral.
  • Trap: Bartter = acidosis — Actually: hypokalaemic alkalosis.
  • Trap: restrictive bariatric surgery → stones — Actually: only malabsorptive.
