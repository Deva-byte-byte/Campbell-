# Instalment 10 — Anatomic Predisposition: Ureteropelvic Junction Obstruction, Horseshoe Kidney, Calyceal Diverticula, Medullary Sponge Kidney; Stones in Pregnancy; Key Points: Pathogenesis; Selected References

## PATHOPHYSIOLOGY OF UPPER URINARY TRACT CALCULI (continued)

### Anatomic Predisposition to Stones

- **Anomalies causing obstruction or stasis carry high stone rates.** The long-running question: **stasis (delayed transit → crystal formation and retention)** or **the same metabolic abnormalities as other stone formers**?

#### Ureteropelvic Junction Obstruction

- **Stones in nearly 20% of patients with ureteropelvic junction obstruction (UPJO).**
- **Evidence that metabolic risk persists after the obstruction is corrected** (adult series):
  - **Most recurred after UPJO repair**, and **a large share of recurrences were in the contralateral kidney**.
  - **Most patients with non-infectious stones had a metabolic abnormality** — a rate **comparable to other stone formers**.
  - **The spectrum mirrored the general stone population** — **hypercalciuria commonest**, then hypocitraturia, hyperuricosuria, primary hyperparathyroidism, RTA.
  - **Treating the metabolic abnormality substantially cut recurrence** compared with conservative management.
- **Pre-repair metabolic evaluation**: UPJO patients **with** stones had **more metabolic abnormalities** (higher urinary calcium, **hypercalciuria and hyperuricosuria**) than UPJO patients without stones.
- **Children**:
  - **Stone-forming children — with or without UPJO — differ in urinary citrate and oxalate from non-stone-forming children with UPJO** (prospective comparison), supporting a metabolic contribution.
  - **UPJO confers a markedly increased (about seventyfold) stone risk** versus normal children.
  - **Most children with non-struvite stones recurred after surgery**, and most had a metabolic abnormality; those who did **not** recur mostly **lacked** one.

#### Horseshoe Kidneys

- **Pooled stone incidence (meta-analysis): ≈ 36% in adults, ≈ 3% in children.**
- **High ureteral insertion → impaired drainage → UPJO tendency** — risk traditionally attributed to **stasis**.
- **But metabolic evaluation found at least one abnormality in every patient tested**; the distribution resembled normal-anatomy formers **except hypocitraturia, which was over-represented**.
- **Composition (meta-analysis)**: **calcium-based ≈ 89%**, struvite ≈ 4%, uric acid ≈ 4%, other ≈ 3%.

#### Calyceal Diverticula

- **Stones in up to 40%.**
- **Conflicting evidence**:
  - One series: **metabolic abnormalities in half**, and **most had stones elsewhere** (synchronous or metachronous) — supports **metabolic risk**.
  - Another: **low metabolic abnormality rates** in patients with and without diverticula — but **pH and citrate were not measured**, so abnormalities were **under-counted** (low pH and hypocitraturia affect about 10% and 28% of recurrent formers).
  - **24-hour urine** of diverticular stone patients **resembled calcium oxalate formers** (hypercalciuria, ↑ calcium oxalate supersaturation) — yet **urine aspirated from the diverticulum had lower calcium oxalate supersaturation** than either renal pelvis.
- **Composition: mainly calcium oxalate monohydrate**; **struvite–carbonate apatite** when infected; **concomitant UTI in up to 40%** (**E. coli, Proteus, Pseudomonas**).

#### Medullary Sponge Kidney

- **Medullary sponge kidney (MSK) = ectasia of the renal collecting ducts**; **nephrocalcinosis and calculi are frequent**.
- **Candidate mechanisms**: **infection and stasis in ectatic ducts**, plus **tubular defects — hypercalciuria, impaired concentrating ability, defective acidification after ammonium chloride load**.
- **Acidification defects**: common on acid loading (mostly **distal RTA**, occasionally proximal); **impaired concentrating ability** in most; **H⁺-ATPase gene mutations (ATP6V1B1, ATP6V0A4)** found in MSK patients — supporting an **MSK–distal RTA link**.
- **Yet three studies of MSK patients *with stones* found no RTA.**
- **Hypercalciuria** is the commonest abnormality in some series (mostly **absorptive**), less frequent in others; sometimes **renal leak** from damaged tubules.
- **Hypocitraturia** the commonest abnormality in other series.
- **Urine proteomics** (Italian network) proposed biomarkers distinguishing MSK from idiopathic calcium formers — **laminin α2, ficolin 1, mannan-binding lectin serine protease 2, complement component 4-binding protein, sphingomyelin, ephrins** — role not yet established.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **UPJO** | **≈ 20%** (children ~70× normal risk) | **Common; same spectrum as general formers** | **Recurrence after repair, often contralateral** | **Metabolic > anatomic** |

| **Calyceal diverticulum** | **Up to 40%** | Half in one series; under-measured in another | **Diverticular urine less supersaturated than pelvic urine**; **UTI up to 40%** | **Metabolic + stasis** |

**Concept —** *Across every anomaly the answer is the same: **anatomy sets the stage but metabolism writes the stone** — which is why patients with anatomical stones still need a full metabolic evaluation, and why surgical correction alone does not prevent recurrence.*

### Stones in Pregnancy

- **Symptomatic stones: 1 in 204 to 1 in 3000 pregnancies**; **incidence has not risen** (1991–2000 versus 2001–2011), unlike the general population.
- **Timing**: mostly **second and third trimesters**, with **flank pain or haematuria**; odds **rise in the second and third trimesters** and **peak 0–3 months post partum**, returning to baseline **by 1 year**.
- **Misdiagnosis in up to 28%** — **appendicitis, diverticulitis, placental abruption**.
- **Physiological hydronephrosis in up to 90%**, persisting **4–6 weeks post partum**:
  - Causes: **progesterone** and — at least contributory, perhaps primary — **compression by the gravid uterus**.
  - **Right side greater** — **engorged uterine vein and dextrorotation of the uterus**.
  - Dilatation may promote **crystallisation through stasis**; ↑ pelvic pressure may **mobilise stones and provoke symptoms**.

| Pregnancy — opposing urinary effects | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Promoters ↑** | **Renal blood flow ↑ → GFR ↑ 30–50% → ↑ filtered calcium, sodium, uric acid**; **placental 1,25(OH)2D3 → ↑ gut calcium absorption, PTH suppressed** → **absorptive-type hypercalciuria (reversible post partum)**; **hyperuricosuria** |
| **Inhibitors ↑** | **↑ Citrate, magnesium and glycoproteins** |
| **Net** | **Overall stone risk similar to non-pregnant females** |

- **Composition**: some studies show no difference, but others find **calcium phosphate predominant** (about three-quarters in one multi-institutional series; **significantly more carbapatite than in age-matched non-pregnant females**) — perhaps from **pregnancy-related urinary pH changes** (speculative).

**Concept —** *Pregnancy **raises promoters and inhibitors together**, so stone risk is roughly unchanged — but when stones do form they skew towards **calcium phosphate**, and the symptomatic peak comes late in pregnancy and just after delivery, when hydronephrosis and stasis are greatest.*

<div class="kp" markdown="1">
**KEY POINTS: PATHOGENESIS**

- **Absorptive hypercalciuria**: **normal serum calcium, normal or suppressed PTH, normal fasting urinary calcium, elevated urinary calcium**.
- **Renal hypercalciuria**: impaired renal calcium reabsorption **stimulates PTH** and causes **fasting hypercalciuria**.
- **Resorptive hypercalciuria**: primarily **primary hyperparathyroidism**; also **granulomatous diseases producing 1,25(OH)2D3**.
- **Low urinary pH is the most important determinant of uric acid stones.**
- **Low urine pH in uric acid formers** probably reflects **impaired ammoniagenesis from insulin resistance plus excess acid production**.
- **In distal RTA, a defective H⁺-ATPase prevents excretion of excess acid into the distal tubule.**
- **Infection stones require alkaline urine achievable only with urease-producing bacteria.**

## ▸ The numbers to lock in

- **UPJO: stones ≈ 20%; paediatric UPJO ~70× stone risk.**
- **Horseshoe: stones ≈ 36% adults, 3% children; ≈ 89% calcium-based; hypocitraturia over-represented.**
- **Calyceal diverticulum: stones up to 40%; UTI up to 40%; calcium oxalate monohydrate.**
- **Recurrent formers: low pH ≈ 10%, hypocitraturia ≈ 28%.**
- **Pregnancy stones 1:204–1:3000; hydronephrosis up to 90% (right > left), resolves 4–6 weeks post partum.**
- **GFR ↑ 30–50% in pregnancy.**
- **Peak symptomatic risk 0–3 months post partum; baseline by 1 year.**
- **Misdiagnosis up to 28% (appendicitis, diverticulitis, abruption).**
- **Pregnancy stones predominantly calcium phosphate.**
- Classic traps:
  - **Fixing the UPJO does not fix the stone disease** — recurrence (often contralateral) is metabolic.
  - **Horseshoe kidney stones need a metabolic cause**, not just stasis.
  - **Diverticular urine is *less* supersaturated** than pelvic urine.
  - **MSK stone formers often have no RTA** — hypercalciuria and hypocitraturia suffice.
  - **Pregnancy: promoters and inhibitors both rise — net risk unchanged.**
  - **Distal RTA key point: defective H⁺-ATPase *prevents* acid excretion.**

## ▸ Source flags

- **Key Points error (Pathogenesis)**: printed "In distal RTA, a defective H⁺-ATPase **accounts for excretion** of excess acid into the distal tubule" — the defect **prevents** acid excretion. Corrected in the Key Points box.
- **Probable inversion (paediatric UPJO study)**: the text states that stone-forming children with and without UPJO had "significantly **higher** urinary levels of **citrate** and **lower** levels of **oxalate**" than non-stone-forming UPJO children. This is the opposite of the stone-promoting direction (low citrate, high oxalate) and of the conclusion drawn; probably reversed in the source. Notes state that the groups **differ** in citrate and oxalate without asserting the printed direction.

- **Uterine rotation**: the text says "**derotation** of the enlarged uterus"; the recognised mechanism is **dextrorotation**. Rendered as dextrorotation.
