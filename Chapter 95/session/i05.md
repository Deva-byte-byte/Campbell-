# Instalment 5 — Hypercalciuria: Evidence and Definitions, Pak Classification, Absorptive Hypercalciuria (CYP24A1, VDR, Renal Phosphate Leak), Renal Hypercalciuria (Dent, FHHNC, Bartter, CaSR), Resorptive Hypercalciuria (Primary Hyperparathyroidism), Hypercalcaemia-Induced Hypercalciuria (Sarcoidosis, Malignancy, Glucocorticoids)

## PATHOPHYSIOLOGY OF UPPER URINARY TRACT CALCULI (continued)

### Calcium Stones

#### Hypercalciuria

- **Hypercalciuria is the commonest abnormality in calcium stone formers** (**35–65%**), though its role is debated because **urinary calcium overlaps** between formers and non-formers.
- **Evidence for a causal role**:
  - **Treatment that lowers urinary calcium reduces recurrence**; **persistent hypercalciuria predicts medical failure**.
  - In three large cohorts with 24-hour urines, **incident stone risk rises with urinary calcium** after adjustment.
  - **Randall plaque number correlates with urinary calcium** and with the number of stone episodes.
- **Mechanism**: high urinary calcium → **↑ saturation of calcium salts** and **↓ inhibitor activity** (calcium **complexes negatively charged inhibitors** such as **citrate and chondroitin sulphate**).
- **Normal load**: kidney filters **≈ 270 mmol calcium/day** and reabsorbs **all but ≈ 4 mmol**.

| Definition of hypercalciuria | Threshold |
|---|---|
| **Strictest** | **> 200 mg/day** after **1 week of 400 mg calcium, 100 mg sodium diet** |
| **Weight-based / sex-based** (Parks and Coe) | **> 4 mg/kg/day**, or **> 7 mmol/day (males)**, **> 6 mmol/day (females)** |

- **Any threshold is artificial** — urinary calcium exerts a **continuous, graded effect** on risk.
- **Pak classification (1974)** — three subtypes by organ of dysregulation (**intestine, kidney, bone**): **absorptive**, **renal** and **resorptive**.
  - Critics: hypercalciuria reflects **multiple interrelated disturbances**; single **gene mutations affect several organs**; the classification **has not improved therapeutic outcomes** and is **not routinely used in practice**. It is retained in the chapter as the standard framework.

<p class="tcap">Pak classification of hypercalciuria — comparison</p>

| Feature | **Absorptive (AH)** | **Renal** | **Resorptive** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary defect** | **↑ Intestinal calcium absorption** | **Renal calcium leak** (↓ tubular reabsorption) | **↑ Bone resorption — primary hyperparathyroidism** |
| **Share of stone formers** | **≈ 30% (Table 95.2: 20–40%)** | **5–8%** | **3–5% (≈ 5%)** |
| **Serum calcium** | **Normal** | **Normal** | **High** (or high-normal) |
| **Serum PTH** | **Normal or suppressed** | **↑ (secondary hyperparathyroidism)** | **↑ (primary)** |
| **Fasting urinary calcium** | **Normal (< 0.11 mg/dL glomerular filtrate)** | **High (≥ 0.11 mg/dL glomerular filtrate)** | High |
| **Urinary calcium after oral calcium load** | **≥ 0.2 mg calcium/mg creatinine** | — | — |
| **Serum phosphorus** | Normal (low in renal phosphate leak) | Normal | **Low** |
| **Sequence** | Hyperabsorption → transient serum calcium rise → **PTH suppressed** → ↑ filtered calcium → hypercalciuria | Renal loss → **PTH ↑** → ↑ calcitriol → gut absorption and bone resorption compensate | **PTH ↑ → bone resorption + ↑ calcitriol → gut absorption** → hypercalcaemia |
| **Discriminating test** | **Normal fasting calcium + suppressed PTH** | **High fasting calcium + high PTH, normal serum calcium** | **High serum calcium + inappropriately high PTH**; **thiazide challenge** |

##### Absorptive Hypercalciuria

- **Definition and discriminators in the table**; severe forms may also show **fasting hypercalciuria**.
- **Type I versus type II**:
  - **Type II — dietary calcium restriction normalises urinary calcium.**
  - **Type I — does not normalise with restriction.**
- **Cause of hyperabsorption is unresolved**: vitamin D-dependent and -independent processes and **VDR up-regulation** proposed; **no mechanism explains all findings**.
- **Genetic candidates**:
  - **CYP24A1** (encodes **1,25(OH)2D-24-hydroxylase**, which inactivates 25(OH)D and 1,25(OH)2D3) — **biallelic loss-of-function → elevated calcitriol**, particularly with high vitamin D intake.
    - Causes **idiopathic infantile hypercalcaemia** (autosomal recessive; vitamin D supplement sensitivity).
    - In adults: **hypercalcaemia, hypercalciuria, nephrocalcinosis and nephrolithiasis**.
    - **Carrier frequency 4–20%**, but **not all affected individuals manifest disease**.
    - GWAS links CYP24A1 variants to **serum vitamin D levels**.
  - **CYP27B1** — generates calcitriol by 1-hydroxylation of 25(OH)D (context for the above).
  - **Vitamin D hypersensitivity** increases absorption; **VDR gene polymorphism** and **linkage to the VDR locus (chromosome 12q12–q14)** reported in familial hypercalciuria — but **not confirmed** by others.
  - **Chromosome 1q23.3–q24** locus for an inherited form of AH, with a putative gene **homologous to rat soluble adenylate cyclase**.
- **Renal phosphate leak** (a subtype of AH):
  - **Sequence**: **renal phosphate wasting → hypophosphataemia → ↑ calcitriol → ↑ gut calcium and phosphate absorption → hypercalciuria**.
  - **Hereditary hypophosphataemic rickets with hypercalciuria (HHRH)** — **autosomal recessive**; candidate genes **SLC34A1 and SLC34A3** (encoding **NaPi-IIa and NaPi-IIc**, apical proximal tubule sodium–phosphate co-transporters).
  - **Rare — at most 2–4% of stone formers.**

##### Renal Hypercalciuria

- The kidney filters **≈ 270 mmol calcium** and must reabsorb **> 98%**; **≈ 70% is reabsorbed proximally**, mostly paracellularly.
- **Fasting urinary calcium plus PTH separates a renal from an intestinal cause** (table).
- **Cause unknown**; monogenic disorders illuminate the transcellular and paracellular pathways:

<p class="tcap">Monogenic disorders of renal calcium handling</p>

| Disorder | Gene / protein | Site | Key features |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dent disease** (**X-linked recessive nephrolithiasis**) | **Chloride channel-5 (ClC-5)** | Proximal tubule, TAL, α-intercalated cells | **Hypercalciuria, low-molecular-weight proteinuria, nephrolithiasis, nephrocalcinosis, progressive renal failure** |
| **Familial hypomagnesaemia with hypercalciuria and nephrocalcinosis (FHHNC)** | **Claudin-16 (paracellin-1) and claudin-19** | TAL, DCT | Loss of **voltage-driven paracellular magnesium and calcium reabsorption** |
| **Claudin-14 variants** | Claudin-14 | TAL | Associated with nephrolithiasis |
| **Bartter syndrome** (autosomal recessive group) | **SLC12A1 (NKCC2, Na⁺-K⁺-2Cl⁻ co-transporter); KCNJ1 (ROMK apical potassium channel); CLCNKB (ClC-Kb basolateral chloride channel); BSND (barttin, subunit of ClC-Ka/ClC-Kb)** | TAL | **Salt wasting, hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis**, variable hypercalciuria and nephrolithiasis |
| **Bartter type V** | **Potent activating CaSR mutation** | TAL | **Salt-losing nephropathy, secondary hyperaldosteronism** (ROMK dysfunction from constitutive CaSR activation) |
| **Autosomal dominant hypocalcaemia** | **Activating CaSR mutation** (lower set-point) | Parathyroid, TAL | **↓ PTH, ↓ renal calcium reabsorption → hypocalcaemia with hypercalciuria** |
| **CaSR loss-of-function SNPs** (two synonymous SNPs reducing renal CaSR mRNA) | CaSR | TAL | **Normocitraturic idiopathic nephrolithiasis**: ↑ TAL paracellular reabsorption → **interstitial calcium precipitation, hypocalciuria**; ↓ distal calcium delivery may impair acidification and concentration → calcium oxalate stones |
| **NKCC2 and ROMK mutations** (autosomal recessive) | NKCC2, ROMK | TAL | **Fasting hypercalciuria and nephrocalcinosis** |

- **CaSR set-point mutations** in **CASR, GNA11 (G-protein α11) or AP2S1 (adaptor-related protein complex 2 σ1)** shift calcium sensing → **hyper- or hypocalcaemic disorders**.
- **CaSR controls TAL calcium reabsorption through paracellular permeability.**

**Concept —** *Renal hypercalciuria is a **leak with compensation**: the kidney loses calcium, PTH rises to defend serum calcium — so the **fasting urine is high and PTH is high while serum calcium is normal**, the exact mirror of absorptive hypercalciuria (fasting urine normal, PTH suppressed).*

##### Resorptive Hypercalciuria

- **Infrequent; most commonly primary hyperparathyroidism (PHPT)**, the cause of **≈ 5% of nephrolithiasis**.
- **Normocalcaemic variant**: **normal serum calcium with inappropriately high PTH** makes diagnosis harder.
- **Thiazide challenge**: thiazide ↑ renal calcium reabsorption → **exacerbates hypercalcaemia without suppressing PTH** in PHPT — unmasks the diagnosis.
- **Nephrolithiasis occurs in < 5% of patients with PHPT.**
- **Suspect PHPT when a stone former has serum calcium > 10.1 mg/dL.**
- **Serum calcium varies by up to 5%** and mild PHPT raises it only slightly → **repeat serum calcium with intact PTH**; **ionised calcium** helps in equivocal cases (may be high when total calcium is normal).
- **PTH also causes proximal bicarbonate and phosphate loss → phosphaturia and mild hyperchloraemic acidosis.**

##### Hypercalcaemia-Induced Hypercalciuria

- **Rare causes**: **malignancy, sarcoidosis, thyrotoxicosis, vitamin D toxicity**.

<p class="tcap">Hypercalcaemic causes of hypercalciuria — comparison</p>

| Cause | Mechanism | PTH | Distinguishing feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary hyperparathyroidism** | Autonomous PTH | **High / inappropriately normal** | **Commonest cause of hypercalcaemia in outpatients**; thiazide challenge |
| **Sarcoidosis (and other granulomas)** | **Macrophage 1α-hydroxylase → extrarenal calcitriol** → gut absorption **and** bone resorption (absorptive + resorptive) | **Suppressed** | **Rapid correction with corticosteroids**; alveolar cells and lymph nodes synthesise calcitriol |
| **Malignancy** | **PTH-related protein (PTHrP)**, osteolytic cytokines, direct bone destruction | **Suppressed (intact PTH low)** | **Commonest cause of hypercalcaemia in hospitalised patients** |
| **Glucocorticoid excess** | ↑ Bone resorption, ↓ bone formation, **↑ PTH release**; but **↓ gut calcium absorption** | Stimulated | **Stones common in Cushing syndrome** |

- **Granulomatous diseases** reported to cause hypercalcaemia: **tuberculosis, sarcoidosis, histoplasmosis, leprosy, silicosis** — **sarcoidosis is the one most associated with urolithiasis**.
- **Malignancy-associated hypercalcaemia**:
  - **Tumour mix**: **lung and breast ≈ 60%**; **renal cell 10–15%**; **head and neck ≈ 10%**; **haematological (lymphoma, myeloma) ≈ 10%**.
  - **Humoral factors**: **PTHrP** (production regulated by tumour-cell CaSR), **transforming growth factor**, **interleukin-1**, **tumour necrosis factor** → osteoclast activation.
  - **PTHrP mechanism**: like PTH, **↑ renal calcium reabsorption**; stimulates osteoblasts to secrete **receptor activator of nuclear factor-κB ligand (RANKL)** → binds **RANK** on osteoclast precursors → mature osteoclasts → bone resorption; also **stimulates 1α-hydroxylation → calcitriol → gut absorption**.
- **Glucocorticoids**:
  - **Most potent effect on bone** — **↑ resorption, ↓ formation → osteopenia**; **↑ PTH release**.
  - **↓ Intestinal calcium absorption** — the basis of **steroid efficacy in sarcoid hypercalciuria**.
  - **Net effect favours stones**: stones in **about half of active Cushing syndrome**, fewer when cured, fewest in controls; active disease shows **hypercalciuria, hypocitraturia and hyperuricosuria** — but **obesity and diabetes confound**.

**Concept —** *The **PTH level sorts the hypercalcaemias**: high or inappropriate in primary hyperparathyroidism, suppressed in everything else (sarcoid calcitriol, tumour PTHrP) — and corticosteroids, which cut gut calcium absorption, are the specific remedy for the calcitriol-driven (granulomatous) form.*

## ▸ The numbers to lock in

- **Hypercalciuria in 35–65% of calcium stone formers — the commonest abnormality.**
- **Definitions: > 200 mg/day on 400 mg calcium/100 mg sodium diet; > 4 mg/kg/day; > 7 mmol/day males, > 6 mmol/day females.**
- **Filtered calcium ≈ 270 mmol/day; > 98% reabsorbed; ≈ 4 mmol excreted.**
- **AH: calcium load ≥ 0.2 mg/mg creatinine; fasting < 0.11 mg/dL GF; ≈ 30% of formers.**
- **Renal hypercalciuria: fasting ≥ 0.11 mg/dL GF + high PTH + normal serum calcium.**
- **AH type II corrects with calcium restriction; type I does not.**
- **CYP24A1 carrier frequency 4–20%.**
- **Renal phosphate leak ≤ 2–4%.**
- **PHPT: cause of ≈ 5% of stones; stones in < 5% of PHPT; suspect if serum calcium > 10.1 mg/dL.**
- **Malignancy hypercalcaemia: lung + breast ≈ 60%, renal cell 10–15%.**
- **Commonest hypercalcaemia: outpatient = PHPT; inpatient = malignancy.**
- Eponyms:
  - **Pak classification (1974)** — absorptive, renal, resorptive hypercalciuria.
  - **Dent disease** — X-linked, ClC-5, proteinuria plus hypercalciuria plus renal failure.
  - **Bartter syndrome** — TAL salt wasting, hypokalaemic alkalosis; NKCC2/ROMK/ClC-Kb/barttin; type V = activating CaSR.
  - **Cushing syndrome** — stones in about half of active cases.
- Classic traps:
  - **AH: normal fasting urinary calcium, suppressed PTH** vs **renal: high fasting calcium, high PTH** — both normocalcaemic.
  - **Thiazide worsens hypercalcaemia in PHPT** — that is the diagnostic challenge, not a treatment.
  - **PHPT is common as a cause of hypercalcaemia but rare as a cause of stones.**
  - **Bartter is an alkalosis, not an acidosis.**
  - **Glucocorticoids cut gut calcium absorption yet still promote stones overall.**
  - **Sarcoid = absorptive plus resorptive, PTH suppressed, steroid-responsive.**

## ▸ Source flags

- **Source error — "idiopathic infantile hyperkalemia"**: CYP24A1 mutations cause **idiopathic infantile hypercalcaemia**. Corrected in notes.
- **Source error — Bartter syndrome "hypokalemic metabolic acidosis"**: Bartter syndrome is a **hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis** (salt wasting with secondary hyperaldosteronism). Corrected in notes.
- **Truncated gene symbol**: "KCNJ, which encodes … ROMK" — the gene is **KCNJ1**. Corrected.
- **Ambiguous expansion**: "transforming growth factor-’" — the corrupted Greek letter could be α or β; **TGF-α** is the classic humoral hypercalcaemia factor, **TGF-β** is released from resorbing bone. Notes give "transforming growth factor" without assigning the letter.
- **Symbol corruption**: "#0.2 mg" = **≥ 0.2**; "!0.11" = **< 0.11**; "#0.11" = **≥ 0.11**; "’-type intercalated cells" = **α-intercalated cells**; "Na*,K*,2Cl%" = **Na⁺-K⁺-2Cl⁻**; "nuclear factor-+B" = **nuclear factor-κB**; "1’-hydroxylase" = **1α-hydroxylase**; "25(OH) D 2 3," = **25(OH)D3**.
- **Internal inconsistency — proximal calcium reabsorption ≈ 70% here versus 60–65% in Mineral Metabolism** (flagged in Instalment 4).
- **Internal inconsistency — AH prevalence ≈ 30% (text) versus 20–40% (Table 95.2)**: consistent range; both carried.
- **PHPT share**: "≈ 5% of nephrolithiasis" (text) versus "3–5%" (Table 95.2) — compatible; both carried.
- **Coverage**: Hypercalciuria (all four subsections: Absorptive, Renal, Resorptive, Hypercalcemic-Induced with its three run-in heads — Sarcoid and Granulomatous Disease, Malignancy-Associated Hypercalcemia, Glucocorticoid-Induced Hypercalcemia) covered.
