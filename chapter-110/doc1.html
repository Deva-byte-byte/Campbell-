Chapter 110 — Overactive Bladder — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else

  • OAB (overactive bladder) = urgency ± frequency, nocturia, UUI (urgency urinary incontinence); no UTI (urinary tract infection) or obvious pathology.
  • OAB = symptom; DO (detrusor overactivity) = urodynamic finding — not interchangeable.
  • Minimum evaluation: history + examination + urinalysis.
  • No urodynamics, cystoscopy or ultrasound in uncomplicated initial work-up.
  • Chronic antimuscarinic >3 months → dementia risk ↑10–65%.
  • Preferred drug: β3 agonist, or ER trospium / darifenacin / fesoterodine.
  • DO in men with UUI >90%; absent in 30–40% of women with UUI.
  • PVR (postvoid residual) +10 mL → catheterisation +27% after onabotulinumtoxinA.
  • SNS (sacral nerve stimulation): 70–80% success at 5 y; revision >30%.
  • AUA/SUFU (American Urological Association / Society of Urodynamics, Female Pelvic Medicine & Urogenital Reconstruction) lines ≠ mandatory sequence; no treatment is acceptable.

Terminology and Definitions

  • Four symptoms: daytime frequency, nocturia, urgency, UUI.
  • Normal daytime frequency ≤7; bother from ≥8 voids (every ≤2 h).
  • Nocturia ≥1 by definition; bother ≥2, marked ≥3.
  • Nocturia: each void preceded and followed by sleep.
  • Urgency: sudden, compelling desire, difficult to defer.
  • Most bothersome LUTS (lower urinary tract symptom), both sexes: UUI.
  • MUI (mixed urinary incontinence) = SUI (stress urinary incontinence) + UUI ("OAB wet").
  • Isolated nocturia → think nocturnal polyuria or poor sleep.
  • OAB vs BPS (bladder pain syndrome): fear of leakage vs fear of pain.
  • BPS filling: steady accelerating pain; OAB: phasic urgency waves.
  • BPS: consistent volumes, can defer; suprapubic pain; no UUI.

Epidemiology and Economics

  • Prevalence: OAB ≈12–29%; UUI ≈1.5–17%.
  • Women vs men: OAB 1.5–2×; UUI 2–3×.
  • Highest: EpiLUTS (loose definition); lowest: EPIC (ICS, International Continence Society, definition).
  • NOBLE: ≥4 urgency episodes/4 wk + ≥8 voids/day or coping.
  • NOBLE: OAB similar by sex; UUI ≈3× in women.
  • Age rise: women 40s, men 50s–60s; UUI after 44 (F) / 64 (M).
  • Slightly highest by race: Black men and women.
  • Incidence 3–8%/y (women); progression to UUI ≈1%/y; remission ≈6%/y.
  • <6% take medication; ≈92% fail anticholinergic by 2 y.
  • Half never refill first prescription; most stop by 6 months.
  • Stop reasons: unmet expectations > side effects > cost.
  • Most cost-effective third-line: onabotulinumtoxinA 100 U; costliest: SNS.

Pathophysiology and Etiology

  • DO in patients with urgency: 44–69%; urgency in DO ≈half.
  • No animal model of OAB — urgency cannot be reported.
  • Main regulatory level: periaqueductal grey + PMC (pontine micturition centre).
  • Aδ fibres = normal volume sensing; C fibres = "silent", recruited in disease.
  • Two bladder-centric theories: urothelium-based; myogenic (patchy denervation, micromotions).
  • Micromotions coordinated across bladder → DO.
  • Bowel crosstalk: rectal distension alters capacity, sensation, DO.
  • BOO (bladder outlet obstruction): indirect, age-accelerating; not direct cause.
  • Anxiety → heat-pain temporal summation ↑ (central sensitisation).

Clinical Assessment

  • Faecal incontinence: 7–15% general vs ≈50% of women with OAB.
  • Antimuscarinic relative CI: closed-angle glaucoma (uncontrolled), cognitive impairment, retention, impaired gastric emptying.
  • β3 agonist relative CI: uncontrolled hypertension, arrhythmia.
  • Most important examination: pelvic/genital ± DRE (digital rectal examination).
  • Women: check prolapse, atrophic vaginitis, caruncle, labial adhesions.
  • All: perineal sensation, anal tone, cognition (MMSE, Mini-Mental State Examination), frailty (TUGT, Timed Up and Go Test, 10 ft).
  • PVR not needed before first/second line in uncomplicated patients.
  • PVR needed: men with voiding LUTS, retention risk, prior outlet/sling surgery, neurological disease.
  • PVR method: bladder scanner first; catheter if unavailable.
  • Urinalysis mandatory; culture only if suspected; creatinine and PSA (prostate-specific antigen) not routine.
  • Diary: 3–7 days ideal; minimum 24–48 h.
  • Max voided volume = functional capacity.
  • Frequency count includes last void before sleep, first on rising.
  • Urgency scale: 0 social · 1 normal · 2 passed · 3 no leak · 4 leak.
  • AUA PROMs (patient-reported outcome measures): UDI (Urogenital Distress Inventory) / UDI-6, IIQ (Incontinence Impact Questionnaire), OAB-q.
  • OAB-V8 = screening; QUID = incontinence type; PRAFAB = treatment effect.
  • LURN SI = both sexes, 29 or 10 items.
  • Urodynamics if: changes invasive/irreversible decision; multiple failures; BOO after outlet surgery.
  • Not if: index patient (woman, low PVR, no prolapse, no neuro, minimal SUI).
  • OAB dry DO: men 60–70%, women 40–50%.
  • DO correlation: best UUI, worst nocturia.
  • No DO → usually increased bladder sensation.
  • Fast filling induces DO; supine second fill reduces DO to unmask SUI.
  • DO persists after TURP (transurethral resection of prostate) in up to 60%.

Management

  • First line: education + behavioural; placebo response large.
  • Fluid ↓25% meaningfully reduces symptoms.
  • Caffeine ↓: helps urgency/frequency, not reliably UUI.
  • Bariatric surgery: each 5% weight loss → 7–8% ↓ incontinence risk.
  • PFMT (pelvic floor muscle therapy)/bladder training: 50–80% fewer incontinence episodes.
  • Antimuscarinic vs placebo: cure likelihood ↑20–70%; high withdrawal.
  • Extended-release (ER) over immediate-release antimuscarinics.
  • Men, voiding LUTS: α1-blocker first-line; add antimuscarinic/β3 for storage.
  • Antimuscarinic ± α1-blocker: retention up to 3% → caution if PVR raised.
  • Minimum trial: 4–12 weeks (behavioural and drug).
  • Third line: PTNS (percutaneous tibial nerve stimulation), onabotulinumtoxinA, SNS.
  • PTNS: 60–80% response; weekly ×12 wk; response wk 8–10.
  • OnabotulinumtoxinA: UUI ↓59%; ≈25% dry; UTI ≈20%.
  • OnabotulinumtoxinA CISC (clean intermittent self-catheterisation): 0–12% trials; ≈20% women, ≤35% men real world.
  • SNS: all eventually need battery change.
  • Frailty: 1-year morbidity up to 50% after minor procedures.
  • Operate (augmentation/diversion) only if refractory to all; weigh vs pads/catheter.
  • Aggressive treatment justified: incontinence-associated dermatitis.

Classic traps

  • Trap: SUI + OAB dry = MUI — Actually: MUI needs UUI.
  • Trap: urinary + faecal = mixed — Actually: double incontinence.
  • Trap: OAB requires DO — Actually: symptom diagnosis; DO often absent.
  • Trap: UUI in women predicts DO — Actually: absent in 30–40%.
  • Trap: BOO causes OAB — Actually: indirect; DO persists after TURP.
  • Trap: urodynamics before first treatment — Actually: not in uncomplicated.
  • Trap: PVR before oral drugs always — Actually: only if complicated.
  • Trap: lines are sequential — Actually: combine or skip per goals.
  • Trap: onabotulinumtoxinA retention 0–12% — Actually: real world 20–35%.
  • Trap: C fibres large-calibre — Actually: smallest, unmyelinated.
