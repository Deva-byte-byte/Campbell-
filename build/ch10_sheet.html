Chapter 10 — Ethics and Informed Consent — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else
  • Four principles (Beauchamp and Childress 1979): autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence, justice
  • Principles equal in weight, each prima facie binding
  • Salgo 1957: coined "informed consent"
  • Canterbury v Spence 1972: reasonable-patient disclosure standard
  • Schloendorff 1914: examination under anaesthesia (EUA) ≠ consent to surgery
  • Consent elements: problem + options (incl. none) → risks/benefits/uncertainty → check understanding → consent
  • Commonest missed element: assessing patient understanding
  • Material risk: proposed ≥1% cut-off, rarer if severe
  • Conscious competent patient may refuse life-saving care (Quackenbush 1978)
  • Surrogate standard: substituted judgement → then best interests

Medical ethics

  • Hippocratic Oath: beneficence, non-maleficence, confidentiality, accountability
  • Oath's urological line: "I will not cut persons labouring under the stone"
  • Oath's gaps (Veatch): no autonomy, no justice
  • "Bioethics" coined: Van Rensselaer Potter, 1970
  • Tuskegee disclosed: early 1970s → research consent
  • Autonomy: Kantian; grounds consent, confidentiality, no deceit; bounded by justice
  • Non-maleficence: primum non nocere
  • Justice ≠ equality: equal treatment can still be unjust
  • Four-Box (Jonsen): medical indications → beneficence/non-maleficence; preferences → autonomy; quality of life → three principles; context → justice
  • Equivocal risk–benefit → defer to patient preference
  • Quality of life: most ephemeral box; beware provider bias

Informed consent

  • Legal root: battery (1905–14) → negligence (Kansas 1960)
  • Mohr v Williams 1905: consultation ≠ consent
  • Luka v Lowrie 1912: implied consent only in emergency, incapable patient
  • Negligence test: below reasonable-physician standard + proximate cause
  • Discuss options before recommending → less undue influence
  • Disclosing uncertainty → better understanding and trust
  • Understanding biases: shortcuts, optimism bias, framing
  • Emergency: consent presumed (Barnett v Bachrach 1943, unconscious patient)
  • Children: no waiver unless immediate harm (Rogers v Sells 1936)
  • Minors <18 y: parents consent; child participates/assents
  • Surrogate failing patient's wishes → ethics board → court conservator
  • Consent forms: often >12th-grade reading level
  • Most effective comprehension tool: repeat-back (time-costly)
  • Decision aids: ↑ knowledge, ↓ decisional conflict
  • Simplified forms alone: mixed results
  • Shared decision making (SDM): ~40 models; only bidirectional model
  • Models: paternalistic (info + recommendation) vs informed (info only) vs shared (+ patient values)

End of life and research

  • Medicare: 16% of annual budget in last 2 months of life
  • Uniform Health-Care Decisions Act (2006): futility judged by providers
  • Patient Self-Determination Act (in force 1991): right to advance directive
  • Advance preferences → DNR (do-not-resuscitate) order: <half
  • Research consent: 45 CFR part 46 — "legally effective informed consent"
  • Waiver: unlinked data, secondary data, or IRB (institutional review board): ≤ minimal risk + impracticable
  • >Minimal risk → disclose compensation and injury treatment
  • Vulnerable groups (Subparts B–D): children, pregnant women/fetuses, disabled, prisoners
Classic traps
  • Trap: Canterbury coined "informed consent" — Actually: Salgo 1957
  • Trap: reasonable-physician disclosure — Actually: reasonable patient (Canterbury)
  • Trap: autonomy trumps all — Actually: equal weight; bounded by justice
  • Trap: justice = equality — Actually: equal treatment may be unjust
  • Trap: emergency overrides conscious refusal — Actually: conscious patient may refuse
  • Trap: family decides by own values — Actually: substituted judgement first
  • Trap: reading the form ensures understanding — Actually: no better comprehension
  • Trap: disclosing uncertainty erodes trust — Actually: increases trust
  • Trap: consenting to EUA covers surgery — Actually: no (Schloendorff)
  • Trap: quality-of-life box = autonomy only — Actually: autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence
