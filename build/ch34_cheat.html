Chapter 34 — Perinatal Urology — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else
  • Urinary tract dilation (UTD): 1–3% of pregnancies; second commonest prenatal anomaly.
  • Normal anterior–posterior diameter (APD): <4 mm (16–27 wk), <7 mm (≥28 wk), <10 mm postnatal.
  • First postnatal US after 48 h, once third-spaced fluid is mobilised.
  • P1 (low risk): APD 10–<15 mm → no antibiotics, no voiding cystourethrogram (VCUG); repeat US at 3–6 mo.
  • P2 (intermediate): prophylaxis if ureter >7 mm; otherwise at discretion.
  • P3 (high): prophylaxis + VCUG + MAG3 (mercaptoacetyltriglycine) renogram; repeat US at 1 mo.
  • Third-trimester APD ≥15 mm predicts UPJ (ureteropelvic junction) surgery.
  • MAG3 surgery triggers: differential function <40%, T½ >20 min, retention on upright images.
  • Fetal LUTO intervention: better survival (lungs), not long-term renal function.
  • Circumcision: NNT (number needed to treat) 10 to prevent one UTI in prenatal hydronephrosis.

Prenatal imaging

  • Screening US: 18–20 wk (ACOG, American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists); ≈20 wk in US practice.
  • Scans per pregnancy: low-risk ≈2; high-risk ≈4.
  • Urine production from 14 wk; amniotic fluid = fetal urine from 16 wk.
  • Amniotic fluid index (AFI): 4 pockets; normal 8–18; oligohydramnios <5–6.
  • Fetal MRI: for anatomy only — cloaca, urogenital sinus, exstrophy.

Grading systems

  • APD measured: transverse mid-kidney at hilum; sagittal = classic error.
  • Prenatal APD severe: >10 mm (2nd trimester); >15 mm (3rd).
  • Society for Fetal Urology (SFU): grades 0–4, postnatal only; SFU 4 = parenchymal thinning.
  • SFU grades: 1 splits sinus; 2 major calyces; 3 minor calyces.
  • SFU 3 → function preserved; SFU 4 → early surgery.
  • UTD system: 6 parameters; APD + SFU + ureter + bladder.
  • Pyramidal thickness ≤3 mm → predicts pyeloplasty, reduced function.
  • Grade vs disease: UPJ obstruction and PUV (posterior urethral valves) rise with grade; VUR (vesicoureteral reflux) does not.

Consultations / myelomeningocele

  • LUTO (lower urinary tract obstruction) fetal intervention: >40% maternal–fetal complications.
  • Fetal myelomeningocele closure (<26 wk): ↓ VP (ventriculoperitoneal) shunt, ↑ motor; no bladder benefit.

Prenatal UTD and fetal intervention

  • A1 (low): 4–<7 mm (16–27 wk) or 7–<10 mm (≥28 wk).
  • A2–3 (increased): ≥7 / ≥10 mm or any abnormal parameter.
  • Follow-up: A1 → repeat after 32 wk; A2–3 → US every 4 wk.
  • Oligohydramnios <27 wk → poor outcome (pulmonary hypoplasia).
  • Poor prognosis: cortical cysts, echogenic kidneys, unfavourable urine.
  • Intervention window: 18–24 wk; need ≈1 in 60,000.
  • Fetal urine markers: Na, Cl, osmolality, β2-microglobulin — unvalidated.
  • ERKNet 2022: fluid, echogenicity, urine biomarkers don't predict renal function.
  • Ruano stage II (preserved function) = the stage to intervene.
  • PLUTO (Percutaneous shunting in LUTO) trial: shunt ↑ survival; no renal benefit; 44% complications; stopped early.
  • Fetal cystoscopy: diagnoses + treats; only it improved renal function in PUV.
  • Cystoscopy fistula (urethrocutaneous/rectourethral): up to 10%.
  • Rodeck shunt: lies flush → less migration than Harrison.
  • Bilateral renal agenesis: serial amnioinfusion (RAFT trial).

Postnatal evaluation

  • P2/P3 APD: ≥15 mm; P2 = peripheral calyces/ureter; P3 = parenchyma/bladder.
  • Resolution: SFU 1 → 100% by ≈2.5 yr; SFU 2 → 95% by 4 yr.
  • VUR: up to 31% of prenatal UTD; 25% have normal first US.
  • VCUG as inpatient if bladder wall ≥0.5 cm, distended bladder, bilateral hydroureteronephrosis.
  • VCUG technique (AAP): non-balloon catheter, bladder cycling, ALARA.
  • MAG3 vs DTPA: MAG3 is gold standard; ≈50% tubular extraction per pass.
  • DMSA (dimercaptosuccinic acid): cortical defects, MCDK (multicystic dysplastic kidney) non-function, duplex polar function.
  • CeVUS (contrast voiding urosonography): no radiation, sensitive for VUR; poor for urethra.

Management by risk / UTI

  • UTI incidence: 8–22%; ≈90% occur in first year.
  • UTI risk factors: female, uncircumcised, high-grade dilation (± ureter, VUR).
  • P3 prophylaxis duration: until UPJ surgery or 1 year.
  • Antibiotics from birth: amoxicillin, first-generation cephalosporin.
  • Nitrofurantoin/trimethoprim: only from ≥8 wk.
  • Bilateral dilation + oligohydramnios → decompress, prophylaxis, evaluate.

Key conditions

  • Commonest cause: transient UTD 50–70%; UPJ 10–30%; VUR 10–40%.
  • LUTO share: 0.1–0.5% of prenatal UTD.
  • Commonest surgical cause: UPJ obstruction.
  • APD 15 mm: sensitivity ≈0.8, specificity ≈0.8; postnatal APD predicts better.
  • Likely DRF >40%: P2, APD <20 mm, pyramidal thickness >3 mm.
  • Observed UPJ losing function: APD 20–40 mm (Great Ormond Street trial).
  • Operate UPJ if: rising APD, DRF <40% + delayed drainage, falling DRF, stone/infection.
  • Prenatal VUR: male, bilateral, high grade, dysplasia (up to 40%).
  • Screen VUR: girls with high-grade UTD, uncircumcised boys; not circumcised low-grade.
  • Megaureter surgery risk: ureter >≈13 mm, DRF <30%.
  • BAPU megaureter: prenatal ureter >7 mm after 30 wk → postnatal RUS, prophylaxis, VCUG.
  • BAPU surgery: DRF <40%, febrile UTI, pain.
  • Megaureter follow-up: into adulthood (late silent deterioration).
  • Megaureter under 1 yr: temporise; definitive reimplant after 1 yr.

LUTO

  • PUV: 1.4–2.2 per 10,000; commonest LUTO.
  • PUV neonatal drainage: 5 Fr feeding tube; confirm on KUB (kidney–ureter–bladder) film.
  • Avoid balloon catheter (posterior urethral obstruction).
  • Prune-belly syndrome (PBS): absent abdominal muscles + impalpable testes + megacystis.
  • Keyhole sign + pop-offs (ascites, urinoma) → PUV/urethral atresia.
  • Urethral atresia: catheter won't pass → vesicostomy → PADUA.
  • No oligohydramnios → megacystis, bilateral VUR, MMIHS.
  • MMIHS (megacystis–microcolon): non-obstructed; prophylaxis only if recurrent UTI.
  • Post-decompression anuria (thick bladder) → antimuscarinics.
  • Renal insufficiency → no immediate VCUG.

Kidney anomalies

  • MCDK: VUR ≈20%; no Wilms risk; no prophylaxis.
  • Solitary kidney: check BP, proteinuria, yearly US to puberty.
  • Remove MCDK only if persistent with solid component.
  • Meyer–Weigert: upper pole → obstructs/ectopic; lower pole → refluxes.
  • Suspect duplex: ureter more dilated than pelvis.
  • Ureterocele + high-grade VUR → ureterocele excision in 96%.
  • Ureterocele on US: thin intravesical cyst; ectopic ureter shows two walls.
  • No VUR → upper-pole surgery alone suffices.

Exstrophy / cloaca / CAH

  • Bladder exstrophy: detected prenatally ≈50%; not an emergency.
  • Cloacal exstrophy (OEIS): major criteria + omphalocele + lumbosacral anomaly.
  • Persistent cloaca: 1 in 50,000; females; calcified meconium.
  • Congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH): 21-hydroxylase gene on chromosome 6p.
  • CAH frequency: 1 in 15,000–16,000; salt-wasting 75%.
  • CAH carrier pregnancies: 1 in 8 affected with ambiguity.
  • Cloaca + polyhydramnios → suspect tracheo-oesophageal fistula.
  • Normal genitalia + trapped vaginal urine → urogenital sinus, not CAH.

Perinatal emergencies

  • Urinary ascites: up to 30% of neonatal ascites; commonest cause PUV.
  • Seldinger drainage: 16G → 3.5 Fr; 14G → 5 Fr feeding tube.
  • Solid tumour <6 mo: mesoblastic nephroma (↑BP, polyhydramnios).
  • Adrenal haemorrhage: right side; 10–15% bilateral; no Doppler flow; eggshell calcification.
  • Ovarian cysts: up to 30% of girls; resolve by 6 mo.
  • Hydrometrocolpos: can mimic distended bladder.
  • Urethral prolapse: midline collar; Black girls; conservative care.
  • Anorectal malformation: genitourinary anomalies up to 75%.
  • Anorectal malformation work-up: spinal US; videourodynamics at 6 wk; prophylaxis.
  • Commonest cause of ambiguous genitalia: CAH (46,XX DSD).
  • Ambiguous genitalia: check electrolytes first; defer gender assignment.
  • First void: within 24 h; after circumcision within 8 h.
  • Renal vein thrombosis (RVT): up to 20% of gross haematuria.
  • RVT triad: haematuria + flank mass + thrombocytopenia.
  • RVT prothrombotic disorder: up to 50%.
  • Renal artery thrombosis: umbilical artery catheter → hypertension; remove catheter.
  • Cortical necrosis: lost corticomedullary differentiation; lifelong renal-failure risk.
  • Torsion salvage: postnatal ≤44%; prenatal ≈0.
  • Prenatal torsion → urgent contralateral orchidopexy.
Classic traps
  • Trap: APD 10 mm always severe — Actually: severe in 2nd trimester, moderate in 3rd.
  • Trap: dilation grade predicts VUR — Actually: no trend; VUR at any grade.
  • Trap: US at 24 h — Actually: wait >48 h; earlier scan under-reads dilation.
  • Trap: APD ≥15 mm = P3 — Actually: parenchymal/bladder abnormality defines P3.
  • Trap: fetal shunt saves kidneys — Actually: saves lungs/survival; renal outcome unchanged.
  • Trap: MCDK needs nephrectomy for Wilms risk — Actually: no Wilms link; observe.
  • Trap: balloon catheter for PUV — Actually: 5 Fr feeding tube, KUB-confirmed.
  • Trap: fetal myelomeningocele closure improves bladder — Actually: urodynamics and CIC need unchanged.
  • Trap: nitrofurantoin/trimethoprim in newborn — Actually: amoxicillin or cephalosporin until 8 wk.
  • Trap: bloody diaper = haematuria — Actually: confirm on urinalysis; oestrogen withdrawal, urates.
