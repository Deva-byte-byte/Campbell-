## Instalment 1 — Triage Framing, Table 35.1 Acuity Tiers, Acute Scrotum (Appendage Torsion, Cord Torsion, TWIST, Neonatal Torsion), Ambiguous Genitalia/CAH, Bladder–Cloacal Exstrophy, Obstructive Uropathy With UTI/AKI, Renal Vein Thrombosis, Paraphimosis, Priapism (Ischaemic, Nonischaemic, Neonatal), Trauma (Renal, Straddle Female/Male, Blunt Penile, Fig 35.1), Acute Urinary Retention, Newborn Hydronephrosis, Gross Haematuria, Renal Tumour, Testicular Tumour, Primary Amenorrhoea, Interlabial Mass, Hernia/Hydrocele, Undescended Testicle, Varicocele
(PDF pp 41–46, book 658–663)

### The numbers to lock in
**Key facts, figures, thresholds, cut-offs**
- Acuity tiers: emergent; urgent within 24 h; semiurgent within 1–3 weeks; routine.
- Cord torsion ≈1 in 4000 males; majority after age 10.
- TWIST (max 7): swelling 2, hard testis 2, absent cremasteric reflex 1, nausea/vomiting 1, high-riding testis 1. 0 = low (no US); 1–5 = intermediate (US); ≥6 = high (explore without US).
- CAH: 21-hydroxylase ≈95%; salt-wasting crisis day 4–15; tests 17-hydroxyprogesterone + electrolytes.
- Bladder exstrophy ≈2.15/100,000 live births (USA); tie cord with 2-0 silk.
- RVT ≈2.6/100,000 live births; two-thirds present within 3 days; male and left predominance; triad haematuria + flank mass + thrombocytopenia.
- Paraphimosis adjuncts: ice 5 min, Coban 15–30 min, granulated sugar 1–2 h.
- Priapism ≥4 h; ischaemic corporal gas pO₂ <40 mm Hg, hypercapnia, acidosis, glucopenia; neonatal priapism average 4–5 days, monitor to 5 days.
- AUA 2020 imaging in stable blunt trauma: haematuria (gross or micro) + SBP <90 mm Hg, or concerning mechanism/examination.
- Straddle: sexual-abuse work-up if <9 months, perianal/hymenal/vaginal injury, severe injury, other injuries, history mismatch; abuse 1 in 7 males, 1 in 3 females.
- Female straddle ≈80% of female genital trauma; urethral injury ≈5% of female pelvic fractures.
- Toilet-seat crush mean age ≈3.9 years; nearly all at home.
- Retention incidence 8.5–30/100,000 ED visits; bladder capacity (mL) = (age + 2) × 30.
- Prenatal HN: third-trimester APD >7 mm or UTD A2–3 in solitary/bilateral/outlet obstruction → pre-discharge US; ideally after 48 h; creatinine maternal for 48 h; UTD P2–P3 bilateral = moderate–severe.
- Wilms: peak 1–5 years; ≈10% syndromic; commonest renal cancer <15 years.
- Testis tumours ≈1% of paediatric solid tumours (0.5–2/100,000); peaks <3 years and postpubertal.
- Primary amenorrhoea: no menses by 16 (normal development) or 14 (no secondary sexual characteristics).
- Varicocele ≈15% of adolescents; Lambert volume L × W × H × 0.71.

**Gold standards, commonest, first-line**
- Commonest acute scrotum: testicular appendage torsion (prepubertal; supportive care).
- Commonest cause of newborn ambiguous genitalia: CAH (21-hydroxylase).
- Commonest acute infected obstruction: obstructing urolithiasis → stent or nephrostomy.
- RVT diagnosis: Doppler US; no evidence-based treatment guidelines.
- Commonest paediatric priapism: ischaemic; commonest cause sickle cell disease.
- Kidney = commonest injured GU organ; blunt trauma commonest mechanism; CT preferred, US acceptable; nonoperative management ideal.
- Suspected male urethral injury: no catheter → RUG; cannot drain → suprapubic tube.
- Female urethral injury: EUA + cystoscopy; SPC + delayed repair (or early realignment if stable).
- Testis tumour markers: AFP, β-hCG, LDH; prepubertal commonest malignancy yolk sac tumour.
- Hernia/communicating hydrocele: high ligation of processus vaginalis at internal ring.

**Eponyms**
- TWIST score — torsion risk score, 0/1–5/≥6 bands.
- Henoch–Schönlein purpura — listed cause of ischaemic priapism.
- Wilms tumour — commonest paediatric renal malignancy.
- Lambert formula — testis volume, constant 0.71.
- Skene, Bartholin, Gartner duct cysts — neonatal interlabial cystic masses.

**Landmark trials** — none in this stretch.
**Dates and legislation** — AUA urotrauma guideline 2020.

**Absolute contraindications**
- Imaging that delays exploration when torsion is clinically suspected.
- Blind urethral catheterisation when urethral injury is suspected (RUG first).
- Early discharge of a newborn with suspected salt-wasting CAH.

**Relative contraindications**
- Cold packs in priapism when sickle cell disease is suspected (can induce priapism).
- Nephrostomy in a small child where an alternative drainage exists (morbidity, quality of life).
- Ultrasound to locate undescended testis (poor sensitivity/specificity; not recommended).

**When to operate / when not**
- Operate: suspected cord torsion (immediate, with contralateral orchiopexy); TWIST ≥6; failed manual reduction of paraphimosis (dorsal slit/circumcision); testicular rupture; hernia on examination or compelling history; resolved incarceration or bowel-containing hernia (timely); high-grade obstruction bilaterally/solitary kidney (diversion; early pyeloplasty in selected cases).
- Do not operate: appendage torsion; most renal trauma; most female straddle injuries without pelvic fracture; RVT (surgery rarely warranted); neonatal priapism (monitor); nonischaemic priapism initially.

**Classic traps**
- Present cremasteric reflex does not exclude torsion.
- Manual detorsion does not obviate exploration.
- Newborn screen may return after discharge and covers only 21-hydroxylase.
- Children compensate for shock; hypotension is late.
- Haematuria, tachycardia and hypotension are unreliable for paediatric renal injury — mechanism predicts.
- Swollen uncircumcised penis mistaken for circumcised → missed paraphimosis; hair tourniquet as differential.
- US <48 h underestimates hydronephrosis — except scan immediately if outlet obstruction suspected.
- Voiding does not equal normal bladder emptying.

## Instalment 2 — Phimosis, Meatal Stenosis, Labial Adhesions (Fig 35.4), Bladder Bowel Dysfunction, Microscopic Haematuria, Key Points (Triage), History, Paediatric Vital Signs (Table 35.3), BMI, Abdominal Examination, Male Genital Examination (Tables 35.4, 35.5), Undescended Testis Technique, Female Genital Examination, Neurologic Examination (Fig 35.5), Urine Studies — UTI Diagnosis, Microhaematuria, Hypercalciuria, Proteinuria (opening)
(PDF pp 47–51, book 664–668; 3 numbered tables)

### The numbers to lock in
**Key facts, figures, thresholds, cut-offs**
- Physiological phimosis ≈1% at 17 years; smegma in ≈5% of healthy children.
- Meatal stenosis: meatus <5 Fr.
- Labial adhesions peak 13–23 months; recurrence 7–55%.
- Microscopic haematuria: >5 RBC/hpf on centrifuged urine on more than two occasions (adult ≥3).
- RR should not stay >60/min; HR ≈ 2–3 × RR; SBP lower limit ≥60 neonate, ≥70 1 month–1 year, ≥70 + 2×age 1–10 years, ≥90 >10 years.
- Cuff ≈two-thirds of arm.
- BMI percentiles ≥2 years (weight-for-length <2 years): underweight <5th, overweight 85th–95th, obese ≥95th.
- Table 35.3 ranges (see notes); Table 35.5: term newborn −2.5 SD ≈1.9 cm; adult mean 13.3, −2.5 SD 9.3 cm.
- Simple dimple: within gluteal cleft, <5 mm, base visible; Fig 35.5 simple dimple <25 mm above anus. Coccygeal tag >5 mm → image.
- AAP 2011: pyuria and/or bacteriuria + ≥50,000 CFU/mL (catheter/SPA); catheter/SPA for febrile infants <24 months.
- Screening microhaematuria 0.5–3%; repeat over 2–3 weeks.
- Urine Ca/Cr abnormal >0.4 (2–5 years), >0.2 (>5 years).

**Gold standards, commonest, first-line**
- Commonest interlabial abnormality: labial adhesions; asymptomatic → observe.
- Symptomatic meatal stenosis → meatotomy.
- Circumcision declined in at-risk phimosis → topical steroids.
- Frog-leg/butterfly position for prepubertal genital examination.
- Commonest causes of isolated microhaematuria: thin basement membrane disease, IgA nephropathy, hypercalciuria.
- Hypercalciuria: fluids + modest sodium restriction; renal US + urology referral.

**Eponyms**
- Tanner staging — five stages; boys genitalia + pubic hair, girls breast + pubic hair.
- Schonfeld and Beebe norms (1942) — basis of micropenis diagnosis.
- Henoch–Schönlein — (see Instalment 1).
- Valsalva manoeuvre — elicits hernia/varicocele.

**Landmark trials** — none. **Guideline bodies**: AAP 2011 UTI guideline (under revision).
**Dates** — penile norms since the 1940s; AAP 2011.

**Absolute contraindications**
- Forcible retraction of a physiologically phimotic foreskin.
- Bag urine specimen to diagnose UTI in a febrile infant <24 months.

**Relative contraindications**
- Routine MRI/neurosurgical referral for a simple sacral dimple.
- Lateral spreading of the labia during examination (causes tension; pull straight out instead).

**When to operate / when not**
- Operate/treat: symptomatic meatal stenosis (meatotomy); pathological phimosis/BXO; symptomatic labial adhesions (topical or manual lysis).
- Do not: asymptomatic labial adhesions; physiological phimosis (except UTI-risk infants); BBD (surgery rarely).

**Classic traps**
- Dipstick blood must be confirmed by microscopy (haemoglobinuria, myoglobinuria cause false positives); exclude UTI first.
- Too small a cuff → falsely high BP; too large → falsely low.
- Shock presents as tachypnoea/tachycardia; hypotension = advanced.
- Tanner II in boys = testicular/scrotal enlargement, not hair.
- Penile norms ignore ethnicity and gestational age.
- Post-void dribbling on standing in girls = vaginal voiding (labial adhesions).
- Sacral dimples are over-evaluated and over-referred.

## Instalment 3 — Proteinuria (completion), Specific Gravity, Complete Blood Count, Serum Creatinine and Cystatin C, Urologic Imaging and ALARA, Table 35.7, Key Points (Evaluation), Multidisciplinary Care, Special Considerations for Child Comfort (Distraction, Sedation, Topical Anaesthetics)
(PDF pp 52–54, book 669–671; 1 numbered table)

### The numbers to lock in
**Key facts, figures, thresholds, cut-offs**
- Protein/creatinine normal <0.7 first year, <0.4 up to 4 years, <0.2 after 5 years; >3.0 nephrotic.
- Persistent non-orthostatic proteinuria on two early-morning samples → nephrology.
- Specific gravity range 1.002–≈1.040; <1.010 dilute (excess water, diabetes insipidus); 1.010–1.020 usual; >1.020 dehydration.
- CKD anaemia once GFR <43 mL/min/1.73 m².
- EMLA 30–45 min before office procedures; LMX = lidocaine 4%; EMLA = lidocaine 2.5% + prilocaine 2.5%.

**Gold standards, commonest, first-line**
- First-morning sample for proteinuria (orthostatic common); confirm with random protein/creatinine.
- Ideal GFR marker: filtered, neither secreted nor reabsorbed.
- ALARA for all paediatric imaging.
- MAG-3 for obstruction + differential function; DMSA for scarring + differential function; CT for severe blunt abdominal trauma; ultrasound for torsion; VCUG for VUR.
- Midazolam before VCUG: less recall/anxiety, results unaffected.

**Eponyms** — none. **Landmark trials** — none. **Dates** — none examinable.

**Absolute contraindications** — none stated.
**Relative contraindications**
- Routine proteinuria screening in healthy children (AAP does not recommend).
- Awake urethral catheterisation, flexible cystoscopy or priapism treatment in young children without sedation/distraction.

**When to operate / when not**
- Avoid general anaesthesia for minor office procedures where topical anaesthesia (EMLA/LMX) ± sedation suffices.

**Classic traps**
- Haemoglobin may be normal initially after acute bleeding, then fall precipitously; neonates tolerate hypovolaemia poorly.
- Many RBCs falsely raise dipstick protein.
- Both nuclear scans give differential function — MAG-3 answers obstruction, DMSA answers scarring.
- Only ultrasound and MRI avoid ionising radiation; MRI needs sedation.
- Lidocaine gel for catheterisation: mixed evidence.
