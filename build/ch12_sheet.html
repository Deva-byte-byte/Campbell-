Chapter 12 — Fundamentals of Intraoperative Patient Safety — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else
  • Universal Protocol (Joint Commission 2004): verification → site marking → time-out
  • WHO (World Health Organization) checklist 2008: 19 items; death ↓47%, complications ↓36%
  • Count discrepancy → ~100× odds of retained surgical item (RSI)
  • Commonest RSI: sponge (~⅔); then drains (Penrose); then needles
  • RSI definition: found after skin closure, even still anaesthetised
  • Mark correct site only; never "No"/"X"
  • Fire triangle: oxidiser (anaesthesia), fuel (nursing), ignition (surgeon)
  • Airway surgery: O₂ ≤30%, stop nitrous oxide (N₂O), 3–5 min before diathermy
  • Alcohol prep: dry ≥3 min; pooling ↑ fire risk
  • Communication failure: up to 70% of OR (operating room) adverse events

Background and checklists

  • To Err Is Human (IOM, Institute of Medicine, 1999): 44,000–98,000 deaths/year
  • 2016 estimate: medical error third leading US cause of death
  • Adverse events: ~50% surgical; ~50% of those preventable
  • Malpractice errors: ~75% intraoperative
  • Pronovost 2006: central-line checklist, 108 intensive care units → bloodstream infection ↓66%
  • WHO pilot: 8 institutions, ~4000 operations
  • WHO phases: sign in (before induction) → time out (before incision) → sign out (before leaving)
  • Sign-in blood-loss flag: >500 mL (7 mL/kg children)
  • Time-out antibiotic check: given within last 60 min

Wrong-site surgery prevention

  • Incidence: ~1 in 112,000 — sentinel never event
  • Definition: wrong side, wrong patient, wrong part
  • Joint Commission: founded 1951; accredits >20,000 organisations
  • Up to 30% of root causes: before the day of surgery
  • Identifiers: full name, date of birth, record number — barcoded band
  • Consent: list contingent procedures
  • Interpreters: professional, never family
  • Marker: present at time-out; patient awake
  • Lower-tract endourology: mark wristband, not meatus
  • Repeat time-out: surgeon change or new procedure
  • Time-out errors: music on, member busy/on break, "we all know the patient"

Systems failures

  • Swiss cheese model: harm when holes align; stopped = near miss
  • Latent (organisation, supervision, preconditions) vs active (unsafe acts) failures
  • Wrong-site risk: emergency, deformity/obesity, time pressure, multiple surgeons/procedures
  • Checklists in urgent cases: ↓ complications and mortality; no start delay
  • Root causes: hierarchy, staffing shortage, temporary staff, pandemics

Retained surgical items

  • Incidence: 1 in 10,000 to 1 in 2800
  • Risk: emergency, change of plan, obesity, multiple teams, open cavity, many items, wrong count, long case, attending absent at closure
  • Trainee present: lower risk
  • Human factors (leadership, communication): dominant cause
  • Count standard: AORN (Association of periOperative Registered Nurses)
  • Discrepancy before closure → delay closure, find item
  • Routine imaging: most RSIs found had correct counts
  • Barcoded sponges count; RF (radiofrequency) tags detect — accurate even in obesity

Sharps safety

  • Universal Precautions: late 1980s, after human immunodeficiency virus epidemic
  • Blood contact: up to half of operations
  • Surgeons + first assistants: ~60% of injuries; ≤24% by colleague
  • ⅓ of injuring sharps recontact the patient
  • Single-glove puncture: up to 61%, ~40 min in
  • Double gloving: blood on solid needle ↓95%
  • Blunt needles: fascia and muscle closure
  • Designated sharps zone: ACS (American College of Surgeons) best practice; no hand-to-hand

Fire safety

  • US incidence: ~600/year
  • Site: 85% head, neck, upper chest; 81% under monitored anaesthesia care
  • Oxidiser: O₂ in 95%; N₂O equals O₂ as oxidiser
  • Ignition: monopolar "Bovie" ~90%; bipolar lowers, not abolishes
  • ChloraPrep: ~70% isopropyl alcohol; povidone-iodine/aqueous chlorhexidine non-flammable
  • Laser: low risk under irrigation; cracked fibre → fire
  • Silverstein score (1 point each): open O₂, ignition source, above xiphoid → 1 low, 2 intermediate, 3 high
  • Most urology: low/intermediate
  • Sequence: pause → saline/water → drapes to floor → extinguisher → RACE
  • RACE: Rescue, Alert, Confine (gases off, doors shut), Extinguish/evacuate

Distractions and communication

  • Sterile cockpit: silence in pre-defined critical steps (e.g. hilar clamping)
  • ACS smartphones: silence, no personal calls, redirect, distinct urgent ringtone
  • Alarm fatigue → individualise alarm settings
  • Communication model: sender, receiver, message, channel; encode/decode; noise
  • OR communication failure: ~30% of exchanges; ⅓ create risk
  • Communication risk: cognitive load, bias, hierarchy, diffusion of responsibility, handoffs
  • Situational awareness: where from, where now, where going
  • TeamSTEPPS: AHRQ (Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality) + Department of Defense, 2006
  • Five principles: structure, communication, leadership, situation monitoring, mutual support
  • SBAR: situation, background, assessment, recommendation
  • Leadership tools: brief, huddle, debrief
  • STEP: status of patient, team, environment, progress
  • I'M SAFE: illness, medication, stress, alcohol/drugs, fatigue, eating/elimination
  • Two-challenge rule: voice concern at least twice
  • CUS: Concerned, Uncomfortable, Safety issue — stops the line
  • Simulation (in situ/high-fidelity) > didactic alone
Classic traps
  • Trap: correct count excludes RSI — Actually: most found RSIs had correct counts
  • Trap: RSI only if found after leaving theatre — Actually: after skin closure
  • Trap: mark non-operative side "No" — Actually: mark correct site only
  • Trap: family can interpret consent — Actually: professional interpreter
  • Trap: skip checklist in emergency — Actually: most needed; ↓ mortality
  • Trap: N₂O safe oxidiser — Actually: equals O₂
  • Trap: 3 min drying abolishes fire — Actually: fires still occur, worse with pooling
  • Trap: MAC here = alveolar concentration — Actually: monitored anaesthesia care
  • Trap: trainee presence raises RSI risk — Actually: lowers it
  • Trap: didactic TeamSTEPPS improves outcomes — Actually: no better than none
