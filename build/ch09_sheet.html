Chapter 9 — Occupational Health in Urology — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else
  • Musculoskeletal disorders (MSDs): 46–90% of urologists
  • Burnout: urology ~50% vs 28% general workforce
  • Elbow 90–120°, shoulders abducted <30° — every modality
  • Monitor top at/below eye level → gaze-down ~20°
  • Radiation: ALARA (as low as reasonably achievable) — time ↓, distance ↑, shielding, collimate
  • Pregnant surgeon: fetal dosimeter at waist, under apron
  • CO₂ laser condylomata → N95 + evacuator tip ≤2 in (5 cm)
  • Intravesical chemo → double chemo gloves, gown, shield, goggles
  • Exercise: 150 min moderate or 75 vigorous + 2 days strength/week
  • Sleep: 7–9 h (25–64 y); 7–8 h (≥65 y)

Physical ergonomics

  • Open: working area 2–4 in below elbow; neck flexion <25°; set for tallest, steps for others
  • Laparoscopic: table at surgeon's pubic symphysis → handles at/below elbow
  • Endourology: target between surgeon's symphysis and umbilicus
  • Robotic console: back flexion <15°; knee 90° rest, max 120° at pedals; forearm parallel to floor
  • Loupes: steep declination, neck <20°; lightest available
  • Lead: two-piece or belted one-piece → weight on hips, not shoulders
  • Lifting: ≥2 people; pivot, never twist
  • Microbreaks/stretches: ↓ pain, ↑ focus, no added case time
  • Workstation: knees 90°, elbows 90°, screen 20–40 in (50–100 cm)
  • Presbyopia: ~10 years into practice; "workspace" (near + intermediate) lenses; multifocals → lower monitor
  • Non-modifiable MSD risks: young, inexperienced, short, small glove, female, high body mass index

Occupational exposures

  • Vaccinate staff (CDC): hepatitis B, influenza, measles–mumps–rubella
  • Bloodborne: human immunodeficiency virus, hepatitis B and C via sharps → safety devices, reporting, post-exposure protocol
  • Smoke: particulates, toxic gases, bioaerosols → evacuation first
  • Chemo spills: closed-system drug-transfer device
  • Radiation harms: cataract, infertility, cancer; dosimeters mandatory
  • Fluoroscopy: low-dose default; pulsed, lowest rate (1 pulse/s); last-image hold
  • Geometry: source far, image intensifier close to patient
  • Surgeon foot-pedal control; spot images, not continuous
  • Pregnancy: maternity/double lead; minimise radiation cases
  • Noise: above concentration limits, usually below hearing-damage levels
  • Protect critical steps: music off, no non-urgent pages, no breaks

Physical health of surgeons

  • Deaths 25–54 y: heart disease, unintentional injury, suicide all top five
  • Most threatening modifiable cause 35–64 y: ASCVD (atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease)
  • Surgeons use primary care least — time, self-sacrifice, self-treatment, fear of judgement
  • Intraoperative stress response: heart rate ↑, heart-rate variability ↓, cortisol ↑ → baseline ~30 min
  • Sleep deprivation: performance ↓ ≥12%; ↑ fatal car crash
  • Biomarkers: low-density lipoprotein, DEXA (dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry) body composition, VO₂ max, oral glucose tolerance test

Mental and emotional health of surgeons

  • Burnout triad (Freudenberger 1980): exhaustion, depersonalisation, low accomplishment
  • Burnout, all medical providers: 40–60%
  • Urology burnout risk: more hours/encounters; mid-career > early; chronic MSD pain
  • ≥20% time on meaningful work → less burnout
  • Second victim → "psychological first aid", peer support
  • Stress continuum: Ready (green) → Reacting (yellow) → Injured (orange) → Ill (red)
  • Ill zone → professional help, sick leave
  • Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) and mindfulness: ↑ resilience (trials); dialectical behavioural therapy = CBT + acceptance
  • Imposter syndrome: cognitive distortion, commonest in trainees → CBT
  • Distortion → tactic: overgeneralisation/depersonalisation → self-compassion; catastrophising/minimisation → double standard; labelling/tunnel vision → exploratory questions; emotional reasoning → restructuring; confirmation bias → defusion
Classic traps
  • Trap: laparoscopic table set to elbow — Actually: symphysis; handles at elbow
  • Trap: one-piece lead is the problem — Actually: shoulder loading; belt it
  • Trap: magnification harmless — Actually: increases dose
  • Trap: intensifier far from patient — Actually: close; source far
  • Trap: microbreaks lengthen cases — Actually: no added time
  • Trap: burnout peaks early career — Actually: mid-career higher
  • Trap: burnout = "Ill" zone — Actually: Injured (orange)
  • Trap: self-care fixes burnout — Actually: system change essential
  • Trap: progressives fine for desk — Actually: lower monitor or workspace lenses
  • Trap: "second victim" universally accepted — Actually: may offend; "traumatised caregiver"
