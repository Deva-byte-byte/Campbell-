Chapter 120 — Aging and Geriatric Urology — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else

  • Age alone ≠ surgical risk; frailty, function, comorbidity decide
  • Fried frail = ≥3 of 5; 1–2 = pre-frail
  • Fried weight loss: >10 lb or 5% in 1 year
  • Bathing lost first, feeding last (activities of daily living, ADLs)
  • Asymptomatic bacteriuria (ASB) → no antibiotics; except pregnancy, before urological instrumentation
  • USPSTF (US Preventive Services Task Force) grade D: ASB screening; PSA (prostate-specific antigen) screening in men >70
  • Life expectancy <10 yr → non-curative prostate plan
  • Radical cystectomy at ≥75: outcomes comparable to younger patients
  • Beers: avoid antimuscarinics in dementia, delirium
  • Vaginal oestrogen safe; systemic HRT (hormone replacement therapy) started ≥60 → harm > benefit

Opening; Demographics of aging

  • Geriatric = ≥65, but chronological age a poor descriptor
  • UN (United Nations) alternative: define by years of life remaining
  • Nascher, 1909: first used "geriatrics" clinically
  • UN Decade of Healthy Ageing: 2021–2030, WHO (World Health Organization)-led
  • Only ophthalmology, cardiology exceed urology in geriatric volume
  • ≥65: fastest-growing; ~doubles to 94.7 M by 2060
  • 2034: over-65s outnumber children (US first)
  • ≥85 triples: 6 → 19 M by 2060

Biology and principles of aging

  • Key driver: cellular senescence (replication stops)
  • Hayflick limit (1961): telomere-driven replicative arrest
  • Senescence triggers: telomeres; also ROS (reactive oxygen species), radiation, DNA damage, unfolded protein response
  • SASP (senescence-associated secretory phenotype): inflammation, tumorigenesis; spreads senescence
  • WHO healthy ageing = functional ability enabling wellbeing
  • Functional ability = intrinsic capacity × environment; 5 abilities

Clinical evaluation

  • First step: ability to function independently
  • Homeostenosis: age-related narrowing of functional reserve
  • Chronic disease: ~4 in 5 over-65s ≥1; ~1 in 4 ≥4
  • BADLs (basic ADLs): bathe, dress, groom, toilet, feed, ambulate
  • IADLs (instrumental ADLs): cook, clean, transport, laundry, finances, phone, medicines
  • Screen: "Can you shower or bathe independently?"
  • Gradient: independent > lose IADL > lose BADL → ↓life expectancy, ↑cost
  • Self-rated poor health → predicts ADL/IADL decline over 3 yr
  • Occupational therapy → restores independence, mobility
  • Worse surgical outcome: long operation, poor ASA (American Society of Anesthesiologists) score, urgent/emergent case
  • Chronological age: rarely an independent surgical predictor
  • "Clearing" for surgery = misnomer; optimise instead
  • Box 120.1 (American College of Surgeons/American Geriatrics Society, 2012): 13-item checklist — only cardiac, pulmonary are organ items
  • Cardiac work-up: ACC/AHA (American College of Cardiology/American Heart Association) non-cardiac surgery algorithm
  • SPAQI (Society for Perioperative Assessment and Quality Improvement, 2018): frailty screen before elective major surgery → diagnostic assessment → CGA
  • CGA (comprehensive geriatric assessment): geriatrician-led; no standard definition
  • CGA sequence: frailty → performance (360° turn, 3 chair rises, pen, jacket) → albumin, creatinine, BNP (brain natriuretic peptide) → FEV1 (forced expiratory volume in 1 s)

Clinical evaluation — geriatric syndromes

  • List: frailty, depression, delirium, falls, incontinence, polypharmacy, vision, hearing (+ pressure ulcers)
  • Model: synergistic, multifactorial — not linear single-cause
  • Lifelines Cohort: ≥1 syndrome → ↑new chronic disease, independent of confounders
  • Dependent care ↑: balance, cognitive impairment; not chronic pain
  • Frailty prevalence: 7–10% at 65 → 25% >85
  • Fried five: weight loss, weak grip, slow gait, low activity, exhaustion
  • Deficit accumulation index (DAI): not predictive postoperatively
  • Comorbidity vs disability vs frailty: co-existing disease vs cannot self-care vs ↓reserve

Clinical evaluation — Beers (Table 120.1)

  • NSAIDs (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs): GI (gastrointestinal) risk age >75; PPI (proton pump inhibitor)/misoprostol if >7 days
  • COX-2 inhibitors: avoid in HF (heart failure), CrCl (creatinine clearance) <30
  • Tramadol: SIADH (inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion); CrCl <30 → avoid extended-release
  • Edoxaban: avoid CrCl <15 or >95; reduce dose at 15–50
  • Dabigatran option only if <75 yr
  • Ciprofloxacin: avoid with theophylline; ↑INR (international normalised ratio) with warfarin; reduce dose at CrCl <30
  • Macrolide exempt: azithromycin
  • Nitrofurantoin: avoid CrCl <30, chronic use; OK in moderate impairment
  • TMP-SMX (trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole): ↓dose at CrCl 15–29; avoid <15; check K⁺ at 5 days
  • Tricyclics: avoid in men with BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia); doxepin listed >6 mg/day
  • Benzodiazepines: avoid with ≥2 other CNS (central nervous system) drugs, opioids
  • Testosterone: only confirmed symptomatic hypogonadism
  • Desmopressin → hyponatraemia; treat nocturia cause; 5-ARI (5α-reductase inhibitor) for BPH
  • Vaginal oestradiol <25 mcg twice weekly after breast cancer (with discussion)
  • Vasomotor: SSRI (selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor; not paroxetine), SNRI (serotonin–noradrenaline reuptake inhibitor), gabapentin
  • Rating codes: A = avoid; C = caution; H = CMS (Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services) high-risk

Clinical evaluation — GSM, FI, prevention

  • GSM (genitourinary syndrome of menopause): coined 2014; 27–84% of postmenopausal individuals
  • GSM → urgency, frequency, UI (urinary incontinence); vaginal oestrogen may relieve UUI (urgency UI)
  • Oestrogen deprivation (breast cancer), ADT (androgen deprivation), 5-ARI → ↑OAB (overactive bladder)
  • Hormone-sensitive cancer: not absolute contraindication to vaginal oestrogen → consult oncologist
  • Caruncle: first-line topical oestrogen; excision rarely needed
  • Postmenopausal bleeding → endometrial cancer in 9%
  • FI (faecal incontinence): most dreaded syndrome; <30% of women report it → ask
  • FI prevalence: 14.4% US; 17% community women >85; 43% nursing home
  • Antihypertensives ↓mortality >80
  • Testosterone Trials: no cognitive benefit; modest sexual benefit
  • ASPREE (aspirin): no disability-free survival gain; ↑mortality

Clinical evaluation — incontinence treatment

  • Antimuscarinics: 20–70% better than placebo; poor persistence
  • Persistence ↑: M3-selective (solifenacin, darifenacin, trospium)
  • Trospium: quaternary amine → less BBB (blood–brain barrier) penetration
  • β3-agonist: dry mouth < placebo; constipation = placebo
  • Mirabegron: metoprolol interaction; ↑BP
  • Tolterodine high dose → QT prolongation, torsades
  • After starting: consider PVR (post-void residual); never exceed maximum dose
  • Nursing-home UI: ~½ short-stay, ~¾ long-stay
  • New UI after admission = unmasked, pre-existing
  • Catheter colonisation: up to 98%

Clinical evaluation — Table 120.2

  • Fever: >100°F or >2°F above baseline
  • Loeb, catheterised: ≥1 of fever, new CVA (costovertebral angle) tenderness, rigors, acute delirium
  • Loeb, no catheter: dysuria, or fever + ≥1 new urinary symptom
  • NHSN (National Healthcare Safety Network): culture ≥10⁵ CFU (colony-forming units) + no other site + signs
  • NHSN leukocytosis: >14,000/mm³; left shift >6% bands or ≥1500/mm³
  • NHSN repeated fever: >99°F

Clinical evaluation — UTI and bacteriuria

  • Lifetime UTI: >50% women; 12% men
  • ASB = positive culture, any pyuria, no symptoms
  • ASB prevalence: up to 22% older women; ~10% older men
  • ASB: AGS Choosing Wisely — one of original five
  • Transplant ASB: no evidence of graft failure
  • First task: symptomatic UTI vs OAB vs ASB

Clinical evaluation — cancer

  • Prostate cancer ≈11% of US cancer treatment cost
  • Bladder cancer: greatest risk factor = age; 4th commonest, 8th cancer death (US men)
  • Restaging TURBT (transurethral resection) for T1 intermediate/high risk → underused in elderly; improves survival
  • BCG (bacille Calmette–Guérin) less effective in elderly (immunosenescence)

End of life care; Additional resources; Key points

  • First step: goals-of-care conversation; family may disagree
  • Palliative ≠ withdrawal; aggressive symptom control
  • 4 Ms: Mobility, Medication, Mentation, what Matters
  • GAYF: "Geriatrics at Your Fingertips" (AGS, annual)
  • POP (pelvic organ prolapse): age alone no bar to surgery
  • Elder abuse: urologists mandatory reporters in most places

Classic traps

  • Trap: old → too risky for surgery — Actually: frailty, ASA, urgency predict; age rarely independent
  • Trap: confused resident + positive culture = UTI — Actually: no causal link; ASB untreated
  • Trap: pyuria makes it a UTI — Actually: ASB defined regardless of pyuria
  • Trap: count deficits for frailty — Actually: DAI not predictive; use Fried
  • Trap: aspirin protects healthy elderly — Actually: ASPREE ↑mortality
  • Trap: breast cancer bars vaginal oestrogen — Actually: not necessarily; consult oncology
  • Trap: nitrofurantoin banned in any CKD — Actually: avoid at CrCl <30 only
  • Trap: caruncle bleeding = benign — Actually: exclude endometrial, cervical cancer
  • Trap: new nursing-home UI = new disease — Actually: often unmasked
  • Trap: HRT is cardio- or neuroprotective — Actually: neither; harm if started ≥60
