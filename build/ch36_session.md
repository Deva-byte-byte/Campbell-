## Instalment 1 — Chapter Preamble, ALARA and Guidelines, Non-ionising vs Ionising Technique, Fetal/Neonatal Sonography, SFU Grading, Hydronephrosis vs Cystic Disease, Solid Renal Masses, Stones, UTI Sonography, CEUS/CeVUS and Table 36.1, Intrarenal Reflux, Renal Trauma, Bladder Sonography (Keyhole Sign) (PDF 55–60)

### The numbers to lock in
**Key facts, figures, thresholds, cut-offs**
- Amniotic fluid = fetal skin dialysate before 8–10 weeks; fetal urine from 10–12 weeks; most anomalies visible by 20 weeks.
- Fetal bladder visible in all fetuses by 10–12 weeks; never completely empty.
- Hydronephrosis underestimated up to 2 days postnatally and in dehydration.
- SFU grades 0–4: 0 none; 1 pelvis only; 2 major calyces; 3 major + minor calyces; 4 = grade 3 + parenchymal thinning.
- Cystic Wilms tumour 2–4 years; multilocular cystic nephroma = infancy.
- Table 36.1: CeVUS AUC 0.97 (both generations); sensitivity 0.92/0.93; specificity 0.94/0.91; overall false-negative 3%.
- CEUS agent: sulfur hexafluoride lipid-type A microspheres (Lumason/SonoVue), FDA-approved IV and intravesical.

**Gold standards, commonest, first-line**
- Diuretic renography = gold standard for evaluating hydronephrosis (obstruction).
- SFU classification = most widely used by paediatric urologists.
- Commonest prenatal sonographic findings = collecting-system dilatation and parenchymal cysts.
- Sonography = first-line for stones in children (not reflex CT/KUB).
- DMSA superior to grey-scale sonography for APN and scarring.

**Eponyms / named signs**
- Keyhole sign — thick bladder + dilated posterior urethra → PUV.
- Twinkling artefact — colour Doppler artefact identifying calculi.

**Landmark trials** — none in this stretch (systematic review of CeVUS is not a named landmark trial).

**Dates and legislation** — ACR Appropriateness Criteria: Hematuria–Child (2018); Urinary Tract Infection–Child (2024).

**Absolute contraindications** — none stated.
**Relative contraindications** — fetal MR in first trimester (avoid; theoretical gene-expression effect).

**When to image / escalate**
- Bilateral hydroureteronephrosis + thickened bladder + poor emptying → VCUG to exclude PUV.
- UTI failing to improve/worsening on antibiotics → sonography.
- Hospitalised UTI → sonography before discharge to exclude obstruction.
- Solid renal mass → contrast CT after sonography.

**Classic traps**
- Newborn corticomedullary differentiation mistaken for hydronephrosis.
- Early postnatal scan underestimates hydronephrosis.
- Dilated ureter assumed to be obstruction (may be VUR).
- Normal sonogram taken as excluding VUR or risk-stratifying APN.
- Parapelvic fat (echogenic, no shadow) mistaken for stone (echogenic with shadow).

## Instalment 2 — Midline Pelvic Cysts and Cloaca, Bladder Tumours (Clot vs Tumour), Viral Cystitis, Exstrophy vs Prune Belly, Acute Scrotum (Whirlpool, Fountain), Testicular Masses, Undescended Testis, DSD Sonography, MRI Fundamentals and Protocol, Sedation, MR Urography, MRI for Renal Masses, Infection, Bladder Rhabdomyosarcoma, Scrotal MRI (PDF 61–66)

### The numbers to lock in
**Key facts, figures, thresholds, cut-offs**
- Whirlpool sign sensitivity ≈96% (vs ≈76% colour Doppler), specificity ≈99%.
- No arterial flow + parenchymal heterogeneity → testes non-viable at exploration (all).
- MRU: furosemide 1 mg/kg, max 20 mg; 3D T2 fat-sat with 1-mm slices; gadolinium.
- MRU protocol up to 1 h; shortened ≈30 min (unvalidated); sedation typically <6 years.
- Fetal MRI with third-level obstetric ultrasound after ≈20 weeks.
- Diffusion-weighted imaging b = 0, 100 or 600 s/mm² (rhabdomyosarcoma protocol).

**Gold standards, commonest, first-line**
- Ultrasonography = initial and usually only genital imaging.
- Commonest prepubertal primary testicular tumour = benign teratoma.
- Sonography = primary imaging for ambiguous genitalia/severe hypospadias with non-palpable gonads.
- MRI = most useful test for bladder masses, especially rhabdomyosarcoma; MRI preferred for locoregional staging of renal tumours.

**Eponyms / named signs**
- Whirlpool sign — twisted spermatic cord (torsion).
- Fountain sign — hyperaemic thickened scrotal wall (acute idiopathic scrotal oedema).
- Onion rings — epidermoid cyst, no internal flow.
- Patlak method — linear-regression graphic analysis of tracer clearance (MRU function).
- Choosing Wisely (AUA) — no ultrasound for routine cryptorchidism.

**Landmark trials** — none.
**Dates** — none examinable.

**Absolute contraindications (to imaging use)**
- Imaging must never delay exploration of suspected torsion.
**Relative contraindications / "no role"**
- Ultrasound for routine undescended testis (exception: obese, difficult-to-examine child).
- MRI for acute scrotum; MRI for scrotal masses (except staging).
- MRI for infection, stones, trauma (not helpful).

**When to operate / not**
- Suspected torsion → explore without waiting for imaging.

**Classic traps**
- Hyperaemic epididymis read as epididymo-orchitis — also seen after spontaneous detorsion.
- Viral cystitis wall thickening mistaken for a bladder mass.
- Clot vs tumour — reimage prone: clot moves.
- Prune belly vs PUV — both hydroureteronephrosis; PUV has thick bladder/keyhole; prune belly has empty scrotum.
- Over-interpreting scrotal ultrasound against the clinical examination.
- Urine masks T2-hyperintense bladder lesions → T1 before gadolinium.

## Instalment 3 — MRI in Undescended Testis and DSD, Plain Radiography (KUB), IVP, Retrograde Pyelography, VCUG Fundamentals, VCUG Upper Tract, VCUG Lower Tract, Occult VUR and PIC Cystography, Pelvic Trauma and Bladder Capacity, VCUG in Genital Anomalies, Urogenitography/Shopfner (PDF 67–68) — first half of split

### The numbers to lock in
**Key facts, figures, thresholds, cut-offs**
- Expected bladder capacity = (age in years + 2) × 30 mL, maximum 400 mL.
- Historic 4–6-week delay before VCUG after UTI — unnecessary.
- Occult VUR: new contralateral VUR in 7–33% of unilateral VUR; VCUG shows VUR in only ≈40% of DMSA-proven APN.
- PIC cystography: contrast hung 1 m above bladder; occult VUR in up to 39% (unilateral VUR) and 59% (negative VCUG + abnormal DMSA); 58% reduction in recurrent UTI with concomitant endoscopic treatment.

**Gold standards, first-line**
- Sonography + urogenitography = usually all that DSD requires.
- VCUG = best test for recurrent UTI/epididymitis in males with ectopic ureter or dilated utricle.
- Retrograde urethrogram before catheterisation when blood at meatus.

**Eponyms**
- Shopfner classification — urogenitogram classification (type III = low urethrovaginal confluence).
- Positional instillation of contrast (PIC) cystography — cystoscopically directed contrast stream to unmask occult VUR.

**Landmark trials / dates** — none.

**Absolute contraindication**
- Urethral catheterisation before retrograde urethrogram when blood is at the meatus.
**Relative**
- Routine VCUG for all hydronephrosis (reserve for dilated ureter).
- VCUG after a single non-febrile UTI (little yield).

**When to image**
- Pelvic trauma + meatal blood/difficulty voiding → contrast study; prompt intervention mandatory for urethral injury.
- Hydronephrosis + dilated ureter(s) → VCUG.
- Negative VCUG + recurrent febrile UTI (esp. abnormal DMSA) → consider PIC.
- Any UTI in a circumcised boy may merit evaluation.

**Classic traps**
- Free retrograde flow ≠ unobstructed antegrade flow (postsurgical flaps).
- Early VCUG in febrile UTI may overgrade VUR (endotoxin ureteric dilatation).
- Catheter not midline → utricle/posterior urethra (male) or ectopic ureter (female); excess catheter knots.
- KUB is poor evidence for constipation.

## Instalment 4 — CT Fundamentals, CT Upper Tract (Obstruction, Tumours, Infection/Abscess, Calculi, Trauma Phase Timing), CT Lower Tract and CT Cystography, CT Genitalia, DMSA Scintigraphy (APN vs Scar, Timing), MAG3 Diuretic Renography (Four Elements, T½, Curve Shape, Hydration and ROI Figures) (PDF 68–72) — second half of split

### The numbers to lock in
**Key facts, figures, thresholds, cut-offs**
- DMSA (piglet model): sensitivity 87%, specificity 100% for APN; SPECT = slightly higher sensitivity, lower specificity, equal accuracy.
- APN lesions resolve over ≈5 months; lesion at 6 months = fixed scar; acute lesions persist in 36–52%.
- Furosemide 1 mg/kg IV when collecting system maximally filled.
- No established paediatric washout T½ defining obstruction.

**Gold standards, first-line**
- Diuretic renography (DTPA or, more commonly, MAG3) = gold standard obstructive vs non-obstructive hydronephrosis.
- Contrast-enhanced CT = routine for paediatric renal tumours and abdominal trauma.
- CT's greatest advantage = urolithiasis detection (but rarely changes management).

**Eponyms**
- "Well-tempered" diuretic renogram — standardised protocol.
- Pie-in-the-sky bladder — elevated bladder neck, pelvic-fracture extraperitoneal injury (Fig. 36.16).

**Landmark trials / dates** — none.

**Contraindications** — none absolute; CT relatively avoided for hydronephrosis, genital conditions (no role), lower-tract obstruction/infection.

**When to image**
- Trauma CT must include delayed excretory images.
- CT/MR for UTI only when non-ionising modalities inconclusive, or for abscess, fistula, postoperative complication.
- CT cystography when bladder/augment rupture suspected — intra- vs extraperitoneal determines management.

**Classic traps**
- Arterial-phase-only trauma CT misses collecting-system and ureteric injury.
- DMSA: APN superimposed on old scar hard to separate; contour is the discriminator.
- Renogram in a dehydrated (fasted) child mimics obstruction.
- T½ timed from tracer injection rather than from maximal filling.
- Wrong ROI → spurious function/drainage; ROI choice can move the apparent level (UPJ vs UVJ).

## Instalment 5 — Serial and Gravity-Assisted Renography, Intermittent Obstruction (Dietl's Crisis), Renography vs Sonography, Radionuclide Cystography (Direct vs Indirect), Radionuclide Testicular Scanning, Chapter Key Points (PDF 73–77)

### The numbers to lock in
**Key facts, figures, thresholds, cut-offs**
- Cortical tracer retention up to 48 h after an episode of intermittent obstruction.
- Gravity-assisted drainage assessed after 15 min upright.
- Testicular scintigraphy in use since the 1970s; sensitivity/specificity up to 100% in experienced centres.

**Gold standards, first-line**
- Renography more reproducible and quantifiable than sonography for obstruction.
- Direct radionuclide cystography: more sensitive and lower dose than VCUG.
- Sonography = initial test for suspected obstruction and for DSD; follow-up of renal injuries.

**Eponyms**
- Dietl's crisis — intermittent UPJ obstruction: nausea/vomiting + flank/abdominal pain; cortical retention persists.

**Landmark trials / dates** — none.

**Relative contraindications / limits**
- Indirect radionuclide cystography — insufficiently sensitive, not routine.
- Testicular scintigraphy — only if it does not delay surgery.

**When to operate**
- Progressive prolongation of T½ / declining drainage on serial renograms → pyeloplasty (Fig. 36.25).
- Predictors of progression: biphasic curve, poor gravity-assisted drainage.

**Classic traps**
- A single normal renogram does not exclude later obstruction.
- Normal initial washout does not exclude intermittent obstruction (rising second limb with pain).
- Moderate (SFU 3) hydronephrosis with preserved cortex can hide high-grade UPJ obstruction.
- Bottom-up (VCUG for all) overtreats low-grade VUR; top-down reserves VCUG for abnormal DMSA.
