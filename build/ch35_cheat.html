Chapter 35 — Urologic Evaluation of the Child — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else
  • Suspected cord torsion → no imaging → immediate exploration + contralateral orchiopexy.
  • TWIST score: 0 no ultrasound (US); 1–5 US; ≥6 straight to theatre.
  • Salt-wasting CAH (congenital adrenal hyperplasia) crisis day 4–15 → don't discharge early.
  • Infected obstructed kidney → prompt stent or nephrostomy.
  • Children hide shock: hypotension = late, decompensated.
  • Bladder capacity (mL) = (age + 2) × 30.
  • Suspected urethral injury → no catheter → RUG (retrograde urethrogram); fails → suprapubic tube.
  • Postnatal US after 48 h — immediately if bladder outlet obstruction suspected.
  • Febrile infant <24 mo: catheter/suprapubic aspirate (SPA) urine; bag → false positives.
  • Undescended testis: ultrasound not recommended.

Triage — emergent referrals

  • Acuity tiers: emergent; urgent <24 h; semiurgent 1–3 wk; routine.
  • Commonest acute scrotum: appendage torsion — prepubertal, supportive care.
  • Appendage torsion on US: epididymal hyperaemia, appendage rarely seen.
  • Cord torsion: ≈1 in 4000 males; most >10 years.
  • TWIST (max 7): swelling 2, hard testis 2; absent cremasteric, nausea/vomiting, high-riding 1 each.
  • Viability inversely related to torsion duration.
  • Manual detorsion never replaces exploration.
  • Neonatal (extravaginal) torsion: firm painless testis; nubbin; urgent exploration preferred.
  • Commonest cause ambiguous genitalia: CAH; 21-hydroxylase ≈95%.
  • CAH tests: 17-hydroxyprogesterone + electrolytes; screen may return post-discharge.
  • Salt-wasting death: hyperkalaemia, dehydration, shock.
  • CAH treatment: lifelong gluco- + mineralocorticoid.
  • Exstrophy: ≈2.15/100,000; cord tied 2-0 silk (no clamp); cling film.
  • Exstrophy → immediate transfer to paediatric centre.
  • Commonest infected obstruction: obstructing stone.
  • PUV (posterior urethral valves) outlet obstruction → vesicostomy if needed.
  • Renal vein thrombosis (RVT) triad: haematuria + flank mass + thrombocytopenia.
  • RVT: male, left, two-thirds in first 3 days; Doppler US diagnosis.
  • Paraphimosis: ask circumcision status; exclude hair tourniquet.
  • Paraphimosis adjuncts: ice 5 min; Coban 15–30 min; sugar 1–2 h.
  • Failed manual reduction → dorsal slit/circumcision under anaesthesia.
  • Priapism: ≥4 h, unrelated to stimulation.
  • Commonest paediatric priapism: ischaemic; commonest cause sickle cell disease.
  • Ischaemic gas: pO₂ <40 mm Hg, hypercapnia, acidosis, glucopenia.
  • Sickle cell priapism: oxygen + intravenous fluids; no cold packs.
  • Nonischaemic: trauma, painless → initially expectant.
  • Neonatal priapism: benign, painless; monitor up to 5 days.
  • Commonest injured genitourinary organ: kidney; blunt mechanism.
  • AUA (American Urological Association) 2020 imaging: haematuria + systolic BP (SBP) <90, or worrying mechanism/signs.
  • Renal imaging: CT (computed tomography) preferred; US acceptable; AAST OIS (American Association for the Surgery of Trauma Organ Injury Scale) as adults.
  • Renal trauma default: nonoperative; else stent/drain/angioembolisation.
  • Abuse work-up in straddle injury: <9 months, hymenal/vaginal/perianal, severe, history mismatch.
  • Female genital trauma: ≈80% straddle; urethral injury ≈5% of pelvic fractures.
  • Female urethral injury: examination under anaesthesia + cystoscopy; suprapubic tube, delayed repair.
  • Male scrotal haematoma → US to exclude rupture.
  • Toilet-seat crush: mean ≈3.9 yr; usually expectant.
  • Retention causes: drugs, UTI/balanoposthitis, constipation, neurogenic, mass, trauma.
  • Retention without catheter: analgesia, sitz bath, disimpaction.

Triage — urgent, semiurgent, routine

  • Prenatal hydronephrosis: APD (anteroposterior diameter) >7 mm third trimester or UTD (urinary tract dilation) A2–3.
  • Pre-discharge US if: solitary kidney, bilateral, or outlet obstruction.
  • Neonatal creatinine = maternal first 48 h; follow downward trend.
  • Male + bilateral hydronephrosis/thick bladder → VCUG (voiding cystourethrogram) before discharge.
  • Most prenatal hydronephrosis needs no neonatal intervention; close follow-up.
  • Suspected outlet obstruction → urethral feeding tube/catheter.
  • Tea-coloured + casts + protein → glomerular: C3, C4, antinuclear antibody, antistreptolysin O.
  • Isolated terminal haematuria → urethrorrhagia/stricture.
  • Post-bone marrow transplant clots: BK virus, cytomegalovirus, adenovirus cystitis.
  • Persistent gross haematuria, normal imaging → cystoscopy + bilateral retrograde pyelograms.
  • Wilms: commonest renal cancer <15 yr; peak 1–5; ≈10% syndromic.
  • Renal tumour: firm non-tender mass, rarely crosses midline.
  • Neonatal renal mass: congenital mesoblastic nephroma.
  • Sickle cell trait + renal mass: renal medullary carcinoma.
  • Testis tumour: ≈1% solid tumours; peaks <3 yr, postpubertal.
  • Prepubertal testis mass usually benign; commonest malignant yolk sac.
  • Testis markers: α-fetoprotein, β-hCG (human chorionic gonadotrophin), lactate dehydrogenase; precocity → Leydig cell.
  • Leydig suspicion: add testosterone, follicle-stimulating and luteinising hormones, prolactin.
  • Primary amenorrhoea: no menses by 16 (normal development) or 14 (none).
  • Cyclic pain + amenorrhoea → outflow obstruction (septum, imperforate hymen).
  • Interlabial mass first step: exclude upstream obstruction by ultrasound.
  • Older-girl interlabial mass: add urethral prolapse.
  • Hernia/communicating hydrocele: operate on examination or compelling history.
  • Communicating hydrocele: fluctuates; smaller in morning.
  • Hernia precautions: pain, firm non-reducible bulge, erythema, inconsolable infant → emergent.
  • Operation: high ligation of processus at internal ring.
  • Empty hemiscrotum at birth: undescended vs in utero torsion.
  • Descended at birth, empty later: retractile vs ascending testis.
  • Varicocele: ≈15% adolescents; Lambert volume = L × W × H × 0.71.
  • Non-decompressing supine/right varicocele → retroperitoneal US.
  • Physiological phimosis: ≈1% at 17 yr; never forcibly retract.
  • Treat asymptomatic phimosis if vesicoureteral reflux (VUR)/urinary tract infection (UTI); steroids if circumcision declined.
  • BXO (balanitis xerotica obliterans) with phimosis → treat.
  • Meatal stenosis: circumcised; meatus <5 Fr; dorsal deflection → meatotomy.
  • Labial adhesions: peak 13–23 mo; asymptomatic → observe; recurrence 7–55%.
  • Labial examination: frog-leg; pull labia straight out, not laterally.
  • Microhaematuria: >5 red cells/high-power field (RBC/hpf), confirm microscopy, exclude UTI.

Evaluation of the paediatric patient

  • Consent from guardian; assent from child.
  • Respiratory rate shouldn't stay >60; heart rate ≈ 2–3 × respiratory rate.
  • Lower-limit SBP: 60 neonate; 70 to 1 yr; 70 + 2×age to 10; 90 after.
  • BP cuff ≈⅔ arm: small → falsely high; large → falsely low.
  • Shock first sign: tachypnoea, tachycardia.
  • Body mass index (BMI) percentiles ≥2 yr: underweight <5th; overweight 85–95th; obese ≥95th.
  • Tanner II (boys): testicular/scrotal enlargement first; V = hair to thighs.
  • Micropenis: stretched length <mean −2.5 SD (term ≈1.9; adult 9.3 cm).
  • Stretched length: pubic symphysis → glans tip.
  • Hard-to-feel testis: frog-leg; cross-legged or catcher's squat.
  • Simple dimple: in cleft, <5 mm, base visible → no imaging.
  • Image: palpable lipoma, tag >5 mm, deep/atypical dimple, deviated crease.
  • AAP (American Academy of Pediatrics) UTI: pyuria/bacteriuria + ≥50,000 colony-forming units/mL, catheter/SPA.
  • Microhaematuria: 0.5–3%; commonest thin basement membrane, IgA nephropathy, hypercalciuria.
  • Dipstick blood false positive: haemoglobinuria, myoglobinuria → confirm microscopy.
  • Microhaematuria threshold: child >5 vs adult ≥3 RBC/hpf.
  • Persistent microhaematuria → repeat 2–3 wk → imaging + calcium/creatinine (Ca/Cr).
  • Ca/Cr abnormal: >0.4 age 2–5; >0.2 age >5.
  • Protein/Cr normal: <0.7 yr 1; <0.4 to 4 yr; <0.2 >5 yr.
  • Hypercalciuria: fluids + modest sodium restriction; renal US.
  • Proteinuria: first-morning sample (orthostatic common); AAP: don't screen.
  • Nephrotic range: protein/Cr >3.0.
  • Persistent non-orthostatic proteinuria (2 morning samples) → nephrology.
  • Specific gravity: range 1.002–1.040; <1.010 dilute/diabetes insipidus; >1.020 dehydration.
  • Chronic kidney disease anaemia once glomerular filtration rate (GFR) <43 mL/min/1.73 m².
  • Ideal GFR marker: filtered, not secreted or reabsorbed.
  • Imaging principle: ALARA (as low as reasonably achievable).
  • MAG-3 (mercaptoacetyltriglycine) = obstruction + split function; DMSA (dimercaptosuccinic acid) = scarring + split function.
  • Radiation-free: ultrasound, magnetic resonance imaging (needs sedation).

Child comfort

  • Tablet distraction ≈ midazolam for preoperative anxiety.
  • Midazolam before VCUG: less recall, results unaffected.
  • EMLA 30–45 min before office procedures → avoids general anaesthesia.
  • LMX = lidocaine 4%; EMLA = lidocaine 2.5% + prilocaine 2.5%.
  • Lidocaine gel for catheterisation: mixed results.
Classic traps
  • Trap: cremasteric reflex present → not torsion — Actually: presence does not exclude torsion.
  • Trap: scan before exploring suspected torsion — Actually: clinical diagnosis; imaging delays.
  • Trap: normal newborn screen excludes CAH — Actually: result may be late; only 21-hydroxylase screened.
  • Trap: normal BP → no shock — Actually: hypotension signals decompensated shock.
  • Trap: cold packs for all priapism — Actually: can induce priapism in sickle cell.
  • Trap: haematuria/tachycardia reliably flag paediatric renal injury — Actually: mechanism predicts better.
  • Trap: day-1 ultrasound for hydronephrosis — Actually: after 48 h (underestimates earlier).
  • Trap: day-1 creatinine reflects neonatal function — Actually: maternal for 48 h.
  • Trap: bag urine for febrile infant UTI — Actually: catheter/SPA; bag false positives.
  • Trap: ultrasound locates undescended testis — Actually: poor sensitivity/specificity; not recommended.
