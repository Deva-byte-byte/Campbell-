#### The numbers to lock in
**Key facts, figures, thresholds and cut-offs**
- Renal blood flow 3–4% → 12–16% of cardiac output in year 1.
- Creatinine reflects maternal function for 48 h; neonatal by day 7; <0.5 mg/dL at term.
- GFR doubles in first 2 weeks; adult by 2 years (term GFR ≈40 mL/min/1.73 m², from CKD section).
- Neonatal total body water 70–90%; free-water loss 1–2% body weight/day over first 5 days; dilution to 50 mOsm/L.
- Na⁺-K⁺-ATPase peaks at 2–3 weeks; Na balance negative week 1 → positive 2–3 weeks; acidification matures over 3–4 weeks; proximal tubule reabsorbs 80% of filtered HCO₃⁻.
- Microscopic haematuria >5 RBC/HPF; ≈1% of school-age children; <25% persist; most resolve in 1 year; definitive diagnosis <30%; gross haematuria ≈0.1% of paediatric emergency visits.
- UPCR normal <0.7 mg/mg newborn, <0.2 mg/mg child; timed <4 mg/m²/h; adults <150 mg/24 h; transient proteinuria >75%.
- Nephrotic: >40 mg/m²/h or UPCR >2.0 mg/mg; microscopic haematuria ≈20%; secondary ≈10%; ~30 genes.
- CNS <3 months; SSNS remission ≤8 weeks (~50% multiple relapses); FRNS ≥2/6 months or ≥4/12 months; SDNS 2 consecutive relapses on steroids; SRNS no remission after 6–8 weeks (15–20%).
- MCNS 90% of <10 y, 70–80% overall, median 2.5 y, ~90% steroid remission, up to 85% long-lasting remission; FSGS <25% steroid response.
- PSGN rare <3 y; C3 <50% normal; oedema 5–10 d, BP 2–3 weeks, C3 normal 8–12 weeks, proteinuria ≤6 months, microhaematuria >1 year. MPGN/C3GN rare <5 y.
- IgAN 20–30% ESKD + 20% progressive CKD. HSP gross haematuria up to 25%; relapses first 4 months; abnormalities 3 months, 2 years in 10–20%.
- Alport ~50% X-linked males ESKD by 25 y. ANCA untreated 90% mortality in 2 years; ~¼ ESKD.
- HUS: aHUS ~10%; O157:H7 >70% of US STEC+ HUS; ~half need dialysis; 5–25% later CKD.
- RTA urine pH >5.5; proximal RTA HCO₃⁻ 14–17 mEq/L.
- MCDK anomalies up to 25%; surveillance 3-monthly year 1 → 6-monthly to involution/≥5 years → annually into adulthood.
- ARPKD 1:40,000; neonatal mortality 25–50% with oligo/anhydramnios; >75% ESKD by 5 y. ADPKD 1:1000.

**Gold standards, commonest, first-line**
- CAKUT = commonest cause of paediatric ESKD.
- UTI = commonest cause of gross haematuria.
- Fresh urine microscopy = critical step in haematuria evaluation.
- MCNS = commonest nephrotic syndrome; steroids = cornerstone; FSGS = majority of SRNS.
- PSGN = commonest PIGN. IgAN = commonest primary GN worldwide; biopsy only definitive test.
- HUS = common cause of severe AKI in healthy young child; eculizumab = therapy of choice for aHUS; supportive care for STEC+ HUS.
- Cystinosis = one of the commonest causes of Fanconi syndrome.
- Genetic testing preferred for cystic disease.

**Eponyms**
- Denys-Drash / Frasier — genetic nephrotic syndrome of infancy.
- Alport — collagen IV α3/4/5; sensorineural deafness.
- Goodpasture — anti-GBM disease with pulmonary haemorrhage.
- Henoch-Schönlein — purpura, arthralgia, abdominal pain, nephritis.
- Churg-Strauss (EGPA) — extremely rare in childhood.
- Fanconi — generalised proximal tubulopathy.
- Lowe, Wilson — metabolic causes of Fanconi.
- Senior-Løken — retinitis pigmentosa; Joubert — vermis aplasia/coloboma; Bardet-Biedl — obesity, diabetes, polydactyly; Jeune / Ellis–van Creveld — skeletal dysplasia.

**Landmark trials** — none named in this stretch.
**Dates and legislation** — none.

**Absolute contraindications**
- Immunosuppression in congenital nephrotic syndrome (non-responsive; not recommended).
- Routine VCUG/cystoscopy for isolated microscopic haematuria (not indicated).

**Relative contraindications**
- Platelet transfusion in STEC+ HUS (only if bleeding or pre-procedure).
- Renal biopsy before steroids in primary nephrotic syndrome.

**When to operate / intervene; when not to**
- Biopsy: SRNS; persistently low C3/atypical PSGN; MPGN/C3GN; persistent proteinuria with normal screen; HSP with nephrotic syndrome or RPGN (treatment guidance); IgAN diagnosis.
- Type 4 RTA from obstructive uropathy → urological evaluation and surgery.
- MCDK → VCUG (especially with hydronephrosis/hydroureter).
- Trauma, flank pain, clots with gross haematuria → urological evaluation, CT.
- Do not: biopsy before steroids; image routinely for isolated microscopic haematuria.

**Classic traps**
- Day-1 creatinine is maternal.
- Red urine = non-glomerular; brown = glomerular.
- Heme-positive dipstick without RBCs = haemoglobin/myoglobin.
- IgAN haematuria concurrent with URTI vs PIGN weeks later.
- Low C3 + normal C4 (PIGN/C3GN) vs low C3 + C4 (lupus/MPGN).
- Proximal RTA can acidify urine below threshold.
- Type 4 RTA hyperkalaemia out of proportion to renal insufficiency.
- MCDK associated anomalies → VCUG despite a non-functioning kidney.

### Instalment 2 — ADPKD and Nephronophthisis, Urolithiasis cross-reference, Hypertension, Acute Kidney Injury (Table 33.1), Chronic Kidney Disease (Table 33.2), ESKD: Dialysis and Transplantation, Summary
(PDF pp 7–10; book pp 624–627. Level A sections: 6; Level B: 0; numbered tables: 2.)

#### The numbers to lock in
**Key facts, figures, thresholds and cut-offs**
- Hypertension: repeated BP >95th percentile; prevalence 3–4%; AAP normal <90th percentile (1–13 y) or <120/80 (≥13 y); white-coat up to ¼.
- Screening ultrasound not recommended >6 y unless abnormal urinalysis/function; echo at drug start.
- Targets <90th percentile; adolescents <130/80; CKD/diabetes <50th (KDIGO 50th in CKD).
- AKI: SCr ↑ ≥0.3 mg/dL or ≥50%, or UO <0.5 mL/kg/h ≥6 h; KDIGO stages per Table 33.1.
- AKI ~4/1000 admissions; up to ⅓ ICU; dialysis-requiring 1–2% of critically ill; mortality 15% vs 0.6%.
- Creatinine rises after ~50% GFR loss; lags 24–72 h. FeNa <1% functional, >3% intrinsic; FeUrea <35% prerenal on diuretics. Early AKI: assume GFR <15.
- AKI → CKD: >⅓ at discharge; ~⅔ long term.
- CKD >3 months; staging excludes <2 y and <3 months; stages ≥90 / 60–89 / 30–59 / 15–29 / <15.
- Malnutrition up to 20%; height ≈1.5 SD below; dyslipidaemia 39–65%; hypertension >half (<50% controlled); CV ≈ half of dialysis deaths; developmental problems up to 25% of infants.
- Dialysis at GFR <15 or complications; PD <9 y and <20 kg; PD 8–12 h overnight; HD >10 y, 3–4×/week, 4 h.
- Transplant: <18 y = <5% of US transplants; ⅔ deceased / ⅓ living; graft half-life 12.5 y deceased vs 20 y living; ≥10 kg and 65 cm; primary graft dysfunction <3%; DGF 5% living vs 15% deceased; year-1 ACR <10% living vs ~15% deceased; malignancy ≈7-fold.

**Gold standards, commonest, first-line**
- Essential hypertension commonest in obese >6 y with family history and adolescents; coarctation/renovascular <1 y; renal parenchymal in school age.
- Lifestyle change first-line for stage 1/primary without end-organ damage.
- Isotonic fluid for prerenal AKI; CRRT = standard of care RRT in AKI.
- Erythropoietin deficiency = commonest cause of CKD anaemia; ESAs cornerstone, iron nearly all.
- CAKUT commonest cause of CKD in young children; glomerular disease in adolescence.
- PD commonest initial modality in small children; CCPD in most US children; HD in >10 y.
- Transplant = ultimate goal; induction thymoglobulin/basiliximab; maintenance CNI + MMF.

**Eponyms** — Wilms tumour (nephroblastoma); nutcracker (from Box 33.1, instalment 1).

**Landmark trials** — none named. **Guideline bodies:** AAP (hypertension), KDIGO (AKI staging, BP target), KDOQI (CKD staging).

**Dates and legislation** — none.

**Absolute contraindications**
- Transplant: active malignancy; uncontrolled infection.
- Potassium-containing fluids before urine output established (avoid).

**Relative contraindications**
- Peritoneal dialysis: omphalocele, gastroschisis, diaphragmatic hernia, bladder exstrophy ("commonly viewed as" contraindications).
- Transplant <10 kg / <65 cm (centre practice).
- Protein restriction in paediatric CKD (no benefit — not recommended).
- Nephrotoxins in AKI.

**When to operate / intervene; when not to**
- RRT: diuretic-resistant overload, nutrition need, refractory hyperkalaemia/metabolic derangement, symptomatic uraemia.
- Dialysis: CKD V or uncontrolled hypertension, overload, metabolic derangement, poor growth, uraemia.
- Pre-transplant: repair/reconstruct urinary tract; immunise.
- Native nephrectomy: chronic/recurrent pyelonephritis, refractory hypertension, uncontrolled nephrotic syndrome.
- Not: screening renal ultrasound >6 y with normal urinalysis/function; not excluding transplant for intellectual disability.

**Classic traps**
- No absolute creatinine defines paediatric AKI or mandates dialysis.
- FeNa unreliable on diuretics → FeUrea.
- CKD children are GH-resistant, not GH-deficient.
- Protein restriction does not slow paediatric CKD; BP control does.
- Echo at drug initiation, not at diagnosis.
- Vesicostomy does not preclude PD.
