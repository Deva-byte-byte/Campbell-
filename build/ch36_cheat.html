Chapter 36 — Pediatric Urogenital Imaging — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else
  • ALARA (as low as reasonably achievable): treat every child as radiation sensitive.
  • Diuretic renography (MAG3, mercaptoacetyltriglycine) = gold standard, obstructive vs non-obstructive hydronephrosis.
  • Furosemide 1 mg/kg only when dilated system maximally filled.
  • No paediatric T½ cut-off defines obstruction → serial scans, curve shape, upright drainage.
  • Bilateral hydroureteronephrosis + thick bladder + poor emptying → VCUG (voiding cystourethrogram) for PUV (posterior urethral valve).
  • Keyhole sign (thick bladder + dilated posterior urethra) → PUV.
  • DMSA (dimercaptosuccinic acid) lesion at ≥6 months = fixed scar; APN (acute pyelonephritis) keeps contour, scar loses it.
  • No ultrasound for routine undescended testis (Choosing Wisely); exception obese child.
  • Suspected torsion → explore; never delay for imaging.
  • Blood at meatus → retrograde urethrogram before catheter.

Technique

  • Sedation risk > contrast risk (NSF — nephrogenic systemic fibrosis; contrast nephropathy).
  • Fetal MR: avoid in first trimester (gene-expression concern).
  • Ultrasound: safe at all ages and all gestations.
  • Ionising harm greater in children: radiosensitive tissue + lifetime cumulative dose.
  • Guideline bodies: AAP, NICE, CPS, PSPN, EAU/ESPU; ACR Appropriateness Criteria.
  • MR hazards: radiofrequency heating/hyperthermia (rare); gadolinium NSF.
  • Risk literature: check estimated vs modelled vs measured risk.

Ultrasonography

  • Amniotic fluid: fetal skin dialysate <8–10 wk; fetal urine from 10–12 wk.
  • Most anomalies detectable by 20 wk; bladder visible by 10–12 wk.
  • Fetal bladder never fully empty; residual always.
  • Normal cortex: isoechoic/slightly hypoechoic to liver.
  • Prenatal ultrasound limited by oligohydramnios, habitus, bowel gas, fetal position → MRI.
  • Low cord insertion/abdominal wall defect → exstrophy/cloacal exstrophy.
  • Hydronephrosis underestimated ≤2 days postnatal and in dehydration.
  • SFU (Society for Fetal Urology) grades: 1 pelvis → 2 major calyces → 3 minor calyces → 4 + parenchymal thinning.
  • Always review images — reporting systems vary.
  • Transition dilated → non-dilated localises obstruction: UPJ (ureteropelvic junction), UVJ (ureterovesical junction) or urethra.
  • Megaureter/ureterocele: obstructive or not → renography or MRU (MR urography).
  • Cysts don't communicate; dilated calyces do.
  • MCDK (multicystic dysplastic kidney): unilateral, random variable-size cysts.
  • ARPKD: bilateral, enlarged homogeneously hyperechoic.
  • ADPKD: bilateral, parenchyma replaced by cysts.
  • Multilocular cystic nephroma = infancy; cystic Wilms = 2–4 yr.
  • Neonate + AKI (acute kidney injury) + hypertension + big kidney → renal vein thrombosis.
  • Suprarenal neonatal mass → adrenal haemorrhage; neonatal renal mass → mesoblastic nephroma.
  • Renal mass: ultrasound + age/presentation → contrast CT.
  • Stones: ultrasound first, not reflex CT (computed tomography)/KUB (kidney–ureter–bladder film).
  • Distal ureteric stones easier to see in children than adults.
  • Stone = echogenic + shadow; fat = echogenic, no shadow; twinkling on Doppler.
  • Hospitalised UTI: ultrasound before discharge to exclude obstruction.
  • Normal ultrasound ≠ no VUR; ≠ risk-stratified APN.
  • UTI failing to improve on antibiotics → ultrasound.
  • CeVUS (contrast-enhanced voiding urosonography): sulfur hexafluoride microspheres; AUC (area under curve) 0.97; false-negative 3%.
  • CeVUS uses: VUR screening/follow-up; post-transplant; video-urodynamics.
  • Intrarenal reflux: commonest lower pole; ↑ with VUR grade; linked to scarring.
  • Contrast ultrasound = contrast CT sensitivity, blunt abdominal trauma.
  • Trauma follow-up: ultrasound for perinephric haematoma/urinoma resolution.
  • Trauma work-up incomplete without delayed CT views or lateral bladder/urethral projections.
  • Clot vs tumour: supine/prone — clot moves.
  • Midline pelvic cyst: hydrometrocolpos, ovarian cyst, bladder, urinary ascites, utricle.
  • Hydrometrocolpos: imperforate hymen, vaginal atresia, transverse septum, urogenital sinus/cloaca.
  • Dilated utricle: midline, posterior to male bladder.
  • Oligohydramnios + dual pelvic cysts below bladder → cloaca (hemivaginas).
  • Viral cystitis: upper respiratory prodrome + gross haematuria + frequency; thick wall mimics mass.
  • Exstrophy: no bladder, low cord, short phallus.
  • Prune belly vs PUV: empty scrotum, no thick bladder/keyhole.
  • Whirlpool sign: sensitivity ~96% vs ~76% colour Doppler.
  • Appendage torsion: avascular upper-pole nodule + surrounding hyperaemia.
  • No arterial flow + heterogeneous parenchyma → non-viable testis.
  • Fountain sign → acute idiopathic scrotal oedema.
  • Scrotal ultrasound overused — history/examination diagnose most; don't overrule examination.
  • Commonest prepubertal testis tumour: teratoma (solid/cystic/calcified).
  • Genital imaging, four indications: acute scrotum, masses, cryptorchidism, ambiguous genitalia.
  • Yolk sac tumour: homogeneous, vascular; epidermoid: onion rings, no flow.
  • CAH (congenital adrenal hyperplasia): bilateral testicular adrenal rests.
  • DSD/non-palpable gonads + hypospadias: ultrasound first for müllerian structures.

Magnetic Resonance Imaging

  • Sedation: usually needed <6 yr; protocol ~1 h (short ~30 min).
  • MRU furosemide: 1 mg/kg, max 20 mg; gadolinium.
  • Fetal MRI with third-level obstetric ultrasound after ~20 wk.
  • MRU: no radiation; equivalence to renography unproven.
  • Rhabdomyosarcoma: low T1, heterogeneous high T2; no subtype prediction.
  • MRI beats CT: synchronous contralateral renal lesions.
  • Renal tumour staging: MRI locoregional; CT (some) for distant metastasis.
  • Bladder MRI: urine masks T2 lesions → T1 before gadolinium.
  • MRU function: Patlak method; complex post-processing limits uptake.
  • No role: acute scrotum, scrotal masses.
  • Undescended testis: MRI marginally better than ultrasound at best.
  • DSD: ultrasound + genitography suffice; MRI for very complex only.
  • Avoid sedation: breath-hold/respiratory triggering (longer), child life specialists.

Conventional Radiography

  • IVP (intravenous pyelography): obsolete.
  • Retrograde pyelogram: needs anaesthesia; intraoperative localisation.
  • KUB for constipation: little evidence; persuades sceptical parents.

Voiding Cystourethrography

  • After UTI: VCUG once improving on antibiotics — no 4–6-week wait.
  • Early VCUG in febrile UTI may overgrade VUR (endotoxin).
  • Hydronephrosis: VCUG only if dilated ureter(s).
  • Catheter off midline: utricle/posterior urethra (male), ectopic ureter (female).
  • Bladder capacity: (age + 2) × 30 mL, max 400 mL.
  • VCUG masses: urachal/bladder diverticula, dilated utricle, fibroepithelial polyp.
  • Upper-tract malformation benefiting from VCUG: UVJ anomalies only.
  • Occult VUR: contralateral new VUR 7–33%; VCUG finds VUR in ~40% of DMSA-proven APN.
  • PIC (positional instillation of contrast): cystoscopic, 1 m; negative VCUG + recurrent febrile UTI.
  • Any UTI in circumcised boy may merit evaluation (risk very low).
  • Recurrent epididymitis in boys → VCUG (ectopic ureter, utricle).
  • Urogenitogram → Shopfner; low urethrovaginal confluence = type III.
  • Pie-in-the-sky bladder + extraperitoneal contrast → pelvic-fracture bladder injury.
  • VCUG scout film: spinal abnormalities.

Computed Tomography

  • Chosen for speed, not accuracy: blunt trauma, polytrauma.
  • Arterial phase only misses collecting system/ureter → delayed images.
  • CT cystography: classifies intra- vs extraperitoneal rupture.
  • Paediatric renal tumours: contrast CT routine.
  • CT/MR for UTI: abscess, fistula, postoperative, non-ionising inconclusive.
  • Stone CT: better burden estimate, rarely changes management.
  • CT scrotum: no role except testicular tumour staging.

DMSA Renal Cortical Scintigraphy

  • Tubular uptake, flow-dependent; APN = photopenia.
  • DMSA timing: days for APN; ≥6 months for scar.
  • DMSA for APN: sensitivity 87%, specificity 100% (piglet model).
  • Acute lesions persist in 36–52%; resolve over ~5 months.
  • DMSA beats ultrasound for APN and scarring.
  • Top-down: DMSA first, VCUG only if abnormal; bottom-up: VCUG → overtreats low-grade VUR.

MAG-3 Diuresis Renography

  • Four elements: hydration, ROI (region of interest)/background, bladder catheter, diuretic timing.
  • T½ from maximal filling, not tracer injection.
  • Progression predictors: biphasic curve; poor upright drainage.
  • Dietl's crisis: cortical tracer retention up to 48 h.
  • Catheter: removes bladder-filling effect, VUR confusion, gonadal dose.
  • Wrong ROI → spurious function/drainage; ROI can localise UPJ vs UVJ.
  • Renography vs ultrasound: more reproducible, quantifiable; SFU 3 can hide high-grade UPJ obstruction.

Radionuclide Cystography and Testicular Scanning

  • Direct: more sensitive, less radiation than VCUG; poor anatomy; grading hard to match.
  • Indirect: no catheter, insensitive → not routine.
  • Testicular scan (99mTc-pertechnetate): cold = torsion, hot = inflammation; ≤100% accurate; only if no surgical delay.
Classic traps
  • Trap: neonatal dark pyramids = hydronephrosis — Actually: normal newborn kidney.
  • Trap: day-1 scan excludes hydronephrosis — Actually: underestimates ≤2 days.
  • Trap: dilated ureter = obstruction — Actually: may be VUR.
  • Trap: normal ultrasound excludes VUR — Actually: poor predictor.
  • Trap: T½ from tracer injection — Actually: from maximal filling.
  • Trap: dehydrated renogram shows obstruction — Actually: poor diuresis mimics it.
  • Trap: one normal renogram excludes obstruction — Actually: may progress; repeat.
  • Trap: free retrograde flow = patent — Actually: flap may block antegrade.
  • Trap: hyperaemic epididymis = epididymitis — Actually: also post-detorsion.
  • Trap: arterial-phase CT clears collecting system — Actually: needs delayed phase.
