Chapter 33 — Urologic Aspects of Pediatric Nephrology — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else
  • CAKUT (congenital anomalies of kidney and urinary tract) = commonest cause of paediatric ESKD (end-stage kidney disease).
  • Neonatal creatinine = maternal for 48 h; own by day 7 (<0.5 mg/dL term).
  • Microscopic haematuria: >5 red cells/high-power field; no routine voiding cystourethrogram (VCUG) or cystoscopy.
  • Nephrotic: urine protein:creatinine ratio (UPCR) >2.0 mg/mg (or >40 mg/m²/h) + low albumin + hyperlipidaemia.
  • No biopsy before steroids; biopsy steroid-resistant (SRNS; no remission 6–8 weeks) → focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS).
  • Low C3, normal C4 → post-infectious GN (PIGN)/C3 glomerulopathy; low C3 + C4 → lupus/membranoproliferative GN (MPGN).
  • Atypical haemolytic uraemic syndrome (aHUS) → eculizumab; Shiga toxin E. coli (STEC+) HUS → supportive, avoid platelets.
  • Type 4 renal tubular acidosis (RTA) from obstructive uropathy → urological surgery.
  • Acute kidney injury (AKI): serum creatinine (SCr) ↑ ≥0.3 mg/dL/48 h or ≥1.5× or urine output (UO) <0.5 mL/kg/h ≥6 h.
  • Transplant only after ≥10 kg, 65 cm and lower-tract reconstruction.

Renal Function and Electrolyte Homeostasis

  • Renal blood flow: 3–4% → 12–16% cardiac output in year 1.
  • GFR (glomerular filtration rate): doubles by 2 weeks; term ≈40; adult by 2 years.
  • Total body water 70–90% in neonates.
  • Neonatal free-water loss 1–2% body weight/day, first 5 days.
  • Dilute to 50 mOsm/L; concentrating ability limited.
  • Na⁺: negative week 1 (atrial natriuretic peptide) → positive by 2–3 weeks.
  • K⁺ higher in neonates: aldosterone high, tubule unresponsive.
  • Bicarbonate low; matures over 3–4 weeks; proximal tubule reabsorbs 80%.

Haematuria

  • Isolated microscopic ≈1% school-age; <25% persist; most resolve by 1 year.
  • Definitive diagnosis <30% microscopic; majority of gross.
  • Commonest cause of gross haematuria: urinary tract infection.
  • Tea-coloured + RBC casts → glomerular; red → non-glomerular.
  • Heme-positive, no RBCs → haemoglobinuria/myoglobinuria.
  • Trauma, flank pain, clots → urology + computed tomography.
  • Glomerular work-up: C3, C4, antistreptolysin O/anti-DNase B, albumin → nephrology.
  • Macroscopic-only causes: urethrorrhagia, tumour, nutcracker, renal vein thrombosis.

Proteinuria

  • UPCR normal: <0.7 newborn, <0.2 mg/mg child; <4 mg/m²/h; adult <150 mg/day.
  • Transient = >75% of isolated proteinuria (fever, exercise).
  • Orthostatic: first-morning UPCR <0.2, raised upright; adolescents; benign.
  • Fixed → full work-up; systemic → C3, C4, antinuclear antibody; normal screen → biopsy.

Glomerular Disease

  • CNS (congenital nephrotic syndrome): <3 months; no immunosuppression.
  • Secondary nephrotic ≈10%; ~30 genes in primary.
  • Steroid-sensitive (SSNS): remission ≤8 weeks; ~50% relapse repeatedly.
  • Frequently relapsing: ≥2 relapses/6 months or ≥4/12 months.
  • Steroid-dependent: 2 consecutive relapses on steroids.
  • SRNS: 15–20%; no remission 6–8 weeks → biopsy.
  • Steroid-sparing: cyclophosphamide, mycophenolate mofetil (MMF), rituximab, calcineurin inhibitors (CNIs).
  • Minimal change (MCNS): 90% <10 years, 70–80% overall; median 2.5 years; ~90% steroid remission.
  • FSGS: <25% steroid-responsive; recurs post-transplant, graft loss.
  • Membranous: rare, mostly secondary (SLE); primary → anti-PLA2R.
  • Post-streptococcal GN (PSGN): weeks after infection; rare <3 years; C3 <50%.
  • PSGN recovery: oedema 5–10 d; BP 2–3 weeks; C3 normal 8–12 weeks.
  • PSGN biopsy if persistently low C3 or atypical course.
  • MPGN/C3GN: rare <5 years; biopsy required.
  • IgA nephropathy (IgAN): commonest primary GN; haematuria with upper respiratory infection (URTI); biopsy only definitive.
  • IgAN ESKD 20–30%; progression: proteinuria, hypertension, ↑ creatinine.
  • Henoch-Schönlein purpura: clinical diagnosis; relapses 4 months; abnormalities 2 years in 10–20%.
  • Alport: X-linked COL4A5 mostly; deafness; ~50% males ESKD by 25.
  • Antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody (ANCA) GN: untreated 90% dead in 2 years; ~¼ ESKD.
  • Granulomatosis with polyangiitis vs microscopic polyangiitis: upper-airway granulomas only in former.
  • Anti-glomerular basement membrane: α3(IV) NC1; after shockwave lithotripsy or Alport transplant.
  • HUS = common cause of severe AKI in healthy young child.
  • STEC+ HUS: O157:H7 >70%; ~half dialysis; 5–25% later CKD.
  • aHUS ~10%: complement defect; ADAMTS13 to exclude thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura; relapses post-transplant.

Tubulopathies

  • RTA: normal anion gap acidosis, urine pH >5.5, positive urine anion gap.
  • Proximal RTA: HCO₃⁻ 14–17 mEq/L; urine pH can fall <5.5.
  • Fanconi: glucosuria, aminoaciduria, phosphaturia; cystinosis common cause.
  • Fanconi drugs: cisplatin, ifosfamide, valproate.
  • Distal RTA: hypokalaemia, hypercalciuria, nephrocalcinosis, rickets.
  • Type 4: hyperkalaemia; CNIs, anti-inflammatories, obstruction.

Cystic Renal Disease

  • Multicystic dysplastic kidney (MCDK): non-communicating cysts; bilateral → lethal.
  • MCDK anomalies up to 25% (vesicoureteral reflux, pelviureteric obstruction, dysplasia) → VCUG.
  • MCDK follow-up: 3-monthly year 1 → 6-monthly to involution/≥5 years.
  • Autosomal recessive polycystic kidney disease (ARPKD): PKHD1; 1:40,000; hepatic fibrosis; >75% ESKD by 5 years.
  • ARPKD neonatal mortality 25–50% with oligohydramnios.
  • Autosomal dominant (ADPKD): PKD1/PKD2; 1:1000; adult presentation usually.
  • MCDK long-term: hypertension/CKD risk → annual review into adulthood.
  • Nephronophthisis: polyuria, anaemia → ESKD early adolescence.
  • Senior-Løken — retinitis pigmentosa; Joubert — vermis aplasia.
  • Bardet-Biedl — obesity, polydactyly; Jeune/Ellis–van Creveld — skeletal.

Hypertension

  • Hypertension: repeated blood pressure (BP) >95th percentile; prevalence 3–4%.
  • Normal (American Academy of Pediatrics): <90th percentile age 1–13; <120/80 age ≥13.
  • White-coat: up to ¼.
  • Emergency = end-organ injury; urgency = none.
  • <1 year: coarctation or renovascular.
  • School age: renal parenchymal (reflux nephropathy).
  • Obese >6 years, family history; adolescents → essential.
  • No screening ultrasound >6 years unless abnormal urinalysis/function.
  • Echo at drug start, not at diagnosis.
  • First-line: lifestyle (stage 1/primary, no end-organ damage).
  • Drugs if: stage 2, end-organ damage, symptoms, secondary, lifestyle failure.
  • Target: <90th percentile; <130/80 adolescents; CKD/diabetes <50th.

Acute Kidney Injury

  • KDIGO (Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes) stage I: 1.5–1.9× or ≥0.3; UO <0.5 for 6–12 h.
  • Stage II: 2.0–2.9×; UO <0.5 ≥12 h.
  • Stage III: ≥3×, SCr ≥4.0, eCrCl <35; UO <0.5 ≥24 h or <0.3 ≥12 h.
  • Incidence: ~4/1000 admissions; up to ⅓ of intensive care admissions.
  • Mortality 15% vs 0.6% without AKI.
  • Creatinine lags 24–72 h; rises only after ~50% GFR loss.
  • Fractional excretion of sodium <1% prerenal; >3% intrinsic; on diuretics urea <35%.
  • Prerenal: vigorous isotonic fluid, no K⁺ until urine flows.
  • Early AKI: dose drugs as GFR <15.
  • Renal replacement therapy if: refractory overload, K⁺/metabolic, uraemia, nutrition need.
  • Gold standard modality: CRRT (continuous renal replacement therapy).
  • AKI → CKD in ~⅔ on long follow-up.

Chronic Kidney Disease

  • Chronic kidney disease (CKD): abnormality >3 months; staging excludes <2 years.
  • Stages (GFR): I ≥90, II 60–89, III 30–59, IV 15–29, V <15.
  • Young child causes: CAKUT (posterior urethral valves, prune belly), cystic; adolescents: glomerular.
  • Height ≈1.5 SD below; growth hormone (GH) resistance, not deficiency.
  • Malnutrition up to 20%; dyslipidaemia 39–65%.
  • Anaemia: erythropoietin deficiency → erythropoiesis-stimulating agent + iron.
  • Cardiovascular ≈ half of deaths on dialysis.
  • Developmental problems up to 25% of infants.
  • CKD mineral bone disease: calcitriol, vitamin D₃, phosphate binders.
  • Protein restriction: not recommended.
  • Only proven brake: BP control → 50th percentile (KDIGO).

ESKD: Dialysis and Transplantation

  • Dialysis start: GFR <15 or complications; no absolute creatinine.
  • Peritoneal dialysis (PD) first <9 years and <20 kg.
  • PD contraindications: omphalocele, gastroschisis, diaphragmatic hernia, bladder exstrophy.
  • Vesicostomy does not preclude PD.
  • Haemodialysis predominant >10 years; 3–4×/week, 4 h.
  • Goal = transplant; pre-emptive if living related donor.
  • Absolute contraindications: active malignancy, uncontrolled infection.
  • Native nephrectomy: recurrent pyelonephritis, refractory hypertension, nephrotic syndrome.
  • Graft 50% survival: living 20 years; deceased 12.5 years.
  • Delayed graft function: 5% living, 15% deceased.
  • Year-1 rejection: <10% living, ~15% deceased.
  • Maintenance: tacrolimus/ciclosporin + MMF; steroid-free in low risk.
  • Primary graft dysfunction <3%; induction thymoglobulin or basiliximab.
  • Malignancy ≈7-fold (renal cell, thyroid, skin).
Classic traps
  • Trap: day-1 creatinine = neonatal function — Actually: maternal until 48 h.
  • Trap: image microscopic haematuria with VCUG/cystoscopy — Actually: not indicated.
  • Trap: biopsy new childhood nephrotic syndrome — Actually: steroids first.
  • Trap: immunosuppress congenital nephrotic syndrome — Actually: unresponsive; not recommended.
  • Trap: IgAN haematuria weeks after URTI — Actually: concurrent; weeks = PIGN.
  • Trap: platelets for STEC+ HUS — Actually: avoid unless bleeding/procedure.
  • Trap: proximal RTA cannot acidify urine — Actually: pH <5.5 below threshold.
  • Trap: CKD child is GH-deficient — Actually: GH-resistant.
  • Trap: restrict protein in paediatric CKD — Actually: no benefit; BP control.
  • Trap: intellectual disability excludes transplant — Actually: not automatically.
