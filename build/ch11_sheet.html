Chapter 11 — Perioperative Care in Urologic Surgery — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else
  • ≥4 METs (metabolic equivalents; 2 flights of stairs) → no further cardiac testing
  • Elective surgery after MI (myocardial infarction): wait 4–6 weeks
  • Warfarin: stop 5 days → INR (international normalised ratio) <1.5
  • Aspirin/clopidogrel: stop 7–10 days; DES (drug-eluting stent) → delay >1 year
  • Stop smoking ≥8 weeks; <8 weeks may ↑ risk
  • Antibiotic: within 60 min of incision; ≤24 h; redose >4 h
  • Normothermia 36–38°C; 1°C drop → ↑ bleeding, ↑ SSI (surgical site infection)
  • Transfuse: rarely Hct (haematocrit) >30%; often <21%
  • TRALI (transfusion-related acute lung injury): commonest transfusion death
  • Stress steroids: hydrocortisone 50–100 mg IV at induction → 25–50 mg 8-hourly 24–48 h

Presurgical testing

  • Routine tests: never shown cost-effective; ASA (American Society of Anesthesiologists) status predicts better
  • ECG: age >40 or cardiac history
  • Pregnancy test: all women of childbearing age, morning of surgery
  • Chest radiograph, PT/PTT (prothrombin/partial thromboplastin time): not routine
  • ASA: III limits activity; IV constant threat to life; V moribund <24 h; VI brain-dead donor; E = emergency suffix
  • ASA grades physical status, not surgical risk; independent mortality predictor
  • Goldman heaviest: S3/JVD (jugular venous distension) 11; recent MI 10
  • Goldman risk: 6–12 → 7%; 13–25 → 14%; ≥26 → 78%
  • Modified Cardiac Risk Index: 6 predictors, 1 point each — ischaemic heart disease, congestive heart failure (CHF), cerebrovascular disease, high-risk surgery, insulin, creatinine >2 mg/dL

Cardiovascular evaluation

  • Major predictors: MI <7 days; MI 7 days–1 month; unstable angina; decompensated heart failure (HF); significant arrhythmia; severe valve disease
  • Intermediate: mild angina, prior MI/Q waves, compensated HF, diabetes, creatinine >2
  • Minor: age, abnormal ECG, non-sinus rhythm, prior stroke, uncontrolled hypertension
  • Old 3–6-month post-MI dictum: abandoned
  • DASI (Duke Activity Status Index): VO₂ = 0.43 × DASI + 9.6; METs = VO₂ ÷ 3.5
  • Surgery risk: high = major emergency/large shifts; intermediate = intraperitoneal, laparoscopic, robotic; low = endoscopic, superficial

Pulmonary and hepatobiliary

  • FEV₁ (forced expiratory volume in 1 s) <0.8 L or <30% predicted → high risk
  • Smokers: 4× pulmonary morbidity; up to 10× mortality
  • Restrictive fares better than obstructive (effective cough)
  • Jaundice visible: bilirubin >3 mg/dL
  • Child mortality: A 10%, B 30%, C 76–82%
  • MELD (Model for End-Stage Liver Disease): bilirubin, creatinine, INR — objective, more accurate

Special populations

  • Age independently predicts morbidity and mortality
  • Delirium may be first sign of sepsis/metabolic upset; regional anaesthesia, fewer opioids
  • BMI (body mass index) ≥45: 8–13 years' life lost; >3 coronary risk factors → non-invasive cardiac testing
  • Pregnancy fetal risk: preimplantation to ~15 weeks; malformation threshold 10 cGy
  • CT ~1 cGy; IVP (intravenous pyelogram) ~0.3 cGy → ultrasound first
  • Safest trimester: second
  • Pregnancy analgesia: opioids yes; NSAIDs (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs) no — ductus arteriosus
  • Severe malnutrition: >20 lb (~9 kg) loss in 3 months
  • TPN (total parenteral nutrition) preop 7–10 days: only if severely malnourished; harms well-nourished
  • Enteral preferred; 5–20 days preop → complications ↓10–15%
  • Postop PN if needs unmet by 7–10 days; commonest line complication = sepsis

Preparation for surgery

  • Only β-blockade (not nitrates/calcium-channel blockers) improved cardiac outcome — Mangano atenolol 1996
  • >6 months' abstinence = non-smoker risk
  • Glucose target: 80–250 mg/dL; stop long-acting oral agents; pump — keep basal rate
  • Hyperthyroid: atrial fibrillation (AF) ~20%; storm → iodine + steroids; continue propylthiouracil/methimazole + β-blocker
  • Hypothyroid: ↑ anaesthetic/opioid sensitivity
  • HPA (hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal) suppression: >20 mg prednisone >3 weeks or cushingoid; ≤5 mg — none
  • Supplement if suppressive dose within 1 year; minor local procedure — no stress dose
  • Warfarin half-life 36–42 h
  • Bridge (American College of Chest Physicians 2012): moderate and high thrombotic risk — low-molecular-weight heparin (LMWH) or IV unfractionated heparin (UFH)
  • High risk: any mitral valve; caged-ball/tilting-disc; CHADS₂ (heart failure, hypertension, age, diabetes, stroke) 5–6; venous thromboembolism (VTE) <3 months; severe thrombophilia; stroke/transient ischaemic attack <3–6 months
  • Low risk: bileaflet aortic, CHADS₂ 0–2, single VTE >12 months
  • Dual antiplatelet: BMS (bare-metal stent) 6 weeks; DES 12 months
  • Delay elective surgery: BMS ≥30 days; DES >1 year
  • Premature antiplatelet stop: 25–50% significant MI
  • Can't wait (cancer), DES: continue aspirin + clopidogrel
  • Mechanical bowel preparation (MBP): no benefit; ↑ SSI
  • Nichols–Condon 1977: oral neomycin + erythromycin; sepsis 43% → 9%
  • Oral antibiotic prep drawback: C. difficile
  • PEG (polyethylene glycol) preferred: elderly, renal, CHF, electrolytes, cirrhosis — non-absorbable
  • Enema still used: transrectal prostate biopsy
  • Descending colon flora: 10¹⁰–10¹² organisms/g
  • Ileus >72–96 h → look for mechanical obstruction
  • Alvimopan (Food and Drug Administration 2008) ↓ stay after cystectomy; gum chewing helps

Intraoperative management

  • Kurz 1996: hypothermia → 3× wound infection, +2.6 days
  • Mild hypothermia SSI range: 34–36°C
  • Harvard Medical Practice Study 1991: 48% adverse events surgical
  • Femoral neuropathy: retractor blade on psoas
  • Neuropathy: 2nd commonest anaesthetic liability claim
  • Arm abduction ≤90° supine
  • Lithotomy: both legs together; hips 80–100° flexion, 30–45° abduction
  • Flank: axillary roll caudal to axilla
  • Nerve map: ulnar groove; radial — spiral groove; median — elbow extension; sciatic — hamstring stretch; peroneal — fibular head
  • Hip position: no effect on femoral neuropathy
  • Steep Trendelenburg: avoid shoulder braces
  • SSI: ~40% of nosocomial infections; +7–10 days
  • Hair: clippers, immediately before
  • Timing: ≤30 min better than 31–60; at/after incision → ~2× SSI
  • Wound classes: clean / clean-contaminated (controlled entry) / contaminated (major break, transrectal ultrasound (TRUS) biopsy) / dirty
  • AUA (American Urological Association) default: single dose; PCNL (percutaneous nephrolithotomy) and prosthetics ≤24 h
  • Cystoscopy + sterile urine: no prophylaxis
  • Transurethral resection (prostate, bladder tumour): cefazolin or trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole
  • TRUS biopsy: fluoroquinolone or cephalosporin + aminoglycoside
  • Colon: add anaerobic cover (metronidazole)
  • Prosthetics: aminoglycoside + cephalosporin or vancomycin; routine vancomycin not recommended
  • Aztreonam replaces aminoglycoside in renal insufficiency
  • SWL (shock-wave lithotripsy): only with risk factors; penile surgery — likely none
  • AUA compliance: <60%

VTE prophylaxis

  • Urology VTE: 10–40% unprophylaxed (historic); 1–5% now
  • VTE: most important non-surgical death in urology
  • Prophylaxis: ↑ minor, not major, bleeding
  • Low risk (<40 y, minor, no factors): early ambulation
  • Moderate/high: UFH 5000 U 12-/8-hourly, or enoxaparin 40 mg daily, or intermittent pneumatic compression (IPC) if bleeding risk
  • Highest: drug + IPC
  • Enoxaparin 30 mg if creatinine clearance <30 mL/min
  • LMWH: less heparin-induced thrombocytopenia, irreversible; UFH: reversible, safe in renal failure

Anaesthesia, skin, transfusion, pain

  • Spinal hypotension 10–40%; post-dural puncture headache <2% (29G pencil-point); serious deficit 0.05%
  • MAC (minimum alveolar concentration): no movement to incision in 50%
  • Nitrous oxide: contraindicated — bowel obstruction, pneumothorax
  • Halothane: hepatitis 1 in 35,000; catecholamine sensitisation
  • Sevoflurane: inhalational induction, difficult airway, bronchospasm
  • Desflurane: faster recovery >3 h
  • Ketamine: hypovolaemic, asthmatic induction
  • Propofol: bronchodilator, nausea-free; hypotension
  • Succinylcholine: only depolariser; ~5 min; malignant hyperthermia, hyperkalaemia
  • TOF (train-of-four) ≥0.9 (was 0.7); quantify before extubation
  • Chlorhexidine 0.5% in spirit edges alcoholic povidone-iodine (clean surgery)
  • Packed red cell Hct ~70% vs whole blood 40%
  • Platelets: <50,000 + surgery; 50–100,000 + high-risk/dysfunction
  • Fresh frozen plasma: warfarin reversal, factor deficit, bleeding + INR >1.5; after 1 blood volume
  • Viral risk: human immunodeficiency virus/hepatitis C ~1 in 2 million; hepatitis B ~1 in 200,000
  • Platelet bacterial contamination: ~1 in 5000
  • Massive transfusion protocol: anticipated >10 units/24 h
  • New persistent opioid use: ~6% after surgery
  • NSAIDs: avoid in renal insufficiency
  • Epidural + general anaesthesia: ↓ perioperative mortality
Classic traps
  • Trap: stop smoking the week before — Actually: <8 weeks may ↑ risk
  • Trap: wait 3–6 months after MI — Actually: 4–6 weeks
  • Trap: modified index uses Goldman weights — Actually: 1 point each
  • Trap: thyroid storm in hypothyroid — Actually: hyperthyroid
  • Trap: MBP reduces leak — Actually: no benefit, ↑ SSI
  • Trap: antibiotic at incision fine — Actually: at/after incision doubles SSI
  • Trap: femoral neuropathy from hip flexion — Actually: psoas retractor compression
  • Trap: axillary roll in axilla — Actually: just caudal
  • Trap: TPN helps all malnourished — Actually: harms well/mildly malnourished
  • Trap: NSAIDs safe in pregnancy — Actually: ductus closure; use opioids
