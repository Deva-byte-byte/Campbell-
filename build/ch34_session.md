## Instalment 1a — Preamble, Maternal Fetal US, Fetal MRI, APRPD Grading (Tables 34.1–34.2), SFU Grading, UTD Grading System (Figs 34.4–34.5), Other Applications of US (pyramidal thickness)
*(PDF pp 11–14. The paste pp 11–16 held nine Level B sections, so it was split here.)*

### The numbers to lock in
- **Key facts / cut-offs**
  - UTD occurs in 1–3% of pregnancies and is the second commonest prenatal anomaly after cardiac defects.
  - Urine production starts at 14 wk. ACOG screening US at 18–20 wk (about 20 wk in the USA). Average scans: 2 in low-risk, 4 in high-risk pregnancies.
  - AFI is the sum of 4 pockets: normal 8–18 cm; oligohydramnios <5–6 cm; polyhydramnios 20–24 cm and above.
  - Physiological APD: <4 mm before 28 wk; <7 mm at ≥28 wk; <10 mm postnatally (with no calyceal or ureteral dilation).
  - Table 34.1 severity bands:
    - mild: 4–<7 mm (2nd trimester), 4–<9 mm (3rd);
    - moderate: 7–≤10 mm, 9–≤15 mm;
    - severe: >10 mm, >15 mm.
  - Table 34.2: "any pathology" rises from about 12% (mild) to 88% (severe). UPJ obstruction and PUV trend with grade; VUR does not (P = .10).
  - Great Ormond Street randomised trial: APD ≥15 mm and DRF >40%. Observed patients who lost function had APD 20–40 mm.
  - SFU grades:
    - 1 — barely splits the sinus;
    - 2 — major calyces dilated;
    - 3 — minor calyces dilated, parenchyma spared;
    - 4 — parenchymal thinning.
  - All SFU 3 kidneys kept DRF >40%; about 70% of SFU 4 did.
  - Pyramidal thickness ≤3 mm predicts pyeloplasty; PT <3 mm after day 3 of life is a risk factor.
- **Gold standard / commonest**
  - APD is the commonest prenatal system; SFU is the commonest postnatal system.
  - UTD is the consensus system that spans both periods.
- **Eponyms**: none.
- **Landmark trials**: none by name. The Great Ormond Street randomised trial of pyeloplasty vs observation gives short-term data only.
- **Dates**: SFU system introduced 1993; UTD consensus 2014.
- **Contraindications**: absolute — none; relative — none.
- **When to operate**: none in this stretch, apart from the APD/PT thresholds that predict surgery.
- **Classic traps**
  - Measuring APD in the sagittal plane.
  - Ignoring GA when reading APD.
  - Assuming grade predicts VUR.
  - Applying SFU prenatally.

## Instalment 1b — Prenatal Multidisciplinary Consultations, Counselling for Fetal LUTO Intervention, Counselling for Fetal Myelomeningocele Closure (ACOG/SMFM 2017), Prenatal UTD Risk Stratification A1 vs A2–3 (Figs 34.6–34.7)
*(PDF pp 15–16.)*

### The numbers to lock in
- **Key facts / cut-offs**
  - Fetal LUTO intervention has a complication rate of over 40%.
  - A1: 4–<7 mm (16–27 wk) or 7–<10 mm (≥28 wk), with all other parameters normal.
  - A2–3: ≥7 mm or ≥10 mm, or any abnormal parameter, including unexplained oligohydramnios.
  - Follow-up:
    - A1 found before 28 wk → repeat after 32 wk;
    - A2–3 → US every 4 wk;
    - both → postnatal US after 48 h.
- **Gold standard**: multidisciplinary prenatal consultation (obstetrics, urology, nephrology, neonatology; neurosurgery for myelomeningocele).
- **Landmark / guideline**: ACOG and Society for Maternal-Fetal Medicine 2017 — non-directive counselling and referral to a fetal therapy centre.
- **Contraindications**: absolute — none stated; relative — none stated.
- **When to operate**: fetal myelomeningocele closure only in women who meet the established criteria, at an expert centre.
- **Classic traps**
  - Expecting fetal myelomeningocele closure to improve the bladder.
  - Expecting LUTO shunting to prevent renal replacement.

## Instalment 2a — Prenatal Intervention for LUTO, Fetal Cystoscopy, PLUTO (Fig. 34.10), VAS vs Fetal Cystoscopy, Ruano Staging (Fig. 34.11), Serial Amnioinfusion (CoBRA, RAFT), Fetal Biomarkers (ERKNet 2022)
*(PDF pp 16–19. The paste pp 17–22 held two Level B sections and eleven Level C subsections of dense trial data, so it was split at the prenatal/postnatal boundary.)*

### The numbers to lock in
- **Key facts / cut-offs**
  - Amniotic fluid becomes fetal urine at 16 wk.
  - Oligohydramnios after 27 wk → favourable lung outcome; before 27 wk → poor outcome.
  - VAS before 16 wk gave better outcomes in one series.
  - Need for intervention ≈1 in 60,000.
  - Early series complication rate 44%: incomplete drainage, preterm labour, urinary ascites, chorioamnionitis.
  - Fetal cystoscopy fistula rate up to 10%.
  - Intervention window 18–24 wk (from the LUTO section); VAS/cystoscopy comparison included diagnoses at 16–26 wk.
  - Somatex shunt placed before 14 wk without early complications.
- **Gold standard / commonest**
  - Percutaneous VAS is the commonest technique.
  - Rodeck shunts lie flush on the abdominal wall (the Harrison shunt migrated).
- **Eponyms**
  - Rodeck shunt: lies flush on the fetal abdominal wall, so less migration.
  - Harrison shunt: the original design, prone to migration.
  - Ruano staging: stage II is the intervene-to-save-kidneys stage.
- **Landmark trials**
  - **PLUTO**: 31 randomised, stopped early for poor recruitment.
    - Alive at 28 days: 8/16 VAS vs 4/15 conservative.
    - No renal benefit.
    - Complications 7/16 (44%).
  - **RAFT**: serial amnioinfusion for anhydramnios; ongoing.
  - 1982: first fetal intervention series.
- **Guideline**: ERKNet 2022 — do not use amniotic fluid volume, echogenicity or fetal urine biomarkers to predict postnatal renal function.
- **Contraindications**
  - Absolute: Ruano stage I (intervention not indicated). CoBRA for shunting or cystoscopy (no urine, so ineffective).
  - Relative: stage III (lungs only; kidneys not saved); abnormal karyotype or other anomalies (excluded in the comparative series).
- **When to operate**: severe LUTO with preserved-function markers (stage II), with early oligohydramnios, at 18–24 wk.
- **Classic traps**
  - "FCA" in the source means fetal cystoscopy.
  - Survival benefit is not the same as renal benefit.
  - Only cystoscopy improved renal function in PUV.

## Instalment 2b — Postnatal Evaluation: Renal US/Resolution, P1–P3 Risk (Fig. 34.12), VCUG (AAP), CeVUS, Renal Scintigraphy (MAG3, well-tempered renogram, DMSA; Fig. 34.13), MRU
*(PDF pp 20–22.)*

### The numbers to lock in
- **Key facts / cut-offs**
  - First US after 48 h; third-spaced fluid mobilises over 24–48 h; the scan can be delayed up to 2 wk.
  - P1: APD 10–<15 mm or central calyces.
  - P2: ≥15 mm with peripheral calyces or ureter.
  - P3: ≥15 mm and/or parenchymal or bladder abnormality.
  - Resolution: SFU 1 → 100% by about 2.5 yr; SFU 2 → 90–95% by 4 yr.
  - VUR in up to 31% of prenatal UTD; up to 25% of those with VUR have a normal first US.
  - Inpatient VCUG if the bladder wall is ≥0.5 cm.
  - MAG3: about 50% proximal tubular extraction per pass.
  - MAG3 surgery triggers: DRF <40%, T½ >20 min, delayed upright retention.
- **Gold standard**
  - MAG3 over DTPA for obstruction.
  - VCUG for urethral anatomy (CeVUS cannot show it).
  - DMSA for cortical defects, MCDK non-function and duplex polar function.
- **Eponyms**: Conway "well-tempered" renogram — standardised hydration, bladder drainage, doses, timing and interpretation.
- **Guideline**: AAP standardised VCUG — ALARA, non-balloon catheter, bladder cycling, KUB/filling/voiding images.
- **Contraindications**: absolute — none; relative — outpatient VCUG when outlet obstruction is suspected.
- **When to operate**: MAG3 criteria above, in the asymptomatic infant.
- **Classic traps**
  - Scanning before 48 h.
  - Giving furosemide too early into a capacious system → false-positive obstruction.
  - Region-of-interest errors.
  - Assuming APD predicts VUR.

## Instalment 3a — Management P1/P2/P3, Severe Bilateral UTD, Urinary Tract Infection (Fig. 34.14), Circumcision, Prophylactic Antibiotics, Etiology (Table 34.3)
*(PDF pp 23–25. The paste pp 23–28 held ten Level B sections, so it was split before Transient UTD.)*

### The numbers to lock in
- **Key facts / cut-offs**
  - P1/SFU 1–2 resolves by 24–48 mo → second US at 3–6 mo; no PA, no VCUG.
  - P2: PA if ureter >7 mm; otherwise at discretion.
  - P3: PA, VCUG and MAG3; second US at 1 mo; PA until UPJ surgery or 1 yr.
  - UTI in 8–22%; about 90% occur in the first year.
  - Circumcision NNT 10; OR about 0.3.
  - PA agents:
    - amoxicillin or first-generation cephalosporin from birth;
    - nitrofurantoin or trimethoprim from ≥8 wk.
  - Table 34.3 incidence:
    - transient 50–70%;
    - UPJ 10–30%;
    - VUR 10–40%;
    - UVJ 5–15%;
    - MCDK 2–5%;
    - duplex 5–7%;
    - LUTO 0.1–0.5%.
- **Gold standard / commonest**
  - Transient UTD is the commonest cause.
  - Agreed UTI risk factors: female sex, high-grade dilation, uncircumcised.
- **Landmark / guidelines**
  - SFU Hydronephrosis Registry vs McMaster registry (comparison built).
  - AAP supports circumcision of newborn males with urological anomalies.
  - 2014 UTD Consensus Statement.
- **Contraindications**
  - Absolute: none.
  - Relative: PA in P1; nitrofurantoin or trimethoprim under 8 wk.
- **When to act**: severe bilateral UTD with oligohydramnios → decompress, start prophylaxis, evaluate at once.
- **Classic traps**
  - Blanket prophylaxis.
  - Megacystis and bilateral VUR do not cause oligohydramnios.
  - Reading the registries' disagreement as a contradiction; they studied different cohorts.

## Instalment 3b — Transient UTD (Fig. 34.15), UPJ Obstruction Pattern and Recommendations, VUR (Figs 34.16–34.17) and Recommendations, Megaureter/UVJ Obstruction and BAPU Recommendations
*(PDF pp 25–28.)*

### The numbers to lock in
- **Key facts / cut-offs**
  - Transient UTD: resolution 41–88%; prenatal resolution 21–56%; mild postnatal dilation resolves in 90–98%.
  - UPJ APD thresholds:
    - 15 mm (sensitivity and specificity about 0.8; OR about 13);
    - 9.5 mm in the 2nd trimester;
    - postnatal 20 mm predicts preserved function.
  - DRF >40% likely if P2, APD <20 mm or PT >3 mm.
  - Severe UTD: about a fifth need surgery; about two-thirds resolve.
  - Prenatal VUR: male, bilateral, high grade; dysplasia in up to 40%.
  - Pooled resolution for prenatal VUR about 50% (2010 AUA guideline).
  - Megaureter:
    - most resolve (half by 7 yr; about three-quarters by 2 yr);
    - surgery predictors: diameter >≈13 mm, DRF <30%;
    - operated ureters average 17 mm vs 12 mm observed.
- **Gold standard / commonest**: UPJ obstruction is the commonest surgical cause.
- **Guidelines**: BAPU 2014 megaureter consensus; 2010 AUA VUR guideline.
- **Contraindications**
  - Absolute: none.
  - Relative: definitive reimplantation under 1 yr → temporise (cutaneous ureterostomy, pyelostomy, refluxing reimplant).
- **When to operate**
  - UPJ: rising APD/UTD; DRF <40% with delayed drainage; falling DRF; upright retention; stone, pyelonephritis, haematuria.
  - Megaureter: DRF <40%, febrile UTI, pain.
  - **Do not operate** for dilation alone.
- **Classic traps**
  - Severe UTD does not automatically mean surgery.
  - Screen for VUR by UTI risk, not by grade.
  - Prenatal VUR resolution is not better in pooled data.

## Instalment 4a — LUTO (Figs 34.18–34.20), Neonatal Bladder Drainage, PUV (Fig. 34.21), PBS/Megalourethra, Urethral Stenosis/Atresia (PADUA), Congenital Megacystis/MMIHS, Recommendations for LUTO (Fig. 34.22), MCDK (Fig. 34.23) and Recommendations
*(PDF pp 28–31. The paste pp 29–34 held ten Level B sections, so it was split after MCDK.)*

### The numbers to lock in
- **Key facts / cut-offs**
  - Highest LUTO risk: oligohydramnios before 27 wk.
  - Intervention window 18–24 wk.
  - Neonatal drainage: 5 Fr feeding tube, confirmed on KUB.
  - PUV 1.4–2.2 per 10,000 births.
  - MCDK: VUR about 20% (17% in a VCUG series); US matched scintigraphy in 100% when the bladder was normal; no Wilms association (review of >1,000 children).
  - Solitary kidney: BP, proteinuria and yearly US through puberty.
- **Gold standard / commonest**
  - PUV is the commonest LUTO.
  - Fetal cystoscopy is the only reliable way to establish the cause prenatally.
- **Eponyms**: PADUA (progressive augmented dilation of the urethra) for anterior urethral stenosis/atresia after vesicostomy.
- **Contraindications**
  - Absolute: balloon catheter in PUV; immediate VCUG in renal insufficiency needing decompression.
  - Relative: routine nephrectomy for MCDK; PA in MCDK.
- **When to operate**: MCDK that persists with a solid component → consider removal.
- **Classic traps**
  - The feeding tube can curl in the posterior urethra and still drain.
  - Creatinine may rise at first (under-resuscitation).
  - Anuria after decompression is from a thick bladder → antimuscarinics.
  - MCDK can look like P3 UTD prenatally.

## Instalment 4b — Duplication Anomalies/Ureterocele/Ectopic Ureter (Figs 34.24–34.28) and Recommendations, Exstrophy: Bladder (Fig. 34.29) and Cloacal (Table 34.4), Cloacal Malformation (Fig. 34.30), Urogenital Sinus/CAH
*(PDF pp 31–34.)*

### The numbers to lock in
- **Key facts / cut-offs**
  - Duplex anomalies: mostly female and bilateral.
  - Ureterocele with high-grade VUR → excision in 96%; low-grade VUR → 40% needed further surgery; no VUR → upper-pole surgery only.
  - Bladder exstrophy detected prenatally in about 50%.
  - Persistent cloaca 1 in 50,000 (females only).
  - CAH:
    - 21-hydroxylase deficiency (gene on chromosome 6p);
    - salt-wasting 75%, simple virilising 25%;
    - 1 in 15,000 (text elsewhere: 1 in 16,000);
    - 1 in 8 of at-risk pregnancies clinically affected.
- **Eponyms**
  - Meyer–Weigert rule: upper pole inserts caudomedially → obstruction/ectopia; lower pole inserts craniolaterally → reflux.
  - "Drooping lily": lower-pole VUR in a duplex system.
- **Table 34.4**
  - Major criteria: non-visualised bladder, infra-umbilical defect/cystic structure, omphalocele, lumbosacral anomalies.
  - Minor criteria: seven (limb, renal, ascites, wide pubic arches, narrow thorax, hydrocephalus, single umbilical artery).
- **Contraindications**: absolute — none; relative — routine prenatal ureterocele puncture (experimental).
- **When to operate**
  - Ureterocele puncture for outlet obstruction or sepsis (increasingly elective as first line).
  - Girl with high-grade lower-pole VUR and functioning upper pole → incision.
  - Boy with no VUR and dysplastic upper pole → upper-pole approach.
- **Classic traps**
  - Ureter more dilated than pelvis → think duplex.
  - Ureterocele = a single thin intravesical wall; ectopic ureter = two walls.
  - Bladder exstrophy is not an emergency.
  - Polyhydramnios with cloaca → tracheo-oesophageal fistula.

## Instalment 5a — Prenatal/Postnatal CAH Treatment, Fetal Myelomeningocele Outcomes, Upper Urinary Tract Obstruction (Fig. 34.31), Neonatal Emergencies, Abdominal Distension, Urinary Ascites, Urinary Retention (Fig. 34.32), Solid/Cystic Renal Masses, Adrenal Haemorrhage, Pelvic Cysts
*(PDF pp 34–37. The paste pp 35–40 held sixteen Level B sections and two tables, so it was split before Anorectal Malformation.)*

### The numbers to lock in
- **Key facts / cut-offs**
  - Fetal myelomeningocele closure before 26 wk → fewer VP shunts; no urodynamic, CIC or reconstruction benefit; complications are preterm labour and uterine dehiscence.
  - Urinary ascites: up to 30% of neonatal ascites; commonest cause PUV.
  - Percutaneous drainage: 16G → 3.5 Fr; 14G → 5 Fr.
  - Adrenal haemorrhage: right-sided; 10–15% bilateral; US every 1–2 wk.
  - Ovarian cysts in up to 30% of female neonates; most resolve by 6 mo.
- **Gold standard / commonest**
  - CMN is the commonest solid renal tumour under 6 months.
  - UPJ is the commonest site of obstruction.
  - 17-hydroxyprogesterone plus genetics confirm CAH.
- **Contraindications**: absolute — none; relative — none.
- **When to act**
  - Bladder perforation → catheter (most heal conservatively); iatrogenic rupture from an umbilical catheter → repair.
  - Failed catheterisation → Seldinger suprapubic drainage.
  - Some urogenital sinus cases → vesicostomy/vaginostomy.
- **Classic traps**
  - Hydrometrocolpos can mimic a distended bladder.
  - Adrenal haemorrhage calcifies as an eggshell; neuroblastoma calcification is stippled.
  - Scrotal haematoma from adrenal haemorrhage can mimic torsion.

## Instalment 5b — Anorectal Malformation, Ambiguous Genitalia, Interlabial Masses, Oliguria/Anuria, Gross Haematuria (Table 34.5; RVT/RAT/cortical necrosis), Scrotal Mass (Table 34.6), Neonatal Testicular Torsion, Key Points
*(PDF pp 37–40.)*

### The numbers to lock in
- **Key facts / cut-offs**
  - Anorectal malformation: genitourinary anomalies up to 75%; videourodynamics at 6 wk; prophylaxis until then.
  - CAH up to 1 in 16,000; commonest cause of ambiguity; 46,XX DSD is the commonest category.
  - Voiding: within 24 h. After circumcision, 75% of breastfed and 100% of formula-fed boys void within 8 h.
  - RVT: up to 20% of gross haematuria; up to 50% have a prothrombotic disorder.
  - Torsion salvage: postnatal up to 44%, prenatal nearly nil. Survey: 43% vs 93% treat as an emergency.
- **Gold standard / first-line**
  - Urinalysis with microscopy, then Doppler US, for haematuria.
  - Electrolytes plus karyotype, 17-OHP, testosterone and DHT for ambiguous genitalia.
  - RVT: hydration and expectant management ± anticoagulation.
  - RAT: remove the umbilical artery catheter ± thrombolysis.
- **Eponyms**: none.
- **Contraindications**
  - Absolute: immediate gender assignment.
  - Relative: inhalational anaesthesia in neonates (spinal preferred for exploration).
- **When to operate**
  - Postnatal torsion → immediate exploration.
  - Nonsalvageable prenatal torsion → urgent contralateral orchidopexy within the first weeks.
  - Imperforate hymen → incision.
  - **Do not operate**: urethral prolapse (conservative); parameatal cyst (resolves).
- **Classic traps**
  - Bloody diaper is not haematuria (oestrogen withdrawal, urates, dermatitis, circumcision).
  - RVT triad: haematuria, flank mass, thrombocytopenia.
  - Midline vs off-midline interlabial mass.
