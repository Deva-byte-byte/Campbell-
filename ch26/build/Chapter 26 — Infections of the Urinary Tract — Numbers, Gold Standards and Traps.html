Chapter 26 — Infections of the Urinary Tract — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

IF YOU READ NOTHING ELSE

  • Cystitis first-line: nitrofurantoin 100 mg twice daily × 5 d; TMP-SMX DS twice daily × 3 d (local resistance ≤20%); fosfomycin 3 g once; pivmecillinam
  • Fluoroquinolones = last resort for cystitis (FDA 2016)
  • Treat ASB only in pregnancy and before mucosa-breaching urological procedures
  • Symptomatic cystitis culture threshold ≥10² CFU/mL; Kass ≥10⁵ was for pyelonephritis
  • Recurrent UTI = 2 in 6 months or ≥3 in 12 months; no routine imaging/cystoscopy if uncomplicated
  • Vaginal (not oral) oestrogen prevents rUTI postmenopausally
  • Pyelonephritis febrile >72 h → CT for obstruction/abscess
  • Renal abscess ≤3 cm → antibiotics; >5 cm → percutaneous drainage; perinephric >3 cm → drain
  • Infected obstructed kidney → stent or nephrostomy plus antibiotics
  • AP: within 60 min (vancomycin/FQ 120 min); single dose default; ≤24 h max

DEFINITIONS AND EPIDEMIOLOGY

  • Complicated UTI: abnormal tract, compromised host, or virulent/resistant organism; male sex = complicated
  • Box 26.1 flags: pregnancy, elderly, DM, immunosuppression, catheter, instrumentation, hospital-acquired, symptoms >7 d
  • Reinfection = new inoculation (same organism >2 weeks, or new organism); persistence = same organism from focus
  • Prophylaxis presumes sterile tract; suppression targets ineradicable focus
  • CAUTI = commonest nosocomial infection (about 80% of nosocomial UTI); commonest cause of secondary bacteraemia
  • Commonest: E. coli (about 85% community, 50% hospital); S. saprophyticus about 10% cystitis in young women
  • About 70% of recurrences = reinfection, different organism
  • Uncomplicated rUTI → no scarring, hypertension or renal failure

PATHOGENESIS

  • Ascending route commonest; haematogenous = S. aureus or Candida
  • Type 1 pili (FimH) → uroplakins → cystitis; mannose-sensitive
  • P pili → glycolipid receptors → pyelonephritis; mannose-resistant
  • IBC → fluxing → QIR (quiescent reservoir) drives recurrence
  • Adult VUR harmless unless high pressure/stasis + UTI
  • Papillary necrosis: DM, analgesics, sickle cell, obstruction
  • HIV/AIDS: UTI about 5×; CD4 <200 → TMP-SMX (also lowers UTI)

EVALUATION, IMAGING, MICROBIOME

  • Nitrite: specific, insensitive; all Enterobacteriaceae +; Gram-positives, Pseudomonas −
  • Best dipstick combination: nitrite, or leucocyte esterase + blood
  • Pyuria: ≥5–10 WBC/HPF; >50 + symptoms justifies antibiotics
  • Sterile pyuria → TB, stones, urothelial injury, interstitial nephritis
  • Culture = gold standard; commensals: CoNS, diphtheroids, α/non-haemolytic streptococci
  • Culture +, no pyuria → catheterised sample
  • Catheter patient: replace catheter before sampling; stoma: catheterise stoma
  • Gram-positives in voided sample rarely cause cystitis alone
  • Painless haematuria → full urological evaluation
  • Stone culture > urine culture predicts post-PCNL sepsis
  • Non-contrast CT = stones; pelvic MRI = urethral diverticulum
  • Box 26.5: image pyelonephritis if no response after 5–6 d

ASYMPTOMATIC BACTERIURIA

  • Women: 2 voided ≥10⁵; men: 1 voided ≥10⁵; catheter: ≥10² CFU/mL
  • Long-term catheter ASB = 100%; long-term care 25–50% women
  • Don't treat: DM, elderly, SCI, catheters, children, renal transplant >1 month, non-urological surgery; pyuria not indication
  • Neutropenia: no consensus
  • Cai trial: treating ASB in rUTI ↑ recurrence (about 70% vs 38%) and resistance
  • Arthroplasty: ASB marks risk; treatment doesn't help

ANTIMICROBIALS

  • Nitrofurantoin: no tissue levels → never pyelonephritis; CrCl cut-off <30 (Beers 2015); G6PD haemolysis; pulmonary fibrosis
  • TMP-SMX: misses Enterococcus, Pseudomonas; commonest antibiotic cause of SJS/TEN
  • Fosfomycin: single dose; covers VRE, MDR; low cross-resistance
  • FQ adjust CrCl <30; FDA warnings: tendon rupture, neuropathy, FQAD, CNS, dysglycaemia, aortic dissection
  • Ceftriaxone misses Pseudomonas; ceftazidime covers
  • Cephalosporins never cover enterococci
  • Carbapenem gaps: E. faecium, MRSA; ertapenem also no Pseudomonas/Enterococcus
  • CDI: ≥3 unformed stools/24 h + toxin; fidaxomicin first line; vancomycin alternative; FMT after ≥2 recurrences; fulminant → oral vancomycin 500 mg + IV metronidazole
  • CDI culprits: cephalosporins, clindamycin, carbapenems, FQ, TMP-SMX

BLADDER INFECTIONS

  • Cystitis duration: 3 d standard; nitrofurantoin 5 d; fosfomycin single
  • Afebrile men: 7 d = 14 d (Drekonja 2021)
  • Complicated UTI: culture mandatory; 10–14 d; correct obstruction
  • Emphysematous cystitis: elderly diabetic women; gas in bladder wall; 90% medical
  • Unresolved bacteriuria — commonest cause: pre-existing resistance
  • Still-sensitive organism on treatment → investigate (azotaemia, papillary necrosis, staghorn)
  • Rapid reinfection mimicking unresolved → enterovesical fistula
  • Struvite: urease (Proteus); remove if fit; else acetohydroxamic acid + suppression
  • Observe asymptomatic non-obstructing stones ≤10 mm
  • Water ≥1.5 L/day halves cystitis (premenopausal)
  • Cranberry ≥36 mg PAC twice daily; safe with warfarin
  • Raz–Stamm 1993: vaginal oestriol cut UTIs about 90%; benefit ≥12 weeks
  • Methenamine hippurate 1 g twice daily: needs acid urine; CrCl <50 contraindicated; useless with urease-splitters; = antibiotic prophylaxis (RCT)
  • Postcoital > daily prophylaxis for coital rUTI

KIDNEY INFECTIONS

  • Outpatient pyelonephritis: ciprofloxacin 500 mg twice daily × 7 d if FQ resistance ≤10%; levofloxacin 750 mg × 5 d; TMP-SMX × 14 d only if susceptible
  • Complicated: 2–3 d IV if blood cultures negative, then oral 10–14 d
  • Solitary obstructed kidney → acute renal failure; dose by GFR
  • Emphysematous pyelonephritis: diabetic women; CT of choice; streaky gas, no fluid → 50–60% mortality; fluid/loculated gas <20%
  • EPN management: drain; nephrectomy for diffuse gas with destruction
  • Emphysematous pyelitis = collecting-system gas; benign
  • Renal abscess: now Gram-negative ascending; haematogenous → antistaphylococcal penicillin (vancomycin if allergic)
  • Perinephric abscess: symptoms >5 d, fever >4 d on antibiotics; aminoglycoside + antistaphylococcal
  • XGP: stones (staghorn) + non-function; Proteus; CT triad; nephrectomy (partial if focal)
  • Malakoplakia: Michaelis–Gutmann bodies, von Hansemann cells; women 4:1; α1-antitrypsin stain; intracellular antibiotics (FQ, TMP, rifampicin, doxycycline); bilateral/transplant → death within 6 months

OTHER INFECTIONS

  • Fournier's: source found about 95%; men 10:1; Buck's → dartos → Colles' → Scarpa's
  • LRINEC ≥6 suspicious, ≥8 strongly predictive
  • CT most specific; debride to normal fascia, re-look 24–48 h; orchidectomy rarely; mortality 20–40%

PREGNANCY AND ELDERLY

  • Screen by culture at 12–16 weeks (or first visit); dipstick misses
  • Untreated bacteriuria → pyelonephritis (historically 15–45%); 60–75% third trimester
  • GFR +30–50%; creatinine 0.5, urea 9 mg/dL; proteinuria abnormal >300 mg/24 h
  • Safe: penicillins, cephalosporins; avoid: FQ, tetracycline (D), chloramphenicol (grey baby), TMP first trimester, nitrofurantoin at term
  • After 1 pyelonephritis or 2 cystitis → nitrofurantoin/cephalexin suppression
  • Pregnant pyelonephritis: admit, IV β-lactam; ultrasound if no response 72 h
  • Elderly: dysuria key; confusion alone ≠ UTI → hydrate, reassess; never culture for clearance; short (3–6 d) = long course

CATHETERS, SCI, TRANSPLANT

  • CAUTI: catheter >2 d + symptom + ≥10⁵; bacteriuria 5–10%/day
  • Catheter significant bacteriuria ≥10²; pyuria non-discriminatory
  • Treat only symptomatic CAUTI; change catheter; meatal care with water only
  • SCI: CIC lowest long-term risk; no screening/treating ASB; no AP
  • SCI significant: indwelling any count; CIC ≥10²; voided men ≥10⁴
  • SCI duration: 5–7 d new afebrile, 7–10 d recurrent, 14 d febrile
  • Catheter >10 years → bladder cancer risk
  • Transplant: uncomplicated 5–10 d; pyelonephritis 14–21 d; stents out within 4 weeks

ANTIMICROBIAL PROPHYLAXIS

  • Diagnostic cystoscopy: no AP (EAU strongly against)
  • TURP: AP all; operating on infected urine → bacteraemia in half
  • SWL: no AP if urine benign; infection stones → full course first
  • PCNL, prostheses: ≤24 h; ureteroscopy single oral dose
  • AP at stent/catheter removal; urine culture poorly predicts stent organism
  • TRUS biopsy: FQ-resistant E. coli commonest cause; antibiotics within 6 months = main risk; swap FQ if local resistance >20%; ≥1 day
  • UDS AP: neurogenic LUTD, raised PVR, ASB, immunosuppression, age >70, catheters, joint <2 years
  • Endocarditis: AP not recommended; high-risk + infected urine → amoxicillin/ampicillin
  • Joint >2 years, pins/plates/screws → no AP; <2 years → FQ or ampicillin + gentamicin
  • Routine vancomycin AP not recommended

CLASSIC TRAPS

  • Trap: pyuria + bacteriuria = UTI — Actually: symptoms decide; treat neither alone
  • Trap: ≥10⁵ needed for cystitis — Actually: ≥10² CFU/mL with dysuria
  • Trap: nitrofurantoin for early pyelonephritis — Actually: no tissue levels; contraindicated
  • Trap: confused elderly + positive culture = UTI — Actually: hydrate and reassess
  • Trap: fever persisting on day 2 of pyelonephritis = failure — Actually: normal to 72 h; image after
  • Trap: negative urine excludes pyonephrosis/abscess — Actually: complete obstruction or haematogenous source gives sterile urine
  • Trap: oral oestrogen prevents rUTI — Actually: only vaginal oestrogen works
  • Trap: treat ASB before arthroplasty — Actually: no benefit; don't screen
  • Trap: cephalosporin covers enterococcus — Actually: never; use ampicillin
  • Trap: XGP on frozen section = clear cell RCC — Actually: foamy macrophages mimic it; remove whole mass
