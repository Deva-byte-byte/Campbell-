# 8-Week Home Workout Plan for Fat Loss (back-friendly, 3 kg dumbbells)

## Starting point

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Height | 171 cm |
| Weight | 65–66 kg |
| BMI | ~22.4 (normal range) |
| Daily steps | ~1,000 (very low) |
| Equipment | Pair of 3 kg dumbbells |
| Back | Mild lower-lumbar disc bulge, sacralisation (L5–S1), currently stable |
| Flexibility | Very tight hamstrings |

**What this means:** your weight is already in the healthy range, so the goal is to lose fat
(a realistic target is 61–63 kg) while keeping muscle. Your biggest problem isn't a lack of
workouts. It's that you barely move during the day, at about 1k steps. Walking more will
burn more calories than any single workout, so **steps are the #1 priority**. Walking is
also one of the best things you can do for a disc bulge.

Realistic rate: **0.25–0.5 kg per week**, or about 2–4 kg over 8 weeks.

---

## 0. Back rules (read first)

With a disc bulge and sacralisation, the lowest part of your spine handles load less well.
The plan follows these rules:

1. **Neutral spine under load.** Every exercise keeps your lower back in its natural curve.
   Bend at the hips, not the waist.
2. **No sit-ups, crunches, Russian twists, toe touches, or seated forward folds.** Repeated or
   loaded bending and twisting of the spine is the main thing that aggravates a disc bulge.
3. **No jumping for the first 4 weeks.** Cardio is low-impact.
4. **No back exercises in the first hour after waking.** Discs are most swollen then. Walking
   is fine.
5. **Brace before each rep.** Tighten your stomach as if someone were about to poke it, and
   keep breathing. Don't suck your stomach in.
6. **Pain rule:** mild muscle soreness is fine. Back pain above 3/10 during or after a
   session means you stop that exercise and go back one level.
7. **Stop and see a doctor right away** if you get pain, numbness or tingling down a leg,
   weakness in a foot, or any change in bladder or bowel control.

It's worth having a physiotherapist check your form once, especially the hip hinge.

## 2. Daily plan

You train every day, but the days rotate so the same muscles always get 48 hours of rest.

| Day | Session | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Daily back routine + **Lower body** | 35–40 min |
| Tue | Daily back routine + **Upper body** | 35–40 min |
| Wed | Daily back routine + **Hamstring & hip mobility** + long walk | 20 min + walk |
| Thu | Daily back routine + **Lower body** | 35–40 min |
| Fri | Daily back routine + **Upper body** | 35–40 min |
| Sat | Daily back routine + **Low-impact cardio circuit** | 25 min |
| Sun | Daily back routine only + walk | 10 min + walk |

### Warm-up (5 min, before Mon/Tue/Thu/Fri/Sat sessions)
March in place 1 min, arm circles 30s, cat-cow ×8 (gentle, small range), hip circles while
standing ×10 each way, bodyweight box squat to a chair ×10.

### Daily back routine (every day, ~10 min)
This is a core routine designed for disc problems. It builds endurance in the muscles that
hold your spine still without bending it.

| Exercise | How | Weeks 1–2 | Weeks 3–4 | Weeks 5–8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Modified curl-up | On your back, one knee bent, hands under your lower back. Lift only your head and shoulders 2–3 cm. Hold. | 5×8s | 6×10s | 4-3-2 reps of 10s holds |
| Side plank (knees bent) | Hips forward, straight line from knees to head | 3×10s each side | 4×10s | 5×10s, then progress to straight legs |
| Bird dog | On hands and knees, extend opposite arm and leg, keep hips level | 6×8s each side | 8×10s | 10×10s |
| Glute bridge | Squeeze glutes, don't arch your lower back at the top | 2×12 | 2×15 | 2×15 with 3s hold |

### Lower body (Mon & Thu)
3 kg is light, so these use slow lowering (3 seconds down) and single-leg work to make it harder.

| Exercise | Weeks 1–2 | Weeks 3–4 | Weeks 5–8 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Goblet box squat (hold 1 dumbbell at chest, sit back to a chair) | 3×10 | 3×12, 3s down | 4×12, 3s down, lower chair |
| Glute bridge (dumbbell on hips) | 3×15 | 3×12 single-leg (each) | 4×12 single-leg, 2s hold |
| Hip hinge / Romanian deadlift (both dumbbells) | 3×8 bodyweight, hands on hips, small range | 3×10 with dumbbells, stop when hamstrings tighten | 3×12, 3s down |
| Reverse lunge (dumbbells at sides, hold a wall if needed) | 2×8 each leg | 3×10 | 3×12 |
| Side-lying leg raise | 2×12 each side | 3×15 | 3×15 with 2s hold |
| Calf raise (dumbbells, on a step) | 3×15 | 3×20 | 3×15 single-leg |

**Romanian deadlift form:** soft knees, push your hips back as if closing a car door with your
bottom, dumbbells slide down your thighs, chest proud. Your hamstrings are tight, so you may
only reach just above your knees. That's correct. **Don't round your back to go lower.**

### Upper body (Tue & Fri)
| Exercise | Weeks 1–2 | Weeks 3–4 | Weeks 5–8 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Push-up (incline on bed/table → floor) | 3×8 incline | 3×10 lower incline | 4×8–12 floor (or knees) |
| Single-arm row, one hand and knee supported on a chair or bed | 3×12 each arm | 3×15 | 4×15, 2s squeeze at top |
| Overhead press (seated, back supported, or standing with glutes squeezed) | 3×10 | 3×12 | 3×15 |
| Floor chest press (on your back, knees bent) | 3×12 | 3×15 | 3×15, 3s down |
| Lateral raise | 2×12 | 3×12 | 3×15 |
| Biceps curl + overhead triceps extension (standing, ribs down) | 2×12 each | 3×12 | 3×15 |
| Dead bug (low back stays pressed to floor) | 3×6 each side | 3×8 | 3×10 holding dumbbell |

Rest 45–75s between sets. When the top of a rep range feels easy, move to the harder column.
Don't add sets just because 3 kg feels light. Use slower tempo instead.

### Low-impact cardio circuit (Sat)
40s work / 20s rest, 4 rounds (weeks 1–4) → 5 rounds (weeks 5–8). 1 min rest between rounds.
1. Fast march with arm swings
2. Step-jacks (step out side to side, arms overhead)
3. Goblet squat to chair, 1 dumbbell
4. Standing alternate knee to opposite elbow, slow, without bending your spine
5. Shadow boxing, light dumbbells in hand

A 30–40 min brisk walk is an equally good replacement on any day.

### Hamstring & hip mobility (Wed, and 5 min after any session)
Tight hamstrings in people with disc bulges are often partly a protective nerve response,
so stretch **gently**. Never bounce, and never stretch by bending forward from the waist.

| Stretch | How | Dose |
|---|---|---|
| Lying hamstring stretch with towel | On your back, loop a towel around one foot, raise the straight leg until you feel a mild stretch. Other knee bent. | 3×30s each leg |
| Legs up the wall | Lie with bottom close to a wall, legs up. Relax. | 2–3 min |
| Half-kneeling hip flexor stretch | Squeeze the glute on the back-knee side, shift forward slightly | 2×30s each side |
| Figure-4 glute stretch, lying down | Ankle on opposite knee, pull the thigh toward you | 2×30s each side |
| Nerve glide (sciatic floss) | Sitting tall, straighten one knee while lifting your head, bend the knee while dropping your chin. Smooth, not a stretch. | 10 each leg |
| Prone press-up (only if it feels good) | Lie on your stomach, press your chest up with hips down, gently | 10 slow reps |

Skip the nerve glide and press-up if either causes leg symptoms.

## 3. Nutrition (this drives fat loss)

- **Small deficit:** eat ~300–500 kcal below maintenance. Don't crash diet.
- **Protein:** ~100–110 g/day (about 1.6 g per kg). Sources: eggs, paneer, dal, chicken, fish, curd/Greek yogurt, soya chunks, tofu.
- Fill half your plate with vegetables. Cut back on sugary drinks, fried snacks and late-night eating.
- Water: 2.5–3 L/day (discs need hydration too). Sleep: 7–8 hours.

### Rules for adjusting
- Weekly average not dropping for 2 weeks → add 1,500 steps/day **or** cut ~150 kcal.
- Losing more than 0.75 kg/week → eat a little more (to protect muscle).
- Waist shrinking while weight stays the same → that's fat loss. Keep going.
- Back pain rises above 3/10 or lasts into the next morning → drop back one level for a week
  and replace the cardio circuit with walking.
- Once 3 kg feels easy at the top of every range (around week 6–8), heavier dumbbells
  (5–8 kg) are the next step.
