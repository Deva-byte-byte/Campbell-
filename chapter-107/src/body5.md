## CLINICAL APPLICATIONS OF URODYNAMICS: EVIDENCE-BASED REVIEW AND GUIDELINES PERTAINING TO URODYNAMICS

- For many situations evidence is insufficient and use rests on **clinical impression**; for some, evidence is strong enough to guide use. Three areas are reviewed: **women with SUI, men and women with LUTS, and NLUTD**.

### Guidelines

- **AUA/SUFU UDS guideline (2012)**: **19 statements** across **four disease states** — SUI/POP, OAB/UUI and MUI, neurogenic bladder, LUTS. **Not reconfirmed since**; guidelines encourage best practice but **do not establish the standard of care**.
- **Shift to disease-specific guidelines**: **AUA/SUFU surgical SUI guideline (2023) — two UDS statements**; AUA/SUFU OAB and bladder pain syndrome/interstitial cystitis guidelines; EAU urinary incontinence guideline (originally six UDS statements for neurologically intact adults; now separate **male and female** guidelines).
- **Many guidelines are redundant**, built on the same data.
- **Oxford grading** (used by AUA UDS guideline):

| Grade | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **A** | **Consistent level 1** — effectively mandatory, placed in care pathways |
| **B** | **Consistent level 2 or 3**, or majority evidence from RCTs |
| **C** | **Level 4**, or majority evidence from level 2/3, or **expert opinion** |

- Guidelines **do not guarantee better outcomes**, do not replace clinical expertise, and are **not a legal standard**.

### Evaluation of Females With Stress Urinary Incontinence

- For **pure SUI** — no urgency, normal emptying, SUI demonstrated on examination — **UDS adds little**.
- **SISTEr trial** (Burch vs pubovaginal sling, pure or stress-predominant MUI, no obvious emptying problem): UDS (**ALPP, DO**) **did not help predict efficacy or postoperative voiding dysfunction**.
- **NICE (2006)**: UDS before SUI surgery **only if** — suspected **DO**, **previous SUI or anterior-compartment prolapse surgery**, or **symptoms of voiding dysfunction**.

<p class="tcap">COMPARISON: RANDOMISED EVIDENCE ON PRE-OPERATIVE UDS IN UNCOMPLICATED SUI</p>

| | ValUE trial (Value of Urodynamic Evaluation) | Dutch multicentre cohort with embedded non-inferiority RCT |
|---|---|---|
| **Population** | **Uncomplicated** pure or stress-predominant MUI; **PVR < 150 mL**; negative urinalysis/culture; **urethral mobility and positive stress test**; excluded prior incontinence surgery, pelvic radiation, recent pelvic surgery, significant anterior/apical POP | SUI or stress-predominant MUI; **all had UDS**; those with **UDS discordant from clinical assessment** randomised |
| **Comparator** | **Office evaluation + UDS vs office evaluation alone** (630 women) | **Immediate mid-urethral sling (MUS) vs UDS-tailored treatment** |
| **Primary endpoint** | **UDI reduction ≥ 70% and PGI-I "much / very much better" at 12 months** | Improvement on UDI incontinence subscale |
| **Result** | **Success 76.9% vs 77.2%** (difference **−0.3 points**, 95% CI −7.5 to 6.9) | **Nearly half had discordant UDS**; most tailored-arm patients still had surgery; subjective and objective cure equal |
| **Verdict** | **Office evaluation alone non-inferior** | **Immediate MUS non-inferior** to tailored therapy |

- **ValUE detail**: UDS changed the sling route in a few women (transobturator ↔ retropubic), with unknown effect; UDS **increased surgeon confidence** in diagnosing ISD, OAB and voiding dysfunction — **but not SUI** — without improving outcome. (UDI = Urogenital Distress Inventory; PGI-I = Patient Global Impression of Improvement.)
- **Conclusion**: **UDS is not essential before surgery for stress-predominant incontinence when SUI is clinically demonstrated.** Its real role is in **mixed symptoms or emptying difficulty**, where symptoms predict urodynamic SUI, DOI and MUI **inconsistently**.
- **Urgency with urodynamic SUI and a normal CMG (no DO)**: excellent cure of **both** stress and urgency symptoms after pubovaginal sling, Burch or tension-free vaginal tape. The "no difference" trials were largely **stress-predominant** — in **urgency-predominant** women DO may matter more.

| Body | Statement | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| **AUA/SUFU UDS (2012)** | **Assess urethral function (ALPP or MUCP)** when diagnosing urodynamic SUI | Recommendation; C |
| | **Assess PVR** before invasive SUI therapy | Expert Opinion |

| | **May perform UDS in non-index patients** | Expert Opinion |
| **EAU (neurologically intact incontinence)** | Replicate symptoms; check recording quality; interpret in clinical context; remember intra-individual variability | **Strong** |

| | **Perform UDS if findings may change invasive treatment** | **Weak** |
| | **Do not routinely use UPP or LPP to grade severity** | **Strong** |

- **AUA non-index patients** (UDS at discretion): **prior anti-incontinence surgery; prior POP surgery; subjective–objective mismatch; significant voiding dysfunction; significant urgency, UUI or OAB; elevated PVR; unconfirmed SUI; NLUTD**.
- **Occult SUI and prolapse surgery**: two RCTs (with and without concomitant anti-incontinence surgery) show **more de novo SUI when occult SUI is present pre-operatively** → UDS with reduction can **risk-stratify** — but this is **debated**. The ICS notes UDS **informs counselling** but there is **no compelling evidence it determines surgical outcome**. **Office evaluation with reduction may perform as well**; UDS **detects more SUI** but **does not better predict post-operative SUI**.
- **"Guidelines of guidelines"** (AUA, EAU, NICE 2019, Canadian Urological Association): **invasive UDS not needed before conservative treatment**; may be used if it would **change management** (before invasive therapy); **agreement to use UDS for recurrent incontinence after failed invasive treatment**; considerable redundancy.

<p class="concept"><b>Concept —</b> <em>In women with SUI, UDS is judged by one test: <b>does the result change the operation?</b> For demonstrable, uncomplicated SUI it does not, so it may be omitted; for mixed, recurrent, complex or non-index patients it may, so it is used.</em></p>

### Evaluation of Males and Females With Lower Urinary Tract Symptoms

- **LUTS is multifactorial** — at least four conditions: **BOO, detrusor underactivity, DO and "sensory urgency"**. Storage and voiding symptoms coexist; **UUI** may come from DO with or without BOO.
- **LUTS are common in men ≥ 50**; **coexistence of BOO and DO rises with age and with BOO severity**.
- **PVR (AUA, Clinical Principle)**: **may** be done as a **safety measure to exclude significant retention**, initially and at follow-up — cannot separate obstruction from underactivity, and **no agreed "elevated" threshold**.
- **Uroflow (AUA, Recommendation; C)**: **may** be used in the initial and ongoing evaluation of **men** with voiding/emptying LUTS — likewise cannot distinguish obstruction from underactivity, but guides therapy and prompts testing.
- **How much diagnosis before treatment?**: **no UDS needed to start an α-blocker** in a man emptying normally; as treatment becomes **more invasive and morbid**, a precise diagnosis becomes more valuable.
- **After TURP, storage symptoms persist in about one-third.** When DO accompanies BOO, **single terminal IDC → storage symptoms more likely to resolve** than continuous or sporadic IDCs — valuable for **counselling** (a man bothered mainly by emptying may choose surgery; one bothered mainly by OAB may not).
- **Evidence for UDS before BPH surgery is mixed** — some support, some find it unnecessary or useful only in inconclusive cases, some find modest predictive value. **6th International Consultation on New Developments in Prostate Cancer and Diseases**: evidence is **level 3** but its quantity supports a **grade B recommendation**.
- **"No obstruction on PFS → less favourable surgical results."** No critical BOOI level for preventing decompensation has been defined, and **no evidence-based trigger for surgery to prevent bladder decompensation** exists — though BOO causes progressive, possibly **irreversible structural bladder change**, prompting some to favour early relief.
- **Significantly impaired compliance is the only "absolute urodynamic indication" for treating BOO.**

- **What the EAU list is testing**: each trigger marks a man in whom **obstruction cannot be assumed** — flow good enough to cast doubt on BOO (**Qmax > 10**), a volume too small to interpret, retention that may be detrusor failure (**PVR > 300**), extremes of age (**< 50** — other causes; **> 80** — underactivity), or failed prior treatment.
- **Trap**: the EAU **Qmax trigger is > 10 mL/s**, not < 10 — a low Qmax already suggests obstruction.
- Across guidelines PFS is generally **optional**; uroflow and PVR usually optional, occasionally "recommended" or mandatory.

*Filling cystometry in LUTS.* **Impaired compliance can only be diagnosed by CMG** — from BOO, **radiation cystitis** or inflammatory disease (**tuberculosis**) — and may justify treatment **independent of symptoms**; UDS is advised when suspected.

- **AUA/SUFU**: **may** perform multichannel filling cystometry to detect **altered compliance, DO or other abnormalities** in **UUI** before invasive, morbid or irreversible treatment (**Option; C**), and in **LUTS** generally (**Expert Opinion**).
- **AUA/SUFU OAB**: **UDS should not be used in the initial work-up of uncomplicated OAB**; latitude for **complicated or refractory** patients.
- **Women with poor emptying or voiding symptoms**: **PFS separates obstruction from impaired contractility** — most useful when the cause is **not obvious**. **Anatomic obstruction** (high-grade POP, post-incontinence surgery, urethral mass) is usually evident; **functional obstruction (dysfunctional voiding, PBNO) is not visible on examination or endoscopy** and needs voiding evaluation. **AUA/SUFU: may perform PFS in women to determine obstruction (Recommendation; C)**; **VUDS** diagnoses and localises.

<p class="concept"><b>Concept —</b> <em>Symptoms cannot tell obstruction from underactivity, so the value of PFS rises with the <b>invasiveness and irreversibility</b> of the planned treatment — and the one finding that mandates treatment regardless of symptoms is a bladder that stores at high pressure.</em></p>

### Evaluation of Neurogenic Lower Urinary Tract Dysfunction

- **Presentations**: incontinence of **bladder origin** (DO, impaired compliance) or **sphincter origin**; poor emptying of **bladder origin** (neurogenic detrusor underactivity, neurogenic acontractile detrusor) or **sphincter origin** (dyssynergia); or **silent upper-tract decompensation** — hydroureteronephrosis and renal insufficiency **without bothersome symptoms**.
- **Goals**: **prevent upper-tract decompensation** and relieve symptoms; achieve physiological filling (and if possible voiding), control symptoms, and a management situation acceptable to the patient. **UDS has its most important role here.**
- **Evidence is mostly level 3 or lower** — **RCTs are considered dangerous and unethical** given the consequences of untreated neurogenic dysfunction.
- **Primary aim: convert to a low-pressure bladder during filling, even at the cost of incomplete emptying and catheterisation.** Therapy depends on whether the detrusor is **overactive or poorly compliant** — **only UDS answers this unequivocally**. UDS is also essential for **monitoring response and sequelae**.

*Ice water test (IWT)* — **Bors and Blinn (1957)**; no agreed protocol.

- **Mechanism**: cold instillation elicits a **C-fibre-mediated spinal reflex detrusor contraction**, normally **inhibited supraspinally**.
- **UMN lesion → inhibition lost → contraction (positive)**; **LMN lesion → no contraction (negative)**.
- Helps distinguish **reflexic from areflexic** bladders; **ICS: optional**, interpreted with other data, and **should not precede standard UDS**.

*Who needs UDS.*

| UDS usually not needed first | UDS helpful | UDS essential (before treatment and in follow-up) |
|---|---|---|
| **High storage pressure not suspected** — UUI after **stroke**; woman with **MS and low PVR** | **Men with possible BPO plus Parkinson disease or MS** | **Potentially harmful storage** — **SCI, myelomeningocele** |

- The two bodies **agree**: non-invasive testing first, **VUDS preferred** in neurogenic patients; the EAU adds technical detail.

<div class="keypoints" markdown="1">
<p class="kptitle">KEY POINTS</p>

- **UDS is not essential before surgery for stress-predominant MUI** in women when SUI is clinically demonstrated.
- **AUA**: PFS to determine obstruction in men with LUTS is important before **invasive, morbid or irreversible** treatment.
- **Women with poor emptying or voiding symptoms** benefit from PFS to separate obstruction from impaired contractility, especially when the cause is not obvious.
- **UDS has its most important role in NLUTD.**
</div>

<p class="concept"><b>Concept —</b> <em>In NLUTD the kidneys, not the symptoms, set the agenda: because damage can be silent and trials are unethical, UDS is the only arbiter of whether the bladder stores safely, and <b>low filling pressure outranks complete emptying</b>.</em></p>

## FUTURE DIRECTIONS

- UDS remains a key **research tool**; future value lies in **shaping decisions, patient understanding and phenotyping**, and predicting long-term management.
- **Non-invasive tests for male BOO** (systematic review): **detrusor wall thickness, near-infrared spectroscopy (NIRS), penile cuff test, uroflowmetry with Qmax < 10 mL/s, intravesical prostatic protrusion > 10 mm** — promising but limited by **heterogeneous definitions and thresholds, small samples and weak methodology**; combinations may be needed.
- **Penile cuff test (UroCuff)**: **cuff pressure needed to interrupt flow** estimates bladder pressure; **no urethral catheter**, **naturally filled bladder**; plotted on a **modified ICS nomogram**. A meta-analysis found it **performs sufficiently** for diagnosing and managing BOO, but obstruction criteria vary.
- **Storage-phase surrogates for DO**: **ultrasound bladder/detrusor wall thickness and bladder weight**; **NIRS** (oxy-/deoxyhaemoglobin changes during IDCs in real time — **limited by motion artefact**); **biomarkers such as nerve growth factor (NGF)** (correlate with DO/OAB but **lack specificity**). None is reliable for routine practice or quantifies storage beyond DO → **filling cystometry remains the gold standard for storage disorders**.
- **Functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI)** with concurrent UDS: maps **brain network activation through the micturition cycle** (controls and OAB) — research only.
- **Implantable wireless bladder pressure monitors**: longer, out-of-office monitoring; mostly **animal data**, recent human work shows **promising reliability, safety and tolerability**, but a **single intravesical channel** needs validation.

<p class="concept"><b>Concept —</b> <em>Each new technology tries to remove the catheter or the laboratory from urodynamics; none yet replaces the subtracted-pressure principle, which is why <b>filling cystometry and pressure–flow remain the reference standards</b> against which they are all judged.</em></p>
