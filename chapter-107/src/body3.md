- **ALPP and intrinsic sphincter deficiency (ISD)** — McGuire's 1993 series of women with SUI:

<p class="tcap">ALPP THRESHOLDS FOR ISD (McGUIRE, 1993)</p>

| ALPP (cm H₂O) | Clinical picture | Inference |
|---|---|---|
| **< 60** | High-grade incontinence, often continuous; **mostly fixed urethra** | **ISD** |
| **60–90** | Mostly **marked hypermobility**, moderate–high-grade leakage | **Equivocal** — component of ISD |
| **≥ 90** | Lesser grades; minimal to gross hypermobility | **Little or no ISD** |

- **Use with caution**:
    - no technique separates **ISD from hypermobility** when both are present (hypermobility = **Q-tip deflection > 30°** from baseline);
    - **no hypermobility → SUI must be ISD, whatever the ALPP**;
    - **hypermobility is equally common at low and high ALPP** — ISD and hypermobility **coexist and are not discrete classes**;
    - **ALPP in isolation has limited value in predicting success** of common female SUI procedures.
- **ALPP ≠ VLPP**. ALPP may be provoked by **Valsalva (VLPP)** or **cough (cough leak point pressure, CLPP)** — in the same person **VLPP is significantly lower than CLPP**, so state the method.
- **Catheter effects**: **larger urethral catheter → lower ALPP** in women; ALPP can be read **without a urethral catheter** from the rectal (or vaginal, stomal) line. A proportion of women with SUI — and a larger proportion of **men with post-prostatectomy SUI** — leak **only with the urethral catheter removed**.
- **Newer inclusive term — urodynamic stress test**: any physical effort raising abdominal pressure during cystometry to examine SUI; report **provocation, catheter size/method, leak detection and volume**.

*Detrusor leak point pressure.* A measure of **Pdet in a poorly compliant bladder**: the **lowest Pdet at which leakage occurs in the absence of a detrusor contraction or raised abdominal pressure** (Fig. 107.5).

- **The higher the urethral resistance, the higher the DLPP.** Low outlet resistance → leakage at a low, "safe" pressure; **high outlet resistance → pressure keeps rising → less leakage but transmission to the upper tracts** (Fig. 107.8).
- **Assess DLPP in**: **upper motor neuron (UMN) lesions** with high storage pressure (DO + DESD); **LMN decentralisation**; **non-neurogenic low compliance** (multiple bladder operations, radiation, tuberculous cystitis).
- **McGuire (1981)**: in myelodysplastic children, **DLPP > 40 cm H₂O → hydronephrosis or VUR in 85%**.
- **But 40 cm H₂O is not a validated "safe" cut-off**: evidence was **low-level retrospective**; in myelodysplastic children **20 cm H₂O is more sensitive** for upper-tract damage; the **ICS 2017 teaching module** states only that **lower DLPP does better than higher**; a systematic review in neurological patients names simply **"high DLPP"** — with no cut-off — as a risk factor, alongside other factors such as compliance.
- **Treatment target**: **as low a pressure as reasonably achievable**.
- **Why an elevated DLPP matters**: bladder pressure is already too high **before the urethral "pop-off" occurs**. Treatment usually **lowers bladder pressure so DLPP is never reached**, or **lowers outlet resistance** (e.g. **sphincterotomy for DESD**) — then **repeat UDS to confirm**.

| ALPP | DLPP |
|---|---|
| Measures the **sphincter's response to abdominal pressure** | Measures the **injured bladder's response to outlet resistance** |
| **Low = bad** (weak sphincter) | **High = bad** (danger to upper tracts) |
| Pressure source: **abdomen**, no detrusor contraction | Pressure source: **detrusor (poor compliance)**, no contraction or strain |
| Relevant in **SUI** | Relevant in **low compliance / neurogenic bladder** |

- **DO leak point pressure** (ICS 2017 adult NLUTD terminology): **lowest Pdet rise with DO at which leakage first occurs**, without voluntary contraction or abdominal strain; clinical significance unexplored.

#### Stress-Induced Detrusor Overactivity

- **Stress-induced DO (SIDO)**: a rise in abdominal pressure **triggers an IDC** — the symptom looks like SUI but the cause is **detrusor, not sphincteric** (Fig. 107.9).
- Note whether there is **also urodynamic SUI and/or DO independent of SIDO**.
- ICS equivalent: **cough-associated DO** — DO (with or without leakage) **immediately after the cough peak**.

#### Occult Stress Incontinence

- **SUI on prolapse reduction** (occult or latent SUI): SUI shown in a **clinically continent woman with POP only when the prolapse is reduced** — by **pessary, packing, Scopette, forceps or manually**. Shown on UDS = **urodynamic occult SUI**.

#### Urethral Pressure Profilometry

- **Brown and Wickham (1969)** method: small catheter with **lateral side holes, continuously perfused**, **slowly withdrawn**; bladder and urethral pressures recorded simultaneously. The transducer registers the pressure needed to **lift the urethral wall off the side holes**. **Accurate only where the urethra is distensible and seals** round the catheter.
- **Clinical relevance is controversial**; many urologists do not perform it routinely. Parameters (Fig. 107.10):
    - **urethral closure pressure (UCP) profile** = urethral pressure − intravesical pressure;
    - **maximum urethral pressure (MUP)** = highest pressure along the profile;
    - **maximum urethral closure pressure (MUCP)** = maximum difference between urethral and intravesical pressure;
    - **functional profile length** = length over which urethral pressure exceeds intravesical pressure (women).
- **Continent women**: functional length **~3 cm**, **MUCP 40–60 cm H₂O** — wide normal variation.
- **MUCP and ISD**: McGuire (1981) found **MUCP ≤ 20 cm H₂O predicted failure of SUI surgery** — the **fixed, open urethra ("type III" SUI)**, **renamed ISD in 1992**. MUCP ≤ 20 shares the problems of ALPP-based ISD definitions.
- **ALPP vs MUCP**: **UPP does not diagnose SUI, and SUI is not required to measure it** — contrary to ALPP.
- **MUCP is lower in incontinent women but overlaps** with continent women and **does not grade severity**.
- **ICS standardisation (2002), unchanged since**: urethral pressure measurement has **unclear clinical utility** — it does not discriminate urethral incompetence from other disorders, does not grade severity, does not reliably predict surgical success, and does not normalise after surgery; **no newer technique has reached clinically relevant sensitivity, specificity or reliability**.

### Voiding and Emptying Phase

#### Normal Voiding and Emptying

- The voiding phase assesses **detrusor contractility and outlet resistance**: the bladder must contract enough to overcome the outlet.
- **Abnormal emptying** = **outlet overactivity** (too much resistance), **detrusor underactivity** (weak force, short duration, slow velocity), or **both**. **PFS is the most accurate way to assess both** (Fig. 107.11).
- **Normal voiding function**: flow and pressure rise **within normal limits**, voiding starts promptly after permission and **ends with an empty bladder**. Male terminology: **normal detrusor contractile function** — an adequate continuous contraction emptying the bladder within a normal time.
- **Women may void normally with little Pdet rise** → the female definition differs.
- **Commonest reason normal voiding is not seen: increased outlet resistance** → **bladder outflow obstruction (BOO)**, a pressure–flow-based cut-off of clinically relevant resistance.
- **Micturition reflex, in sequence** (Fig. 107.12): **striated sphincter relaxes** → **detrusor contracts** → **bladder neck and urethra open** → **flow begins**. Coordinated by the **pontine and sacral micturition centres** with suprapontine voluntary control.

<p class="tcap">COMPARISON: ICS DETRUSOR FUNCTION DURING VOIDING</p>

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Normal detrusor function** | **Voluntarily initiated continuous contraction** → complete emptying **within a normal time**, **without obstruction** |
| **Detrusor underactivity** | Contraction of **reduced strength and/or duration** → prolonged emptying and/or **incomplete emptying** in a normal time |
| **Acontractile detrusor** | **No demonstrable contraction** during UDS |
| **Neurogenic acontractile detrusor** | Replaces "**areflexia**" when the cause is neurological |
| **Situational inability to void as usual** | Attempted void judged **unrepresentative** — **not** "acontractile" |

- **Detrusor underactivity is "nebulously defined"**: what constitutes reduced strength, duration or prolonged emptying is subjective, and **voiding pressure depends on outlet resistance** — correlate with the clinical picture.
- **Flow and PVR** show emptying is incomplete but **not why** (obstruction vs impaired contractility).
- **Qmax depends on VV**: **linear above 150 mL, hyperbolic below** → a **minimum VV of 150 mL** is widely recommended — a major limitation, as many patients never void that much. Alternatives: **corrected Qmax (Qmax / √VV)** and **nomograms** correcting for VV — **Siroky (men)** and **Liverpool (men and women)**.

*Bethanechol supersensitivity test* — **Lapides (1962)**, applying **Cannon's law of denervation** (denervated structures become supersensitive to chemical stimulation) to distinguish **neurogenic from myogenic** underactivity.

- **Sequence**: fill at **1 mL/s to 100 mL**, record pressure (up to three times, averaged) → **bethanechol 2.5 mg subcutaneously** (later **0.035 mg/kg**) → repeat at **10, 20 and 30 minutes**.
- **Negative (normal or myogenic)**: rise **< 15 cm H₂O** above control at 100 mL at 30 minutes. **Positive (sensory or motor paralytic bladder)**: rise **≥ 15 cm H₂O**.
- **Limited role**: **unreliable** (poor sensitivity and specificity); treatment is usually the **same whatever the result (CIC)**; **bethanechol is not a consistently effective treatment** for underactivity, and a positive test **does not predict therapeutic response**.

#### Voiding Pressure–Flow Studies

- Begins **at cystometric capacity**, same bladder and rectal (or vaginal, stomal) lines, simultaneous uroflowmetry; ideally a **voluntary** void.
- **Pelvic floor contraction to stop leakage during an IDC** should be **annotated**; the **public, instrumented setting** inhibits some patients — account for this.
- **Pdet during voiding is a function of outlet resistance**: a normal detrusor meets obstruction with **higher pressure and lower flow** and still empties; over time it may **decompensate** → incomplete emptying or retention.

<p class="tcap">PRESSURE–FLOW PATTERNS</p>

| Pdet | Flow | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **Low (or normal)** | **High (or normal)** | **Normal, unobstructed** |
| **High** | **Low (or normal)** | **Obstruction** |
| **Low** | **Low** | **Detrusor underactivity** — obstruction may coexist but be **undiagnosable** if the detrusor is too decompensated |

- **BOO = high-pressure, low-flow voiding**; long-standing BOO may end in **detrusor underactivity**.
- **From rigid tubes to dynamic relations**: early resistance equations (from **1962**) used rigid-tube hydrodynamics, ignoring the **active, distensible urethra** and **bladder volume**, and were abandoned.

| Bladder output relation (BOR) — Castro and Griffiths, 1972 | Urethral resistance relation (URR) — Griffiths, 1975 |
|---|---|
| Pressure–flow relation **of the bladder, independent of the urethra**, at a given volume | Pressure–flow relation **of the urethra, independent of the bladder** |
| Weakening contraction shifts the curve down | **No flow until bladder pressure equals intrinsic urethral pressure**, then flow rises rapidly; urethral relaxation moves the URR **left**, tightening **right** |

- **Actual Pdet and flow = intersection of BOR and URR** (Fig. 107.14): changing one relation moves the point **along the other curve**.

*Stop test* — for suspected underactivity, estimates **isovolumetric detrusor pressure (Piso)**:

| Voluntary stop test | Mechanical stop test | Continuous occlusion test |
|---|---|---|
| Patient **interrupts flow midstream** with the external sphincter | **Balloon pulled against the bladder neck** midstream, or urethra clamped/squeezed | Outflow **occluded before contraction**; patient voids against it |
| **Gives the lowest Piso** | — | — |

- The three **correlate well** in men and women.

#### Bladder Outlet Obstruction and Detrusor Underactivity in Males

- Premise: **surgical outcomes for BPH-related LUTS are better when obstruction is documented**; BPH/BPO's high prevalence made it the **model for defining obstruction**.

<p class="tcap">COMPARISON: MALE PRESSURE–FLOW NOMOGRAMS</p>

| Nomogram | Basis | Output |
|---|---|---|
| **Abrams–Griffiths** (1979) | PdetQmax vs Qmax | Obstructed / equivocal / unobstructed |
| **URA** — group-specific urethral resistance factor (Griffiths, 1989) | Urethral resistance | Obstructed / equivocal / unobstructed |
| **Linear passive urethral resistance relation (LinPURR; Schäfer)** (1990) | Linearised URR | **Grades** of obstruction (incl. "slightly obstructed") **and contractility** |
| **ICS provisional nomogram** (Griffiths, 1997) | **BOOI** | **Recommended for men with LUTS suggestive of BPH** |

- All derived from **men undergoing surgery (mainly TURP)**: after surgery, **pressure at maximum flow (PdetQmax) fell in the obstructed**, fell **unpredictably in the equivocal**, and was **unchanged in the unobstructed**. **All three classify patients similarly.**
- **Bladder outlet obstruction index (BOOI)** (the "AG number") — the slope of the **Abrams–Griffiths obstructed/equivocal line**, identical to Schäfer's obstructed/slightly obstructed line:
    - **BOOI = PdetQmax − 2Qmax**;
    - **> 40 obstructed · 20–40 equivocal · < 20 unobstructed** (Fig. 107.15).
- **Reproducibility is good**: random variation of roughly **9–14 cm H₂O** in pressure and **0.4–2 mL/s** in Qmax; repeated studies in one session show a small **systematic fall** in Pdet and Qmax. Only a minority change ICS class, **almost always by one class only**.
- **Bladder contractility index (BCI)** — from Schäfer's contractility groups (strong, normal, weak, very weak):
    - **BCI = PdetQmax + 5Qmax**;
    - **> 150 strong · 100–150 normal · < 100 weak** (Fig. 107.16);
    - **the most widely used contractility measure** — easy to calculate, linked to BOOI and the ICS nomogram.

| BOOI | BCI |
|---|---|
| PdetQmax **− 2**Qmax | PdetQmax **+ 5**Qmax |
| **> 40** obstructed; **< 20** unobstructed | **> 150** strong; **< 100** weak |
| Measures **outlet** | Measures **detrusor** |
