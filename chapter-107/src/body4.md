- **Obstruction can be invisible in an underactive detrusor**: unequivocal obstruction needs **BOOI ≥ 40**, i.e. **Pdet ≥ 40 cm H₂O even at zero flow**; at Qmax 5 mL/s, **PdetQmax ≥ 50**. The ICS nomogram therefore **misses men whose weak detrusor is itself the result of long-term obstruction** → clinical judgement.

*Watts factor (WF)* — **detrusor power per unit bladder surface area**, corrected for the power needed for isometric contraction or unloaded shortening:

- **WF = [(Pdet + a)(vdet + b) − ab] / 2π**, where vdet = **detrusor shortening velocity**, **a ≈ 25 cm H₂O**, **b ≈ 6 mm/s**.
- WF varies through the void; representative points are **maximum power (Wmax)** and **power at maximum flow (WQmax)**.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| **Minimally dependent on bladder volume** | **No measure of sustainability** of contraction |
| **Unaffected by BOO** | **Complex calculation** — limited clinical use |
|  | **Normal threshold not validated** (Wmax **7.0 W/m²** suggested) |

- **BOO also disturbs storage** (ultrastructural change): **about two-thirds of men with symptomatic BPO have DO, which resolves in half to two-thirds after relief of obstruction**; **reduced compliance also improves after TURP**.

*Micturitional urethral pressure profile (MUPP)* — voiding profilometry (Yalla): **both diagnoses and localises obstruction**.

- **Technique**: triple-lumen catheter under fluoroscopy → **slowly withdrawn during voiding** from bladder neck to anterior urethra.
- **Normal**: bladder **isobaric with the prostatic urethra**, then a **20–30 cm H₂O drop across the membranous urethra** (the **narrowest segment during voiding**), then gradual decay.
- **BPH obstruction**: a **pressure drop > 5 cm H₂O within the prostatic urethra** marks obstruction at that point.
- **As effective as standard PFS** in diagnosing BOO; treatment based on MUPP has given successful outcomes.
- **Piso in men**: when PFS cannot separate obstruction from underactivity (inefficient emptying, neither diagnosis made), **Piso < 50 cm H₂O is uncommon and considered diagnostic of detrusor underactivity**.
- **Contraction-strength methods compared (LinPURR, BCI, Wmax)** — all correlate well; **LinPURR is simple and intuitive and assesses contraction and BOO together**; **Wmax** suited to studies aiming to improve contractility.
- **No universal criteria for detrusor underactivity**: UDS is "required" for the diagnosis, yet ICS gives **no standard for normal contraction duration or normal micturition time**.

#### Bladder Outlet Obstruction in Females

- **More of a diagnostic dilemma**:
    - **no highly prevalent cause** equivalent to BPH to build nomograms on;
    - **male nomograms do not apply** — voiding dynamics differ;
    - many women **void by pelvic floor relaxation alone**, some by **straining**;
    - **small Pdet rises or flow falls**, insignificant in men, may signify BOO.
- **High index of suspicion**: LUTS, incomplete emptying, **persistent UTIs**, previous **anti-incontinence surgery, prolapse**.

<p class="tcap">COMPARISON: FEMALE BOO CUT-POINT CRITERIA</p>

| Study | Controls | Qmax | PdetQmax |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chassagne (1998)** | Women with SUI | **≤ 15 mL/s** | **≥ 20 cm H₂O** |
| **Lemack and Zimmern (2000)** | Expanded population | **≤ 11 mL/s** | **≥ 21 cm H₂O** |
| **Defreitas (2004)** | **Normal asymptomatic women** | **≤ 12 mL/s** | **≥ 25 cm H₂O** |

- **Limitation**: obstruction was **predefined clinically** and **anatomic only** — **functional obstruction** (primary bladder neck obstruction (PBNO), dysfunctional voiding) was excluded. **Anatomic and functional obstruction share voiding pressures and overlap widely in flow** → future definitions need not exclude either.

<p class="tcap">COMPARISON: METHODS OF DIAGNOSING FEMALE BOO</p>

| Method | Definition | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cut-points** (above) | Qmax and PdetQmax thresholds | Simple | Anatomic cases only; **large overlap** |
| **VUDS criteria (Nitti, 1999)** | **Radiographic obstruction between bladder neck and distal urethra with a sustained detrusor contraction of any magnitude** | **Localises**; diagnoses obstruction **despite impaired contractility** | Needs fluoroscopy |
| **Blaivas–Groutz nomogram (2000)** | **Free (non-invasive) Qmax vs Pdetmax**; **four zones**: none, mild, moderate, severe | Uses the higher uncatheterised flow | Built from **two separate voids**; **overdiagnoses obstruction** |
| **Solomon–Greenwell nomogram (2017)** | VUDS as referent; axis **PdetQmax = 2.2 × Qmax**; **probability of obstruction** | Includes **functional and anatomic** obstruction | Newer, less validated |

- **VUDS and cut-point criteria show good concordance**; **Blaivas–Groutz overdiagnoses**.
- **Absolute pressure–flow values overlap** between obstructed and unobstructed women → **a second parameter (radiographic or clinical) is needed**.
- **Solomon–Greenwell**: **PdetQmax − 2.2 × Qmax < 0 → low chance (< 10%)**; **> 5 → likely (> 50%)**; **> 18 → almost certain (> 90%)**.
- **The ICS nomogram and BOOI cannot be used in women** — they **grossly underestimate female BOO**, because women normally void at **much lower pressures**. Concept the same (**higher pressure, lower flow**); values different and less well defined.
- **Piso in women**: not validated for characterisation; **significantly lower in older women than in men**; in older women with UUI, **voluntary stop test gives the lowest value**, mechanical and continuous occlusion higher.
- **Female detrusor underactivity**: no universal criteria; women with underactivity (no voiding contraction > 10 cm H₂O, voiding by straining or unable to void) have **reduced bladder sensation** — **contractility and sensation are inversely associated**.

<p class="concept"><b>Concept —</b> <em>Every male index is built on the <b>BPH model</b>, which is why it fails in women: without a common obstructing disease and with physiologically low-pressure voiding, female obstruction can only be defined by <b>adding a second, non-pressure criterion</b> — ideally an image of where the outlet narrows.</em></p>

### Sphincter Coordination

#### The External Sphincter

- **Normal voluntary voiding**: **urethral pressure falls and EMG quietens before Pdet rises**; sphincter stays relaxed until voiding ends, then activity returns (Fig. 107.13). **Failure to relax or stay relaxed is abnormal.**
- **Guarding reflex**: **EMG rises with an involuntary contraction** to inhibit the IDC — **normal**, not dyssynergia.

<p class="tcap">COMPARISON: DYSSYNERGIA VS DYSFUNCTIONAL VOIDING</p>

| DESD | Dysfunctional voiding |
|---|---|
| **Involuntary external sphincter contraction during DO and voiding** | **Learned** failure to relax or involuntary contraction of the external sphincter **during voiding** |
| **Requires a neurological lesion above the sacral micturition centre** (suprasacral cord); **the higher the lesion, the more likely** | **No neurological lesion** |
| **High pressures → impaired compliance over time** → **urodynamic risk factor** for upper-tract deterioration (Box 107.1) | Described in children; also **adult men and women** — a major cause of LUTS; storage symptoms common |
| — | **Confirm the intermittent/reduced flow on free uroflowmetry** to exclude a test artefact; **fluoroscopy improves accuracy** |

- **Adult male diagnostic criteria for dysfunctional voiding**: no neurological history or signs → **transient, intermittent external sphincter closure during voiding on EMG and fluoroscopy** → **raised sphincter EMG without abdominal pressure rise** → **discontinuous free flow**.
- **"Non-relaxing" sphincter** → reduced flow; **delayed relaxation of the urethral sphincter** → delayed flow onset, in adult neurological dysfunction.

#### The Internal Sphincter

- **DISD**: a suprasacral lesion **above the sympathetic ganglia (T10–L1)** may cause **DISD together with DESD**.
- **Bladder neck dyssynergia / PBNO**: a recognised cause of LUTS in **non-neuropathic men, women and children**; aetiology unknown.
- **Internal sphincter dysfunction requires VUDS for diagnosis.**

<div class="keypoints" markdown="1">
<p class="kptitle">KEY POINTS</p>

- **Pdet near zero throughout filling**; subtraction separates detrusor from abdominal rises.
- **Confirm contractions are involuntary**; usual behaviour is not always reproduced.
- **Prolonged high-pressure storage damages the upper tracts.**
- **Low ALPP = weak sphincter; high DLPP = dangerous outlet resistance.**
- **BOO = high-pressure, low-flow voiding**; underactivity = reduced strength and/or duration.
- **EMG rise with an IDC is a normal guarding reflex; true DESD requires a suprasacral lesion.**
</div>

<p class="concept"><b>Concept —</b> <em>The same sphincter behaviour — failure to relax during voiding — is named by its <b>cause, not its tracing</b>: with a suprasacral lesion it is DESD and a threat to the kidneys; without one it is dysfunctional voiding, a learned behaviour. The bladder neck is invisible to EMG, so its dysfunction can only be seen on fluoroscopy.</em></p>

## VIDEOURODYNAMICS

- **VUDS = simultaneous UDS and fluoroscopy** — the **most precise evaluation** of voiding function, especially where **anatomy and function interact**.
- **Uses**:
    - **localisation of obstruction**;
    - **incontinence not seen on examination**;
    - **VUR during storage and/or voiding**;
    - **NLUTD**;
    - **the only way to evaluate bladder neck function**;
    - **confirms sphincter dysfunction** diagnosed by EMG, or diagnoses it when EMG is equivocal;
    - determines whether a **known anomaly** (bladder or urethral diverticulum, VUR) contributes.
- **Equipment**: usually a **C-arm** (most flexible), or a fixed tilting table (90–180°).
- **Position for the question**: **SUI — standing** (both sexes); **voiding — usual position (women sitting, men standing)**.
- **Limit screening time** to high-yield moments: **provocative manoeuvres, pressure rises (compliance loss, IDCs) and voiding**.

*Functional obstruction in women.* VUDS criteria (Nitti, 1999): **radiographic obstruction between bladder neck and meatus during voluntary voiding defines and localises obstruction**. **PBNO can only be diagnosed on VUDS.** The two commonest functional causes are **PBNO (Fig. 107.18)** and **dysfunctional voiding (Fig. 107.19)**.

- **VUDS is more accurate than surface EMG** for diagnosing dysfunctional voiding and separating it from PBNO — surface EMG alone **misdiagnoses a substantial minority** of dysfunctional voiders, and some PBNO patients show raised EMG during voiding.

*Young men — PBNO.* PFS alone can show obstruction, but surgeons want it **localised** before operating on a young man; PBNO may lack the classic pattern. Three types:

| Type | Pattern |
|---|---|
| **I** | **Classic high-pressure, low-flow** |
| **II** | **Normal pressure, low flow**, with **narrowing at the bladder neck** |
| **III** | **Delayed opening of the bladder neck** |

- **Fluoroscopy is critical, especially for types II and III**; it also localises BPO, bladder neck contracture and urethral stricture once urodynamic obstruction is confirmed.
- **AUA/SUFU UDS guideline**: VUDS supported in **young men and women without an obvious anatomical cause** — separates **PBNO from dysfunctional voiding**; the **only tool that documents pressure–flow and localises functional bladder neck obstruction**. **No outcome comparison** with non-VUDS-directed treatment exists.

*Neurogenic and high-pressure bladders.*

- **VUR**: record **volume and pressure at onset**, side and grade. **Reflux is a pop-off**: without fluoroscopy, **measured compliance overestimates true compliance** — a potentially unsafe error (Fig. 107.21).
- **DLPP can be observed** in patients who cannot be positioned to see leakage (e.g. some tetraplegics).
- **DISD — often with DESD — can only be diagnosed by VUDS**, and changes treatment (Fig. 107.22).

<p class="tcap">COMPARISON: GUIDELINE POSITIONS ON VUDS IN NLUTD</p>

| European Association of Urology (EAU) | AUA/SUFU |
|---|---|
| **VUDS is the optimum test** for neuro-urological disorders | VUDS **adds worthwhile information** beyond CMG or PFS alone |
| **Unavailable → filling CMG plus PFS** | **Radiation is additive** → lowest dose that answers the question |

- **Balance**: diagnostic gain in selected populations vs **radiation exposure**, **cost** and **limited availability**.
- **VUDS is the diagnostic tool of choice for bladder neck dysfunction in both sexes.**
- **Refer to a VUDS centre** when diagnosis is otherwise incomplete in high-risk patients: **known or suspected NLUTD, unexplained retention in women, previous radical pelvic surgery, urinary diversion, pre- or post-renal transplant, prior pelvic radiation**.

<p class="concept"><b>Concept —</b> <em>Fluoroscopy adds the one thing pressure lines cannot supply: <b>where</b>. It localises obstruction, reveals the bladder neck, and exposes pop-off reflux that makes a dangerous bladder look safe — which is why it is indispensable in neurogenic and functional-obstruction cases and optional elsewhere.</em></p>
