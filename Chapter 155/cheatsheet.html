Chapter 155 — Prostate Biopsy: Techniques and Imaging — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else

  • Gold standard (chapter): systematic 12-core transrectal ultrasound (TRUS) biopsy ± targeting; 6 cores inadequate.
  • European Association of Urology (EAU) 2023: transperineal preferred over transrectal (strong).
  • Prostate Cancer Prevention Trial (PCPT): no safe PSA threshold; PSA ≤4 → 15% cancer.
  • PSA density (PSAD) >0.15 ng/mL/cc → suggests malignancy.
  • 39% of cancers isoechoic; hypoechoic lesion not pathognomonic.
  • Transrectal prophylaxis for all; ≥1 h before; ≤24 h.
  • Post-biopsy sepsis → carbapenem; avoid empirical fluoroquinolone.
  • PROMIS: MRI sensitivity 93% vs TRUS 48%; 27% avoid biopsy.
  • PRECISION: MRI-targeted 38% vs 26% significant cancer; 28% avoid biopsy.
  • PIRADS: DWI dominates PZ; T2WI dominates TZ; tie-breakers only at 3.

Ultrasonographic anatomy

  • Zones not visible on TRUS; PZ/CZ echogenic, homogeneous vs TZ heterogeneous.
  • Corpora amylacea: surgical-capsule calcification marking PZ–TZ plane; age-related, normal.
  • Normal seminal vesicle (SV): 4.5–5.5 × 2 cm.
  • Solid SV mass → likely malignant; cystic → benign. Endemic area → schistosomiasis.
  • Absent SV → ipsilateral renal agenesis 79%.

Grey-scale TRUS

  • Probes 6–10 MHz; end- vs side-fire: no detection difference.
  • 7 MHz: focal range 1–4 cm, best for PZ. 4 MHz: 2–8 cm, better volume.
  • Gain reference: normal PZ = medium grey.
  • Positions: transrectal → left lateral decubitus; transperineal → lithotomy.
  • Side-fire rotation: clockwise → left lobe; anticlockwise → right.
  • End-fire, left lateral decubitus: handle down → right; handle up → left.
  • Handle toward scrotum → cephalad; toward sacrum → caudal.
  • Stepper: rotation only.
  • Ellipsoid π/6 × T × AP × L; sphere π/6 × T³; prolate spheroid π/6 × T² × AP.
  • All formulas r >0.90; 1 cm³ ≈ 1 g.
  • Measure AP sagittally; axial AP overestimates.
  • Normal 20–25 g until ~50; ≈48 g at 60–70.
  • Planimetry (3–5 mm steps) = most accurate → brachytherapy.
  • Utricle: midline; hypospadias commonest association.
  • Müllerian duct cyst: midline, pear-shaped, neck to verumontanum → check kidneys.
  • Ejaculatory duct cyst: off midline → azoospermia.
  • SV cyst → renal agenesis up to two-thirds.
  • Acquired TZ cyst = haemorrhagic BPH nodule.
  • Hyperechoic cancer ≤1%.
  • Commonest false-positive hypoechoic: BPH, prostatitis.
  • Extraprostatic extension (EPE): focal loss of bright periprostatic fat.
  • Androgen deprivation: ≈30% volume ↓ (10–60%).
  • 5α-reductase inhibitor: ≈21% ↓ at 6 months.
  • Brachytherapy: ~⅓ ↓ at 1 y, >½ by 8 y.
  • Seeds: hyperechoic with shadowing; periurethral sparing.
  • Post-prostatectomy recurrence: hypoechoic in 75–95%.
  • Post-prostatectomy normal: smooth bladder-neck taper; anterior nodule = dorsal vein ligature.
  • Blind anastomotic biopsy (PSA only) → uninformative.
  • Urothelial carcinoma: stromal lesions hypoechoic in 71%.
  • Hypoechoic lesion after BCG: granuloma vs tumour → biopsy.
  • Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC): anterior hyperechoic mass.
  • Adenoid cystic: multiple small anechoic cysts.
  • Rhabdomyosarcoma: isoechoic.
  • Lymphoma: invisible.

Prostate biopsy: techniques and outcomes

  • Initial biopsy yield ≈30%. Core biopsy = technique of choice; fine-needle aspiration obsolete.
  • ERSPC benefit vs PLCO none; lead-time reanalysis: both compatible with benefit.
  • AUA/SUO 2023: baseline PSA 45–50; every 2–4 y at 50–69.
  • AUA/SUO 2023, high risk: germline mutation, family history, African ancestry → start 40–45.
  • USPSTF 2018: 55–69 individual decision.
  • Elevated-risk PSA: >1 ng/mL at 40; >2 ng/mL at 60.
  • NCCN biopsy trigger: abnormal DRE + raised PSA.
  • NCCN biopsy trigger: repeat PSA >3 + PIRADS ≥3.
  • Not alone: PSA velocity; abnormal DRE with normal PSA.
  • Suspicious nodule: 5–30% cancer risk.
  • ASAP / atypical intraductal proliferation: 50% → repeat biopsy.
  • Absolute contraindications: significant coagulopathy; severe immunosuppression; acute UTI or prostatitis.
  • No anus → in-bore MRI or transperineal biopsy.
  • Anal stenosis or pain → general or regional anaesthesia.
  • Low-dose aspirin: continue.
  • Warfarin, clopidogrel: stop 5–7 days.
  • Direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs): stop 2–5 days.
  • International normalised ratio (INR) <1.5 before biopsy.
  • Bridging: mechanical valve; rivaroxaban.
  • Class III (contaminated) procedure.
  • AUA 2019 prophylaxis: fluoroquinolone; 1st/2nd-gen cephalosporin ± aminoglycoside; 3rd-gen cephalosporin.
  • Route: oral only for quinolones; intramuscular (IM) for all.
  • Single-dose agents: levofloxacin 500 mg; gentamicin 5 mg/kg.
  • Single-dose agents: amikacin 15 mg/kg; ceftriaxone 1–2 g.
  • Renal impairment: aztreonam replaces aminoglycoside.
  • Endocarditis or prosthesis risk: IV ampicillin (vancomycin) + gentamicin → 2–3 days fluoroquinolone.
  • Infection risk factors: antibiotics <6 months = most consistent → augmented or targeted prophylaxis.
  • Charlson score >1 = infection risk factor.
  • Periprostatic block: 1–2% lidocaine, 7-inch 22G.
  • Periprostatic block: 5 mL each side at SV–prostate junction.
  • Saturation biopsy: ≤22 mL of 1% lidocaine.
  • Topical rectal anaesthetic: inferior to periprostatic block.
  • Needle: 18G gun; throw 15–25 mm; core 1.0–1.5 cm.
  • Tip overshoot: 0.5 cm beyond core → indent capsule, withdraw, fire.
  • Specimen jars: ≤2 cores per jar.
  • Hodge 1989: sextant; parasagittal cores miss posterolateral PZ, where most cancers arise.
  • 6 → 12 cores: ↑ detection.
  • 12 → 18–21 cores (initial): no ↑ detection.
  • TZ cores if gland >50 mL or repeat.
  • SV biopsy: palpable abnormality or PSA >30.
  • Solitary TZ cancer <5% → transurethral biopsy obsolete.
  • ERSPC repeat biopsies: 22 → 10 → 5 → 4%.
  • 3rd/4th biopsy: only if high suspicion; target the apex.
  • Serious complication <2%.
  • 30-day admission: 1.0 → 4.1% (mostly infection).
  • Haematuria 23–63%; clot retention 0.7%.
  • Rectal bleeding 2.1–21.7%.
  • Haematospermia 9.8–50.4% (~1 month).
  • Infection 0.1–7%; sepsis 0.3–3.1%.
  • Infective admission 0.6–4.1%; UTI 2–6%.
  • Vasovagal 1.4–5.3% → Trendelenburg + IV fluids.
  • Retention 0.2–0.4% (large gland, high IPSS).
  • Sepsis: carbapenem; up to 2 weeks antibiotics if admitted.
  • E. coli resistant: fluoroquinolone, trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX), ampicillin.
  • E. coli susceptible: 2nd/3rd-gen cephalosporins, amikacin, carbapenems.
  • Rectal bleed escalation: pressure → balloon tamponade → endoscopic adrenaline/sclerosant → embolisation → suture.

Advanced and investigational techniques

  • Colour Doppler: frequency shift, direction-dependent; red toward, blue away.
  • Power Doppler: amplitude; slow flow; direction-independent.
  • Doppler vs grey scale: neither Doppler mode superior; neither replaces systematic biopsy.
  • Tumour microvessels 10–15 µm: invisible without contrast.
  • Microbubbles: 1–10 µm.
  • Flash replenishment: high-power flash destroys bubbles → low-power refill imaged.
  • Elastography: stiffness; complements, never replaces, systematic biopsy.
  • Micro-ultrasound: 29 MHz, 70 µm; PRI-MUS score.
  • Micro-ultrasound: no contrast → renal failure.
  • PIRADS v1: ESUR 2012. PIRADS v2: ACR + ESUR + AdMeTech 2015.
  • PIRADS significant cancer: Gleason ≥7 / >0.5 cm³ / EPE.
  • PZ: DWI 3 + DCE positive → 4.
  • TZ: T2WI 3 + DWI 5 → 4.
  • Automatic PIRADS 5: >1.5 cm or EPE.
  • PIRADS cancer rates: 3 ≈ ⅓, 4 >½, 5 ≈ 90%.
  • PROMIS: 576 men; template mapping reference.
  • PROMIS definition: significant = Grade Group ≥3 or core ≥6 mm.
  • PROMIS: NPV 89 vs 74%; +18% significant cancer with targeting.
  • PRECISION: 500 men, randomised non-inferiority; targets PIRADS ≥3.
  • MRI false negatives: ≈⅓ of misses primary pattern 4.
  • In-bore vs cognitive vs fusion: no accuracy difference (meta-analysis).
  • Cognitive targeting: 2–4 cores per target + systematic.
  • AUA/SUO 2023: target any PIRADS ≥3 lesion; 2–3 cores per target.
  • AUA/SAR 2016: MRI after negative biopsy + rising PSA.
  • UroNav: freehand, electromagnetic tracking.
  • Artemis: mechanical arm.
  • Transperineal perineum: scrotum taped up; perineum on tension.
  • Periapical triangle: levator ani (lateral), rhabdosphincter (medial), external anal sphincter (posterior), apex (superior).
  • Periapical block: 1–2 mL skin → 5 mL triangle → 3 mL capsule.
  • Transperineal pain: VAS <4; injection hurts most.
  • Freehand learning curve: 4–6 weeks; 12–14 min.
  • Transperineal access: two 14G cannulas cheapest; PrecisionPoint commonest kit.
  • Transperineal infection: lower than transrectal.
  • Transperineal antibiotics: skin-flora only or none.
  • Transperineal bleeding: no rectal bleeding.
  • Modified Barzell: 20 cores.
  • Ginsburg: adds base sector if >50 g.
  • MUSIC template: 12-core parity; no midline cores.
  • Anterior-only cancers: 6–20% → transperineal reaches them.
  • Barringer 1922: first transperineal biopsy.

Classic traps

  • Trap: no hypoechoic lesion → no biopsy — Actually: 39% isoechoic; biopsy anyway.
  • Trap: PSA ≤4 is safe — Actually: PCPT, no safe threshold.
  • Trap: infection commonest complication — Actually: bleeding commonest; infection commonest serious.
  • Trap: saturation beats 12-core initially — Actually: no gain; reserve for repeat.
  • Trap: oral cephalosporin for transrectal prophylaxis — Actually: oral only for quinolones.
  • Trap: stop aspirin — Actually: low-dose aspirin continues.
  • Trap: DCE matters in TZ — Actually: TZ uses DWI tie-breaker; DCE only PZ 3 → 4.
  • Trap: renal agenesis mainly with müllerian cysts — Actually: wolffian SV cysts.
  • Trap: fire after passing capsule — Actually: 0.5 cm overshoot samples TZ; withdraw first.
  • Trap: empirical ciprofloxacin for post-biopsy sepsis — Actually: carbapenem.
