- **Early prostate cancer is rarely symptomatic**; symptoms imply locally advanced or metastatic disease.
- **Locally advanced disease**: obstructive urinary symptoms, ureteric obstruction (pain or renal failure), haematuria, reduced ejaculate volume and, rarely, erectile dysfunction.
- **Metastatic disease**: bone pain, pathological fracture, lower-limb oedema; less commonly malignant retroperitoneal fibrosis, paraneoplastic syndromes, disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC) and paralysis from spinal cord compression.
- **Advanced presentation is now uncommon** owing to screening with prostate-specific antigen (PSA) and digital rectal examination (DRE); diagnosis is usually by **transrectal ultrasound (TRUS)-guided needle biopsy before symptoms**.
- **Staging defines extent and risk** for management and prognosis: PSA, DRE, biopsy **grade and cancer volume**, and imaging where indicated.

## DIAGNOSIS

### Screening

#### General Concepts of Screening

- **Screening** tests healthy, asymptomatic populations; **diagnosis** identifies disease in people with signs or symptoms.
- **Early diagnosis is not invariably beneficial**: screening harms through **overdiagnosis and overtreatment**.
- Screening **reduces advanced disease at diagnosis** and, in recent data, **prostate cancer mortality**; the dispute is the **balance of benefit and harm**, which depends on the individual's characteristics and preferences.
- Since PSA testing spread (early 1990s), **prostate cancer mortality has fallen by more than 40%** and advanced disease at diagnosis by about three-quarters (Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results [SEER]); modelling attributes **half to two-thirds of the mortality fall** to screening.
- **Randomised trials of screened versus unscreened populations** are the highest level of evidence.

#### Randomised Trials

<p class="tt">Comparison — The two screening trials: ERSPC vs PLCO</p>

| | ERSPC | PLCO |
|---|---|---|
| Name | European Randomised Study of Screening for Prostate Cancer | Prostate, Lung, Colorectal and Ovarian Cancer Screening Trial (National Cancer Institute, NCI) |
| Started · primary endpoint | 1993 · prostate cancer-specific mortality | 1993 · prostate cancer-specific mortality |
| Median follow-up | 16 years | 17 years |
| Mortality result | **20% relative reduction** | **No difference** |
| Other effects | Fewer high-grade and locally advanced or metastatic cancers | — |
| To prevent one death | **Invite 570, diagnose 18 extra** (16 years) | — |
| Why it differs | — | **Contamination** (control-arm PSA testing) and **threefold fewer events → low power** |

- Both trials show **overdiagnosis** (cancers otherwise never detected) and overtreatment — **worst after 65 years**, where a randomised trial of radical prostatectomy versus watchful waiting showed minimal benefit; **average age at diagnosis is about 66**.

<p class="concept"><strong>Concept —</strong> The ERSPC–PLCO discordance is a matter of trial design, not biology: <strong>control-arm contamination and too few events</strong> can erase a real screening effect.</p>

#### Specialty Group Recommendations

<p class="tt">Comparison — Screening recommendations by body</p>

| Body | Who and when | Stance |
|---|---|---|
| U.S. Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) 2008 | <75 years · ≥75 years | Insufficient evidence · **against** |
| USPSTF 2012 (draft 2011) | All ages | **Grade D — against screening** |

<p class="concept"><strong>Concept —</strong> Every body converges on <strong>informed or shared decision making</strong>; they differ only on the age window — NCCN starts earliest (45), USPSTF stops most clearly (70).</p>

### Diagnostic Modalities

#### Digital Rectal Examination

- Before PSA, **DRE was the only detection tool**.
- **Limitations**: fair reproducibility even among experts; misses many early cancers; **little value at PSA <3.0 ng/mL**; no proven mortality benefit, and some advise against routine screening DRE.
- **Drawbacks**: separate attendance (blood allows reflex testing on one sample), cost, and it **deters screening uptake**.
- **Why it persists**: an abnormal DRE carries real cancer risk and the examination is simple, so most urologists **combine PSA and DRE**; many risk nomograms include DRE.

#### Prostate-Specific Antigen

- **Human kallikrein-family protease** secreted at very high concentration (mg/mL) into seminal fluid; circulates **complexed (bound) and free**, measured by U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved assays.

##### Factors influencing PSA

<p class="tt">Comparison — What moves serum PSA</p>

| Factor | Effect | Point to hold |
|---|---|---|
| **Prostate disease** — cancer, benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH), prostatitis | ↑ | **Most important factor**; not cancer-specific; disease possible with normal PSA |
| Prostate volume · age | ↑ · ↑ | Volume explains far more PSA variance than age |
| Black ethnicity | ↑ | Higher PSA even without cancer |
| Massage, biopsy, transurethral resection | ↑ | Architectural disruption releases PSA into blood |
| DRE | Slight ↑ | Within assay error; **rarely a false positive** |
| Ejaculation | Conflicting | If minimally raised, **repeat after 48 hours' abstinence** |
| Hypogonadism | ↓ | **Androgen-dependent** (appears at puberty with rising luteinising hormone and testosterone); may mask cancer |
| Obesity · statins | Slight ↓ · may ↓ | Obesity by haemodilution |
| 5α-reductase inhibitors | ↓ | See below |
| BPH surgery | ↓ | Removes the transition zone; **resets baseline** variably |
| Cancer treatment — luteinising hormone-releasing hormone agonists, orchidectomy, radiotherapy, prostatectomy, cryotherapy | ↓ | Less epithelium, less PSA per cell |
| Change of assay | Variable | **Pseudo-acceleration or pseudo-deceleration** on serial testing |
| Genetics | Variable | Genetically adjusted PSA proposed |

- **5α-reductase inhibitors lower PSA**: finasteride (type 2) and dutasteride (types 1 and 2); **finasteride 1 mg for alopecia lowers PSA as much as 5 mg for BPH**.
- **Baseline PSA before starting**, then serial measurement.
- Correction: **×2 after ≥12 months**; response varies, so **×2.3 at 2 years, ×2.5 at 7 years** proposed; or take the **on-treatment nadir as new baseline** and investigate any rise.
- **Interpret every PSA against** age, urinary tract infection or prostate disease, recent procedures and prostate-directed treatment.

##### Clinical use for diagnosis

- Original FDA-approved assays (1994) measured **free PSA (fPSA) plus PSA complexed to α1-antichymotrypsin (cPSA)** = total PSA (tPSA); separate fPSA and cPSA assays followed.
- PSA testing detects **more cancers, and more organ-confined cancers**, than detection without it.
- **Risk is continuous**: future cancer risk and the present chance of a positive biopsy both rise incrementally with PSA across its full range (Table 157.1); baseline PSA predicts later detection.
- **Malmö Preventive Project**: **PSA measured in the 40s predicts metastasis and death more than 25 years later**.

- **What it is for**: PSA and DRE are **independent, additive** indicators, and **no PSA level carries zero risk**.
- **Pattern**: yield and high-grade rate climb with every PSA band; a **suspicious DRE roughly doubles the yield** at a given PSA and multiplies the high-grade rate.
- **Traps**: PSA 0–1 ng/mL still yields ~9% (Prostate Cancer Prevention Trial, PCPT) — **"normal" PSA does not exclude cancer**; **detection rate is not yield**.

##### Triggers for prostate biopsy

- **No agreed threshold**: dichotomising PSA discards information; **PCPT shows risk is continuous**, so some avoid calling PSA "elevated" or "abnormal".
- **Trade-off**: a higher threshold misses curable cancer; a lower threshold adds unnecessary biopsies and overdiagnosis.
- **Thresholds**: **4 ng/mL** original FDA upper limit; many clinicians use **2.5–3 ng/mL**; most ERSPC centres used **3 ng/mL**; **10 ng/mL** suggested **over 70 years** to reduce harm (AUA).
- **Repeat a suspicious PSA before biopsy** — physiological fluctuation causes false positives.
- **No empirical antibiotics for a raised PSA alone** without urinary symptoms (Choosing Wisely).
- Combine PSA with **family history, ethnicity and DRE**.

#### Prostate-Specific Antigen Derivatives and Molecular Forms

- All aim to raise **specificity**, by **volume adjustment, kinetics or molecular form**.
- **Volume** (ultrasonography or magnetic resonance imaging [MRI]) corrects for common BPH-driven PSA rises.
- **Why fPSA is lower in cancer**: differential isoform expression — **transition zone (origin of BPH) vs peripheral zone (origin of most cancers)** — so cancer carries more complexed PSA and a **lower percentage free (%fPSA)**.
- **Isoform sequence**: proPSA (precursor, secreted by luminal epithelium) → active fPSA → cleaved to **benign PSA (bPSA) or intact PSA (iPSA)**, both inactive and uncomplexed.

<p class="tt">Comparison — PSA derivatives and molecular forms</p>

| Marker | Definition | Cut-off / finding | Use and limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PSA density (PSAD)** | PSA ÷ prostate volume | **≥0.15 → biopsy** (PSA 4–10, normal DRE) | Counselling at 4–10; repeat biopsy; **active surveillance eligibility**; inconsistently validated |
| Complexed PSAD · transition zone density | cPSA ÷ volume · PSA ÷ transition zone volume | — | Transition zone is the main PSA source without cancer |
| **PSA velocity (PSAV)** | PSA change over time | **>0.75 ng/mL/yr** (PSA 4–10); **0.4** at lower PSA | Confounded by physiological fluctuation, calculation method and timing |
| PSAV — prognostic | — | **>0.35 ng/mL/yr** 10–15 years before diagnosis → **life-threatening cancer**; **>2 ng/mL/yr** in the year before diagnosis → **cancer death after surgery or radiotherapy** | Systematic review: no gain over PSA alone; later studies: some gain — unresolved |
| **%fPSA** | fPSA ÷ tPSA; low in cancer | **25%** (PSA 4–10, benign DRE): ~95% of cancers found, ~20% of biopsies spared | Best at 4–10, unclear <4; **no volume needed**; **unaffected by ethnicity or 5α-reductase inhibitors**; rises with age and volume; assay- and gland-size-dependent |
| **cPSA** | Complexed fraction; high in cancer | — | At high sensitivity: **specificity > tPSA, ≈ %fPSA** (PSA 4–10 and 2.6–4.0) |
| proPSA | Precursor; relatively high in cancer | — | May help at PSA 2–10; not always additive to %fPSA |
| bPSA | Found in nodular BPH tissue | — | **Marker of BPH** |
| **Human kallikrein 2 (hK2)** | Related serine protease; high in poorly differentiated cancer | — | Inconsistent for detection; **tracks grade and volume** |

- At 95% sensitivity, **%fPSA 25% matches PSAD 0.078 in specificity** without a volume measurement.

##### Multiplex tests

- **Prostate Health Index (phi)**, FDA-approved: tPSA + fPSA + [−2]proPSA; **outperforms tPSA and %fPSA** for any and high-grade cancer in prospective trials.
- **4Kscore**: tPSA, fPSA, iPSA and hK2; **predicts clinically significant cancer**.

#### Other Markers

<p class="tt">Comparison — Blood, urine and tissue markers beyond PSA</p>

| Test | Sample | Basis | Bottom line |
|---|---|---|---|
| IsoPSA | Serum | Cancer-specific structural PSA isoforms (aqueous two-phase partitioning) | Validated ≥50 years, tPSA ≥4 ng/mL; **beats tPSA and %fPSA** |

| ConfirmMDx | **Benign biopsy tissue** | Multiplex DNA methylation | **After a negative biopsy**: high negative predictive value → avoids repeat biopsy |

- **tPSA alone rarely triggers biopsy**: add serum or urine biomarkers plus age, family history and ethnicity.

<p class="concept"><strong>Concept —</strong> Every refinement attacks one weakness: <strong>PSA is organ-specific, not cancer-specific</strong>. Volume corrects for BPH, kinetics for noise, molecular forms for zone of origin — none replaces biopsy, but together they select who needs one.</p>
