## STAGING

### General Concepts of Staging

- **Clinical staging predicts extent from pretreatment parameters** — DRE, PSA and derivatives, biopsy, imaging — for prognosis and treatment choice.

#### Clinical Versus Pathological Staging

| | Clinical stage | Pathological stage |
|---|---|---|
| Basis | DRE, PSA, needle biopsy, imaging — **before treatment** | Histology of prostate, seminal vesicles and pelvic nodes (if dissected) — **after removal** |
| Value | Guides initial decisions | **More accurate; better predicts outcome** |

- **Biochemical recurrence-free and cancer-specific survival both fall as pathological stage rises**.
- **Pathological predictors after radical prostatectomy**: tumour grade, surgical margin status, extracapsular extension, seminal vesicle invasion, pelvic node involvement.

#### Classifications

- **Whitmore–Jewett** system: historical interest only.
- **TNM (tumour, node, metastasis)** is current — adopted by the American Joint Committee on Cancer in **1975**, repeatedly revised.
- The latest version builds **prognostic stage groups from TNM + PSA + grade group 1–5**.

<p class="tt">TABLE 157.2 2017 TNM Clinical Staging Systems for Prostate Cancer</p>

| Category | Stage | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **cT — not palpable** | TX · T0 | Cannot be assessed · no evidence of primary |
| | T1a · T1b | Incidental at resection: **≤5%** · **>5%** of resected tissue |
| | T1c | **Found by needle biopsy** (one or both sides), not palpable |
| **cT — palpable, confined** | T2a · T2b · T2c | **≤ half of one side** · > half of one side, not both · **both sides** |
| **cT — extraprostatic** | T3a | Extraprostatic extension (unilateral or bilateral), not fixed |
| | T3b | **Seminal vesicle invasion** |
| | T4 | **Fixed**, or invades structures **other than seminal vesicles**: external sphincter, rectum, bladder, levator muscles, pelvic wall |
| **cN** | NX · N0 · N1 | Not assessed · no regional nodes · **regional node metastasis** |
| **cM** | MX · M0 | Not assessed · none |
| | M1a · M1b · M1c | **Non-regional nodes** · **bone** · other sites ± bone |

- **What it is for**: clinical T stage is set by **palpability**, and T1 by how the cancer was found.
- **Pattern**: T1 impalpable → T2 palpable and confined → T3 beyond capsule → T4 fixed or invading neighbours.
- **Traps**: **seminal vesicle invasion is T3b, not T4**; **non-regional nodes are M1a, not N1**; bilateral impalpable biopsy-detected cancer stays **T1c, not T2c**.

### Prediction of Tumour Extent

#### Prostate-Specific Antigen

- PSA **rises with pathological stage** (volume link disputed) but **cannot stage an individual**: stages overlap, BPH contributes, and **poorly differentiated tumours make less PSA per gram**.
- **Organ-confined disease**: **~80% at PSA <4**, **~66% at 4–10**, **<50% at >10 ng/mL**.
- **Pelvic node involvement**: **~20% at PSA >20**, **~75% at PSA >50 ng/mL**.
- PSA derivatives and newer PSA-based tests are also studied for grade and extent.
- **Prostatic acid phosphatase** relates to stage and progression but has been **displaced by PSA**.

#### Digital Rectal Examination

- Establishes palpability → **clinical T stage**; an abnormal DRE raises the risk of **grade group 4–5** cancer in screened men.
- **Poor sensitivity and reproducibility → over- and understaging**; useful only combined with other parameters.

### Prostate Needle Biopsy

- **Histological grade is the most important biopsy finding**; the **Gleason system** is the most widely used.
- **Gleason score**: at low power, grade (1–5) the **predominant pattern (largest area)** → grade the **second most common** → sum = 2–10.
- **Tertiary pattern 5** (2005 consensus): 3 + 4 or 4 + 3 with tertiary 5 is recorded as **3 + 5 or 4 + 5**.
- Scores 2–5 are no longer assigned, so the **lowest score in practice is 6** — confusing to patients and a **barrier to active surveillance** → **grade groups**:

| Grade group | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gleason score | ≤6 | **3 + 4 = 7** | **4 + 3 = 7** | 8 (4 + 4, 3 + 5, 5 + 3) | 9–10 |

- Grade groups simplify grading, **label the lowest risk as group 1 rather than "score 6"**, and predict prognosis accurately.
- **Grade is never used alone**: number and percentage of **positive cores** and **intraductal or cribriform patterns** are prognostic and guide active surveillance selection.
- **Seminal vesicle or periprostatic fat on biopsy → worse prognosis**; seminal vesicle or capsular biopsy: routine for some, **only for a large palpable basal tumour** for others.

<p class="concept"><strong>Concept —</strong> Grade groups <strong>renumbered the Gleason scale without re-measuring it</strong>: the change is communicative, and the examinable point is that 3 + 4 and 4 + 3 are different groups.</p>

### Combined Use of Pretreatment Parameters

- **Nomograms combining T stage, PSA and Gleason score or grade group** predict extent and long-term outcome better than any single parameter.

<p class="tt">Comparison — D'Amico risk groups</p>

| Risk group | Criteria | Freedom from disease at 10 years after radical prostatectomy |
|---|---|---|
| **Low** | T1–T2a **and** PSA ≤10 **and** Gleason ≤6 | 83% |
| **Intermediate** | T2b **or** PSA >10 to <20 **or** Gleason 7 | 46% |
| **High** | T2c **or** PSA >20 **or** Gleason 8–10 | 29% |

- **Trap**: low risk needs **all three**; intermediate or high needs **any one**.
- Later validated schemes include the **Cancer of the Prostate Risk Assessment (CAPRA) score**. Multiparameter risk stratification is the basis of counselling.

### Imaging

- **Multiparametric MRI (mpMRI)** = T2-weighted + **diffusion-weighted + dynamic contrast-enhanced** sequences → better locoregional characterisation.
- **Uses**: planning before definitive local therapy; **active surveillance candidacy**; **MRI–TRUS fusion targeted biopsy**.
- AUA and Society of Abdominal Radiology: **strongly consider high-quality MRI before repeat biopsy after a negative biopsy**; a **suspicious lesion warrants targeted biopsy**. The key points go further: **MRI before any biopsy**.
- **Staging is risk-based**, weighing pre-test probability of metastasis against test sensitivity and specificity:

| Asymptomatic low or intermediate risk | High risk |
|---|---|
| Metastasis unlikely → **no routine CT abdomen–pelvis or bone scan** | **Bone scan + CT or mpMRI** |

- **Imaging is overused**: nodal disease is rare in contemporary screened populations.
- **Ultrasound advances** — colour and power Doppler, three-dimensional contrast Doppler, **HistoScanning** (computer-aided tissue characterisation) — remain investigational.
- **Prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA) positron emission tomography (PET)**: **⁶⁸Ga-PSMA-11 and piflufolastat F-18 (¹⁸F-DCFPyL)** are **FDA-approved for initial staging at high risk of metastasis**; **more sensitive and specific than conventional imaging**; benefit after negative conventional imaging unproven — consider in high risk.

<p class="concept"><strong>Concept —</strong> Staging imaging follows <strong>pre-test probability</strong>: yield is negligible in low- and intermediate-risk disease, so only high-risk disease is scanned.</p>

### Molecular Staging

<p class="tt">Comparison — Tissue-based genomic tests</p>

| Test | Basis |
|---|---|
| Prolaris | **Cell-cycle progression** gene panel (proliferation) |
| Oncotype DX Prostate | Quantitative reverse-transcriptase polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR): **17 genes, 5 pathways** |
| Decipher | **22 RNA biomarkers**, multiple pathways |

- Variously predict **adverse pathology, biochemical recurrence, metastasis and cancer death**; may refine standard risk groups, but **need prospective validation**.

### Pelvic Lymphadenectomy

- **No consistent oncological benefit** from pelvic lymph node dissection (PLND); its value is **staging** — refined risk stratification, counselling and guiding selective secondary therapy.
- **Decide by nomogram-predicted risk** of nodal disease (guideline thresholds vary), balanced against PLND morbidity.

<p class="concept"><strong>Concept —</strong> PLND is <strong>a staging operation, not a therapeutic one</strong>; it is justified by the information it yields, so the decision rests on predicted nodal risk.</p>

<p class="kpt">KEY POINTS: DIAGNOSIS AND STAGING OF PROSTATE CANCER</p>

- **Offer informed choice** on PSA testing; **combine PSA with DRE and clinical risk**.
- **Repeat PSA or add biomarkers** before biopsy; **MRI before biopsy** improves detection of significant cancer.
- **Assign a risk group at diagnosis** — it directs metastatic imaging and treatment.
