# Chapter 157 — Diagnosis and Staging of Prostate Cancer — Session Record

## Instalment 1 — Presentation, Screening Concepts, ERSPC and PLCO, Guideline Recommendations, Digital Rectal Examination, Factors Influencing PSA, 5α-Reductase Inhibitors, Table 157.1, Biopsy Triggers, PSA Density, Velocity, Free and Complexed PSA, Isoforms, hK2, Multiplex Tests, Other Markers

- **Early prostate cancer is rarely symptomatic**; symptoms imply locally advanced or metastatic disease.
- **Locally advanced disease**: obstructive urinary symptoms, ureteric obstruction (pain or renal failure), haematuria, reduced ejaculate volume and, rarely, erectile dysfunction.
- **Metastatic disease**: bone pain, pathological fracture, lower-limb oedema; less commonly malignant retroperitoneal fibrosis, paraneoplastic syndromes, disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC) and paralysis from spinal cord compression.
- **Advanced presentation is now uncommon** owing to screening with prostate-specific antigen (PSA) and digital rectal examination (DRE); diagnosis is usually by **transrectal ultrasound (TRUS)-guided needle biopsy before symptoms**.
- **Staging defines extent and risk** for management and prognosis: PSA, DRE, biopsy **grade and cancer volume**, and imaging where indicated.

## DIAGNOSIS

### Screening

#### General Concepts of Screening

- **Screening** tests healthy, asymptomatic populations; **diagnosis** identifies disease in people with signs or symptoms.
- **Early diagnosis is not invariably beneficial**: screening harms through **overdiagnosis and overtreatment**.
- Screening **reduces advanced disease at diagnosis** and, in recent data, **prostate cancer mortality**; the dispute is the **balance of benefit and harm**, which depends on the individual's characteristics and preferences.
- Since PSA testing spread (early 1990s), **prostate cancer mortality has fallen by more than 40%** and advanced disease at diagnosis by about three-quarters (Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results [SEER]); modelling attributes **half to two-thirds of the mortality fall** to screening.
- **Randomised trials of screened versus unscreened populations** are the highest level of evidence.

#### Randomised Trials

<p class="tt">Comparison — The two screening trials: ERSPC vs PLCO</p>

| | ERSPC | PLCO |
|---|---|---|
| Name | European Randomised Study of Screening for Prostate Cancer | Prostate, Lung, Colorectal and Ovarian Cancer Screening Trial (National Cancer Institute, NCI) |
| Started · primary endpoint | 1993 · prostate cancer-specific mortality | 1993 · prostate cancer-specific mortality |
| Median follow-up | 16 years | 17 years |
| Mortality result | **20% relative reduction** | **No difference** |
| Other effects | Fewer high-grade and locally advanced or metastatic cancers | — |
| To prevent one death | **Invite 570, diagnose 18 extra** (16 years) | — |
| Why it differs | — | **Contamination** (control-arm PSA testing) and **threefold fewer events → low power** |

- Both trials show **overdiagnosis** (cancers otherwise never detected) and overtreatment — **worst after 65 years**, where a randomised trial of radical prostatectomy versus watchful waiting showed minimal benefit; **average age at diagnosis is about 66**.

<p class="concept"><strong>Concept —</strong> The ERSPC–PLCO discordance is a matter of trial design, not biology: <strong>control-arm contamination and too few events</strong> can erase a real screening effect.</p>

#### Specialty Group Recommendations

<p class="tt">Comparison — Screening recommendations by body</p>

| Body | Who and when | Stance |
|---|---|---|
| U.S. Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) 2008 | <75 years · ≥75 years | Insufficient evidence · **against** |
| USPSTF 2012 (draft 2011) | All ages | **Grade D — against screening** |
| USPSTF 2018 (draft 2017) | **55–69 years** · ≥70 years | **Grade C — shared decision making** · against |
| American College of Preventive Medicine | Population | Insufficient evidence for routine PSA + DRE |
| American Cancer Society | Life expectancy ≥10 years; **from 50** (average risk); **earlier** for Black people or a first-degree relative (father, brother) diagnosed <65 years | Informed decision |
| American Urological Association (AUA) 2013, validated 2018 | **55–69 years** | Shared decision making; **2-yearly interval** (from ERSPC) keeps benefit, reduces harm |
| National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) | **From 45 years** | Risk–benefit discussion, baseline PSA; repeat every **1–4 years by PSA result** |

- USPSTF credits **active surveillance** (observation with selective delayed treatment) with reducing screening harms.
- Ages and interval **will stay debated**: further randomised trials are logistically unlikely.
- **Opportunistic screening is prevalent** in the United States; adults at periodic health examinations should be told the benefits and risks.

<p class="concept"><strong>Concept —</strong> Every body converges on <strong>informed or shared decision making</strong>; they differ only on the age window — NCCN starts earliest (45), USPSTF stops most clearly (70).</p>

### Diagnostic Modalities

#### Digital Rectal Examination

- Before PSA, **DRE was the only detection tool**.
- **Limitations**: fair reproducibility even among experts; misses many early cancers; **little value at PSA <3.0 ng/mL**; no proven mortality benefit, and some advise against routine screening DRE.
- **Drawbacks**: separate attendance (blood allows reflex testing on one sample), cost, and it **deters screening uptake**.
- **Why it persists**: an abnormal DRE carries real cancer risk and the examination is simple, so most urologists **combine PSA and DRE**; many risk nomograms include DRE.

#### Prostate-Specific Antigen

- **Human kallikrein-family protease** secreted at very high concentration (mg/mL) into seminal fluid; circulates **complexed (bound) and free**, measured by U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved assays.

##### Factors influencing PSA

<p class="tt">Comparison — What moves serum PSA</p>

| Factor | Effect | Point to hold |
|---|---|---|
| **Prostate disease** — cancer, benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH), prostatitis | ↑ | **Most important factor**; not cancer-specific; disease possible with normal PSA |
| Prostate volume · age | ↑ · ↑ | Volume explains far more PSA variance than age |
| Black ethnicity | ↑ | Higher PSA even without cancer |
| Massage, biopsy, transurethral resection | ↑ | Architectural disruption releases PSA into blood |
| DRE | Slight ↑ | Within assay error; **rarely a false positive** |
| Ejaculation | Conflicting | If minimally raised, **repeat after 48 hours' abstinence** |
| Hypogonadism | ↓ | **Androgen-dependent** (appears at puberty with rising luteinising hormone and testosterone); may mask cancer |
| Obesity · statins | Slight ↓ · may ↓ | Obesity by haemodilution |
| 5α-reductase inhibitors | ↓ | See below |
| BPH surgery | ↓ | Removes the transition zone; **resets baseline** variably |
| Cancer treatment — luteinising hormone-releasing hormone agonists, orchidectomy, radiotherapy, prostatectomy, cryotherapy | ↓ | Less epithelium, less PSA per cell |
| Change of assay | Variable | **Pseudo-acceleration or pseudo-deceleration** on serial testing |
| Genetics | Variable | Genetically adjusted PSA proposed |

- **5α-reductase inhibitors lower PSA**: finasteride (type 2) and dutasteride (types 1 and 2); **finasteride 1 mg for alopecia lowers PSA as much as 5 mg for BPH**.
- **Baseline PSA before starting**, then serial measurement.
- Correction: **×2 after ≥12 months**; response varies, so **×2.3 at 2 years, ×2.5 at 7 years** proposed; or take the **on-treatment nadir as new baseline** and investigate any rise.
- **Interpret every PSA against** age, urinary tract infection or prostate disease, recent procedures and prostate-directed treatment.

##### Clinical use for diagnosis

- Original FDA-approved assays (1994) measured **free PSA (fPSA) plus PSA complexed to α1-antichymotrypsin (cPSA)** = total PSA (tPSA); separate fPSA and cPSA assays followed.
- PSA testing detects **more cancers, and more organ-confined cancers**, than detection without it.
- **Risk is continuous**: future cancer risk and the present chance of a positive biopsy both rise incrementally with PSA across its full range (Table 157.1); baseline PSA predicts later detection.
- **Malmö Preventive Project**: **PSA measured in the 40s predicts metastasis and death more than 25 years later**.

<p class="tt">TABLE 157.1 Prostate Cancer Detection as a Function of Serum Prostate-Specific Antigen Level and Digital Rectal Examination Findings in Contemporary Series</p>

| PSA (ng/mL) | Yield on biopsy, DRE non-suspicious (%) | Yield on biopsy, DRE suspicious (%) | High-grade (Gleason ≥7) on biopsy (%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–1 | 8.8 | — | 0.9 |
| 1–2 | 17.0 | — | 2.0 |
| 0–2 | 8–12 | — | 1.4 |
| 2–4 | 15–25 | — | 5.2 |
| <4 | 15 | 13–17 | 2.3 (DRE –) |
| 4–10 | 17–32 | **45–51** | 4.1 (–) · 11.7 (+) |
| >4 | 23–38 | 55–63 | 5.8 (–) · 20.6 (+) |
| >10 | 43–65 | **70–90** | 19.4 (–) · **50.5 (+)** |

<p class="src">Data from several contemporary series (Andriole et al., 2005; Catalona et al., 1998; Crawford et al., 1996; Schröder et al., 1998; Thompson et al., 2004). Yield = cancers ÷ men biopsied; detection rate (cancers ÷ men screened) is far lower: 1–3% at PSA <4, 11–27% at 4–10, 31–76% at >10.</p>

- **What it is for**: PSA and DRE are **independent, additive** indicators, and **no PSA level carries zero risk**.
- **Pattern**: yield and high-grade rate climb with every PSA band; a **suspicious DRE roughly doubles the yield** at a given PSA and multiplies the high-grade rate.
- **Traps**: PSA 0–1 ng/mL still yields ~9% (Prostate Cancer Prevention Trial, PCPT) — **"normal" PSA does not exclude cancer**; **detection rate is not yield**.

##### Triggers for prostate biopsy

- **No agreed threshold**: dichotomising PSA discards information; **PCPT shows risk is continuous**, so some avoid calling PSA "elevated" or "abnormal".
- **Trade-off**: a higher threshold misses curable cancer; a lower threshold adds unnecessary biopsies and overdiagnosis.
- **Thresholds**: **4 ng/mL** original FDA upper limit; many clinicians use **2.5–3 ng/mL**; most ERSPC centres used **3 ng/mL**; **10 ng/mL** suggested **over 70 years** to reduce harm (AUA).
- **Repeat a suspicious PSA before biopsy** — physiological fluctuation causes false positives.
- **No empirical antibiotics for a raised PSA alone** without urinary symptoms (Choosing Wisely).
- Combine PSA with **family history, ethnicity and DRE**.

#### Prostate-Specific Antigen Derivatives and Molecular Forms

- All aim to raise **specificity**, by **volume adjustment, kinetics or molecular form**.
- **Volume** (ultrasonography or magnetic resonance imaging [MRI]) corrects for common BPH-driven PSA rises.
- **Why fPSA is lower in cancer**: differential isoform expression — **transition zone (origin of BPH) vs peripheral zone (origin of most cancers)** — so cancer carries more complexed PSA and a **lower percentage free (%fPSA)**.
- **Isoform sequence**: proPSA (precursor, secreted by luminal epithelium) → active fPSA → cleaved to **benign PSA (bPSA) or intact PSA (iPSA)**, both inactive and uncomplexed.

<p class="tt">Comparison — PSA derivatives and molecular forms</p>

| Marker | Definition | Cut-off / finding | Use and limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PSA density (PSAD)** | PSA ÷ prostate volume | **≥0.15 → biopsy** (PSA 4–10, normal DRE) | Counselling at 4–10; repeat biopsy; **active surveillance eligibility**; inconsistently validated |
| Complexed PSAD · transition zone density | cPSA ÷ volume · PSA ÷ transition zone volume | — | Transition zone is the main PSA source without cancer |
| **PSA velocity (PSAV)** | PSA change over time | **>0.75 ng/mL/yr** (PSA 4–10); **0.4** at lower PSA | Confounded by physiological fluctuation, calculation method and timing |
| PSAV — prognostic | — | **>0.35 ng/mL/yr** 10–15 years before diagnosis → **life-threatening cancer**; **>2 ng/mL/yr** in the year before diagnosis → **cancer death after surgery or radiotherapy** | Systematic review: no gain over PSA alone; later studies: some gain — unresolved |
| **%fPSA** | fPSA ÷ tPSA; low in cancer | **25%** (PSA 4–10, benign DRE): ~95% of cancers found, ~20% of biopsies spared | Best at 4–10, unclear <4; **no volume needed**; **unaffected by ethnicity or 5α-reductase inhibitors**; rises with age and volume; assay- and gland-size-dependent |
| **cPSA** | Complexed fraction; high in cancer | — | At high sensitivity: **specificity > tPSA, ≈ %fPSA** (PSA 4–10 and 2.6–4.0) |
| proPSA | Precursor; relatively high in cancer | — | May help at PSA 2–10; not always additive to %fPSA |
| bPSA | Found in nodular BPH tissue | — | **Marker of BPH** |
| **Human kallikrein 2 (hK2)** | Related serine protease; high in poorly differentiated cancer | — | Inconsistent for detection; **tracks grade and volume** |

- At 95% sensitivity, **%fPSA 25% matches PSAD 0.078 in specificity** without a volume measurement.

##### Multiplex tests

- **Prostate Health Index (phi)**, FDA-approved: tPSA + fPSA + [−2]proPSA; **outperforms tPSA and %fPSA** for any and high-grade cancer in prospective trials.
- **4Kscore**: tPSA, fPSA, iPSA and hK2; **predicts clinically significant cancer**.

#### Other Markers

<p class="tt">Comparison — Blood, urine and tissue markers beyond PSA</p>

| Test | Sample | Basis | Bottom line |
|---|---|---|---|
| IsoPSA | Serum | Cancer-specific structural PSA isoforms (aqueous two-phase partitioning) | Validated ≥50 years, tPSA ≥4 ng/mL; **beats tPSA and %fPSA** |
| SelectMDx | Urine mRNA | HOXC6, DLX1 | Biopsy-naive: spares biopsies, few high-grade missed |
| ExoDx | Urine exosome RNA | Three-gene expression | Separates **Gleason ≥7** from Gleason 6 and benign; avoids about a quarter of biopsies |
| **Prostate cancer gene 3 (PCA3)** | Urine mRNA | Noncoding prostate-specific mRNA overexpressed in cancer | Predicts positive **initial or repeat** biopsy; **conflicting for aggressiveness** |
| TMPRSS2:ERG | Urine | Gene fusion (cf. BCR-ABL in leukaemia) | Higher specificity; **conflicting for aggressiveness** |
| MyProstateScore (MPS) | Urine + serum | tPSA + PCA3 + TMPRSS2:ERG | About half spared immediate biopsy |
| ConfirmMDx | **Benign biopsy tissue** | Multiplex DNA methylation | **After a negative biopsy**: high negative predictive value → avoids repeat biopsy |

- **tPSA alone rarely triggers biopsy**: add serum or urine biomarkers plus age, family history and ethnicity.

<p class="concept"><strong>Concept —</strong> Every refinement attacks one weakness: <strong>PSA is organ-specific, not cancer-specific</strong>. Volume corrects for BPH, kinetics for noise, molecular forms for zone of origin — none replaces biopsy, but together they select who needs one.</p>


### The numbers to lock in — Instalment 1

**Key facts, figures, thresholds**

- **PSA biopsy thresholds**: 4 ng/mL (original FDA) · 2.5–3 (common) · 3 (ERSPC) · 10 over 70 years (AUA).
- **DRE adds little at PSA <3.0 ng/mL**.
- **PSAD ≥0.15** → biopsy at PSA 4–10 with normal DRE.
- **PSAV >0.75 ng/mL/yr** (PSA 4–10); **0.4** at lower PSA; **>0.35** 10–15 years before diagnosis → life-threatening cancer; **>2** in the year before diagnosis → cancer death after treatment.
- **%fPSA 25%** (PSA 4–10, benign DRE) → ~95% of cancers detected, ~20% of biopsies spared; ≈ PSAD 0.078.
- **5α-reductase inhibitor correction**: ×2 at ≥12 months; ×2.3 at 2 years; ×2.5 at 7 years; finasteride 1 mg = 5 mg effect.
- **Repeat PSA after 48 hours' abstinence** if minimally raised.
- **Table 157.1**: PSA 0–1 → ~9% yield; 4–10 → 17–32% (DRE –) vs 45–51% (DRE +); >10 → 43–65% vs 70–90%.
- **PSA era**: mortality down >40%; average age at diagnosis ~66.

**Commonest / first-line**

- **Most important influence on PSA**: prostate disease (cancer, BPH, prostatitis).
- **Most urologists combine PSA + DRE**; tPSA alone rarely triggers biopsy.

**Landmark trials**

- **ERSPC**: 20% relative reduction in cancer mortality at 16 years; invite 570 and diagnose 18 to prevent 1 death.
- **PLCO**: no mortality difference at 17 years — contamination, low power.
- **PCPT**: risk continuous with PSA; cancer present even at PSA 0–1.
- **Malmö Preventive Project**: PSA in the 40s predicts metastasis and death >25 years later.

**Dates and guidelines**

- **1993**: ERSPC and PLCO begin. **1994**: FDA approves PSA for early detection.
- **USPSTF**: 2008 against ≥75 · 2012 grade D · 2018 grade C for 55–69, against ≥70.
- **AUA** 55–69, 2-yearly; **NCCN** baseline at 45, repeat every 1–4 years; **American Cancer Society** from 50, earlier if high risk.

**Classic traps**

- **"Normal" PSA does not exclude cancer.**
- **Detection rate (per man screened) is not yield (per man biopsied).**
- **5α-reductase inhibitors lower tPSA but leave %fPSA unchanged**; both finasteride doses have the same effect.
- **DRE does not cause a false-positive PSA**; biopsy and transurethral resection raise PSA.
- **No antibiotics for a raised PSA alone.**
- **Hypogonadism lowers PSA** and can mask cancer.
- **PLCO's null result reflects contamination, not a lack of screening effect.**
- **fPSA is lower, not higher, in cancer**; bPSA marks BPH, proPSA marks cancer.
- **Changing assay** produces spurious PSA kinetics.


## Instalment 2 — Clinical vs Pathological Staging, TNM (Table 157.2), PSA and DRE for Extent, Needle Biopsy and Grade Groups, D'Amico and CAPRA, MRI, Staging Imaging, PSMA PET, Molecular Staging, Pelvic Lymphadenectomy, Key Points

## STAGING

### General Concepts of Staging

- **Clinical staging predicts extent from pretreatment parameters** — DRE, PSA and derivatives, biopsy, imaging — for prognosis and treatment choice.

#### Clinical Versus Pathological Staging

| | Clinical stage | Pathological stage |
|---|---|---|
| Basis | DRE, PSA, needle biopsy, imaging — **before treatment** | Histology of prostate, seminal vesicles and pelvic nodes (if dissected) — **after removal** |
| Value | Guides initial decisions | **More accurate; better predicts outcome** |

- **Biochemical recurrence-free and cancer-specific survival both fall as pathological stage rises**.
- **Pathological predictors after radical prostatectomy**: tumour grade, surgical margin status, extracapsular extension, seminal vesicle invasion, pelvic node involvement.

#### Classifications

- **Whitmore–Jewett** system: historical interest only.
- **TNM (tumour, node, metastasis)** is current — adopted by the American Joint Committee on Cancer in **1975**, repeatedly revised.
- The latest version builds **prognostic stage groups from TNM + PSA + grade group 1–5**.

<p class="tt">TABLE 157.2 2017 TNM Clinical Staging Systems for Prostate Cancer</p>

| Category | Stage | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **cT — not palpable** | TX · T0 | Cannot be assessed · no evidence of primary |
| | T1a · T1b | Incidental at resection: **≤5%** · **>5%** of resected tissue |
| | T1c | **Found by needle biopsy** (one or both sides), not palpable |
| **cT — palpable, confined** | T2a · T2b · T2c | **≤ half of one side** · > half of one side, not both · **both sides** |
| **cT — extraprostatic** | T3a | Extraprostatic extension (unilateral or bilateral), not fixed |
| | T3b | **Seminal vesicle invasion** |
| | T4 | **Fixed**, or invades structures **other than seminal vesicles**: external sphincter, rectum, bladder, levator muscles, pelvic wall |
| **cN** | NX · N0 · N1 | Not assessed · no regional nodes · **regional node metastasis** |
| **cM** | MX · M0 | Not assessed · none |
| | M1a · M1b · M1c | **Non-regional nodes** · **bone** · other sites ± bone |

- **What it is for**: clinical T stage is set by **palpability**, and T1 by how the cancer was found.
- **Pattern**: T1 impalpable → T2 palpable and confined → T3 beyond capsule → T4 fixed or invading neighbours.
- **Traps**: **seminal vesicle invasion is T3b, not T4**; **non-regional nodes are M1a, not N1**; bilateral impalpable biopsy-detected cancer stays **T1c, not T2c**.

### Prediction of Tumour Extent

#### Prostate-Specific Antigen

- PSA **rises with pathological stage** (volume link disputed) but **cannot stage an individual**: stages overlap, BPH contributes, and **poorly differentiated tumours make less PSA per gram**.
- **Organ-confined disease**: **~80% at PSA <4**, **~66% at 4–10**, **<50% at >10 ng/mL**.
- **Pelvic node involvement**: **~20% at PSA >20**, **~75% at PSA >50 ng/mL**.
- PSA derivatives and newer PSA-based tests are also studied for grade and extent.
- **Prostatic acid phosphatase** relates to stage and progression but has been **displaced by PSA**.

#### Digital Rectal Examination

- Establishes palpability → **clinical T stage**; an abnormal DRE raises the risk of **grade group 4–5** cancer in screened men.
- **Poor sensitivity and reproducibility → over- and understaging**; useful only combined with other parameters.

### Prostate Needle Biopsy

- **Histological grade is the most important biopsy finding**; the **Gleason system** is the most widely used.
- **Gleason score**: at low power, grade (1–5) the **predominant pattern (largest area)** → grade the **second most common** → sum = 2–10.
- **Tertiary pattern 5** (2005 consensus): 3 + 4 or 4 + 3 with tertiary 5 is recorded as **3 + 5 or 4 + 5**.
- Scores 2–5 are no longer assigned, so the **lowest score in practice is 6** — confusing to patients and a **barrier to active surveillance** → **grade groups**:

| Grade group | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gleason score | ≤6 | **3 + 4 = 7** | **4 + 3 = 7** | 8 (4 + 4, 3 + 5, 5 + 3) | 9–10 |

- Grade groups simplify grading, **label the lowest risk as group 1 rather than "score 6"**, and predict prognosis accurately.
- **Grade is never used alone**: number and percentage of **positive cores** and **intraductal or cribriform patterns** are prognostic and guide active surveillance selection.
- **Seminal vesicle or periprostatic fat on biopsy → worse prognosis**; seminal vesicle or capsular biopsy: routine for some, **only for a large palpable basal tumour** for others.

<p class="concept"><strong>Concept —</strong> Grade groups <strong>renumbered the Gleason scale without re-measuring it</strong>: the change is communicative, and the examinable point is that 3 + 4 and 4 + 3 are different groups.</p>

### Combined Use of Pretreatment Parameters

- **Nomograms combining T stage, PSA and Gleason score or grade group** predict extent and long-term outcome better than any single parameter.

<p class="tt">Comparison — D'Amico risk groups</p>

| Risk group | Criteria | Freedom from disease at 10 years after radical prostatectomy |
|---|---|---|
| **Low** | T1–T2a **and** PSA ≤10 **and** Gleason ≤6 | 83% |
| **Intermediate** | T2b **or** PSA >10 to <20 **or** Gleason 7 | 46% |
| **High** | T2c **or** PSA >20 **or** Gleason 8–10 | 29% |

- **Trap**: low risk needs **all three**; intermediate or high needs **any one**.
- Later validated schemes include the **Cancer of the Prostate Risk Assessment (CAPRA) score**. Multiparameter risk stratification is the basis of counselling.

### Imaging

- **Multiparametric MRI (mpMRI)** = T2-weighted + **diffusion-weighted + dynamic contrast-enhanced** sequences → better locoregional characterisation.
- **Uses**: planning before definitive local therapy; **active surveillance candidacy**; **MRI–TRUS fusion targeted biopsy**.
- AUA and Society of Abdominal Radiology: **strongly consider high-quality MRI before repeat biopsy after a negative biopsy**; a **suspicious lesion warrants targeted biopsy**. The key points go further: **MRI before any biopsy**.
- **Staging is risk-based**, weighing pre-test probability of metastasis against test sensitivity and specificity:

| Asymptomatic low or intermediate risk | High risk |
|---|---|
| Metastasis unlikely → **no routine CT abdomen–pelvis or bone scan** | **Bone scan + CT or mpMRI** |

- **Imaging is overused**: nodal disease is rare in contemporary screened populations.
- **Ultrasound advances** — colour and power Doppler, three-dimensional contrast Doppler, **HistoScanning** (computer-aided tissue characterisation) — remain investigational.
- **Prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA) positron emission tomography (PET)**: **⁶⁸Ga-PSMA-11 and piflufolastat F-18 (¹⁸F-DCFPyL)** are **FDA-approved for initial staging at high risk of metastasis**; **more sensitive and specific than conventional imaging**; benefit after negative conventional imaging unproven — consider in high risk.

<p class="concept"><strong>Concept —</strong> Staging imaging follows <strong>pre-test probability</strong>: yield is negligible in low- and intermediate-risk disease, so only high-risk disease is scanned.</p>

### Molecular Staging

<p class="tt">Comparison — Tissue-based genomic tests</p>

| Test | Basis |
|---|---|
| Prolaris | **Cell-cycle progression** gene panel (proliferation) |
| Oncotype DX Prostate | Quantitative reverse-transcriptase polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR): **17 genes, 5 pathways** |
| Decipher | **22 RNA biomarkers**, multiple pathways |

- Variously predict **adverse pathology, biochemical recurrence, metastasis and cancer death**; may refine standard risk groups, but **need prospective validation**.

### Pelvic Lymphadenectomy

- **No consistent oncological benefit** from pelvic lymph node dissection (PLND); its value is **staging** — refined risk stratification, counselling and guiding selective secondary therapy.
- **Decide by nomogram-predicted risk** of nodal disease (guideline thresholds vary), balanced against PLND morbidity.

<p class="concept"><strong>Concept —</strong> PLND is <strong>a staging operation, not a therapeutic one</strong>; it is justified by the information it yields, so the decision rests on predicted nodal risk.</p>

### The numbers to lock in — Instalment 2

**Key facts, figures, thresholds**

- **Organ-confined disease by PSA**: ~80% <4 · ~66% 4–10 · <50% >10.
- **Nodal disease by PSA**: ~20% >20 · ~75% >50.
- **T1a ≤5% vs T1b >5%** of resected tissue; T1c needle-biopsy detected.
- **T2a ≤ half one side · T2b > half one side · T2c both sides**.
- **Grade groups**: 1 ≤6 · 2 = 3 + 4 · 3 = 4 + 3 · 4 = 8 · 5 = 9–10.
- **D'Amico**: low T1–2a + PSA ≤10 + Gleason ≤6 (83%); intermediate T2b or PSA >10–<20 or Gleason 7 (46%); high T2c or PSA >20 or Gleason 8–10 (29%) — 10-year freedom from disease after radical prostatectomy.
- **Oncotype DX 17 genes; Decipher 22 RNA biomarkers**; Prolaris cell-cycle progression.

**Gold standards / first-line**

- **Most important biopsy information**: histological grade; **most used system**: Gleason.
- **Pathological stage is more accurate than clinical stage**.
- **Staging imaging, high risk**: bone scan + CT or mpMRI; PSMA PET approved for initial high-risk staging.
- **Before repeat biopsy after a negative biopsy**: MRI.

**Eponyms and named systems**

- **Whitmore–Jewett**: historical staging system, replaced by TNM.
- **Gleason**: sum of two most prevalent patterns, 2–10.
- **D'Amico**: three-tier risk from T stage, PSA, Gleason.
- **CAPRA**: validated multiparameter risk score.

**Dates**

- **1975**: TNM adopted by the American Joint Committee on Cancer. **2005**: Gleason consensus adds tertiary pattern 5. **2017**: current TNM.

**When to operate / not operate**

- **PLND**: by nomogram-predicted nodal risk — staging, not therapeutic.
- **Seminal vesicle or capsular biopsy**: only for a large palpable basal tumour (selective view).

**Classic traps**

- **Seminal vesicle invasion = T3b, not T4.**
- **Non-regional nodes = M1a, not N1.**
- **Bilateral impalpable biopsy-detected cancer = T1c, not T2c.**
- **3 + 4 (group 2) ≠ 4 + 3 (group 3).**
- **D'Amico low risk needs all three criteria; higher groups need only one.**
- **No routine CT or bone scan** in asymptomatic low or intermediate risk.
- **High-grade tumours make less PSA per gram**, so PSA understates them.
- **Prostatic acid phosphatase is obsolete.**
