## Instalment 1 — Chapter Opening; Overview of Genetics; Renal Agenesis and Dysplasia (Definition, Aetiology, Ultrasound) and Key Points; Hypoplasia and Hypodysplasia (Renal Hypoplasia, Oligomeganephronia, Ask-Upmark Kidney, Renal Hypodysplasia incl. PUV and Prune-Belly) and Key Points; Cystic Diseases Introduction and Cyst Biology; Figs 50.1, 50.3, 50.5, 50.6

Book pages 1001–1004.

### The numbers to lock in

- **Genes**:
  - **PAX2** → hypodysplasia or aplasia;
  - **EYA1** → BOR syndrome;
  - **HNF1B** → renal cysts and diabetes;
  - **PKD1/PKD2** → polycystic kidney disease (ciliary).
- **Definitions**:
  - AAP Section on Urology definitions, 1987;
  - dysgenesis = size, shape or structure; subtypes dysplasia and hypoplasia;
  - **dysplasia is histological — primitive duct (Ericsson and Ivemark, 1958)**.
- **Commonest**: **dysplasia is the primary cause of childhood ESRD.**
- **Ultrasound of dysplasia**: small, hyperechoic vs liver, loss of corticomedullary differentiation.
- **Oligomeganephronia**:
  - described 1962; usually bilateral;
  - **proteinuria first**; about 2 g/24 h in the teens; ESRD in the second decade;
  - protein 1.5 g/kg/day; ACE inhibitor even if normotensive;
  - **living related donor acceptable (not familial)**.
- **Ask-Upmark kidney**:
  - ≥10 years; young females; **severe hypertension**; retinopathy in half; 12–35 g; **slit-scar**; thyroid-like tubules;
  - **nephrectomy cures hypertension regardless of renin**;
  - reflux correction does **not** cure the hypertension.
- **Hypodysplasia**:
  - degree of dysplasia ∝ degree of ureteric ectopia (lateral ectopia → VUR; medial → hypodysplasia);
  - PUV: mild (subcapsular cysts) vs severe (large cysts, cartilage) forms.
- **Cyst biology**: three processes — proliferation ("second hit"), fluid secretion, matrix change; **primary cilium central**.
- **Eponyms**: Ericsson–Ivemark primitive duct; Ask-Upmark; Osathanondh–Potter (PUV dysplasia).
- **Classic traps**:
  - dysplasia is **histological, not clinical**;
  - not all cysts are dysplasia and not all dysplasia is cystic;
  - oligomeganephronia = **fewer, bigger** nephrons, whereas simple hypoplasia has normal nephron number;
  - Ask-Upmark is mostly **acquired** (reflux);
  - failed BP response after nephrectomy → look for a contralateral scar;
  - multicystic dysplasia arises **before** nephrons form; multilocular cyst is a **neoplasm**.

Nephrectomy and ARegPKD) and Key Points; ADPKD (Genetics, Contiguous Gene Syndrome, Clinical Features, Extrarenal Manifestations, RCC, Evaluation incl. Ravine Criteria, Treatment, Aneurysm Screening, Emerging Therapeutics — HALT-PKD, TEMPO, REPRISE) and Key Points; Table 50.2; Figs 50.8, 50.11, 50.12

Book pages 1004–1011. **Split note**: this stretch holds one box and two numbered tables with about eight Level B/C sections. It is under the four-table threshold but at the edge of the six-section guide, so ARPKD and ADPKD were handled as consecutive blocks with full numbers each. No formal split was required.

### The numbers to lock in

- **ARPKD**:
  - **PKHD1, chromosome 6**;
  - 1 in 10,000–50,000 (table: 1 in 5000–40,000);
  - up to 50% of neonates die in days (30–50% shortly after birth);
  - about half of neonatal survivors alive at 10 years;
  - presents to 13 years, rarely 20;
  - **all have congenital hepatic fibrosis**; no neoplasm association.
- **ARPKD nephrectomy (ARegPKD)**: very early bilateral nephrectomy (<3 months) → most neurological complications. **Be cautious.**
- **ADPKD**:
  - 1 in 400–1000 (table: 1 in 500–1100); **commonest inheritable**; 7–15% of haemodialysis;
  - 50% of offspring; 10% sporadic; 96% manifest by 90;
  - renal failure rare before 40.
- **PKD1 vs PKD2**:
  - **PKD1**: 16p, polycystin-1, 85%, cysts by 20, ESRD in the 50s;
  - **PKD2**: 4q, polycystin-2, 15%, ESRD in the 70s.
- **Contiguous gene syndrome**: **PKD1–TSC2 on chromosome 16**.
- **Clinical**:
  - asymmetric or "unilateral" in 17%; symptoms at 30–50;
  - haematuria 50% (presenting sign in 19–35%);
  - **pain = commonest adult presenting symptom**;
  - stones 20–30% (uric acid = calcium oxalate);
  - **anaemia unusual (erythropoietin)**.
- **Hypertension**: about 50% at 20–35 with normal function; >60% before impairment; about 100% at ESRD; renin-mediated.
- **Early ESRD risk factors**: PKD1, male sex, haematuria <30, hypertension <35, hyperlipidaemia, sickle trait.
- **Extrarenal**:
  - liver cysts almost all by 50 (females larger; oestrogen);
  - **ICA 10–30%, about 9% of these die of SAH**; average about 6 mm;
  - seminal vesicle cysts 40%, arachnoid 8%, pancreas 5%;
  - diverticulosis → more hepatic cysts and symptomatic aneurysms.
- **RCC in ADPKD**: **not increased**, but bilateral 12%, multicentric 28%, sarcomatoid 33%; adenomas 1 in 4–5.
- **Ravine criteria**: <30 years ≥2 cysts; 30–59 years ≥2 each kidney; ≥60 years ≥4 each kidney. Sensitivity only about 67% for PKD2 carriers <30.
- **No family history**: bilateral cysts + ≥2 of (bilateral enlargement, ≥3 liver cysts, cerebral aneurysm, solitary arachnoid, pineal, pancreatic or splenic cyst).
- **Haemorrhagic cyst on CT**: 50–90 HU.
- **Treatment**:
  - BP <130/80 standard; **ACE inhibitor or ARB first line**;
  - avoid NSAIDs;
  - lipophilic antibiotics for cyst infection (trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole, chloramphenicol, fluoroquinolones);
  - unroofing helps pain only; nephrectomy for symptomatic ESRD.
- **Aneurysm management**:
  - screen only high-risk patients (family history, prior rupture, major surgery, pilot, anxiety);
  - **<7 mm → conservative**; >7–10 mm (up to 2% per year) or symptomatic → clip or coil.
- **Landmark trials**:
  - **HALT-PKD**: ACE inhibitor + ARB = ACE inhibitor alone for TKV; rigorous BP 95–110/60–75 slowed TKV growth, no eGFR change, lowered LVMI and albuminuria;
  - **TEMPO and REPRISE**: tolvaptan slowed TKV growth and function decline; aquaresis and liver toxicity; **FDA approval April 2018**.
- **Classic traps**:
  - multicystic ≠ polycystic;
  - ARPKD kidneys are **homogeneous microcystic** (pyramids blend);
  - neonatal ADPKD can look identical → scan the parents;
  - **RCC is not increased in ADPKD**, unlike ARCD;
  - portal hypertension points to ARPKD;
  - a negative Ravine scan <30 does not exclude PKD2;
  - screen for aneurysms only if high risk;
  - anaemia is **unusual** in ADPKD ESRD.

## Instalment 3 — Juvenile Nephronophthisis and Medullary Cystic Disease Complex and Key Points; Multiple Malformation Syndromes with Renal Cysts; Tuberous Sclerosis Complex (Diagnostic Criteria, Clinical Features, RCC, Radiographic Evaluation incl. Fat-Poor AML, Treatment, International Consensus — Kidney); von Hippel–Lindau Disease (Aetiology and HIF, Clinical Features, Evaluation, Screening, Treatment); Tables 50.4 and 50.5; Key Points: Multiple Malformation Syndromes; Figs 50.13–50.15

Book pages 1011–1016.

### The numbers to lock in

- **NPH vs MCKD**:
  - NPH: autosomal recessive; **10–20% of childhood renal failure**; ESRD mean 13, almost always <25; **no hypertension**; extrarenal 20% (retinitis pigmentosa 16%, Senior–Løken);
  - MCKD: autosomal dominant; ESRD in the third–fourth decade; **hypertension**; MCKD2 hyperuricaemia and gout in teenage males;
  - both: polyuria and polydipsia >80%, salt wasting, renal failure 5–10 years after presentation.
- **NPH treatment**: sodium replacement; avoid salt restriction and diuretics; allografts do not recur.
- **TSC**:
  - up to 1 in 6000; TSC1 chromosome 9, TSC2 chromosome 16;
  - epilepsy 80%, intellectual disability 60%, adenoma sebaceum 75% (after 2 years);
  - ash-leaf macule the earliest sign.
- **TSC diagnosis**: 2 major, or 1 major + 2 minor. **Renal AML = major**; **multiple renal cysts = minor**.
- **AML in TSC**: 40–80%; rare <6 years, common >10 years; bilateral and multiple; never metastasises.
- **RCC in TSC**: about 2% (table 3%); female, younger, bilateral. Suspect with enlarging fat-free lesion or calcification.
- **Consensus**:
  - **MRI preferred** (25–30% of AMLs fat-poor, missed on ultrasound); contrast acceptable if GFR ≥60;
  - **AML >3 cm and growing → mTOR inhibitor (everolimus) first line**;
  - second line: embolisation plus steroids, resection, ablation;
  - acute bleed → embolisation plus steroids;
  - **avoid nephrectomy**;
  - imaging every 1–3 years (yearly if nearing 3 cm);
  - annual function, proteinuria and BP;
  - RAAS inhibitor first line for hypertension.
- **Older rule**: embolise or excise AML >4 cm.
- **VHL**:
  - 1 in 30,000–50,000; >95% penetrance by 65; **3p25**; mean age 35–40; no sex preference;
  - renal cysts 76% (bilateral 75%, multifocal 87%);
  - **RCC about 50%** (table 35–38%);
  - phaeochromocytoma 10–17% (key points 10–12%);
  - pancreatic cysts up to 70%; islet cell tumours about 12%;
  - epididymal cystadenoma 10–26%;
  - cerebellar haemangioblastoma symptomatic at 15–40 years (more than half of deaths).
- **VHL diagnosis**: with a family history, one lesion; without, ≥2 haemangioblastomas or 1 plus another lesion.
- **VHL treatment**:
  - **open nephron-sparing surgery is the standard for low-grade RCC**;
  - recurrence 75–85%; 10-year disease-specific survival 81–94%; radical nephrectomy historically 50%;
  - bilateral nephrectomy for high grade;
  - **partial adrenalectomy** (recurrence 15%);
  - ablation investigational.
- **VHL screening**:
  - Table 50.4 (Maher, 1990) vs Table 50.5 (VHL Alliance, 2020);
  - eye from before 1 year; BP from 2; metanephrines from 5; brain and spine MRI plus audiogram every 2 years from 11; abdominal MRI every 2 years from 15; IAC MRI once at 15; stop routine at 65; everything before conception.
- **Eponyms**:
  - **Senior–Løken**: NPH plus retinitis pigmentosa;
  - **Bardet–Biedl**: obesity, intellectual disability, polydactyly, retinitis pigmentosa, hypogenitalism;
  - **Cogan**: ocular motor apraxia;
  - **Joubert** phenotype;
  - **phakomatoses** (Sturge–Weber, TSC, neurofibromatosis, VHL).
- **HIF pair**: normoxia → pVHL destroys HIF; VHL loss → HIF-1α stable → VEGF, PDGF, TGF-β.
- **Classic traps**:
  - **no hypertension in juvenile NPH** (the key point wrongly lists it as a hallmark);
  - salt restriction harms NPH;
  - TSC cysts without AML mimic ADPKD;
  - fat-poor AML is benign, so don't operate;
  - **mTOR inhibitor, not nephrectomy**, for AML;
  - VHL goal = **control, not cure**;
  - phaeochromocytoma clusters in specific VHL families;
  - screen only genetic carriers.

## Instalment 4 — Multicystic Dysplastic Kidney (Types, Aetiology, Clinical Features, Evaluation incl. MCDK vs PUJ Obstruction, Treatment and Prognosis) and Key Points; Benign Multilocular Cyst (Spectrum, Clinical Features, Evaluation, Treatment) and Key Points; Simple Cysts (Incidence, Clinical Features, Evaluation: Ultrasound and CT Criteria, Hyperdense Cysts, Cyst Puncture, Paediatric Cysts); Figs 50.17, 50.19–50.21, 50.23, 50.24

Book pages 1017–1021.

### The numbers to lock in

- **MCDK**:
  - 1 per 1000–4000 (Chapter 49 gives 1 in 4300); male 55–70%;
  - **commonest renal cystic disease**; second commonest infant abdominal mass;
  - contralateral PUJ obstruction 3–12%, VUR 18–43%;
  - **about 40% involute**; no hypertension or neoplasm risk in large series.
- **MCDK types**: infundibulopelvic (commoner; pelvis and ureter atretic) vs hydronephrotic (upper ureter only).
- **MCDK genes**: EYA1, SIX1, WNT, WT1, GDNF, AT2, PAX2 (UB theory).
- **MCDK imaging**:
  - repeat ultrasound in the first days and at 1 month;
  - DMSA or MAG3: no uptake;
  - VCUG of little value if ultrasound is normal and there has been no UTI.
- **Remove MCDK if**: mass effect (respiration, feeding), hypertension, rupture, increasing solid tissue. Otherwise ignore; surveillance every 3–12 months has no proven value.
- **Multilocular cyst**:
  - 95% <4 or >30 years; <4 years male 2:1; >30 years female 8:1;
  - **nephrectomy (or partial) for all**; Wilms variants treated per NWTS; diagnosis only by excision.
- **Simple cysts**: children 0.1–0.45% (mean 0.22%); 20% by 40; 50% after 60; from 14 weeks in utero.
- **Ultrasound criteria (4)**: no echoes; thin smooth wall; acoustic enhancement; spherical.
- **CT criteria**: −10 to +20 HU; no enhancement; accuracy about 100%.
- **Hyperdense cyst**: 20–90 HU, no enhancement, ≤3 cm, ≥¼ circumference exophytic.
- **Calcification**: simple cyst 2% (peripheral) vs RCC 10% (central).
- **Cyst puncture (3)**: infection; low-level echoes on ultrasound but classic on CT; borderline lesion in a poor surgical candidate.
- **Paediatric simple cyst**: <15 mm, asymptomatic, <18 years → repeat ultrasound at 2 years, discharge if stable.
- **Classic traps**:
  - MCDK vs PUJ obstruction (central pelvis, communicating cysts, function);
  - multilocular cyst ≠ segment of MCDK;
  - imaging cannot exclude Wilms or RCC within a multilocular cyst;
  - enhancement = vascular tissue;
  - a cluster of cysts may hide a carcinoma;
  - don't label bilateral simple cysts as ADPKD without family history or genetics.

## Instalment 5 — Bosniak Classification (Box 50.2, IIF, Paediatric Modifications, Version 2019); Simple Cysts Treatment and Key Points; Medullary Sponge Kidney and Key Points; Sporadic Glomerulocystic Kidney Disease; Acquired Renal Cystic Disease (Aetiology, Clinical Features, Evaluation, Treatment, Screening, Transplantation) and Key Points; Calyceal Diverticulum (Pyelogenic Cyst); Parapelvic and Renal Sinus Cysts; Figs 50.25, 50.29, 50.30

Book pages 1021–1026.

### The numbers to lock in

- **Bosniak**:
  - 1986; clarified 1997; **IIF added 2003 (Israel and Bosniak)**; version 2019 incorporates MRI;
  - **I and II no surgery; IIF follow (about 11% progress); III surgery (more often benign); IV radical nephrectomy**;
  - paediatric: III–IV >90% intermediate or malignant pathology.
- **Simple cyst treatment**: none if asymptomatic. Aspiration ± sclerosant, or laparoscopic unroofing, for symptoms.
- **MSK**:
  - presents after 20 years;
  - renal colic 50–60%, UTI 20–33%, gross haematuria 10–18%;
  - 2.6–21% of stone formers;
  - hypercalciuria one-third to one-half; incomplete distal RTA 30–40%.
- **MSK first-line treatment**: potassium citrate. Thiazide for stone formers even without hypercalciuria. Inorganic phosphate if no thiazide — **not with urease infection**.
- **Sporadic GCKD**: non-heritable; small cysts of Bowman space; no family history or anomalies.
- **ARCD**:
  - defined as ≥3 cysts per kidney in ESRD; first described 1977;
  - 10% before dialysis → 44% at 3 years → 60% at 5 years → >90% at ≥10 years;
  - male 3:1; children 21.6–45.8%.
- **ARCD complications**:
  - bleeding up to 50%;
  - adenomas 10% (20–25% with ARCD);
  - RCC prevalence on haemodialysis about 1%;
  - RCC 3× with ARCD, 6× in large cystic kidneys;
  - renal malignancy on dialysis 5–50× general population;
  - post-transplant RCC >15×; native kidneys 4.5% of transplant malignancies; >70% of transplant RCCs in native kidneys; new cysts after transplant 18%; RCC 3–8 years post-transplant.
- **ARCD vs ADPKD**: <300 g vs >800 g.
- **ARCD tumours**:
  - **>3 cm → excise**; <3 cm → nephrectomy (fit) or annual CT;
  - **screen after >3 years of haemodialysis** (6-monthly ultrasound ± CT); restrict to <55 years, ≥3 years on dialysis, good health.
- **Haematuria threshold**: >5 red cells per high-power field → image.
- **Contrast**: avoid iodinated contrast pre-dialysis; gadolinium → NSF risk.
- **Renal sinus cysts**: 15–20% at autopsy; conservative management.
- **Eponyms and signs**:
  - MSK "brush bristles" / "bouquet of flowers";
  - Bosniak categories;
  - pyelogenic cyst = diverticulum from the pelvis.
- **Classic traps**:
  - calcification matters less than enhancement (IIF);
  - Bosniak III is more often benign but is still explored;
  - ARCD is caused by **uraemia, not dialysis**;
  - RCC on dialysis is indolent, after transplant aggressive;
  - inorganic phosphate with urease infection → struvite;
  - renal sinus cysts mimic hydronephrosis on unenhanced CT;
  - parapelvic describes location, renal sinus cyst describes origin.
