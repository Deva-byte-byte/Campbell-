# Chapter 50 — Renal Dysgenesis and Cystic Disease of the Kidney — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

<div class="top" markdown="1">
**If you read nothing else**

- **Dysplasia = histological; hallmark primitive duct**; commonest cause of childhood ESRD (end-stage renal disease).
- **Autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD)**: PKD1 (chromosome 16, 85%, ESRD 50s) vs PKD2 (chromosome 4, 15%, ESRD 70s).
- **Ravine**: <30 y ≥2 cysts; 30–59 y ≥2 each kidney; ≥60 y ≥4 each.
- **ADPKD: RCC (renal cell carcinoma) not increased**; berry aneurysm 10–30%.
- **Autosomal recessive PKD (ARPKD): chromosome 6 (PKHD1); congenital hepatic fibrosis always.**
- **Tuberous sclerosis (TSC) angiomyolipoma (AML) >3 cm, growing → mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) inhibitor (everolimus) first line.**
- **von Hippel–Lindau (VHL): 3p25; RCC about 50%; nephron-sparing surgery = standard (control, not cure).**
- **Multicystic dysplastic kidney (MCDK)**: commonest cystic disease; 40% involute; no hypertension or cancer risk.
- **Bosniak: I–II no surgery; IIF follow (11% progress); III explore; IV nephrectomy.**
- **Acquired renal cystic disease (ARCD)**: >90% after 10 years' dialysis; tumour >3 cm → excise.

## Overview of Genetics

- **Genes**: PAX2 → hypodysplasia; EYA1 → branchio-oto-renal; HNF1B → cysts + diabetes.
## Renal Agenesis and Dysplasia

- **Dysplasia**: primitive ducts, cartilage; primary (genetic) or secondary (obstruction).
- **Ultrasound dysplasia**: small, brighter than liver, lost corticomedullary differentiation.
## Hypoplasia and Hypodysplasia

- **Degree of dysplasia ∝ degree of ureteric ectopia.**
- **Lateral ectopia → reflux; medial ectopia (± ureterocele) → hypodysplasia.**
- **Oligomeganephronia**: fewer, bigger nephrons; bilateral; **proteinuria first**; ESRD second decade.
- **Oligomeganephronia treatment**: protein 1.5 g/kg/day; ACE (angiotensin-converting enzyme) inhibitor even if normotensive.
- **Oligomeganephronia not familial → living related donor acceptable.**
- **Ask-Upmark**: young females, ≥10 y; **severe hypertension**; midzone **slit-scar**; 12–35 g.
- **Ask-Upmark: nephrectomy cures hypertension regardless of renin**; reflux repair does not.
- **Posterior urethral valves**: mild (subcapsular cysts) vs severe (large cysts, cartilage) hypodysplasia.

## Cystic Diseases of the Kidney

- **Multicystic = dysplastic; polycystic = normal development, no dysplasia.**
- **Cyst biology**: proliferation ("second hit"), fluid secretion, matrix change; **primary cilium**.
- **Chromosome 16 = PKD1 + TSC2 → contiguous gene syndrome.**

## Autosomal Recessive (Infantile) Polycystic Kidney Disease

- **1:10,000–50,000** (table 1:5000–40,000); **up to 50% die in first days**.
- **About half of neonatal survivors alive at 10 years.**
- **Ultrasound**: huge, **homogeneous hyperechoic** kidneys; microcysts; pyramids blend.
- **Older survivors**: portal hypertension, varices; liver enzymes normal.
- **Very early bilateral nephrectomy (<3 months) → worst neurological outcome** (ARegPKD registry).
- **No renal neoplasm association.**

## Autosomal Dominant (Adult) Polycystic Kidney Disease

- **1:400–1000**; commonest inheritable; **7–15% of haemodialysis**; 10% sporadic.
- **96% manifest by 90; renal failure rare <40.**
- **Symptoms 30–50 y; pain = commonest adult presentation.**
- **Haematuria 50%; stones 20–30% (uric acid = calcium oxalate).**
- **Anaemia unusual** (erythropoietin raised).
- **Hypertension**: 50% at 20–35 y; >60% before impairment; ~100% at ESRD; renin-mediated.
- **Early ESRD risk**: PKD1, male, haematuria <30 y, hypertension <35 y, sickle trait.
- **Liver cysts: commonest extrarenal; almost all by 50; worse in women.**
- **Seminal vesicle cysts 40%; arachnoid 8%; pancreas 5%.**
- **Subarachnoid haemorrhage (SAH) death about 9%** of those with aneurysm.
- **RCC in ADPKD**: bilateral 12%, multicentric 28%, sarcomatoid 33%.
- **PKD2 <30 y: ultrasound sensitivity only 67%** → genetic testing.
- **No family history**: bilateral cysts + ≥2 of (enlargement, ≥3 liver cysts, aneurysm, other cyst).
- **Acute cyst haemorrhage 50–90 HU (Hounsfield units)** on CT (computed tomography).
- **First-line antihypertensive: ACE inhibitor or ARB (angiotensin receptor blocker)**; target <130/80.
- **Avoid NSAIDs (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs)**; limit opioids.
- **Cyst infection: lipophilic antibiotics** — trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole, fluoroquinolone, chloramphenicol.
- **Unroofing relieves pain only; nephrectomy for symptomatic ESRD.**
- **Screen for aneurysm only if**: family history, prior rupture, major surgery, pilot, anxiety.
- **Aneurysm <7 mm → observe; >7–10 mm or symptomatic → clip or coil.**
- **HALT-PKD**: ACE inhibitor + ARB = ACE inhibitor alone; rigorous BP (blood pressure) slows kidney growth.
- **TEMPO/REPRISE**: tolvaptan (V2 antagonist) slows growth and decline; aquaresis, liver toxicity.

## Juvenile Nephronophthisis and Medullary Cystic Disease Complex

- **Juvenile nephronophthisis (NPH)**: recessive; **10–20% of childhood renal failure**; ESRD mean 13, <25.
- **Medullary cystic kidney disease (MCKD)**: dominant; ESRD third–fourth decade; **hypertension**; type 2 gout.
- **Both: polyuria/polydipsia >80%, vasopressin-resistant salt wasting; corticomedullary cysts.**
- **Senior–Løken**: NPH + retinitis pigmentosa (16%).
- **Treat NPH: replace sodium; avoid salt restriction and diuretics.**

## Multiple Malformation Syndromes With Renal Cysts

- **TSC**: up to 1:6000; TSC1 chromosome 9, TSC2 chromosome 16.
- **TSC triad**: epilepsy 80%, intellectual disability 60%, adenoma sebaceum 75%.
- **Earliest TSC skin sign: ash-leaf macule**; ultraviolet light.
- **TSC diagnosis: 2 major, or 1 major + 2 minor**; renal AML major, renal cysts minor.
- **TSC AML 40–80%**; rare <6 y, common >10 y; never metastasises.
- **TSC RCC about 2%** (table 3%); female, young, bilateral.
- **Suspect RCC in TSC: enlarging fat-free or calcified lesion.**
- **TSC imaging: magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) preferred** (25–30% AML fat-poor); contrast safe if GFR (glomerular filtration rate) ≥60.
- **AML second line**: embolisation + steroids; resection; ablation. **Acute bleed: embolise + steroids.**
- **TSC: avoid nephrectomy.** Image every 1–3 years; yearly if near 3 cm.
- **VHL**: 1:30,000–50,000; >95% penetrance by 65; mean age 35–40.
- **VHL**: renal cysts 76% (bilateral 75%); phaeochromocytoma 10–17%; pancreatic cysts up to 70%.
- **VHL epididymal cystadenoma 10–26%** — benign; observe.
- **Cerebellar haemangioblastoma: most characteristic; over half of VHL deaths.**
- **VHL diagnosis**: family history + 1 lesion; none → ≥2 haemangioblastomas or 1 + another.
- **VHL surgery**: recurrence 75–85%, 10-year disease-specific survival 81–94%.
- **VHL phaeochromocytoma → partial adrenalectomy** (recurrence 15%).
- **VHL Alliance 2020**: eye <1 y; brain, spine MRI + audiogram 2-yearly from 11; abdominal MRI 2-yearly from 15.
- **VHL: screen only genetic carriers.**

## Multicystic Dysplastic Kidney

- **MCDK 1:1000–4000; male 55–70%**; contralateral VUR (vesicoureteric reflux) 18–43%.
- **Contralateral pelviureteric junction (PUJ) obstruction 3–12%.**
- **MCDK vs PUJ obstruction**: MCDK has no central pelvis, no communication, no function.
- **Remove MCDK only if**: mass effect, hypertension, rupture, increasing solid tissue.
## Benign Multilocular Cyst (Cystic Nephroma)

- **Multilocular cyst**: 95% <4 y (boys 2:1) or >30 y (women 8:1).
- **Multilocular cyst → nephrectomy (partial if possible); diagnosis only by excision.**

## Simple Cysts

- **Simple cysts: children 0.22%; 20% by 40; 50% after 60.**
- **Ultrasound**: no echoes, thin smooth wall, acoustic enhancement, spherical.
- **CT**: −10 to +20 HU, no enhancement → about 100% accuracy.
- **Hyperdense cyst benign if**: 20–90 HU, no enhancement, ≤3 cm, ≥¼ exophytic.
- **Calcification: cyst 2% (peripheral) vs RCC 10% (central).**
- **Cyst puncture only if**: infection; ultrasound echoes but classic CT; poor surgical candidate.
- **Child: simple cyst <15 mm → repeat ultrasound at 2 years, then discharge.**
- **Paediatric Bosniak III–IV: >90% intermediate or malignant.**

## Medullary Sponge Kidney

- **Medullary sponge kidney (MSK)**: "brush bristles"; colic 50–60%, UTI (urinary tract infection) 20–33%.
- **MSK: hypercalciuria one-third to one-half; incomplete distal renal tubular acidosis 30–40%.**
- **MSK first line: potassium citrate**; thiazide for stone formers even if normocalciuric.
## Sporadic Glomerulocystic Kidney Disease

- **Sporadic glomerulocystic disease**: no family history, no anomalies.
## Acquired Renal Cystic Disease

- **ARCD: ≥3 cysts per kidney**; 10% pre-dialysis → 44% at 3 y → 60% at 5 y.
- **ARCD: uraemia, not dialysis, is the cause; male 3:1.**
- **ARCD kidneys <300 g vs ADPKD >800 g.**
- **Dialysis renal malignancy 5–50×; post-transplant RCC >15×.**
- **>70% of transplant RCCs arise in native kidneys.**
- **ARCD screening: after 3 years' dialysis**; <55 y, good health.
- **Retroperitoneal bleed, RCC not excluded → nephrectomy.**
- **Gadolinium in ESRD → nephrogenic systemic fibrosis risk.**
## Parapelvic and Renal Sinus Cysts

- **Renal sinus cysts: 15–20% at autopsy; mimic hydronephrosis; conservative.**

<div class="traps" markdown="1">
**Classic traps**

- **Trap**: multicystic = polycystic — **Actually**: multicystic is dysplastic; polycystic is not.
- **Trap**: ADPKD raises RCC incidence — **Actually**: not increased; ARCD does.
- **Trap**: juvenile NPH causes hypertension — **Actually**: only MCKD does.
- **Trap**: salt restriction in NPH — **Actually**: replace sodium; avoid diuretics.
- **Trap**: neonatal echogenic kidneys = ARPKD — **Actually**: ADPKD can mimic; scan parents.
- **Trap**: negative Ravine scan <30 excludes PKD2 — **Actually**: sensitivity only 67%.
- **Trap**: TSC AML >4 cm → embolise — **Actually**: >3 cm growing → mTOR inhibitor.
- **Trap**: VHL surgery aims at cure — **Actually**: control, spare nephrons.
- **Trap**: calcification makes a cyst malignant — **Actually**: enhancement does (IIF calcified).
- **Trap**: Ask-Upmark hypertension cured by reflux repair — **Actually**: needs nephrectomy.
</div>
