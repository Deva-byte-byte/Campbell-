## The numbers to lock in

### Instalment 1
- Prevalence of male UI: 11% at 60–64 years; 31% at >85 years.
- Continence keeps improving for up to 2 years after RP. Nerve sparing gives faster, not better final, continence.
- AUS cuff: circumferential compression about 2 cm long. Standard bulbar PRB 61–70 cm H2O.
- Transobturator sling = repositioning, no compression. Virtue = compression plus elevation. ATOMS and ProACT = adjustable compression through scrotal ports.
- Voiding diary 2–7 days; pad test 24 hours.
- Standing cough test grades 0–4: reported only / delayed drops / early drops / delayed stream / early persistent stream.
- Most important history item: classify the UI type.
- Cystoscopy is mandatory if stricture, VUAS or bladder neck contracture is suspected. A poorly coapting sphincter → AUS.
- UDS is not routine. Indications: mixed UI, SUI not demonstrable, overflow, suspected poor compliance. Hypocontractility → AUS.
- Eponym: Credé manoeuvre — voiding by suprapubic pressure with the cuff open.
- Traps: a transobturator sling does not compress; poor compliance does not predict a worse AUS result, but it threatens the upper tracts.

### Instalment 2
- Gold standard: AUS, for all degrees of SUI. First-line surgery: AUS and perineal sling. Bulking agents only for small-volume leakage in men unfit for more.
- PFMT: no benefit beyond 12 months. Operate on severe or gravitational SUI that is unimproved at 6 months.
- Absolute contraindications (an alternative to SUI surgery is needed): low-compliance bladder threatening the kidneys; low-capacity bladder; tissue unfit for any implant.
- Relative contraindications: frequent future transurethral work (bladder cancer, refractory VUAS, radiation cystitis).
- Radiation cystitis: hyperbaric oxygen and 6 months free of refractory bleeding before AUS.
- AUS: cure about 56%; social continence (0–1 pad/day) 55–77%; satisfaction about 90%; revision 10–29% at 3 years.
- Sling cure about 60%. Advance XP durable. I-STP TOMS deteriorates to about 15% at 5 years. ATOMS about 67% dry. ProACT about 60% dry.
- Severity: mild <150 g, moderate <400 g (24-hour pad weight).
- MASTER RCT: sling non-inferior to AUS overall; AUS favoured when pad weight >250 g or after RT.
- AUA/SUFU: severe UI or any RT → AUS first-line. AUS lifespan 8–10 years. Expect one thin pad a day.
- Erosion: RT 3–33% versus non-RT 1–13%. Median time to erosion about 1 year irradiated versus about 3 years non-irradiated. Prior erosion → 5-year re-erosion about 50%. Age >80 and CAD are associated. Urethroplasty is a greater risk than radiation.
- Transcorporal AUS: George Webster, 2002. High-risk PRB 51–60 cm H2O; cuff 0.5 cm larger than measured.
- Traps: radiation predicts the first explant, not later ones; testosterone supplementation is unproven.

### Instalment 3
- Antibiotics within 60 minutes: aminoglycoside 5 mg/kg plus a first- or second-generation cephalosporin or vancomycin.
- Cuff proximal to corporal fusion, usually 4.0–4.5 cm. Sizes 3.5–11 cm. The cuff is slightly tighter than the tape. Looser cuffs give better continence.
- 3.5-cm cuff plus radiation → erosion; prefer TC and/or a 51–60 PRB.
- PRB fill 23 mL. Contrast is diluted with sterile water; no contrast if iodine allergy. Ectopic or inguinal PRB is contraindicated after mesh hernia repair, cystectomy or extensive abdominal surgery.
- PRB pressures: 51–60 high-risk / 61–70 bulbar / 71–80 bladder neck.
- Tandem cuff: up to 11% fail a single cuff. Second cuff 1.5–2 cm distal; Y-connector; add 3 mL; deactivated 6 weeks; not first-line.
- TC cuff: urethra <3.5 cm; more retention; erections usually preserved; 76–84% cured or improved in early high-risk series.
- Eponyms: Wilson (transscrotal, single incision, inferior continence); Gullwing (complete tunical wrap).
- Bladder neck AUS: cuff ≥8 cm, PRB 71–80; contraindicated after RP; less erosion and atrophy.
- Deactivate for 4–6 weeks after implant.

### Instalment 4
- Transobturator sling: Rehder and Gozzi, 2007. Trocar passed outside-in, one fingerbreadth below adductor longus; two pops. Four sutures. Tension to coaptation; bulb moves 3–4 cm.
- ATOMS: bulbospongiosus not opened; port filled with 9 mL.
- AUS failure: sudden onset → deactivation or leak; gradual → atrophy. Hold the deactivation button ≥30 seconds to reactivate.
- AUS failure algorithm: PRB >20 mL → cuff options; PRB empty → replace the device. Infection or erosion → explant urgently; catheter about 3 weeks after erosion; replace at 3–6 months.
- Sling loosened within 6 weeks → re-explore. Failed sling → AUS is the gold standard. A second Advance succeeds in about 56%.
- Retention: catheter for 24–48 hours → suprapubic catheter after a second failed trial. Retention lasting weeks means the cuff is undersized.
- Infection: 1–3% (10% high-risk). Organisms: *S. epidermidis* and *S. aureus*. Late infection = >4 months. Scrotal pain is the key sign. InhibiZone has not reduced AUS infection.
- Absolute contraindications to salvage: sepsis, ketoacidosis, necrotising infection, erosion, immunosuppression, gross pus.
- Erosion: 2–15% (35% with multiple risk factors); stricture afterwards in 8–35%. <50% urethral loss → catheter. Reimplant at 3–6 months at a new site. Re-erosion up to 35%; nightly deactivation.
- Device life 7–10 years. Failure within 3 years → replace the failed part; >3 years → replace the whole device.

### Instalment 5
- ROBUST III: paclitaxel balloon 83.2% versus 21.7% 1-year freedom from intervention; 67% functional success at 3 years.
- Scope of 22–24 Fr proximal to the cuff → uncouple the cuff. VUAS <12 Fr can be dilated at the same time as AUS placement.
- Neobladder SUI 33–95%. AUS helps about three-quarters, with 60% revision. PRB goes behind rectus.
- Dual AUS + IPP: synchronous = metachronous. Staged → AUS first. Dual implants are revised more than either alone.
- Climacturia: 20–68%. Mini-jupette: 3-cm corporotomies.
- Sling complications: transobturator erosion 0–0.4%; ATOMS explant 10–16%; ProACT perforation about 5% (the commonest), revision 22%.
- More than 150,000 men implanted. About 90% improve in QoL.

### Systematic character substitution (text-extraction artefact, not a printed error)
The key is stated once. In the body font, `!` = `>` (except "!2 cm" = `~2 cm` and "!1 fingerbreadth" = `~1`) and `#` = `<`. In the bold font, `!` = `<` and `"` = `>`. In "usually "8 cm", `"` = `≥`. Every instance was checked against the rendered page. The printed text is correct.

The word "morbidity" and probably a short linking phrase are lost at the page-column break. The meaning is recoverable; nothing substantive appears to be missing.

### Source errors and inconsistencies (corrected value carried silently in the documents)
1. **"USPTF"** should be **USPSTF** (U.S. Preventive Services Task Force).
2. **"Fig. 121.3"** (Incontinence After Previous AUS) should be **Fig. 123.3** — a leftover cross-reference from the previous edition's chapter number.
3. **MASTER trial.** The chapter says MASTER "demonstrated AUS superiority to slings" where pad weight is >250 g or after radiotherapy. MASTER's primary result was that the **male sling was non-inferior to AUS for continence at 12 months**. The >250 g and radiotherapy points are the chapter's clinical emphasis. Both are carried, labelled as such.
4. **Summary vs MASTER.** The Summary says "No sling procedure has been directly compared to AUS in a controlled clinical trial", which contradicts the chapter's own MASTER paragraph. Corrected to "MASTER is the only RCT".
5. **Erosion risk factors.** The High-Risk section says diabetes and smoking are *not* independent risk factors, and lists hypogonadism as established (Key Points). The Urethral Erosion section calls smoking and diabetes "well-established" and hypogonadism "mixed". Both positions are tabulated in the risk-factor comparison table, with the inconsistency named.
6. **AUS lifespan.** The text gives 7–10 years; Fig. 123.5 gives 8–10 years. Both are carried, with the reason (guideline figure versus the authors' estimate).
7. **Deactivation period.** Key Points give 4–6 weeks; the tandem-cuff text gives 6 weeks. These are compatible. Both are carried.
8. **Bulking agents.** The text says bulking agents are "not part of the algorithm", but Fig. 123.5 lists them under "Other procedures (rarely durable)". This is a minor tension, noted in the notes.
9. **Fig. 123.12, "increase PRB pressure".** This conflicts with the Urethral Atrophy text, which says a higher-pressure PRB increases erosion. Both are carried, with the caution stated.
10. **"Crede maneuver"** → Credé manoeuvre (accent and British spelling).

### Judgement calls
- **No numbered tables.** The chapter has no numbered tables. It has 13 figures and 8 KEY POINTS boxes, all covered. The four AUA/SUFU algorithm figures (123.3, 123.5, 123.12, 123.13) were rebuilt as sequences or tables. Anatomical and photographic figures are kept only where the legend adds a fact (123.1B, 123.4, 123.6A, 123.11). Figs 123.2, 123.7–123.10 are referenced inline; their legends duplicate the text.

MASTER and ROBUST III keep their names and numbers as landmark trials.
