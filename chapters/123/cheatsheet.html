Chapter 123 — Sphincteric Incontinence in the Male: AUS and Perineal Slings — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else
  • Artificial urinary sphincter (AUS) = gold standard, all degrees of sphincteric urinary incontinence (SUI)
  • Severe UI or any radiotherapy (RT) → AUS first-line (AUA/SUFU)
  • Sling = mild–moderate: 24-h pad weight mild <150 g, moderate <400 g
  • Pressure-regulating balloon (PRB): 61–70 bulbar; 51–60 high-risk; 71–80 bladder neck (cm H2O)
  • Bulbar cuff proximal to corporal fusion; usually 4.0–4.5 cm
  • Deactivate 4–6 weeks after implant
  • Erosion risk: RT, prior erosion, urethroplasty (urethroplasty ≥ RT)
  • Infection or erosion → explant everything; reimplant 3–6 months
  • Device >3 years old failing → replace whole AUS (PRB)
  • Failed second void trial at 48 h → suprapubic catheter, not urethral

Aetiology

  • Male SUI needs both sphincters impaired; commonest cause: radical prostatectomy (RP)
  • Prevalence: 11% at 60–64; 31% >85
  • Continence improves up to 2 years post-RP
  • Nerve sparing → faster, not better final, continence
  • Climacturia 20–68% post-RP (includes arousal leakage)

Mechanism of Devices

  • AUS: circumferential compression, cuff ~2 cm long; voids by straining or Credé
  • Advance XP (transobturator): repositions and lengthens membranous urethra — no compression; needs coapting sphincter
  • Virtue: quadratic — prepubic compression + transobturator elevation
  • ATOMS: cushion compression; scrotal port adjustment
  • ProACT: two bladder-neck balloons, lateral compression; scrotal ports

Evaluation

  • Core: history, examination, postvoid residual (PVR), urinalysis
  • Most important history point: classify UI (stress, urge, mixed, overflow)
  • Voiding diary 2–7 days; pad test 24 h
  • Cough test grades: 0 reported only · 1 delayed drops · 2 early drops · 3 delayed stream · 4 early persistent stream
  • Cystoscopy mandatory if stricture, vesicourethral anastomotic stenosis (VUAS) or bladder neck contracture suspected
  • Poorly coapting sphincter on cystoscopy → AUS, not sling
  • Urodynamics (UDS) not routine: mixed UI, SUI not shown, overflow, poor compliance
  • Detrusor hypocontractility → AUS
  • Urinalysis ± culture mandatory; eradicate UTI pre-op

Indications for Surgery

  • Operate if: irreversible sphincter incompetence + UI affecting quality of life
  • Severe or gravitational SUI unimproved at 6 months → operate; don't wait
  • Pelvic floor muscle training: no benefit beyond 12 months
  • First-line: AUS and perineal sling; bulking agents only if small-volume and unfit
  • Absolute (need non-SUI alternative): low-compliance bladder threatening kidneys; low-capacity bladder; tissue unfit for any implant
  • Relative: frequent future transurethral work — bladder cancer, refractory VUAS, radiation cystitis
  • Radiation cystitis: hyperbaric O2, 6 months bleed-free before AUS
  • No AUS: poor dexterity, cognitive impairment, poor urethra, severely low compliance
  • AUS: cure ~56%; social continence (0–1 pad) 55–77%; satisfaction ~90%; revision 10–29% at 3 years
  • Transobturator sling cure ~60%; ATOMS ~67% dry; ProACT ~60% dry
  • MASTER RCT: sling non-inferior to AUS overall; AUS favoured >250 g or post-RT
  • Sling failure predictors: RT, severity, previous surgery
  • AUS lifespan 8–10 years (AUA/SUFU); expect one thin pad/day

High-Risk Patient

  • Erosion: RT 3–33% vs non-RT 1–13%; RT erodes sooner (~1 vs ~3 years)
  • Prior erosion → 5-year re-erosion ~50%
  • Age >80, coronary artery disease → erosion; diabetes and smoking not shown independently
  • Low testosterone predicts erosion; replacement unproven
  • Transcorporal (TC) AUS: Webster 2002 — tunica layer over dorsal urethra

Technique

  • Antibiotics within 60 min: aminoglycoside 5 mg/kg + cephalosporin or vancomycin
  • Cuff sizes 3.5–11 cm; large sizes bladder neck
  • Cuff slightly tighter than tape; looser cuffs → better continence
  • 3.5-cm cuff + RT → erosion; prefer TC ± 51–60 PRB
  • PRB fill 23 mL; contrast diluted with sterile water; no contrast if iodine allergy
  • Ectopic or inguinal PRB contraindicated: mesh hernia repair, cystectomy, extensive abdominal surgery
  • Closed cuff → slight blanching on urethroscopy
  • Tandem cuff: ≥1.5–2 cm distal; Y-connector; +3 mL; off 6 weeks; not first-line
  • Single-cuff failure up to 11%
  • TC: urethra <3.5 cm; more retention; erections usually kept
  • Wilson transscrotal: one scrotal incision; inferior continence
  • Bladder neck AUS: cuff ≥8 cm, PRB 71–80; neuropathic (myelomeningocele); contraindicated after RP

Male Slings

  • Transobturator: Rehder and Gozzi 2007; after RP, TURP or laser enucleation
  • Trocar outside-in, 1 fingerbreadth below adductor longus; two pops
  • Tension under cystoscopy to coaptation; bulb moves 3–4 cm
  • ATOMS: bulbospongiosus not opened; port 9 mL

Persistent Incontinence

  • Sudden → deactivation or leak; gradual → atrophy
  • Reactivate: hold button ≥30 s, squeeze pump edges
  • PRB >20 mL on imaging → cuff problem; empty PRB → replace device
  • Sling loosened in first 6 weeks → re-explore, replace
  • Failed sling → AUS = gold standard; 2nd Advance ~56%, only if AUS refused

AUS Complications

  • Retention: confirm deactivated → catheter 24–48 h → suprapubic catheter (image-guided); weeks → cuff undersized
  • Infection 1–3% (10% high-risk); Staphylococcus epidermidis, S. aureus; late >4 months
  • Earliest sign: scrotal pain; explant almost always
  • InhibiZone coating: no proven AUS infection benefit
  • Salvage contraindicated: sepsis, ketoacidosis, necrotising infection, erosion, immunosuppression, pus
  • Erosion 2–15% (35% with multiple risk factors); stricture after 8–35%
  • Erosion <50% urethral loss → catheter; ≥50% unresolved
  • Erosion → urethral catheter ~3 weeks; new cuff away from old site
  • Re-erosion up to 35%; nightly deactivation after reimplant
  • Atrophy: downsize or relocate, TC, tandem
  • Device life 7–10 years; <3 years → replace failed part (pump or cuff)

Special Circumstances

  • ROBUST III: paclitaxel balloon 83.2% vs 21.7% 1-year freedom from reintervention
  • Scope 22–24 Fr proximal to cuff → uncouple cuff
  • VUAS <12 Fr: dilate at the same time as AUS — no penalty
  • Neobladder SUI 33–95%; AUS revision 60%; PRB behind rectus
  • Dual AUS + penile prosthesis: synchronous = metachronous; staged → AUS first
  • Dual implants revised more than either alone
  • Mini-jupette: 3-cm corporotomies + graft at penile prosthesis — climacturia and mild SUI

Sling Complications

  • Transobturator: pain and wound commonest; erosion 0–0.4%; chronic pain 1.3%
  • ATOMS: erosion and infection ~10%; explant 10–16%
  • ProACT: perforation ~5% commonest; migration ~6.5%; revision 22%

Summary

  • ~90% improve in quality of life after surgery, whatever the severity
  • >150,000 men implanted
Classic traps
  • Trap: transobturator sling compresses — Actually: repositions; needs a functioning sphincter
  • Trap: sling after RT or severe UI — Actually: AUS first-line
  • Trap: atrophy → raise PRB pressure — Actually: downsize or relocate cuff; higher PRB erodes
  • Trap: urethral catheter for AUS retention — Actually: suprapubic after second failed trial
  • Trap: bladder neck AUS after RP — Actually: contraindicated
  • Trap: tandem cuff first-line — Actually: distal cuff erodes; wastes revision sites
  • Trap: 3.5-cm cuff for irradiated atrophy — Actually: TC ± 51–60 PRB
  • Trap: ProACT's commonest complication is erosion — Actually: perforation
  • Trap: treat infected AUS with antibiotics — Actually: explant; salvage only if non-eroded
  • Trap: dry after AUS = safe bladder — Actually: poor compliance may silently harm upper tracts
