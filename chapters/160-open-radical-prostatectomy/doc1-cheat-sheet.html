Chapter 160 — Open Radical Prostatectomy — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else

  • Radical prostatectomy (RP): only treatment with randomised controlled trial (RCT) proof ↓ metastases and death (SPCG-4)
  • Goals in order: cancer → complications → continence → potency
  • Defer surgery: 6 wk after biopsy, 12 wk after transurethral resection of the prostate (TURP)
  • Commonest positive margin site: apex → posterior → posterolateral
  • Interfascial dissection = correct plane; intrafascial never
  • Neurovascular bundle (NVB): clips, never thermal energy
  • Induration or fixed bundle → excise NVB
  • Hypotension needing transfusion → explore and evacuate haematoma
  • Deep vein thrombosis (DVT) or pulmonary embolism (PE) = main cause of death; peak 14–28 days
  • Rectal injury → 2-layer repair + omental interposition; colostomy if irradiated

Opening

  • Robotic RP dominant; no proven oncological or functional superiority over open
  • Main robotic advantage: less blood loss only
  • Man 65–70 y: 50% chance of living 15 more years

Surgical Anatomy

  • Deep dorsal vein → 3 branches: superficial + right and left lateral plexuses
  • Superficial branch: outside anterior prostatic fascia, between puboprostatic ligaments
  • Dorsal vein transected at common trunk, directly over urethra
  • Prostatic artery: inferior vesical → urethral (periurethral) + capsular (outer gland) groups
  • Capsular vessels = macroscopic landmark for cavernous nerves (NVB of Walsh)
  • Cavernous artery source: internal pudendal; accessory pudendals from obturator or vesical arteries
  • Pelvic plexus: parasympathetic S2–S4 + sympathetic via hypogastric nerve
  • Plexus position: 5–11 cm from anal verge; midpoint at seminal vesicle tip
  • Mid-pedicle ligation cuts vessels and nerves
  • NVB forms 20–30 mm distal to prostatovesical junction
  • NVB plane: between levator fascia and prostatic fascia
  • Nerves on the capsule enter the prostate, not the corpora
  • Striated sphincter = vertical tube (Oelrich), not horizontal "sandwich"
  • Passive continence = striated sphincter (slow-twitch) vs active = levator ani; both pudendal nerve
  • Denonvilliers: layers inseparable → excise completely
  • Perineal approach reflects fascia → less bleeding vs retropubic: DVC must be ligated

Surgical Technique

Preoperative:

  • Coexisting inguinal hernia ~15%; after open RP up to 20% → examine standing with Valsalva; repair preperitoneally at RP
  • No autologous donation; erythropoietic agents ↑ haematocrit
  • Prophylaxis: 1st- or 2nd-generation cephalosporin, or aminoglycoside + metronidazole or clindamycin
  • Coronary stent: low-dose aspirin usually continued; plan with cardiologist
  • Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) guides NVB and membranous urethra preservation

Gold standard / essentials:

  • Fibreoptic headlight: essential; Balfour retractor required
  • General endotracheal anaesthesia; systolic ≤100 mm Hg; crystalloid ≤1500 mL until prostate out
  • 16-Fr silicone Foley (eases mucosal suturing); right-hander stands on left

Lymphadenectomy:

  • Pelvic lymph node dissection (PLND) for intermediate or high risk; omit for Grade Group 1–2 low-volume favourable
  • Spare tissue over external iliac artery → avoids leg oedema and lymphocele
  • Node of Cloquet: removal unnecessary
  • Extended PLND → better staging ± therapeutic benefit

Apex and dorsal vein complex (DVC):

  • Endopelvic fascia: incise at sidewall reflection; too medial → Santorini bleeding
  • Pudendal perforators: clip, not coagulate
  • Spare pubourethral ligament component → anterior sphincter fixation
  • Accessory pudendal: cadaver ~70% vs angiography ~7%; large ~4%
  • Unilateral accessory artery → do contralateral side first
  • Stapler on DVC: fire only if Foley moves freely; ~200–400 mL less blood loss
  • Suture ligation: 3-0 through DVC + pubic perichondrium (figure-of-eight)
  • Divide sphincter edges obliquely midway apex ↔ pelvic floor (NVB tethered at apex)
  • Circumflex veins at 5 and 7 o'clock → clips

Urethra:

  • Standard release: urethra divided first; high release: urethra divided after
  • 6 × 3-0 Monocryl: mucosa + submucosa only; smooth muscle bites delay continence
  • Posterior sphincter: clamp midway, left then right then centre; never all from one side

NVB:

  • Most men: both NVBs preserved; bilateral excision almost never
  • Extracapsular extension rarely >1–2 mm
  • Margin rate same nerve-sparing vs non-nerve-sparing; Walsh overall ~5%
  • Final NVB decision: intraoperative, never preoperative
  • Release apex → base, from rectal surface; roll prostate, no upward traction
  • Posterior branch over seminal vesicle → ligate → NVB falls away
  • Tsuzuki: ≥2 of prostate-specific antigen (PSA) ≥2 of PSA >10gt;10, Gleason >6, core >20%, cores >33%, perineural invasion, abnormal digital rectal examination → >10% extension risk
  • High anterior release: after DVC ligation, before urethra; unilateral = bilateral → do one side
  • Wide excision: free contralateral NVB first; clamp medial → lateral on rectum

Pedicles, bladder neck, anastomosis:

  • Lateral pedicle: 3 layers (superficial, middle, deep)
  • Sponge stick on catheter, never on prostate
  • Pedicle branches to bladder neck at 5 and 7 o'clock
  • Pelvic plexus lateral to seminal vesicle → hug seminal vesicle
  • Complete seminal vesicle excision essential for cancer control
  • Doubtful posterolateral margin on specimen → excise that NVB
  • Tennis-racquet closure 2-0; mucosal eversion 4-0 → mucosa-to-mucosa
  • Intussusception: 2-0 Maxon ~2 cm from neck; "turtle sign"
  • Babcock on bladder while tying (no catheter traction); anterior suture tied first
  • Drain between, not through, recti

Postoperative:

  • Mean stay ~1.7 days; discharge day 1–2
  • Ketorolac only if creatinine <1.2 mg/dL, blood loss <1500 mL, no uncontrolled diabetes or haematuria
  • Drain out <50 mL/day
  • Catheter out at 1 wk: cystogram, or no imaging if haematocrit fall <6%
  • Delayed anastomotic healing = perianastomotic haematoma
  • Spasm: oxybutynin 5 mg or diazepam 5–10 mg

Complications

  • Mortality 0.2%
  • Commonest intraoperative complication: venous haemorrhage
  • Troublesome DVC bleed → divide completely, oversew
  • Blood loss 300–1000 mL; type and screen for all
  • Obturator nerve cut → reanastomose, fine non-absorbable
  • Rectal injury: apical dissection of small prostates
  • Ureteric injury (trigone dissection) → reimplant
  • Significant bleed = transfusion needed to support blood pressure
  • DVT risk highest with PLND; vigilance 4 wk; compression + early ambulation
  • DVT or PE treatment: twice-daily subcutaneous low-molecular-weight heparin ≥ intravenous unfractionated heparin
  • Bladder neck contracture: 0.5–10%; presents ~3 months
  • Contracture ladder: dilatation → cold-knife 3/6/9 o'clock → triamcinolone 200 mg → artificial urinary sphincter
  • Incontinence = intrinsic sphincter deficiency; main cause DVC injury
  • Stop α-blockers until dry
  • Continence (0–1 pad): 80 / 90 / 93 / 97% at 3 / 6 / 12 / 24 mo
  • Potency (Walsh): 38 / 54 / 73 / 86% at 3 / 6 / 12 / 18 mo
  • Potency predictors: age <65, preoperative potency, no diabetes, bilateral NVB
  • Erectile recovery continues up to 4 y
  • Padma-Nathan: nightly sildenafil 50 mg × 9 mo → better function at 1 y
  • REINVENT: vardenafil on-demand > nightly
  • Other sexual effects: penile shortening, climacturia
  • Moderate-to-severe International Prostate Symptom Score → lower urinary tract symptoms improve after RP for up to 15 y

Classic traps

  • Trap: excise NVB to avoid margins — Actually: apex commonest; rates equal
  • Trap: intrafascial is safer — Actually: high margin risk; never used
  • Trap: nerves on capsule are cavernous — Actually: they enter the prostate
  • Trap: sphincter is a horizontal diaphragm — Actually: vertical tube
  • Trap: catheter out day 4 if no leak — Actually: high retention rate
  • Trap: unstable bleed → observe — Actually: explore early
  • Trap: clamp lateral → medial on rectum — Actually: medial → lateral
  • Trap: nightly phosphodiesterase-5 (PDE5) inhibitor best — Actually: on-demand ≥ nightly
  • Trap: partial DVC division controls bleeding — Actually: divide fully
  • Trap: bilateral high release needed — Actually: unilateral equivalent
