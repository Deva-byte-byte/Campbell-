## Instalment 1 — Chapter Opening, Bilateral Renal Agenesis (Incidence, Embryology, Gross Pathology, Phenotype, Amniotic Fluid and Lung Development, Diagnosis, Postnatal Imaging, Prognosis and the RAFT Trial); Figs 49.1–49.2

Book pages 974–977.

### The numbers to lock in

- **BRA**: about 1 in 4800–5000 births; about 75% male; 40% stillborn; the rest die within 24–48 h.
- **Recurrence**: first-degree relatives of BRA about 1000× population risk (URA about 4.5%, BRA about 3.5% of relatives).
- **Congenital solitary kidney proband families**: offspring 7%, parents 4%, siblings 2.5%. BRA in offspring about 1%.
- **Embryology**: UB from the caudal WD at 5–7 weeks. Gonad absent → insult before week 5. Fetal urine from 10–12 weeks.
- **Lungs**: pseudoglandular phase from week 5; branching 12–26 weeks; **canalicular phase 16–26 weeks (amniotic-fluid-dependent)**; BRA lung defect begins before 16 weeks.
- **Fetal urine** is >90% of amniotic fluid by the third trimester.
- **Neonatal voiding**: all neonates void within 24 h. Anuria >24 h without bladder distension → BRA.
- **Phenotype**: birth weight 1000–2500 g; GI anomalies 60%; undescended testes 43%; testicular agenesis about 10%.
- **Gold standard when ultrasound is inconclusive**: <sup>99m</sup>Tc-DMSA — no uptake in the fossae or ectopically confirms BRA.
- **Pathognomonic**: Potter facies plus oligohydramnios. **Not** pathognomonic: amnion nodosum.
- **Eponyms**:
  - **Potter facies**: infraorbital semicircular fold; ears only *appear* low set;
  - **"Lying down" adrenal sign**: flattened orthotopic adrenal = absent ipsilateral kidney;
  - **Fraser syndrome**: syndactyly, cryptophthalmos, renal and genital anomalies;
  - **Peters two-step model**: early renal factor, then amniotic-fluid-dependent lung growth.
- **Landmark trial — RAFT**: serial amnioinfusion before 26 weeks in isolated BRA; 94% live births; median delivery 32 weeks; 82% met ≥14-day survival with dialysis access; **35% survived to discharge**; stopped early for neonatal morbidity and mortality.
- **When not to intervene**: amnioinfusion for BRA is experimental — only under IRB oversight in centres with neonatal kidney replacement therapy.
- **Classic traps**:
  - facial features are **deformations, not malformations** (monoamniotic twin proof);
  - ears **appear** low set but the canals are normally sited;
  - the adrenal is **present** in BRA (independent embryology);
  - maternal diabetes does **not** raise BRA incidence, although it triples URA and dysplasia risk;
  - bladder hypoplasia is from absent UB/WD, not absent urine (exstrophy vs bilateral ectopic ureter).

### Source flags

- **Duplicate within the paste**: the incidence figure "1 in 5000" recurs in the Key Points and Table 49.1. Summarised once.
- **Study data removed per the style rules**: non-landmark author–year citations and sample sizes (Riddle, Bienstock, Cameron case reports) are reduced to bottom lines. RAFT is kept as a landmark trial.

## Instalment 2 — Unilateral Renal Agenesis (Incidence, Genetic and Syndromic Associations, Embryology and Magee Classification, Genitourinary and Adrenal Anomalies, Male and Female Anomalies, MRKH and MURCS, Other Systems, Diagnosis and VCUG, Sport, Prognosis, Adult Prognosis and SFK Surveillance); Supernumerary Kidney; Key Points: Anomalies of Renal Number; Figs 49.3–49.6

Book pages 977–983.

### The numbers to lock in

- **URA**: 1 in 1100–1200; male:female 1.8:1; left more often; autosomal dominant with 50–90% penetrance.
- **MCDK incidence**: 1 in 4300. **Renal aplasia**: about 1 in 1300, possibly the commonest cause of congenital solitary kidney.
- **Associations**: maternal diabetes 3× risk of agenesis or dysplasia; URA in 30% of VACTERL.
- **Ipsilateral ureter** absent in 60%.
- **Contralateral**: PUJ obstruction 11%; VUJ obstruction 7%; VUR 30% (28% on VCUG series).
- **Adrenal agenesis** <10% (17% on CT).
- **Reproductive anomalies**: 20–40% overall; **females 25–50% vs males 10–15%**.
- **Male**: WD derivatives absent in almost 50%. **79% of men with absent vas have ipsilateral renal agenesis** (left 3.5:1).
- **Female**: one-quarter to one-third of females with URA have a genital anomaly; **43% of females with internal genital anomalies have URA**; infertility up to 33%.
- **MRKH**: 1 in 4500 females; renal anomalies 40–60% in type II (atypical).
- **Magee timing**: type I before week 4; type II early week 4; type III after week 4.
- **Compensatory hypertrophy** from 20 weeks in about 90%.
- **CAKUT**: 1 in 600 births; 20% of chromosomal anomalies. **Nephron number**: 200,000–1.8 million (eightfold).
- **Prognosis**:
  - solitary kidney about 50% dialysis by 30 in one single-centre cohort;
  - **KIMONO**: about one-third with renal injury by about 10 years; GFR falling from about 9 years, microalbuminuria about 16 years;
  - Israeli national cohort: about 4× ESRD risk.
- **Surveillance**:
  - BP and microalbumin annually (no CAKUT) or twice yearly (CAKUT);
  - **microalbuminuria >30 mg/24 h**;
  - GFR <60 → 2–4× per year;
  - GFR >60 and no medication → creatinine every 5 years.
- **Supernumerary kidney**: 60% caudal; common-stem ureter 50% vs separate ureters 50%; Weigert–Meyer rule broken in 10%; 25% found at autopsy.
- **First-line**:
  - DMSA to confirm URA and find an ectopic kidney;
  - **ACE inhibitor** for progressive SFK injury.
- **Eponyms**:
  - **Zinner syndrome**: seminal vesicle cyst plus ipsilateral agenesis;
  - **Herlyn–Werner–Wunderlich / OHVIRA**: obstructed hemivagina plus ipsilateral renal anomaly;
  - **MRKH**: müllerian agenesis in a 46,XX female (type II has renal anomalies);
  - **MURCS**: müllerian aplasia, renal aplasia, cervicothoracic somite dysplasia (C5–T1);
  - **Magee classification**: timing of the WD/UB insult;
  - **Brenner hyperfiltration hypothesis**;
  - **Weigert–Meyer rule**.
- **Key studies**: KIMONO (named study) — about one-third renal injury by about 10 years.
- **Screening triggers**:
  - renal ultrasound for ear anomaly **plus** another malformation, or ≥2 congenital anomalies;
  - whole-body imaging including spine for VACTERL;
  - pelvic ultrasound after thelarche but before menarche in girls with URA.
- **Classic traps**:
  - isolated preauricular pits or tags and isolated single umbilical artery do **not** mandate a renal scan;
  - "hemitrigone" is a misnomer (the trigone is urogenital sinus-derived);
  - URA is commoner in males, yet genital anomalies are commoner in **females**;
  - a congenital SFK is **not** equivalent to a donor nephrectomy;
  - sport is **not** contraindicated (motor vehicle accidents cause more renal trauma);
  - an involuted MCDK masquerades as URA — clues are curvilinear calcification and a normally sited colonic flexure;
  - a supernumerary kidney needs its own blood supply and capsule, unlike a duplex kidney.

## Instalment 3 — Anomalies of Renal Ascent (Simple Renal Ectopia: Incidence, Description, Diagnosis, Prognosis; Cephalad Renal Ectopia; Thoracic Kidney); Key Points: Ascent; Crossed Renal Ectopia With and Without Fusion (Classification, Six Fusion Forms, Incidence, Embryology, Description); Figs 49.7–49.14

Book pages 983–989.

### The numbers to lock in

- **Ascent**: UB at the end of week 4; metanephric cap by week 5; **ascent complete 6–9 weeks**.
- **Simple ectopia**: autopsy 1 in 900 (range 1 in 500 to 1 in 1200); solitary ectopic kidney 1 in 22,000; bilateral 10%; left > right; no sex difference.
- **Commonest site**: pelvic (opposite the sacrum, below the aortic bifurcation).
- **Hydronephrosis in ectopia**: 50% obstruction (PUJ 70%, VUJ 30%); 25% VUR ≥ grade III; 25% malrotation alone.
- **VUR** in 30–50%; when reflux accompanies unilateral ectopia it usually goes into the **orthotopic** kidney.
- **Genital anomalies**: 15–45% overall (Key Points: 15%); females 20–66%; males about 5% each for hypospadias and undescended testis.
- **Cloacal malformation**: 14% have an ectopic kidney.
- **Cephalad ectopia**: kidney near T10; classic with **omphalocele**.
- **Thoracic kidney**: <5% of ectopia; 1 in 13,000 autopsies; left 1.5:1; male 2:1; **foramen of Bochdalek**, not in the pleural space; 0.25% of diaphragmatic hernias.
- **Crossed ectopia**: 90% fused; second commonest fusion anomaly.
  - crossed fused about 1 in 1000 live births (clinical) vs 1 in 2000 (autopsy);
  - left-to-right 3:1; non-fused about 10%; bilateral crossed is the rarest.
  - **Commonest form: unilateral fused with inferior ectopia (two-thirds)**; second: sigmoid; least common: superior ectopia.
- **Solitary crossed ectopia**: kidney at L1–L3; ureter crosses above S2.
- **Imaging**:
  - DMSA and/or MRU for a small ectopic kidney misread as URA;
  - CT or MRU for thoracic kidney;
  - MRU and MRA (no radiation) before surgery in children.
- **When to operate on a thoracic kidney**: only for respiratory distress. Incidental cases are observed.
- **Eponyms and classifications**:
  - **McDonald and McClellan (1957)**: four types of crossed ectopia;
  - **foramen of Bochdalek**: thoracic kidney.
- **Classic traps**:
  - **the aberrant vasculature is a result, not the cause, of ectopia**;
  - ectopic kidney is same-side, crossed is opposite-side;
  - the adrenal is normally placed in ectopia;
  - global function is normal but the **differential function** of the ectopic kidney is reduced;
  - **continuous incontinence in a girl with an apparent solitary kidney → ectopic kidney with ectopic ureter**;
  - a solitary pelvic kidney mistaken for a pelvic tumour;
  - anterior pelvis = early fusion vs medial pelvis = late fusion.

## Instalment 4 — Crossed Ectopia (Associated Anomalies, Symptoms, Diagnosis, Prognosis) and its Key Points; Horseshoe Kidney (Incidence, Description, Associated Anomalies, Diagnosis, Symptoms, Prognosis including Wilms Tumour and Transplantation); Table 49.1; Key Points: Horseshoe; Figs 49.15–49.19

Book pages 989–994.

### The numbers to lock in

- **Crossed ectopia**:
  - ectopic orifice from the crossed unit about 3%;
  - **VUR 20% (71% in bilateral crossed)**;
  - imperforate anus 20% in **solitary** crossed ectopia (4% otherwise);
  - symptoms in the third–fourth decade;
  - asymptomatic mass in one-third.
- **Horseshoe kidney**:
  - **0.25% (1 in 400)**, imaging 1 in 474–666; male >2:1;
  - lower-pole fusion 95%; isthmus at L3/L4 **below the IMA**, anterior to the aorta and IVC;
  - fusion midline about 40%, lateral 60% (left > right).
- **Blood supply**: one artery per moiety is the exception.
- **Associations**:
  - **Turner syndrome: 60% have horseshoe kidney** (commonest syndrome), then caudal regression;
  - neural tube defects 3%;
  - extrarenal anomalies in about half;
  - hypospadias or undescended testis 4%; bicornuate uterus or septate vagina 7%.
- **Complications**:
  - VUR 8–32%; **PUJ obstruction 13–34%** (Key Points: up to one-third);
  - UTI 30%; calculi 20–80% (adults about 36% by meta-analysis);
  - asymmetric moiety function in about two-thirds;
  - at least 50% asymptomatic.
- **Wilms tumour in horseshoe kidney**: 1.76–7.93× expected; 0.48% of NWTS Wilms tumours; 37% initially inoperable; about 75% parenchyma preserved after preoperative chemotherapy; favourable histology.
- **Tumour behaviour**: RCC incidence **not** increased; renal pelvic tumours increased.
- **Transplantation**: about 80% five-year graft survival, en bloc or split.
- **Commonest symptom**: vague abdominal pain radiating to the lower lumbar region.
- **Management**: VUR in horseshoe kidney is managed as in normal kidneys.
- **Classic traps**:
  - **calyces point posteriorly** (failed rotation);
  - the **IMA** blocks ascent;
  - PUJ obstruction has three causes (high insertion, anterior course over the isthmus, crossing vessels);
  - most hydronephrosis in horseshoe kidney is non-obstructed on renography;
  - stone metabolic defects are the same as in other stone formers;
  - survival from a tumour depends on stage and histology, **not** the anomaly;
  - pregnancy is unaffected.

## Instalment 5 — Anomalies of Renal Rotation (Incidence, Weyrauch Positions, Symptoms, Diagnosis, Prognosis) and Key Points; Anomalies of Renal Vasculature (Terminology, Segments, Incidence, Embryology, Symptoms, Diagnosis, Prognosis and Crossing Vessels); Renal Artery Aneurysm; Renal Arteriovenous Fistula; Key Points: Vasculature; Anomalies of the Collecting System (Bifid Pelvis, Calyceal Diverticulum, Hydrocalycosis, Megacalycosis, Infundibulopelvic Stenosis) and Key Points; Figs 49.20–49.26

Book pages 994–1000.

### The numbers to lock in

- **Rotation**:
  - normal 90° ventromedial, weeks 6–9;
  - malrotation 1 in 939 autopsies; male 2:1;
  - Turner association;
  - **ventral = commonest**, **dorsal (180°) = rarest**.
- **Vessels**:
  - single renal artery in 71–85%;
  - five segments (apical, upper, middle, lower, posterior);
  - apical artery the most variable;
  - inferior renal vein tributary crosses the anterior–inferior pelvis in 40%;
  - crossing vessel at a normal PUJ about 19–25%;
  - hypertension **not** increased with multiple arteries.
- **RAA**:
  - about 0.09–0.1%; saccular 93%; 90% <2 cm; bilateral up to 15%; 75% asymptomatic;
  - hypertension 10–55%; wreath-like calcification 30%;
  - rupture 0.3% overall, **18% if >3 cm**.
- **Operate on RAA if**: rupture; rapid expansion; **>2 cm**; pregnancy or planned pregnancy; symptoms (uncontrolled hypertension, pain, haematuria, embolic infarction).
- **Congenital AVF**:
  - <25% of AVFs; female 3:1; third–fourth decade;
  - upper pole 45%;
  - bruit 75%; hypertension 50%; high-output failure 50%; haematuria >75%; mass 10%.
- **Gold standard for AVF**: selective arteriography or DSA — cirsoid pattern, prompt venous filling.
- **Collecting system**:
  - bifid pelvis 10% (a normal variant);
  - **calyceal diverticulum 4.5 per 1000**, stones in almost 40%;
  - megacalycosis male 6:1, 12–20 calyces, White patients only;
  - infundibulopelvic stenosis usually bilateral, with over a third reaching renal insufficiency or ESRD (all bilateral).
- **Gold standard for calyceal diverticulum**: CT or MRU (delayed contrast pooling).
- **Operate on calyceal diverticulum if**: enlargement with pain or infection, abscess, urosepsis, symptomatic stone. Options: ureteroscopic, percutaneous or laparoscopic.
- **Do not operate**:
  - asymptomatic diverticulum;
  - megacalycosis (non-progressive);
  - infundibulopelvic stenosis unless obstruction harms function.
- **Eponyms**:
  - **Weyrauch classification**: malrotation by pelvic position;
  - **Abeshouse classification**: RAA types;
  - milk-of-calcium layering sign.
- **Classic traps**:
  - **vascular course is the only clue to the direction of rotation**;
  - "multiple" vs "aberrant" vs "accessory" vessels;
  - a crossing vessel alone ≠ PUJ obstruction;
  - **fusiform RAA at the bifurcation = congenital**;
  - acquired AVF may close spontaneously, congenital rarely does;
  - megacalycosis mimics PUJ obstruction but has a normal renogram and an undilated pelvis;
  - bilateral malrotation mimics horseshoe kidney — look for the isthmus.
