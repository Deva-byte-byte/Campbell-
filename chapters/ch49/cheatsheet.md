# Chapter 49 — Anomalies of the Kidney and Collecting System — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

<div class="top" markdown="1">
**If you read nothing else**

- **Incidence ladder**: horseshoe 1:400 > ectopia 1:900 > URA 1:1200 > crossed 1:2000 > BRA 1:5000.
- **Bilateral renal agenesis (BRA)** fatal from **pulmonary hypoplasia**; lung window 16–26 weeks.
- **Unilateral renal agenesis (URA)**: genital anomaly **females 25–50% vs males 10–15%**.
- **Absent vas → ipsilateral renal agenesis in 79%**.
- **Solitary kidney: annual blood pressure (BP) + microalbumin; >30 mg/24 h = injury**.
- **Crossed ectopia 90% fused**; inferior ectopia commonest (two-thirds).
- **Horseshoe: isthmus below inferior mesenteric artery (IMA); calyces point posteriorly**; Turner 60%.
- **Horseshoe pelviureteric junction (PUJ) obstruction 13–34%**; Wilms 1.76–7.93× expected.
- **Renal artery aneurysm (RAA): treat if >2 cm, expanding, pregnancy, rupture, symptoms**.
- **Calyceal diverticulum: computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance urography (MRU)**; treat only if symptomatic or stone.

## Anomalies of Renal Number

- **BRA**: 1 in 4800–5000; **75% male**; 40% stillborn.
- **First-degree relatives**: renal anomaly risk **1000×** population → **screen parents, siblings by ultrasound**.
- **Anuria >24 h, no bladder distension** → suspect BRA; all neonates void <24 h.
- **Gold standard if ultrasound equivocal**: **99mTc-DMSA (dimercaptosuccinic acid)** — no uptake anywhere = BRA.
- **Pathognomonic**: Potter facies + oligohydramnios; amnion nodosum **not** pathognomonic.
- **Potter facies**: infraorbital fold, blunt nose; ears only **appear** low set.
- **"Lying down" adrenal**: flattened orthotopic adrenal = absent kidney.
- **Fetal urine >90%** of third-trimester amniotic fluid.
- **Undescended testes 43%** in BRA; gastrointestinal anomalies 60%.
- **RAFT trial (serial amnioinfusion <26 weeks)**: 94% live births; **35% survived to discharge**; stopped early.
- **Amnioinfusion for BRA = experimental**; institutional review board oversight only.
- **URA**: **1 in 1200**; male 1.8:1; left; autosomal dominant, 50–90% penetrance.
- **Maternal diabetes → 3× agenesis/dysplasia**; **VACTERL (vertebral, anal, cardiac, tracheo-oesophageal, renal, limb) → URA 30%**.
- **Ipsilateral ureter absent 60%**; adrenal agenesis <10%.
- **Contralateral**: vesicoureteric reflux (VUR) 30%; PUJ obstruction 11%; vesicoureteric junction (VUJ) obstruction 7%.
- **Commonest associated urological problem with URA: VUR.**
- **Confirm URA: DMSA** — also finds an ectopic or crossed kidney.
- **Involuted multicystic dysplastic kidney (MCDK) mimics URA**: curvilinear calcification; colonic flexure normally sited.
- **Screening VCUG (voiding cystourethrogram) in URA**: benefit unproven → shared decision.
- **Reproductive anomalies overall 20–40%**; male wolffian duct derivatives absent almost 50%.
- **43% of females with internal genital anomaly have URA.**
- **Infertility up to one-third** with agenesis + unicornuate uterus.
- **Zinner**: seminal vesicle cyst + ipsilateral agenesis.
- **OHVIRA / Herlyn–Werner–Wunderlich**: obstructed hemivagina + ipsilateral renal anomaly.
- **MRKH (Mayer–Rokitansky–Küster–Hauser)**: 1 in 4500 females; **type II (atypical, commoner) → renal anomaly 40–60%**.
- **MURCS**: müllerian aplasia, renal aplasia, cervicothoracic (C5–T1) somite dysplasia.
- **Magee timing**: type I <4 weeks (unicornuate); II early week 4 (didelphys, obstructed); III >4 weeks (isolated).
- **Renal ultrasound if**: ear anomaly **plus** other malformation, or ≥2 anomalies.
- **VACTERL → image all systems including spine (mandatory).**
- **Pelvic ultrasound after thelarche, before menarche** in girls with URA.
- **Renal aplasia (1:1300)** possibly commonest cause of congenital solitary kidney; MCDK 1:4300.
- **Compensatory hypertrophy from 20 weeks** in about 90%.
- **CAKUT (congenital anomalies of kidney and urinary tract)**: 1 in 600.
- **Nephron number range 200,000–1.8 million** (eightfold).
- **KIMONO study**: about one-third renal injury by about 10 years.
- **Solitary kidney**: about 50% dialysis by 30 (one cohort); population ESRD (end-stage renal disease) risk 4×.
- **Solitary kidney + CAKUT → BP, albumin twice yearly.**
- **GFR (glomerular filtration rate) <60 → BP, albumin, creatinine 2–4×/year.**
- **GFR >60, no medication → creatinine every 5 years.**
- **First-line for progressive solitary-kidney injury: ACE (angiotensin-converting enzyme) inhibitor**; limit salt, excess protein.
- **Supernumerary kidney**: own capsule and blood supply; **60% caudal**; 50% common-stem ureter.
- **Supernumerary**: Weigert–Meyer rule broken in 10%; 25% found only at autopsy.

## Anomalies of Renal Ascent

- **Ascent complete 6–9 weeks.**
- **Ectopia 1 in 900 (autopsy)**; bilateral 10%; solitary ectopic 1 in 22,000.
- **Commonest site: pelvic** (below aortic bifurcation).
- **Hydronephrosis in ectopia**: 50% obstruction (PUJ 70%, VUJ 30%), 25% VUR, 25% malrotation.
- **VUR 30–50%**; with unilateral ectopia reflux usually into **orthotopic** kidney.
- **Genital anomalies about 15%** (series 15–45%); females 20–66%.
- **Ectopic kidney: normal global, reduced differential function on DMSA.**
- **Girl, continuous incontinence, "solitary kidney"** → ectopic kidney with ectopic ureter.
- **Cephalad ectopia**: near T10; **omphalocele**.
- **Thoracic kidney**: 1 in 13,000; **foramen of Bochdalek**, not intrapleural; left, male 2:1.
- **Thoracic kidney imaging: CT or MRU.**
- **Thoracic kidney in only 0.25% of diaphragmatic hernias.**
- **Operate on thoracic kidney only for respiratory distress.**

## Anomalies of Renal Form and Fusion

- **Crossed ectopia**: second commonest fusion anomaly; fused 1:1000 live births, 1:2000 autopsy.
- **Left-to-right crossover 3:1**; non-fused about 10%; bilateral crossed rarest.
- **Six fused forms**: inferior (commonest) > sigmoid (second) … superior ectopia (least).
- **Anterior pelvis = early fusion; medial pelvis = late fusion.**
- **VUR 20% crossed; 71% bilateral crossed.** Ectopic orifice about 3%.
- **Solitary crossed ectopia → imperforate anus 20%**; kidney L1–L3, ureter crosses above S2.
- **Stone in crossed ureter → pain on anephric side.**
- **Before paediatric surgery: MRU + MRA (magnetic resonance angiography)**, no radiation.
- **Horseshoe**: 0.25%; male >2:1; lower-pole fusion 95%; isthmus L3/L4.
- **Isthmus usually anterior to aorta and IVC (inferior vena cava).**
- **Horseshoe blood supply**: single artery per moiety is the exception → image before surgery.
- **Horseshoe associations**: Turner 60% (commonest syndrome); neural tube defect 3%; VUR 8–32%.
- **Horseshoe**: urinary tract infection 30%; stones 20–80%; ≥50% asymptomatic.
- **Horseshoe PUJ obstruction causes**: high insertion, course over isthmus, crossing vessels.
- **Horseshoe tumours**: renal cell carcinoma (RCC) not increased; renal pelvic tumours increased.
- **Horseshoe Wilms**: 37% initially inoperable → preoperative chemotherapy; favourable histology.
- **Horseshoe transplant**: about 80% five-year graft survival.
- **Horseshoe stones**: calcium; metabolic defects as in other stone formers.
- **Horseshoe moieties: asymmetric DMSA function in about two-thirds.**

## Anomalies of Renal Rotation

- **Normal: 90° ventromedial, weeks 6–9.** Malrotation 1:939; male 2:1; **Turner**.
- **Weyrauch**: ventral = **commonest**; dorsal (180°) = **rarest**.
- **Only vessel course reveals direction of rotation.**

## Anomalies of Renal Vasculature

- **Single renal artery 71–85%**; five segments; apical artery most variable.
- **Multiple** = >1 from aorta; **aberrant** = non-aortic origin; **accessory** = same segment.
- **Crossing vessel at a normal PUJ about 19–25%.**
- **RAA**: 0.1%; saccular 93%; 90% <2 cm; 75% asymptomatic; wreath calcification 30%.
- **RAA rupture 0.3% overall; 18% if >3 cm.**
- **Fusiform RAA at bifurcation = congenital.**
- **RAA hypertension 10–55%, renin-mediated.**
- **Multiple renal arteries do not raise hypertension risk.**
- **Congenital arteriovenous fistula (AVF)**: female 3:1; upper pole 45%; bruit 75%; haematuria >75%.
- **Congenital AVF: hypertension 50%, high-output failure 50%.**
- **AVF gold standard: selective arteriography or DSA (digital subtraction angiography)** — cirsoid, prompt venous filling.
- **Acquired AVF (biopsy) may close spontaneously; congenital rarely → embolise or resect.**

## Anomalies of the Renal Collecting System

- **Bifid pelvis 10%** — normal variant.
- **Calyceal diverticulum**: 4.5/1000; stones almost 40%; milk of calcium layers.
- **Operate on diverticulum if**: enlarging with pain or infection, abscess, urosepsis, symptomatic stone.
- **Diverticulum options**: ureteroscopic, percutaneous (fulgurate lining) or laparoscopic marsupialisation.
- **Megacalycosis**: male 6:1; 12–20 calyces; pelvis undilated; **normal renogram → no surgery**.
- **Megacalycosis: bilateral in males; segmental unilateral in females.**
- **Hydrocalycosis**: dilated major calyx connected to pelvis; infundibular obstruction.
- **Infundibulopelvic stenosis**: bilateral; monitor yearly creatinine and GFR.
- **Bilateral anomalies → ESRD in over a third**; ACE inhibitor, protein restriction.
- **Infundibulopelvic stenosis surgery only if obstruction impairs function.**

<div class="traps" markdown="1">
**Classic traps**

- **Trap**: Potter features are malformations — **Actually**: deformations from absent amniotic fluid.
- **Trap**: adrenal absent in agenesis — **Actually**: present, flattened ("lying down").
- **Trap**: isolated preauricular pit needs renal scan — **Actually**: only with another anomaly.
- **Trap**: URA genital anomalies commoner in males — **Actually**: commoner in females.
- **Trap**: aberrant vessels cause ectopia — **Actually**: they result from ectopia.
- **Trap**: solitary kidney bars sport — **Actually**: motor vehicle accidents injure more kidneys.
- **Trap**: crossing vessel proves PUJ obstruction — **Actually**: present at 19–25% of normal PUJs.
- **Trap**: horseshoe calyces point medially — **Actually**: posteriorly (failed rotation).
- **Trap**: megacalycosis needs pyeloplasty — **Actually**: non-obstructive, normal renogram.
- **Trap**: horseshoe raises RCC risk — **Actually**: raises Wilms and renal pelvic tumours only.
</div>
