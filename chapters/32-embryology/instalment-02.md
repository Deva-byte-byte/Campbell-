### DEVELOPMENT OF THE FEMALE EXTERNAL GENITALIA

- **The clitoris is built like the penis, minus the urethra.** It forms in the same way as the penis, **except that no urethra is incorporated into it**.
- **Vestibular plate.** This is the female homologue of the urethral plate. It **canalises (opening zipper)** into a wide, diamond-shaped **vestibular groove**.
- **Canalisation is androgen-independent.** It happens in both sexes, so it cannot need androgen.
- **Fusion is the androgen-dependent step.** The **vestibular folds** (homologues of the urethral folds) **do not fuse**, so the vestibular groove stays open.
- **Glans clitoris.** It caps the narrowed paired corporal (clitoral) bodies.
  - Where the corporal bodies fuse, they form a **midline septum**. It starts ventrally and runs about **halfway into the glans**.
- **Innervation** mirrors the penis:
  - large nerve bundles are **densest dorsally**;
  - there is a **nerve-free zone at 12 o'clock**;
  - nerves extend ventrolaterally around the tunica;
  - the glans is supplied by **perforating branches of the dorsal nerves** at the dorsal corporoglandular junction;
  - nerve density in the glans is **lowest ventrally, next to the glans septum**.

**Male vs female external genitalia — the governing contrast**

| Step | Male | Female |
|---|---|---|
| Plate | Urethral plate | Vestibular plate |
| Opening zipper (canalisation) | Yes | Yes — **androgen-independent** |
| Closing zipper (fold fusion) | **Yes → tubular penile urethra and raphe** | **No → open vestibular groove** |
| Urethra within the organ | Penile urethra | None |
| Nerve-free zone | 12 o'clock | 12 o'clock |
| Orientation at 16 weeks | Penis at about 90° to the body | Clitoris recessed to the body wall |

### Clinical Correlation: Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia

- **Congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH)** is **autosomal recessive** and **impairs cortisol synthesis**. It causes varying **virilisation of 46,XX fetuses** → genital atypia at birth.
- **Differential diagnosis:** it can be mistaken for **hypospadias with bilateral non-palpable testes**.
- **Mechanism:** the vestibular groove **closes to a varying degree**, forming a **clitoral-shaft urethra anatomically indistinguishable from a male urethra**.
- **Most severe form:** a penis-like clitoris with a **terminal meatus, glans and foreskin**.
- **The only distinguishing features:** **no palpable gonads**, and **excess pigmentation**.

**Concept —** *External genital sex comes down to **one androgen-dependent step, fusion of the folds**. Canalisation is common to both sexes. So androgen excess in a female (CAH) closes the vestibular groove into a male-type urethra, and androgen deficiency in a male leaves it open.*

### KEY POINTS: EXTERNAL GENITALIA

- **Androgens start differentiation.** The ambisexual genital tubercle begins to become a penis at **8 weeks**.
- **The penile urethra forms in two steps:** **opening zipper** (canalisation of the plate) → **closing zipper** (fusion of the folds).
- **Origin of the urethra.** The epithelium of the **entire male urethra comes from the urogenital sinus**.
- **The clitoris is analogous to the penis** but has **no urethra**.
- **The hypospadiac penis is otherwise normal.** Only the urethra, spongiosum, overlying skin and ventral prepuce are abnormal.
- **12 o'clock nerve-free zone.** It is present on both the penile and clitoral tunica, and is the site for **dorsal plication** in mild-to-moderate curvature.

### DEVELOPMENT OF THE HUMAN PROSTATE

#### Onset, evidence base and stages

- **First sign.** At **10 weeks**, prostatic buds emerge from the urogenital sinus epithelium (UGE) **caudal to the bladder base**.
- **Evidence base.** Human fetal data are **sparse**. Mechanism and endocrinology come mainly from **animal studies**:
  - **Jost** and **Price** established androgen dependence;
  - the finding was later confirmed in humans.
- **Six stages, the same in human and mouse:**
  1. prebud UGS;
  2. **solid epithelial buds** emerge from the UGE;
  3. bud elongation and branching;
  4. **canalisation** of the solid ducts;
  5. differentiation into **luminal and basal cells**;
  6. **secretory cytodifferentiation**.
- **Endodermal origin.** The prostate develops **below the bladder** from **FOXA1-positive UGE**. FOXA1 marks the endodermal lineage.

#### Verumontanum — a uniquely human feature

- **What it is:** a hillock on the **dorsal wall of the UGS**, elongated from cranial to caudal.
- **What opens onto its apex:**
  - the **Wolffian (mesonephric) ducts**;
  - the **fused Müllerian ducts (prostatic utricle)**.
- **Why it matters:** it is the **interface between mesodermal epithelium** (Wolffian ducts and utricle) **and endodermal UGE**.

#### Budding and branching

- **Timing.** Buds appear at **10 weeks**. Reports range from **9.5 to 10 weeks**, because fetal age is hard to estimate.
- **Site.** Most buds emerge in the **gutters lateral to the verumontanum**. This is best seen in the adult prostate, where the ducts open lateral to the verumontanum.
- **Budding is stochastic** (mouse data, apparently true in humans too). Cords of UGE cells grow in and then regress.
  - Evidence: **ventral buds seen in the fetus are not represented in the adult gland**.
- **Branching.** From **about 11 weeks**, growth and branching follow a **fixed spatial pattern** that **sets up the adult zonal anatomy**.
- **Canalisation.** Ducts canalise **proximal (urethral end) → distal (ductal tips)**, and canalisation goes hand in hand with **luminal and basal cell differentiation**.

**TABLE 32.1 Marker Expression Profiles in Urogenital Sinus Epithelium (UGE), Solid Ducts, and Adult Human Prostate** *(modified from Wang et al., 2001)*

| Marker group | Prebud UGE | Solid ducts | Adult luminal cells | Adult basal cells |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Basal-type markers:** TP63, KRT5, KRT14, KRT19, GSTpi | + | + | − | **+** |
| **Luminal-type markers:** KRT8, KRT18 | + | + | **+** | − |

- **What the table shows:** the precursor epithelium, both prebud UGE and solid ducts, **co-expresses basal and luminal markers**.
- **The rule it demonstrates:** as cells differentiate, **future luminal cells keep luminal markers and lose basal ones, and future basal cells do the reverse**.
- **The trap:**
  - **TP63** and **KRT5/14** are **basal**;
  - **KRT8/18** are **luminal**;
  - **KRT19** and **GSTpi** go with the **basal** set in the adult, even though both are present in every precursor.

#### Stromal differentiation

- **What the stroma becomes.** Urogenital sinus mesenchyme (UGM) differentiates into **smooth muscle**, the main component of adult prostatic stroma.
- **Direction.** Smooth muscle appears **first ventrally**, then spreads **laterally and dorsally**.
- **At 19 weeks:** ductal branching is **most intense near the capsule**, where smooth muscle is most abundant.

#### Endocrinology

- **Androgens are essential.** Androgens from the **fetal testis** act through the **nuclear androgen receptor (AR)**, which is activated by testosterone or DHT.
- **Evidence for androgen dependence:**
  - there is **no prostate when the AR is non-functional**, in mice and in humans;
  - the **female UGS forms a prostate when exposed to androgen**.
- **DHT is critical**, even though testosterone can bind the AR directly:
  - DHT is made **within the UGS by type 2 5α-reductase (5αR2)**;
  - it has **about tenfold greater affinity for the AR** than testosterone.
- **Evidence for 5αR2:**
  - **5αR2 deficiency → a rudimentary prostate** (and abnormal male external genitalia);
  - mice lacking both 5αR1 and 5αR2 have smaller prostates and seminal vesicles.
- **Site of 5αR2:** expressed mainly in the **prostatic mesenchyme**.
- **5αR1's role is unclear.** The **5αR1 gene is normal in 5αR2-deficient patients**.

**Testosterone vs DHT in prostatic development**

| | Testosterone | DHT |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Leydig cells of the fetal testis | Local conversion **in the UGS mesenchyme by 5αR2** |
| AR affinity | Lower | **About tenfold higher** |
| Loss-of-function phenotype | — | 5αR2 deficiency → **rudimentary prostate, severe hypospadias** |

#### Mesenchymal–epithelial interactions

- **Androgens act through the mesenchyme.** Androgen triggers the whole process through the AR, but **UGM induces and specifies prostatic identity in the epithelium**.
- **Instructive induction.** UGM can induce prostate even in **embryonic or adult bladder epithelium** (rodent and human).
- **The interaction runs both ways.** **Prostatic epithelium is needed for UGM to become smooth muscle.**

#### Oestrogen effects

- **Oestrogens disrupt prostatic development.**
- **Acute effect of exogenous oestrogen:** **squamous metaplasia**, for example with **diethylstilbestrol (DES)**.
- **Receptor involved: ESR1 (oestrogen receptor α).**
  - Metaplasia occurs in wild-type and **ESR2 (oestrogen receptor β)-knockout** mice;
  - it does **not** occur in ESR1-null mice.

**TABLE 32.2 Squamous Metaplasia in Tissue Recombinants Composed of Wild-Type (Wt) and ESR1-Null Prostatic Epithelium and Mesenchyme**

| Mesenchyme | Epithelium | Squamous metaplasia |
|---|---|---|
| **Wild-type** | **Wild-type** | **+** |
| Wild-type | ESR1-null | − |
| ESR1-null | Wild-type | − |
| ESR1-null | ESR1-null | − |

- **What the table is for.** It shows which compartment needs the receptor. **Metaplasia occurs only when ESR1 is present in both the stroma and the epithelium.**
- **The trap:** assuming that epithelial ESR1 alone is enough. **Removing ESR1 from either compartment abolishes the response.**
- **Clinical corollary.** **Fetal prostatic squamous metaplasia is normal** in the third trimester, because of **high maternal and placental oestrogen**.

**Concept —** *In prostatic development, **the hormone acts through the mesenchyme and the mesenchyme instructs the epithelium**. The same two-compartment rule explains three findings: DHT is made in the stroma, stroma can impose prostatic identity even on bladder epithelium, and oestrogen-induced metaplasia needs receptor in both compartments.*

### Development of the Human Seminal Vesicle

- **Literature is minimal** — only two human papers are identified.
- **Origin:** a **diverticulum of the Wolffian duct** at **10–12 weeks**.
- **Growth:** it elongates and folds back on itself → a **hook-like duct**, with **extensive side ducts by months 4–5**.
- **Autonomic innervation** of its muscle coat is detectable by **13 weeks**.
- **Pathology:** malformation and malignancy are **extremely rare**; the main malformation is a **seminal vesicle cyst**.

**Prostate vs seminal vesicle — origin**

| | Prostate | Seminal vesicle |
|---|---|---|
| Epithelial origin | **Endodermal UGE** (FOXA1-positive) | **Mesodermal Wolffian duct** |
| Onset | 10 weeks (buds) | 10–12 weeks (diverticulum) |

### KEY POINTS: PROSTATE DEVELOPMENT

- **Androgens from the fetal testis** are critical for prostatic development.
- **Mesenchymal–epithelial interactions** are fundamental, even though the whole process is triggered by androgen acting through the AR.

## The numbers to lock in

**Key facts, figures and cut-offs**
- **Prostatic buds:** 10 weeks (reported range 9.5–10). **Branching:** from about 11 weeks.
- **Seminal vesicle diverticulum:** 10–12 weeks. **Side ducts:** months 4–5. **Innervation:** 13 weeks.
- **DHT affinity for the AR:** about 10 times that of testosterone.
- **Smooth muscle:** appears first ventrally, then laterally and dorsally.
- **Ductal canalisation:** proximal → distal.
- **Six stages of prostatic development:** prebud → solid buds → elongation and branching → canalisation → luminal and basal differentiation → secretion.

**Gold standard / commonest**
- **Prostatic buds** arise mostly from the **gutters lateral to the verumontanum**.
- **Endodermal lineage marker:** FOXA1.

**Eponyms**
- **Jost and Price:** animal studies establishing androgen dependence of the prostate.
- **Verumontanum:** the Wolffian–Müllerian (mesodermal) and UGE (endodermal) interface; unique to humans.

**Classic traps**
- **Trap:** canalisation of the vestibular or urethral plate needs androgen. **Actually:** it is androgen-independent; only fold fusion is androgen-dependent.
- **Trap:** CAH is identified by the phallus. **Actually:** the urethra can be male-identical; the clues are impalpable gonads and hyperpigmentation.
- **Trap:** the seminal vesicle comes from the UGS like the prostate. **Actually:** it comes from the Wolffian duct (mesoderm).
- **Trap:** epithelial ESR1 alone causes squamous metaplasia. **Actually:** ESR1 is needed in both stroma and epithelium.
- **Trap:** fetal prostatic squamous metaplasia is pathological. **Actually:** it is normal in the third trimester, caused by maternal oestrogen.
- **Trap:** 5αR1 compensates for 5αR2 deficiency. **Actually:** 5αR1 is normal in these patients, yet the prostate is still rudimentary.
- **Trap:** fetal ventral buds persist into the adult gland. **Actually:** budding is stochastic, and ventral buds are not represented in the adult.
