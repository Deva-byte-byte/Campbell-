### DEVELOPMENT OF THE GONADS

#### The indifferent gonad

- **What the gonads make.** Testes produce sperm and ovaries produce oocytes, but **development begins identically in both sexes**.
- **Germinal ridge.** It forms at **4 weeks** on the medial side of the urogenital ridge, which contains the mesonephric duct and tubules.
- **Four components of the indifferent gonad:**
  1. **coelomic epithelium** of the germinal ridge;
  2. the underlying **mesenchyme**;
  3. the adjacent **mesonephros**;
  4. **primordial germ cells (PGCs)**.
- **Shape tells the sex apart later.** The gonads are **macroscopically identical at 7–8 weeks**. After that, **testes become spherical** and **ovaries stay oblong (and longer)**.

#### Primordial germ cells and sex cords

- **Migration route:** PGCs arise in the **yolk sac wall in week 3** → **dorsal mesentery of the hindgut** → **gonadal ridges**.
- **Primary sex cords.** In **week 6**, surface epithelium invades the ridge mesenchyme to form the primary sex cords.
  - In humans these cords **do not form the seminiferous cords**.
  - Human **seminiferous cords appear to come from the mesonephric tubules**.
- **First sex-specific histology:**
  - **testis:** **testicular cords at about 6 weeks**;
  - **ovary:** **about 8 weeks**, when it **segregates into a cortical zone (ovigerous cords) and a medullary zone**.
- **Origin of the ovarian zones — competing views:**
  - two successive proliferations of the germinal epithelium; or
  - ovigerous cords derived from the mesonephric tubules.

#### Testicular Formation

- **SRY-dependent step.** Under the SRY gene product, medullary cells coalesce into **seminiferous cords of Sertoli cells enclosing the PGCs**.
  - The testicular medulla is a zone of **mesenchymal-to-epithelial transition**.
- **Sertoli cells differentiate only if SRY protein is present.** Without it, **the gonad becomes an ovary by default**.
- **Sertoli–germ cell contact.** Direct contact soon after the PGCs arrive is thought to be **key to normal male gamete development**.
- **Cords → tubules.** The cords **canalise into seminiferous tubules**.
- **Duct connections:**
  - the **rete testis** (thin-walled ducts) develops with the cords;
  - at the hilum, it joins **5–12 residual mesonephric tubules**, which become the **efferent ductules** to the head of the epididymis;
  - the **full connection** — tubules → rete → efferent ductules → epididymis — is **not established until the third month**.
- **Epididymis and vas deferens** come from the **Wolffian (mesonephric) duct**.
- **Change of shape.** The testis goes from oblong to round, which **reduces its contact with the mesonephros**.
- **Tunica albuginea.** A thick capsule forms beneath the surface mesothelium.

**Müllerian-inhibiting substance (MIS; also called anti-Müllerian hormone, AMH)**
- **Source and action.** It is a glycoprotein from **Sertoli cells**, and makes the **paramesonephric (Müllerian) ducts regress between weeks 8 and 10**.
- **Male Müllerian remnants:**
  - the **appendix testis** (a small epithelial cyst at the upper pole);
  - the **prostatic utricle** (a pouch at the apex of the verumontanum).
- **In females, MIS is absent**, so the Müllerian ducts persist and form **most of the female internal genitalia**.

**Leydig cells and Wolffian derivatives**
- **Leydig cells.** From **8 weeks** they differentiate from the genital-ridge mesenchyme in response to SRY, and **begin testosterone production at 8 weeks**.
- **Control of testosterone secretion** shifts over time: **placental chorionic gonadotrophin early** → **fetal pituitary gonadotrophins later**.
- **Weeks 8–12:** testosterone turns the Wolffian ducts into the paired **epididymis, vas deferens and seminal vesicle**.
- **Remnants:**
  - the cranial Wolffian duct degenerates → **appendix epididymis**;
  - the mesonephric tubules near the lower pole degenerate → **paradidymis**.

**MIS vs testosterone — the two testicular hormones**

| | MIS (AMH) | Testosterone |
|---|---|---|
| Cell | **Sertoli** | **Leydig** |
| Target | Müllerian (paramesonephric) duct | Wolffian (mesonephric) duct, UGS, genital tubercle |
| Effect | **Regression** (weeks 8–10) | **Stabilisation and differentiation** (weeks 8–12) → epididymis, vas, seminal vesicle |
| Male remnants | Appendix testis, prostatic utricle | Appendix epididymis, paradidymis (from Wolffian structures left undifferentiated) |
| If absent | Uterus and tubes persist | Wolffian ducts regress |

#### Ovarian Formation

- **Why the ovary is the default.** With **no Y chromosome, there is no SRY**. So there are no Sertoli-cell cords, no early MIS, no Leydig cells and no androgen, and **female development ensues**.
- **First ovary-specific event:** segregation into **cortex and medulla at about 8 weeks**. The cortex fills with **anastomosing cortical cords**.
- **Origin of the cortical cords is disputed:**
  - from the mesonephric tubules; or
  - from gonadal-ridge epithelial-like cells at or near the surface, which become the somatic ovarian cells.
  - Either way, the cords **break into clusters that the germ cells enter**.
- **Second ovary-specific event:** **germ cell nests at 10–11 weeks**.
  - These are clusters of **2–4 germ cells joined by intercellular bridges**.
  - Nests are **clonal**, so one nest may come from a single PGC.
  - They **enlarge by proliferation**, then **break up by germ-cell apoptosis**.

**Folliculogenesis — sequence**
1. **FOXL2-positive** somatic interstitial cells surround single germ cells → **primordial follicle**: an oocyte with a single layer of **squamous** follicular cells.
2. **About 20 weeks:** the follicular cells become **cuboidal** → **primary follicle**.
3. **At term:** granulosa cells proliferate → **secondary follicle**, with **multiple granulosa layers**.
4. **Postnatally:**
   - **antral follicles** appear, with a fluid-filled cavity;
   - the **theca** organises around the growing follicle. It is vascular, and it:
     - supports the follicle structurally;
     - **nourishes the avascular granulosa**;
     - **synthesises sex steroids**.

- **Meiotic arrest.** Oogonia enlarge and **enter meiosis I as primary oocytes during fetal life**, then **arrest until puberty**. After puberty, individual oocytes resume under the **monthly gonadotrophin surge**.

**Testis vs ovary — timeline and default**

| | Testis | Ovary |
|---|---|---|
| Trigger | **SRY → Sertoli cells** | **Absence of SRY** (default pathway) |
| First specific feature | Testicular cords, about **6 weeks** | Cortex and medulla segregate, about **8 weeks** |
| Second feature | Leydig cells and testosterone, 8 weeks | Germ cell nests, 10–11 weeks |
| Germ cells | Enclosed by Sertoli cells in cords → seminiferous tubules | Nests → primordial (squamous) → primary (cuboidal, ~20 weeks) → secondary (term) follicles |
| Shape | Spherical | Oblong, longer |
| Hormones in fetal life | MIS and testosterone | None required for duct development |

**Concept —** *Gonadal sex is a **single switch, SRY in the pre-Sertoli cell**. Everything male follows from Sertoli cells: MIS and Leydig-cell androgen. Everything female follows from their absence. So the male phenotype is active and hormone-driven, while the female internal phenotype is the default.*

**TABLE 32.3 Embryologic Origin of Urogenital Structures** *(modified from Moore, Persaud and Torchia, 2016)*

| Embryonic structure | Male derivative | Female derivative |
|---|---|---|
| **Indifferent gonad** (cortex / medulla) | Testis; seminiferous tubules; **rete testis** (mediastinum testis) | Ovary; ovarian follicles; **rete ovarii** (hilum, analogous to rete testis) |
| **Gubernaculum** | Gubernaculum testis (descent into the scrotum) | **Ovarian ligament** and **round ligament of the uterus** |
| **Mesonephric tubules** (mesonephros, second kidney) | **Efferent ductules**; **paradidymis** (vestigial tubules above the epididymis) | **Epoöphoron, paroöphoron** (non-functional, between the ovary and tube) |
| **Wolffian (mesonephric) duct**; stalk of the ureteric bud | Appendix of epididymis; **ductus deferens**; **ejaculatory duct and seminal vesicle**; ureter, pelvis, calyces, collecting tubules | **Gartner duct** (anterosuperior vaginal wall); ureter, pelvis, calyces, collecting tubules |
| **Intermediate mesoderm** (metanephric blastema) | Kidney (metanephros); nephrons (glomerulus, proximal tubule, loop of Henle, distal tubule) | Same |
| **Müllerian (paramesonephric) duct** | **Appendix testis** | **Paratubal cyst**; uterine (fallopian) tube; uterus; cervix |
| **Urogenital sinus** | Bladder and trigone; urethra; **prostate**; verumontanum (seminal colliculus); **prostatic utricle**; urethral glands (**Littre**); bulbourethral glands (**Cowper**) | Bladder and trigone; urethra; **vagina, hymen**; urethral glands (**Skene**, lesser vestibular); greater vestibular glands (**Bartholin**) |
| **Genital tubercle** | Penis; glans; corpora cavernosa; **corpus spongiosum** | Clitoris; glans; corpora cavernosa; **bulb of the vestibule** |
| **Urethral / vestibular folds** | Ventral aspect of the penis | **Labia minora** |
| **Labioscrotal swellings** | Scrotum | **Labia majora** |

- **What the table is for:** a homologue map. **Each primordium gives a paired male and female structure**, so a remnant or anomaly in one sex can be predicted from the other.
- **Pattern across the rows:**
  - **Wolffian-duct and mesonephric derivatives persist in the male and leave vestigial remnants in the female**, and the Müllerian duct shows the mirror image;
  - **the ureter and collecting system come from the Wolffian system in both sexes**, which is why Wolffian anomalies carry renal anomalies (see OHVIRA, Instalment 5).
- **Homologue pairs worth knowing:**
  - Littre ↔ Skene glands;
  - **Cowper ↔ Bartholin glands**;
  - **corpus spongiosum ↔ bulb of the vestibule**;
  - rete testis ↔ rete ovarii;
  - paradidymis ↔ paroöphoron.
- **The traps:**
  - The **prostatic utricle** appears under the **UGS** row in this table, but the text and Fig. 32.25 call it a **Müllerian remnant** (the fused Müllerian ducts opening at the verumontanum). Learn it as **Müllerian**; see the flag below.
  - The table lists the **vagina under the UGS**, **not under the Müllerian duct**. This fits the chapter's conclusion that vaginal epithelium comes from the UGS (Instalment 5).
  - The **appendix testis is Müllerian**, whereas the **appendix epididymis is Wolffian**.

## The numbers to lock in

**Key facts, figures and cut-offs**
- **Germinal ridge:** 4 weeks. **PGCs in the yolk sac:** week 3. **Primary sex cords:** week 6.
- **Testicular cords:** about 6 weeks. **Ovarian cortex and medulla:** about 8 weeks. **Gonads macroscopically identical:** 7–8 weeks.
- **MIS-driven Müllerian regression:** weeks 8–10.
- **Leydig testosterone:** from 8 weeks. **Wolffian differentiation:** weeks 8–12.
- **Seminiferous tubule → epididymis continuity:** third month.
- **Efferent ductules:** 5–12 residual mesonephric tubules.
- **Germ cell nests:** 10–11 weeks, 2–4 cells. **Primary follicles:** about 20 weeks. **Secondary follicles:** at term. **Antral follicles and theca:** postnatal.
- **Oocytes:** arrest in meiosis I from fetal life until puberty.

**Eponyms**
- **Gartner duct / cyst:** Wolffian remnant, anterolateral (anterosuperior) vaginal wall.
- **Littre glands:** male urethral glands. Female homologue: **Skene glands**.
- **Cowper glands:** bulbourethral. Female homologue: **Bartholin glands**.
- **Epoöphoron and paroöphoron:** Wolffian (mesonephric) remnants in the broad ligament.

**Classic traps**
- **Trap:** the appendix testis and appendix epididymis share an origin. **Actually:** the appendix testis is Müllerian and the appendix epididymis is Wolffian.
- **Trap:** MIS comes from Leydig cells. **Actually:** it comes from Sertoli cells; Leydig cells make testosterone.
- **Trap:** human seminiferous cords come from the primary sex cords. **Actually:** they appear to come from the mesonephric tubules.
- **Trap:** early fetal testosterone is under fetal pituitary control. **Actually:** it is under placental hCG early and the fetal pituitary later.
- **Trap:** the ovary is actively induced. **Actually:** it is the default in the absence of SRY and Sertoli cells.

- **Terminology error (p. 597):** germ cells "linked by intracellular bridges" → **"intercellular bridges"**. The bridges connect neighbouring cells; "intracellular" is wrong.

- **Internal inconsistency (prostatic utricle).** Table 32.3 lists it under the urogenital sinus, while the text (pp. 590, 594–595) and the Fig. 32.25 legend call it a Müllerian-duct remnant. The chapter's own weight of statement is **Müllerian**, and that is what the notes teach.
