Chapter 116 — Retropubic Suspension Surgery for Incontinence in Females — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else

  1. Retropubic suspension needs urethral mobility — fixed urethra / pure ISD (intrinsic sphincter deficiency) → sling or AUS (artificial urinary sphincter).
  2. Burch → Cooper's ligament; MMK (Marshall–Marchetti–Krantz) → pubic periosteum; paravaginal → ATFP (arcus tendineus fascia pelvis); VOS (vagino-obturator shelf) → obturator fascia.
  3. Open Burch success 85–90% at 1 yr → ~70% at 5 yr.
  4. Burch niche: concomitant abdominal surgery, poor vaginal access, mesh avoidance.
  5. No concomitant abdominal surgery → choose vaginal option, not retropubic.
  6. SISTEr: autologous sling > Burch success, but more morbid and obstructive.
  7. Ward–Hilton: TVT (tension-free vaginal tape) ≈ colposuspension at 6 mo, 2 yr, 5 yr.
  8. CARE: prophylactic Burch at sacrocolpopexy ↓ de novo SUI in continent females.
  9. Intraoperative cystoscopy mandatory — no efflux → release sutures.
  10. Osteitis pubis: MMK, 0.7–2.5%, 2–12 wk.

Definitions, Prevalence, and Cost

  • SUI (stress urinary incontinence) = involuntary loss on effort (ICS, International Continence Society).
  • Commonest subtype: SUI only (~26%) > MUI (mixed) (~16%) > UUI (urgency) only (~10%).
  • Any UI >50% of US females >20 yr; only 25–45% seek care.
  • ~10% of diagnosed SUI → surgery.
  • Nulliparous pure SUI: BMI >25 and age >45 only independent risks.

General Considerations

  • SUI mechanisms: hypermobility vs ISD; no test apportions each.
  • Hammock hypothesis (DeLancey): backstop compression, not urethral position.
  • Enhorning: pressure-transmission theory — basis of suspension.
  • Burch 1961: 2–4 sutures/side to Cooper's; Tanagho 1976: more lateral, looser.
  • MMK 1949; Pereyra 1959 needle suspension; VOS 1990; laparoscopic 1991.
  • Needle suspension abandoned: suture pull-through, vaginal tearing → lower cure.
  • Laparoscopic ≈ open subjective cure; long-term cost-effective (QALY).
  • Slings ~90% of US SUI operations by 2009.
  • AUS gold standard: post-prostatectomy SUI in males.
  • AUS in females: second-line (ICS, EAU, AUA); NICE → recurrent SUI.
  • Transvaginal AUS disfavoured: infection, cuff erosion.

Patient Selection

  • Not indicated: UUI without SUI. MUI: treats stress component only.
  • AUA/SUFU surgical options: MUS (midurethral sling), autologous PVS (pubovaginal sling), Burch, bulking.
  • Cough test negative supine → repeat standing, comfortably full bladder.
  • Cotton-swab (Q-tip) test: >30° = hypermobility; no longer recommended (AUA/SUFU).
  • Minimum work-up: PVR (post-void residual) + urinalysis.
  • UDS (urodynamics) not needed in index patient if SUI seen on examination.
  • UDS indicated: SUI history, not demonstrated objectively.
  • ISD: VLPP (Valsalva leak point pressure) <60 cm H₂O or MUCP (maximal urethral closure pressure) <20 cm H₂O.
  • Voiding diary: 1–3 days.
  • Short pad test (20 min–2 h) confirms: >1.4 g positive.
  • 24-h pad test: 4.4–20 g mild; 21–74 g moderate; >75 g severe.
  • ICS 1-h test: 500 mL over 15 min, or fill 150–300 mL / 50–75% capacity.
  • Burch failure predictors: BMI >25, age >60, anticholinergics, prior SUI surgery.
  • MUS failure predictors: BMI >30, smoking, diabetes, anticholinergics, prior surgery.
  • Most important selection factor: patient goals; document mesh counselling.

Specific Discussion Regarding Retropubic Suspensions

  • Space of Retzius: avascular, preperitoneal; symphysis anterior, bladder posterior.
  • ATFP: pubic symphysis → ischial spine; weakness → paravaginal defect.
  • Precluding: no vaginal mobility; no urethral mobility (~5× failure); ISD sole cause.
  • Relative risks of failure: urgency, obesity, menopause, prior hysterectomy/SUI surgery, advanced POP.
  • ISD settings: recurrent SUI after surgery; neurogenic SUI.
  • Lead-pipe urethra: immobile, rigid, fixed open → AUS territory.
  • Position: low lithotomy / supine, legs gently abducted; Foley in.
  • Cherney incision: rectus tendon divided transversely above pubis.
  • Santorini's plexus: avoid midline dissection over urethra / bladder neck.
  • Burch sutures: 0 or 1-0, SH needle; mid-urethra first; cephalad more lateral.
  • Pass medial → lateral; don't enter vaginal lumen; distal tied first.
  • Knots above ligament; leave ~2 fingerbreadths bladder neck–pubis.
  • MMK: two suture pairs, full-thickness vaginal wall → periosteum.
  • VOS = Burch + paravaginal hybrid.
  • Suture-held (Burch, MMK) → V-angle, more obstructive; scar-held (paravaginal, VOS) → flatter, less.
  • AUS in females: cuff at bladder neck; pump labia majora; reservoir preperitoneal.
  • Transvaginal AUS: inverted-U incision; copious antibiotic irrigation.
  • Postoperative voiding: CIC preferred to suprapubic tube; continue until PVR <150 mL.

Outcomes

  • Objective success: cough stress test + 1-h pad test.
  • In-person review by 6 mo: history, examination, PVR.
  • Follow-up: short 1–2 yr; intermediate to 47 mo; long >48 mo.
  • SISTEr (655 randomised): PVS > Burch at 2 yr; more urinary tract infection, de novo UUI.
  • SISTEr 5 yr: Burch continence lower; sling satisfaction higher.
  • Ward–Hilton: 179 TVT vs 169 colposuspension; 6-mo dry 36% vs 28%.
  • TVT: more bladder perforation; colposuspension: more voiding dysfunction.
  • Retropubic MUS vs Burch (meta-analysis): MUS higher cure, more perforation.
  • Laparoscopic Burch vs TVT: TVT better, shorter operation.
  • Transobturator MUS vs Burch: comparable; less retention with transobturator.
  • Surgery vs collagen: surgery more successful; collagen fewer complications.
  • CARE (322): Burch in stress-continent sacrocolpopexy → less SUI at 2 yr.
  • Preoperative SUI at sacrocolpopexy: MUS > Burch.
  • POP after colposuspension: 2.5–27%.
  • AUS: erosion rises beyond 5 yr; minimally invasive → less mechanical failure.
  • No efflux on cystoscopy → release sutures, repeat until patent.
  • Osteitis pubis: suprapubic pain to legs, tender symphysis → antibiotics → debridement.

Classic traps

  • Trap: Burch for fixed-urethra ISD — Actually: needs mobility; sling or AUS.
  • Trap: Burch as default standalone — Actually: only with abdominal surgery / mesh avoidance.
  • Trap: Burch anchors to pubic periosteum — Actually: that is MMK; Burch → Cooper's.
  • Trap: Burch offered for UUI — Actually: not indicated without SUI.
  • Trap: UDS routine before SUI surgery — Actually: not if SUI demonstrated on examination.
  • Trap: VLPP / MUCP predicts success — Actually: leak point pressure does not.
  • Trap: Burch equals sling in SISTEr — Actually: PVS more successful, more morbid.
  • Trap: sutures near bladder neck, midline — Actually: lateral; else obstruction, bleeding.
  • Trap: osteitis pubis after Burch — Actually: classically MMK.
  • Trap: transvaginal AUS preferred in females — Actually: open abdominal; infection, erosion.
