Chapter 79 — Neoplasms of the Testis — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else
  • Solid testicular mass = germ cell tumour (GCT) until proven otherwise → radical inguinal orchidectomy.
  • Trans-scrotal orchidectomy/biopsy contraindicated — alters lymphatic drainage.
  • Raised alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) = non-seminoma (NSGCT), even if histology says pure seminoma.
  • Half-lives: AFP 5–7 days; hCG (human chorionic gonadotrophin) 24–36 h.
  • Rising post-orchidectomy markers → induction chemotherapy.
  • International Germ Cell Cancer Collaborative Group (IGCCCG): good → BEP×3 (bleomycin, etoposide, cisplatin) or EP×4 (etoposide, cisplatin); intermediate/poor → BEP×4.
  • CS I NSGCT (clinical stage I) occult metastasis 20–30%; predictors: lymphovascular invasion (LVI), embryonal carcinoma (EC) predominance.
  • CS I seminoma → surveillance preferred; risk factors: size >4 cm, rete testis invasion.
  • NSGCT residual mass >1 cm → resect; teratoma ≈45%, viable ≈15%.
  • Seminoma residual >3 cm → FDG-PET (fluorodeoxyglucose positron emission tomography) ≥6 weeks; PET+ → resect; <3 cm or PET− → observe.

Epidemiology and Risk

  • Commonest: solid malignancy, males 20–40; incidence peak 25–35.
  • GCT >95% of testis tumours; 5% extragonadal.
  • Seminoma 52–56% vs NSGCT 44–48%.
  • Bilateral 2%, mostly metachronous; synchronous 0.5%.
  • Cryptorchidism relative risk (RR) 4–6 → 2–3 if orchidopexy before puberty.
  • Affected brother RR 8–12 vs father 2–4.
  • Prior GCT: contralateral risk ×12; 15-year incidence only 2%.
  • GCNIS (germ cell neoplasia in situ): invasive GCT 50% at 5 years, 70% at 7.
  • Contralateral GCNIS 5–9%; ≈36% if atrophy/cryptorchidism/age <40.
  • Metastatic cure: 5–10% pre-cisplatin → 80–90% now.

Histology

  • i(12p) in all postpubertal GCT except spermatocytic tumour.
  • Any NSGCT element → classify as NSGCT.
  • Seminoma: CD117+, CD30−, placental alkaline phosphatase (PLAP) strongly +; hCG in 15%.
  • Spermatocytic tumour: sixth decade, no GCNIS, benign; orchidectomy curative.
  • EC: c-KIT−; metastasises with normal markers.
  • Yolk sac: AFP, Schiller–Duval body.
  • Choriocarcinoma: very high hCG, haematogenous, haemorrhage; thyrotoxicosis (shared α-subunit).
  • Teratoma: chemoresistant; in ≈half of mixed GCT; malignant transformation → rhabdomyosarcoma commonest.

Presentation and Diagnosis

  • Metastatic at diagnosis: ≈two-thirds NSGCT vs 15% seminoma.
  • Misdiagnosed initially ≈one-third (epididymitis, hydrocele).
  • Suspect GCT, age 15–50: testicular mass, midline retroperitoneal mass, left supraclavicular mass.
  • Epididymo-orchitis: re-examine 2–4 weeks after antibiotics.
  • Ultrasound first-line; microlithiasis alone → no action.
  • Impalpable lesion: <1 cm ≈50% malignant; 1–2 cm ≥80%.
  • Orchidectomy: within 1–2 weeks; cord to internal ring.
  • Orchidectomy alone cures: 80–85% CS I seminoma; 70–80% CS I NSGCT.
  • Testis-sparing: only bilateral/solitary testis, tumour <2–3 cm; frozen section.
  • GCNIS dose: radiotherapy ≥20 Gy (<20 Gy → recurrence); up to 40% need testosterone.
  • hCG >5000 IU/L → usually NSGCT.
  • False-positive hCG: luteinising hormone (LH) cross-reactivity (hypogonadism) → normalises 48–72 h after testosterone.
  • miR-371a-3p: best microRNA marker; negative in teratoma.
  • Unstable, markers very high → chemotherapy first, orchidectomy after (sanctuary site).
  • Extragonadal pattern + raised AFP/hCG → treat without biopsy.

Staging

  • Landing zone: right → interaortocaval; left → para-aortic (PA)/left renal hilar.
  • Crossover right → left common; left → right implies bulk.
  • Computed tomography (CT) false-negative: 25–35% CS I NSGCT (1-cm cut-off).
  • Suspicious node: 5–9 mm in landing zone, anterior to great vessels.
  • CS II: IIA ≤2 cm; IIB 2–5 cm; IIC >5 cm.
  • pN: pN1 ≤5 nodes ≤2 cm; pN2 >5 nodes or 2–5 cm; pN3 >5 cm.
  • S category: S1 AFP <1000, hCG <5000, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) <1.5×N (upper limit of normal); S3 AFP >10,000, hCG >50,000, LDH >10×N.
  • LVI → pT2.
  • Imaging and markers: within 4 weeks of treatment; equivocal CT → repeat 6–8 weeks.
  • Brain imaging if hCG >10,000; no routine bone scan or diagnostic PET.
  • IGCCCG NSGCT good: AFP <1000, hCG <5000, LDH <1.5×N, no NPVM (non-pulmonary visceral metastases); 56%, 5-year survival 92%.
  • IGCCCG NSGCT poor: mediastinal primary, NPVM, AFP >10,000, hCG >50,000, LDH >10×N; 16%, survival 48%.
  • IGCCCG seminoma: NPVM only factor; 90% good; no poor risk.
  • Fertility: recovery after chemotherapy 50% at 2 years, 80% at 5; bank sperm before RPLND/chemotherapy/radiotherapy.

Non-Seminoma

  • CS I options: surveillance, RPLND (retroperitoneal lymph node dissection), BEP×1 — all ≈100% survival.
  • No LVI/EC: occult metastasis <20%.
  • Surveillance: relapse ≈28%; >90% within 2 years; 65–75% retroperitoneal.
  • Surveillance best if relapse risk <33–37%; active treatment >46–54%.
  • Full bilateral template: recurrence <2%; antegrade ejaculation >90% (nerve-sparing).
  • Albers trial: BEP×1 vs unilateral RPLND → 2-year progression-free survival 99% vs 92%; no deaths.
  • pN2–3 relapse >50% → adjuvant BEP×2/EP×2 → ≤1%.
  • Primary BEP×1: relapse ≈2–3%, but relapses chemoresistant.
  • CS IS → induction chemotherapy.
  • CS IIA–B chemotherapy if: raised markers or nodes >3 cm.
  • CS IIA–B RPLND if: normal markers, nodes <3 cm (teratoma risk).
  • Gold standard first line: BEP; PVB (cisplatin, vinblastine, bleomycin) replaced (neuromuscular toxicity).
  • VIP×4 (etoposide, ifosfamide, cisplatin) replaces BEP×4 when lungs compromised/major chest surgery.
  • Complete response: residual ≤1 cm; residual ≤20 mm still teratoma ≈30%.
  • Resect RPLND first; necrosis there predicts necrosis elsewhere.
  • Post-chemotherapy viable cancer: bad if IGCCCG intermediate/poor, incomplete resection, >10% viable.
  • Second line: VIP, VeIP, TIP (ifosfamide–cisplatin with etoposide, vinblastine or paclitaxel); HDCT (high-dose chemotherapy) specialist centres only.
  • Growing teratoma syndrome: falling markers + enlarging mass → stop chemotherapy, resect.
  • Late relapse >2 years (≈3%): yolk sac commonest; surgery; TIP best chemotherapy.
  • Desperation surgery: rising markers, single resectable site → survival 33–57%.

Seminoma

  • CS I ≈80%; occult metastasis 10–15%; surveillance relapse-free 80–86%.
  • Seminoma relapse later: median 14 months vs 4–8 (NSGCT).
  • CT detects >85% of relapses; hCG <5%.
  • PA radiotherapy 20 Gy = 30 Gy; PA → pelvic recurrence 2% → pelvic CT.
  • MRC TE19/EORTC 30982 (Medical Research Council / European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer): carboplatin×1 vs radiotherapy, relapse-free 94.7% vs 96%.
  • Carboplatin dose = 7 × (glomerular filtration rate [GFR] + 25) mg; isotope GFR, not estimated.
  • Non-compliant: radiotherapy or carboplatin×2.
  • CS IIA–B: dog-leg (DL) radiotherapy 25–30 Gy (boost 5–10 Gy), BEP×3/EP×4, or RPLND ≤3 cm.
  • Bulky >3 cm/multiple → chemotherapy.
  • SEMS: RPLND, 2-year recurrence-free 81%, survival 100%.
  • Advanced: 90% good → BEP×3/EP×4; intermediate → BEP×4.
  • Residual histology: necrosis 90%; viable in 13–55% if >3 cm vs 0–4% <3 cm.
  • PET >3 cm: specificity 100%, sensitivity 80%.
  • Relapse after carboplatin = chemo-naive → cisplatin-based first line.

Brain Metastases and Toxicity

  • Brain metastases: 1% at diagnosis; choriocarcinoma; haemorrhage deaths 4–10%.
  • At diagnosis: BEP×4 → resect residual.
  • 5-year survival: NSGCT 33%, seminoma 57%.
  • Toxic death: 0–2.4% good risk; 3–4.4% intermediate/poor.
  • SMN (second malignancy): +60–100% after cisplatin or radiotherapy; +200% after both.
  • After radiotherapy: SMN ≈18% at 25 years.
Classic traps
  • Trap: normal markers exclude GCT — Actually: orchidectomy regardless.
  • Trap: pre-orchidectomy markers stage disease — Actually: post-orchidectomy values.
  • Trap: IGCCCG for relapse — Actually: diagnosis of metastatic disease only.
  • Trap: LVI predicts seminoma relapse — Actually: size >4 cm, rete testis.
  • Trap: spermatocytic tumour is a seminoma — Actually: distinct, benign, no GCNIS.
  • Trap: PET for NSGCT residual — Actually: useless; teratoma not FDG-avid.
  • Trap: no teratoma in primary excludes it — Actually: does not; resect.
  • Trap: carboplatin for metastatic GCT — Actually: inferior to cisplatin.
  • Trap: mediastinal seminoma is poor risk — Actually: seminoma has no poor risk.
  • Trap: high-dose chemotherapy is standard — Actually: three negative randomised trials.
