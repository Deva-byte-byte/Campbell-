Chapter 156 — Pathology of Prostatic Neoplasia — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

If you read nothing else
  • Isolated high-grade prostatic intraepithelial neoplasia (HGPIN), even multifocal → no repeat biopsy.
  • Intraductal carcinoma (IDC-P) + grade group (GG) 1 → not for active surveillance (AS); >80% upgraded.
  • Atypical, suspicious → ~40% cancer; PSA does not stratify.
  • Atypical → expert review before re-biopsy.
  • T1a ≤5% vs T1b >5% of TURP (transurethral resection) chips.
  • GG: 1 = 3+3; 2 = 3+4; 3 = 4+3; 4 = 8; 5 = 9–10.
  • Pure 3+3 → no nodal metastatic potential.
  • Report % pattern 4 for GG 2–3 on biopsy.
  • Focal vs nonfocal positive margin: 3 mm.
  • Hormone-treated cancer → no Gleason score; cryo/HIFU residual → graded.

Prostatic Intraepithelial Neoplasia

Cut-offs:

  • HGPIN on biopsy ~4–5% (reported 0–25%).
  • TMPRSS2:ERG: ~20% HGPIN vs ~50% cancers.
  • IDC-P alone on biopsy <0.06%.
  • IDC-P prevalence: ~2% low-risk → ~56% metastatic; ~60% post-ADT (androgen deprivation therapy)/chemotherapy.

Gold standard / rules:

  • Low-grade PIN (LGPIN) never reported.
  • HGPIN on TUR → no further sampling.
  • PINATYP → manage as atypical.
  • Atypical intraductal proliferation (AIP), benign or GG1 biopsy → consider re-biopsy.
  • IDC-P alone → definitive therapy acceptable (or re-biopsy).
  • Cribriform HGPIN no longer diagnosed → IDC-P or AIP.

Eponym:

  • Guo–Epstein criteria — stringent, highly specific IDC-P definition.

Adenocarcinoma

Staging:

  • T1c = non-palpable, needle biopsy-detected.
  • pT2+ = positive margin at intraprostatic incision or ill-defined apex.
  • pT3a = EPE (extraprostatic extension), incl. microscopic bladder neck.
  • pT3b = seminal vesicle muscular wall invasion.

Location / spread:

  • Peripheral zone posterior; 85% of T1c; anterior ~15%.
  • Anterior: same BCR (biochemical recurrence) risk; more positive margins with EPE.
  • Multifocal >85%; ~20% contralateral adverse pathology.
  • EPE via perineural spaces, posterior/posterolateral.
  • SVI (seminal vesicle invasion) commonest route: out at base → periseminal tissue.
  • Metastases: nodes, bone → lung (with bone) → liver, adrenal.
  • Left supraclavicular node may be presenting site.

Volume:

  • EPE uncommon <0.5 cm³; nodes/SVI uncommon <4 cm³.
  • Transition zone tumours exit at larger volumes.

Grade:

  • Biopsy score = commonest + highest (2005).
  • Lowest in practice 3+3=6.
  • GG4 includes 3+5 and 5+3.
  • Grade groups: WHO 2016, CAP, AJCC endorsed.
  • 5-yr BCR-free by GG 1–5: 96, 88, 63, 48, 26%.
  • Nodes at RP (radical prostatectomy): 3+3 0%, 3+4 0.6%, 4+3 4.3%.

Upgrading:

  • Biopsy GG1 → RP: ~⅓ upgraded; GG2 → GG3 ~20%.
  • Very low-risk AS (1–2 cores, ≤50%/core, PSAD <0.15) → ~15%.
  • GG1 + negative mpMRI → ~20% upgraded at 6 years.
  • Predictors: more cancer, higher PSA, smaller prostate, fewer cores.

Needle biopsy:

  • Separate/inked cores → score per core; pooled → one score.
  • Max 2–3 cores per block.
  • Atypical ~5% of biopsies; 80% of cancers found = GG1, 5% GG4–5.
  • Report: positive cores + one extent measure.
  • Perineural invasion on biopsy → report.
  • GG3 60% pattern 4 → may skip neoadjuvant hormones; 90% → give.

TUR:

  • 6–8 cassettes → all T1b; 8–10 → >90% T1a.
  • Embed all if <65 years (some centres).
  • Adenosis: transition zone mimic; no cancer risk.

Radical prostatectomy:

  • Grade each nodule; dominant nodule usually worst.
  • Tertiary pattern 5 <5% → stays in its GG.
  • SVI → 65% 5-yr progression.
  • Positive margins ~15%; only ~50% progress.
  • Intraprostatic incision = risk of focal EPE + positive margin.
  • Node-positive, 15-yr: BCR-free ~7%, metastasis-free ~42%, CSS ~58%.
  • Independent predictors: grade, EPE extent, margins, SVI, nodes.
  • Vascular invasion: 7% of <4 cm³; independently prognostic.

Treatment effect:

  • Post-radiotherapy: benign / cancer no effect (graded) / cancer with effect (not graded ≈ no cancer).
  • Radiation atypia persists up to 72 months.
  • Finasteride → no consistent histological change.

Subtypes of Prostate Adenocarcinoma

  • Mucinous = acinar behaviour.
  • Small cell: survival <1 yr; ~50% mixed; not graded.
  • Ductal: pure 0.4–0.8%, mixed ~5%; pattern 4 (PIN-like 3; comedonecrosis 5).
  • Ductal: verumontanum papillary mass, normal PSA/DRE; visceral recurrence.
  • Squamous: osteolytic, hormone-unresponsive.
  • Sarcomatoid: dismal.

Urothelial carcinoma:

  • 35–45% of cystoprostatectomies involve prostate.
  • Intraductal only → no prognostic effect.
  • From prostatic urethral CIS: suburethral pT1; stroma pT2.
  • Direct from bladder → pT4.
  • GATA3 best urothelial marker; prostate: PSA, P501S, NKX3.1.

Mesenchymal / other:

  • Sarcomas 0.1–0.2%.
  • Commonest: child rhabdomyosarcoma; adult leiomyosarcoma.
  • Inflammatory myofibroblastic tumour mimics leiomyosarcoma; post-TUR.
  • STUMP → follow-up; resect if young.
  • Leukaemia: commonest CLL/SLL.
Classic traps
  • Trap: multifocal HGPIN → re-biopsy — Actually: no.
  • Trap: GG1 + IDC-P → AS — Actually: excluded.
  • Trap: bladder neck invasion = pT4 — Actually: microscopic = pT3a.
  • Trap: 3+5 = GG5 — Actually: GG4.
  • Trap: 3+4 ≈ 4+3 — Actually: GG2 vs GG3.
  • Trap: Gleason 6 = mid-grade — Actually: lowest assigned.
  • Trap: perineural invasion reported on RP — Actually: biopsy only.
  • Trap: tumour volume independent at RP — Actually: no.
  • Trap: tumour-to-ink distance matters — Actually: no.
  • Trap: urothelial stromal invasion always pT4 — Actually: pT2 via urethral CIS.
