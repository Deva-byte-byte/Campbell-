## Ch 124 · Bladder Surgery for Benign Disease (incl. vesicovaginal fistula)
# Ch 124 rewrite log

**Missed-in-paper points added**
- Diverticulum (P2-083, P3-029): most acquired sacs incidental; endoscopic fulguration option; VCUG shows hidden sacs and bladder neck obstruction; ultrasound misses ureter/stones; extraperitoneal open route avoids intraperitoneal leak (gold standard); extravasation commonest complication, confirmed by drain creatinine and CT cystography.
- Boari / psoas hitch (P3-090): operative findings decide hitch vs flap; flap length = defect + ≈3 cm (overlap, tunnel), wider base for longer flaps; hitch cystotomy across half the dome, open contralateral endopelvic fascia; superolateral dome relatively immobile; ureteroureterostomy as beyond-reach option.
- Catheterisable channels (P2-081): minimally invasive appendicovesicostomy mainly in children, MIS Monti rare; caecal cuff widens stoma, caecal mobilisation for length; bladder drained 2–4 weeks before channel use.
- VVF (P3-046, P2-025): leak 1–3 weeks after hysterectomy; coexisting ureteric injury ≈10%; ureterovaginal fistula: stent first, then ureteroneocystostomy with or without psoas hitch (all marked †, outside source).
