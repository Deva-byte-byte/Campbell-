# Chapter 109 — Neuromuscular Dysfunction of the Lower Urinary Tract — Numbers, Gold Standards and Traps

<div class="box" markdown="1">
If you read nothing else

- **Suprapontine** → detrusor overactivity (DO) + **synergic** sphincters; true DSD never occurs
- **Suprasacral cord** → DO + smooth synergia + **striated detrusor–sphincter dyssynergia (DSD)**
- **Sacral/infrasacral** → **areflexia**, compliance may fall, fixed striated tone
- **DSD needs lesion between pontine micturition centre (PMC) and sacral cord**
- **Smooth sphincter dyssynergia** if cord lesion **≥T7–T8** (column T6)
- **Autonomic dysreflexia:** lesion **above T6**; **first act = drain bladder**; **nitroglycerin 2% paste** above lesion
- **Spinal shock 6–12 weeks** (up to 1–2 years); **clean intermittent catheterisation (CIC) preferred**
- **CIC = gold standard** emptying; volumes **<500 mL**
- **Cauda equina → decompress <48 h**
- **Manage by urodynamics, not neurologic level**
</div>

## Objectives

- **Five urodynamic parameters:** sensation, detrusor activity, compliance, smooth sphincter, striated sphincter
- **Open bladder neck:** multiple system atrophy (MSA), radical pelvic surgery, myelodysplasia, sacral injury

## Patterns: Neuroplasticity

- **Bladder outlet obstruction (BOO):** ↑ **type III : type I collagen** → ↓ compliance
- **Afferent plasticity:** nerve growth factor (NGF); **ice-water test = C fibres**
- **Plasticity** explains persisting lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS) after de-obstruction

## Diseases at or Above the Brainstem

- **Stroke urinary incontinence (UI):** 32–79% admission → 25–28% discharge → 12–19% later
- **UI persisting 7 days** → poor survival and dependence
- **Commonest UI type post-stroke: impaired awareness** — worst prognosis
- **Acute stroke:** retention (areflexia, "cerebral shock"); **chronic: phasic DO**
- **Basal ganglia/thalamus** lesion → voluntary sphincter control **preserved**
- **"Uninhibited sphincter relaxation"** = misnomer
- **Brainstem:** midbrain none; **pontine → low capacity**; **medullary → emptying failure**
- **Dementia:** UI 3× commoner; **anticholinergic → 10–65% ↑ dementia risk** → **β3 agonist preferred**
- **Traumatic brain injury (TBI):** above PMC → DO synergic; **below PMC → DSD possible**
- **Brain tumour:** **superior frontal lobe**; posterior fossa 32–70% dysfunction
- **Normal-pressure hydrocephalus (NPH) triad:** dementia, ataxia, **UI (98–100%)**; **ventriculoperitoneal shunt**
- **Cerebral palsy adults:** detrusor leak point pressure (DLPP) ≥40 cm H₂O in ~half → full work-up
- **Parkinson disease (PD):** LUTS 35–70%; **storage 50–75%**; **DO commonest**; **gold standard diagnosis = neuropathology (Lewy bodies)**
- **PD:** prevalence **3% ≥65**; 2nd neurodegenerative after Alzheimer
- **PD preferred drugs:** **mirabegron, extended-release fesoterodine, trospium, darifenacin**
- **Subthalamic deep brain stimulation** improves sensation/capacity
- **MSA = α-synucleinopathy**; MSA-P / MSA-C replace Shy–Drager etc.
- **MSA:** UI + high post-void residual (PVR) + **open bladder neck** + **sphincter denervation** + erectile dysfunction early
- **MSA:** **outlet surgery → worse UI**; desmopressin for nocturia
- **Progressive supranuclear palsy:** vertical gaze palsy; **impaired contraction**; Onuf loss

## Spinal Cord

- **Multiple sclerosis (MS):** female ≥2:1; LUTS **50–90%**; UI 37–72%; **DO 34–99%**; **DSD 30–65%**; areflexia 12–38%; ~10% normal
- **MS presenting LUTS up to 15%** (acute retention/urgency)
- **MS complication risks:** **DSD in men, high filling pressure, indwelling catheter**
- **MS early: antimuscarinic + CIC**; **Credé/Valsalva contraindicated**; indwelling → **suprapubic**
- **Spinal cord injury (SCI):** 54/million/year; **80% male**; mean age 42; **incomplete tetraplegia commonest**
- **Sacral cord at column T12–L1; cord ends ~L2**
- **Death now: pneumonia, septicaemia** (not renal)
- **SCI stones:** ~7% in 10 years; **peak first 3 months**; **98% apatite/struvite**
- **Spinal shock:** closed bladder neck; guarding reflex absent; **bulbocavernosus may persist**
- **DSD consequences:** complete > incomplete; continuous > intermittent; **male > female**
- **DSD type 1 → incomplete; types 2, 3 → complete**; not level
- **Sphincterotomy/sphincter toxin:** only if CIC impossible/refused
- **Low compliance <12.5 mL/cm H₂O**; **leak point pressure ≥75 cm H₂O** → upper tract damage
- **DO > ⅓ of filling** → renal damage
- **Thoracolumbar (T10–L2) lesions least predictable**
- **Autonomic dysreflexia (AD):** **cervical 60% vs thoracic 20%**; ≥20 mm Hg systolic rise
- **AD triggers:** bladder/rectal distension; **bradycardia typical**
- **AD prophylaxis: terazosin, prazosin**; nifedipine banned in many centres
- **AD intractable:** sacral rhizotomy
- **Vesicoureteral reflux (VUR) in SCI 17–25%**; **first fix pressures, not reflux**
- **Urethral catheter worst** for upper tracts
- **VUR artefact:** capacity ↑, pressure ↓ on urodynamics
- **Myelomeningocele:** ~90% of cystica; **1/1000**; LUT dysfunction ~90%; **lumbosacral 47%**
- **Myelodysplasia typical:** **areflexic + open bladder neck**; DSD 10–15%
- **Dysraphic SUI:** **bulking safer than sling/artificial urinary sphincter**
- **Tethered cord:** 3–15% of myelomeningocele; **urinary recovery lags** (~4 months)
- **Sensory neurogenic bladder:** tabes, pernicious anaemia/subacute combined degeneration; **motor:** polio

## Distal to the Cord

- **Disc prolapse central in 1–15%**; L4–5, L5–S1; **S2–S4 perineal** sensory loss
- **Disc:** **areflexic, normally compliant** bladder
- **Cauda equina:** 1–5% of disc prolapses; **somatic recovers before detrusor**
- **Radical pelvic surgery:** abdominoperineal resection 20–68%, radical hysterectomy 16–80%; **permanent 15–20%**
- **Radical pelvic:** open bladder neck + **fixed striated tone** + ↓ compliance; **bethanechol supersensitivity +**
- **Post-pelvic surgery: CIC and wait 6–12 months**
- **Nerve-sparing radical hysterectomy** ↓ LUTS (randomised trial)
- **EPINCONT:** caesarean ↓ moderate–severe UI; **UUI equal**
- **Zoster:** retention days–weeks post-rash; **resolves 1–2 months**
- **Elsberg syndrome:** herpes simplex sacral radiculitis + **cerebrospinal fluid pleocytosis**
- **Diabetes:** commonest peripheral neuropathy; **↓ sensation → ↑ capacity → ↓ contractility → ↑ PVR**
- **Diabetes vs BOO:** **pressure–flow** distinguishes
- **Diabetes: timed voiding**
- **Guillain–Barré syndrome:** demyelinating 85%; LUT 25–80%; retention 11–30%; **reversible therapy**

## Miscellaneous Neurologic Diseases

- **AIDS neurogenic LUTS:** ~40% dead within 8 months
- **HTLV-1 myelopathy:** DSD + DO; LUTS up to 60%
- **Schistosomal myelopathy:** DSD → upper tract damage

## Miscellaneous Related Conditions

- **Untreated DSD:** ~50% of men → complications
- **DLPP ≥40 cm H₂O after sphincterotomy = failure**
- **Blaivas type 1 DSD → observe** unless VUR, hydronephrosis, dysreflexia
- **DSD options:** CIC + DO control (preferred); stents **abandoned**
- **Hinman syndrome** = dysfunctional voiding, no neurology
- **Chronic retention:** **PVR ≥300 mL, ≥6 months, ≥2 occasions**; long-term → **suprapubic**
- **Primary bladder neck obstruction (men):** **bladder neck incision** definitive
- **Trapped prostate** (Turner-Warwick) → small transurethral resection of prostate (TURP)/incision incl. bladder neck
- **Female BOO (Groutz):** **Qmax ≤12 mL/s + Pdet@Qmax ≥20 cm H₂O**
- **Nitti:** fluoroscopic obstruction; **dysfunctional voiding commonest cause**
- **Prolapse:** test voiding with pelvic organ prolapse reduced before repair
- **Paruresis:** ambulatory urodynamics useful only **>40 years**
- **Fowler syndrome:** women <30; **capacity >1 L, no urge**; **complex repetitive discharges + decelerating bursts**; closure pressure >100 cm H₂O
- **Fowler treatment: sacral neuromodulation (SNM) (~70%)**; toxin/hormones fail
- **Post-op retention:** decompress 18–24 h after joint replacement; **phenoxybenzamine** helps; tamsulosin before spine surgery does not
- **Myasthenia gravis:** **post-prostatectomy UI ↑**
- **Radiation early reaction peak 4–6 weeks, recovers by 6 months**
- **Defunctionalised bladder transplant:** capacity **≥100 mL**, voiding pressure **≤100 cm H₂O**

## Evaluation, Surveillance and Treatment

- **Guidelines:** American Urological Association/Society of Urodynamics, Female Pelvic Medicine and Urogenital Reconstruction (AUA/SUFU) neurogenic lower urinary tract dysfunction (NLUTD) **2021**; European Association of Urology (EAU) Neuro-urology **2022**
- **Minimum:** history, examination, urinalysis; **PVR if voiding**
- **Low risk (all):** suprapontine/peripheral, spontaneous void, low PVR, stable → **no routine tests**
- **High risk (any):** poor compliance, ↑ storage pressure, DSD, VUR, hydronephrosis, abnormal renal function
- **Not low risk = unknown** until urodynamics + imaging + renal function
- **Defer assessment during spinal shock/acute brain injury**
- **Chronic catheter: suprapubic > urethral**
- **Hydrophilic catheters ↓ urinary tract infection (UTI)**
- **Neurogenic DO: drugs first-line → onabotulinumtoxinA**
- **Posterior tibial nerve stimulation (PTNS):** spontaneous voiders; **SNM:** NLUTD without SCI/spina bifida
- **UTI:** treat only symptomatic; **≥5 days; 7–14 relapse**; **no prophylaxis with CIC/indwelling**
- **Bladder cancer:** risk = **catheter, stones, UTI**; often **muscle-invasive**
- **AUA/SUFU: no screening cystoscopy**
- **AUA/SUFU follow-up (moderate/high):** yearly renal function; **imaging every 1–2 years**
- **Bowel reconstruction:** annual electrolytes, renal function, imaging
- **American Paraplegia Society:** annual 5–10 years; **renal plasma flow fall >20% → investigate**
- **EAU:** dipstick 2-monthly; **ultrasound 6-monthly**; specialist review 1–2 years

<div class="traps" markdown="1">
Classic traps

- **Trap:** EMG flare in stroke/PD = DSD — **Actually:** pseudodyssynergia
- **Trap:** TURP contraindicated in PD — **Actually:** MSA; confirm PD first
- **Trap:** Neurologic level predicts bladder — **Actually:** urodynamics required
- **Trap:** Areflexia excludes suprasacral lesion — **Actually:** occurs in ~¼
- **Trap:** MS upper tracts behave like SCI — **Actually:** rarely damaged
- **Trap:** VUR must be corrected first — **Actually:** lower bladder pressure first
- **Trap:** Upper tracts safe without VUR — **Actually:** high pressure alone damages
- **Trap:** Prostatectomy for post-pelvic-surgery retention — **Actually:** only if BOO proven
- **Trap:** Diabetes = hypocontractile only — **Actually:** DO most frequent finding
- **Trap:** Catheterised NLUTD need screening cystoscopy — **Actually:** not recommended
</div>
